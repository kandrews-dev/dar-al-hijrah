Ḥadīth 20 of 42 · al-BukhārīYear 2

إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْت

If You Feel No Shame, Do as You Wish

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say who narrated this ḥadīth and where it is reported, and parse the sentence «إِنَّ مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاس» — where its khabar and its ism fall.
  • Give the two readings of «إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْت» — the one that returns to the doer and the one that returns to the act.
  • State the three categories into which what is transmitted from the earlier prophethood divides, and the ruling on each.
  • Use the story of Dāwūd to tell the slander of Banū Isrāʾīl from the real lesson of the āyāt.
  • Give the two divisions of ḥayāʾ — by object and by origin — and the one case in which ḥayāʾ is blameworthy.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٣٠–٢٣٤
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 230–234.

عَنْ أَبِي مَسْعُودٍ عُقْبَةَ بْنِ عَمْرٍو الأَنْصَارِيِّ البَدْرِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ: «إِنَّ مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاسُ مِنْ كَلَامِ النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى: إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْتَ». رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ.

Abū Masʿūd ʿUqbah ibn ʿAmr al-Anṣārī al-Badrī (may Allah be pleased with him) said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Among what people have grasped from the words of the earliest prophethood is: If you feel no shame, then do as you wish." Narrated by al-Bukhārī.

Narrated by Abū Masʿūd al-Badrī · رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ — al-Bukhārī, in his Ṣaḥīḥ (Kitāb al-Adab), so the ḥadīth is ṣaḥīḥ.

«إِنَّ مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاس» Reading the Sentence

The sentence is built so that its subject is a saying. «إِنَّ» is for emphasis; its khabar comes first — «مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاس» — and its ism is the quoted saying that follows: «إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْت», reported as speech. The sense is: among what people grasped from the words of the earliest prophethood is this saying.

«مِمَّا» — the «مِنْ» is for partition: some of what people grasped, not all of it. And «النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى» means the earlier prophethood — the prophets who came before — for this word reached the people and was passed down among them.

مَا هُوَ الحَيَاء؟ What Ḥayāʾ Is

Ḥayāʾ is a reaction that arises in a person at doing what does not befit him and does not adorn him — he recoils within himself, and shame results. It is the inner brake that holds a person back from the ugly.

«إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْت» The Two Readings

The words carry two meanings, and the Shaykh keeps both. On one, the command is really a statement and a reproach; on the other, it is a genuine permission. The difference is whether the clause looks at the doer or at the act.

المَعْنَى الأَوَّل · returns to the doerIf you have no ḥayāʾ, you do whatever you want. Here the imperative stands in the place of a statement, and it is blame (ذَمّ): a man with no shame does what harms murūʾah and what does not, caring for nothing.
المَعْنَى الثَّانِي · returns to the actIf the act is not something to be ashamed of, then do it and do not mind. Here the imperative is for permissibility (إِبَاحَة): whatever carries no shame in it, there is no blame in doing — so do not leave it out of a false shame.
مَا يَبْقَى مِنَ الأُمَمِ السَّابِقَة What Reaches Us From Earlier Nations

First benefit: reports of the earlier nations may remain into this ummah — which is exactly what the ḥadīth says: that this word was grasped «مِنْ كَلَامِ النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى». What reaches us travels one of three roads: by revelation in the Qurʾān, by the Sunnah, or by what people pass down. And the Qurʾān itself points to the earliest scriptures:

إِنَّ هَٰذَا لَفِي الصُّحُفِ الْأُولَىٰ * صُحُفِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَمُوسَىٰ

Indeed, this is in the former scriptures — the scriptures of Ibrāhīm and Mūsā.

سُورَةُ الأَعْلَى · 87:18–19

But what is passed down from the earlier prophethood is not all of one rank. The Shaykh divides it into three, and this division is the justice (العَدْل) in dealing with it:

١ · شَهِدَ شَرْعُنَا بِصِحَّتِهWhat our own sharīʿah testifies is true — then it is ṣaḥīḥ, accepted (صَحِيحٌ مَقْبُول).
٢ · شَهِدَ شَرْعُنَا بِبُطْلَانِهWhat our sharīʿah testifies is false — then it is bāṭil, rejected (بَاطِلٌ مَرْدُود).
٣ · لَمْ يَرِدْ شَرْعُنَا بِتَأْيِيدٍ وَلَا تَفْنِيدWhat our sharīʿah neither confirms nor denies — then we withhold (نَتَوَقَّفُ فِيه); there is no harm in mentioning it in admonition, but it is neither believed nor denied.
قِصَّةُ دَاوُدَ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام The Story of Dāwūd — Slander and Truth

A worked example of the third category. Banū Isrāʾīl told of Dāwūd ﷺ a vile tale — that he coveted the wife of one of his soldiers and sent the man to the front to be killed so he could take her. This is a lie: it does not occur from the common of people, let alone a prophet; it contradicts the ʿiṣmah of the prophets and the justice they were sent with. What contradicts the prophets’ protection and character, we know to be false.

So what did the āyāt really tell? Two disputants climbed the wall of his prayer-chamber and startled him; one said the other had ninety-nine ewes to his one, and had pressured him in speech:

إِنَّ هَٰذَا أَخِي لَهُ تِسْعٌ وَتِسْعُونَ نَعْجَةً وَلِيَ نَعْجَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ

Indeed this is my brother; he has ninety-nine ewes, and I have one ewe.

سُورَةُ ص · Ṣād 38:23

Dāwūd answered at once that the man had wronged him — and in that lay the test, for a judge may not rule by one party’s word before hearing the other:

لَقَدْ ظَلَمَكَ بِسُؤَالِ نَعْجَتِكَ إِلَىٰ نِعَاجِهِ

He has certainly wronged you in demanding your ewe [to add] to his ewes.

سُورَةُ ص · Ṣād 38:24

The real matter the āyāt point to was a slip in three things, not the lie of Banū Isrāʾīl:

(1) He secluded himself in his chamber away from judging between the people, though Allah had made him a khalīfah to rule. (2) He locked the door, forcing the disputants to climb the wall, with the risk of harm. (3) He ruled for one before hearing the other. So he knew Allah had tested him — «وَظَنَّ دَاوُودُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّاهُ فَاسْتَغْفَرَ رَبَّهُ وَخَرَّ رَاكِعًا وَأَنَاب» — and he sought forgiveness, bowed, and turned back to Allah.

وَظَنَّ دَاوُودُ أَنَّمَا فَتَنَّاهُ فَاسْتَغْفَرَ رَبَّهُ وَخَرَّ رَاكِعًا وَأَنَابَ

And Dāwūd realised that We had tried him, so he asked forgiveness of his Lord and fell down bowing and turned in repentance.

سُورَةُ ص · Ṣād 38:24

الثَّنَاءُ عَلَى الحَيَاء The Praise of Ḥayāʾ, and Its Divisions

On either reading, the ḥadīth praises ḥayāʾ — the Prophet ﷺ affirmed: «الحَيَاءُ شُعْبَةٌ مِنَ الإِيمَان». And ḥayāʾ divides two ways.

First, by what it is owed to:

حَيَاءٌ مِنَ اللهToward the right of Allah: that you be ashamed for Allah to see you where He forbade you, or to miss you where He commanded you.
حَيَاءٌ مِنَ الخَلْقToward creation: that you refrain from all that breaks murūʾah and good character. In a gathering of knowledge, a man in the front row stretching out his legs is no ḥayāʾ — it breaks murūʾah; yet among his friends it does not, though to ask leave is better.

Second, by where it comes from:

غَرِيزِيٌّ طَبِيعِيّInnate — Allah grants some people ḥayāʾ from childhood; they speak and act only at need. This is the better of the two.
مُكْتَسَبAcquired — a bold person trains himself and keeps the company of people of ḥayāʾ and goodness, and takes it from them.

One limit on the praise: ḥayāʾ is a praised character except when it prevents an obligation or drives one into the forbidden. If shyness keeps a man from forbidding an evil he is obliged to forbid, that is blameworthy ḥayāʾ — forbid the evil and do not mind, by its proper levels and conditions. The praised ḥayāʾ is the one that never drops a duty and never falls into a ḥarām.

الرَّدُّ عَلَى الجَبْرِيَّة A Refutation of the Jabriyyah

A last benefit, from the single word «شِئْت». The servant is addressed with a real will of his own — «do as you wish» — which affirms the servant’s mashīʾah and refutes the Jabriyyah, who claim a person is compelled in his deeds with no will at all. He acts by his own choice, under the will of Allah.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
الحَيَاءُ انْفِعَالٌ يَحْدُثُ لِلْإِنْسَانِ عِنْدَ فِعْلِ مَا لَا يُجَمِّلُهُ وَلَا يُزَيِّنُهُ. Ḥayāʾ is a reaction that arises in a person at doing what does not befit or adorn him.
«مِنْ» فِي «مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاس» لِلتَّبْعِيضِ، أَيْ بَعْضُ مَا أَدْرَكُوه. The «مِنْ» in «some of what people grasped» is for partition — part of what they grasped.
كُلُّ مَا يُنْقَلُ عَنِ النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى يُقْبَلُ وَيُصَدَّقُ بِهِ عَلَى كُلِّ حَال. Everything transmitted from the earlier prophethood is accepted and believed in every case.
مَا شَهِدَ شَرْعُنَا بِبُطْلَانِهِ مِنْ أَخْبَارِ الأُمَمِ فَهُوَ بَاطِلٌ مَرْدُود. What our sharīʿah testifies to be false, of the reports of the nations, is false and rejected.
مَا رَوَتْهُ بَنُو إِسْرَائِيلَ عَنْ دَاوُدَ مِمَّا يُنَافِي عِصْمَةَ الأَنْبِيَاءِ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُ كَذِب. What Banū Isrāʾīl narrated about Dāwūd that contradicts the ʿiṣmah of the prophets, we know to be a lie.
يَجُوزُ لِلْحَاكِمِ أَنْ يَحْكُمَ بِقَوْلِ أَحَدِ الخَصْمَيْنِ قَبْلَ سَمَاعِ الآخَر. A judge may rule by the word of one of the two disputants before hearing the other.
الحَيَاءُ خُلُقٌ مَحْمُودٌ إِلَّا إِذَا مَنَعَ مِنْ وَاجِبٍ أَوْ أَوْقَعَ فِي مُحَرَّم. Ḥayāʾ is a praised character except when it prevents an obligation or drives one into the forbidden.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

إِنَّ مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاسُ مِنْ كَلَامِ النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى: إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْتَ

A word so old it reached this ummah from the earliest prophethood. It carries two truths at once: if a man has no shame, he does whatever he wants — a reproach that returns to the doer; and whatever carries no shame, there is no blame in doing — a permission that returns to the act. Around it the Shaykh gathers the test of Dāwūd and the justice of hearing both sides, the praise of ḥayāʾ toward Allah and toward people, and — in «mā shiʾt» — the servant’s own will, against those who say he is compelled. Ḥayāʾ is a branch of faith; keep the shame that keeps you.

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
