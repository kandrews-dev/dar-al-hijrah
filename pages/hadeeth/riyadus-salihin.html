رِيَاضُ الصَّالِحِين

The Gardens of the Righteous — an Introduction

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Know what Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn is and who compiled it.
  • Understand how the book is organised — by topics of character and worship, chapter by chapter.
  • Know how each chapter is built: Qurʾānic āyāt first, then the aḥādīth.
  • Meet the book's opening theme — sincerity — and a representative ḥadīth on good character.
ما هو الكتاب؟ What Is Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn?

Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn ("The Gardens of the Righteous") is a collection of authentic aḥādīth compiled by Imam Yaḥyā ibn Sharaf al-Nawawī (d. 676 AH) — the same scholar behind the Forty Ḥadīth. Where the Forty gathers the foundations of the religion, Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn gathers about 1,900 aḥādīth arranged under hundreds of topics — sincerity, patience, truthfulness, mercy, good character, remembrance of Allah, guarding the tongue, the rights of neighbours and parents, and much more. Because he chose almost entirely authentic reports and arranged them so practically, it became — for centuries and across the world — the most widely taught book for building a Muslim's character and daily worship. It is the perfect companion to the Forty: one shapes what you believe, the other shapes how you live.

كيف تُبنى الأبواب How Each Chapter Is Built

Al-Nawawī opens almost every chapter (bāb) the same way: first he gathers the relevant āyāt of the Qurʾān on the theme, then he lists the aḥādīth. This models a principle of our religion — the Sunnah walks beside the Qurʾān, explaining and applying it. A student who reads a chapter comes away with both the Book of Allah and the words of His Messenger ﷺ on that single subject, gathered in one place.

باب الإخلاص The Opening Theme — Sincerity

Fittingly, the very first chapter is بَاب الإخلاص — sincerity and having a true intention — because no deed is accepted without it. Al-Nawawī opens it with āyāt such as وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ ("They were commanded only to worship Allah, sincere to Him in religion" — al-Bayyinah 98:5), and then the first ḥadīth of the whole book is the ḥadīth of intentions — "Actions are but by intentions" — which we study in Ḥadīth 1. The book begins where every deed begins: in the heart.

حديث من الكتاب A Taste — a Ḥadīth on Character

عَنْ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ عَمْرٍو رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا عَنِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ قَالَ: «الْمُسْلِمُ مَنْ سَلِمَ الْمُسْلِمُونَ مِنْ لِسَانِهِ وَيَدِهِ، وَالْمُهَاجِرُ مَنْ هَجَرَ مَا نَهَى اللَّهُ عَنْهُ».

"The Muslim is the one from whose tongue and hand the Muslims are safe; and the emigrant (muhājir) is the one who abandons what Allah has forbidden."

Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr · al-Bukhārī & Muslim

This ḥadīth captures the spirit of Riyāḍ al-Ṣāliḥīn. True Islam is not only rituals — it shows in how a person treats others: guarding the tongue from insult, lies, and backbiting, and the hand from harm and injustice, so that those around him are safe from him. And it redefines hijrah: beyond the migration of the body, the greater migration is to leave every sin Allah has forbidden. Chapter by chapter, this is the character the book plants in the heart.

Memorize & Renew — اِحْفَظْ وجدّد

الْمُسْلِمُ مَنْ سَلِمَ الْمُسْلِمُونَ مِنْ لِسَانِهِ وَيَدِهِ
