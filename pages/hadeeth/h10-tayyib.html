Ḥadīth 10 of 42 · Muslim

طِيبُ الْمَطْعَمِ وَإِجَابَةُ الدُّعَاءِ

Allah Is Pure and Accepts Only the Pure

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Memorize the opening of the ḥadīth: إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا.
  • Understand طَيِّب (pure/good) as a description of Allah, and that He accepts only pure earnings, pure food, and pure deeds.
  • Identify the two āyāt quoted in the ḥadīth — al-Muʼminūn 23:51 and al-Baqarah 2:172 — and the honour they carry for the believers.
  • Unpack the image of the traveller: the four causes of answered دعاء that were present, and the single impediment that blocked them all.
  • Take the practical fruit home: purify your income before you polish your words.

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قَالَ رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا، وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ فَقَالَ: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ كُلُوا مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَاعْمَلُوا صَالِحًا﴾ وَقَالَ: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ﴾ ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ الرَّجُلَ يُطِيلُ السَّفَرَ أَشْعَثَ أَغْبَرَ يَمُدُّ يَدَيْهِ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ: يَا رَبِّ يَا رَبِّ، وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ وَغُذِيَ بِالْحَرَامِ، فَأَنَّى يُسْتَجَابُ لِذَلِكَ» رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ.

Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Allah, Most High, is ṭayyib (pure and good) and accepts only what is ṭayyib. And Allah commanded the believers with what He commanded the Messengers, saying: 'O Messengers, eat of the good things and do righteousness' (al-Muʼminūn 23:51), and He said: 'O you who believe, eat of the good things We have provided you' (al-Baqarah 2:172). Then he ﷺ mentioned the man who lengthens his journey, dishevelled and dusty, stretching his hands to the sky — 'O my Lord, O my Lord' — while his food is haram, his drink is haram, his clothing is haram, and he has been nourished on haram: so how can he be answered?"

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah · Muslim

الراوي أبو هريرة The Narrator — Abū Hurayrah

أبو هريرة (Abū Hurayrah) — his name is most famously given as عبد الرحمن بن صخر الدوسي — is the most prolific narrator of ḥadīth in the entire Ummah. He accepted Islam in the year of خيبر and accompanied the Prophet ﷺ for only about four years, yet in those years he did what no one else did: he gave up trade and land entirely, lived among أهل الصُّفَّة — the poor students who slept in the masjid — and clung to the Prophet ﷺ on a full stomach or an empty one, memorizing everything he heard. He once complained of forgetting, so the Prophet ﷺ told him to spread out his cloak, made a movement over it, and told him to gather it to himself — and after that, he said, he never forgot a thing he heard. It is fitting that the ḥadīth of pure sustenance comes through a man who chose hunger with the Prophet ﷺ over wealth without him.

إِنَّ اللهَ طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا Allah Is Ṭayyib — and Accepts Only the Ṭayyib

طَيِّب (pure, good) here is a description of Allah, Most High: He is perfect and pure, exalted above every deficiency, defect, and need. And because He is طَيِّب, He accepts only what is طَيِّب. Ibn Rajab in جامع العلوم والحكم and Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn in his sharḥ draw the meaning out in three circles:

  1. Pure earnings — wealth gathered from what Allah made halal, free of theft, deception, and الرِّبَا (usury). Charity from stolen or haram wealth is not accepted; Allah does not accept a "gift" of what He forbade.
  2. Pure food and drink — what enters the body must be halal in itself and halal in how it was obtained, for it becomes the very flesh the worshipper stands with.
  3. Pure deeds — a deed is طَيِّب only when it is cleansed of شِرْك and رِيَاء (showing off) and done for Allah alone. This is الإخلاص (sincerity), the lesson of the very first ḥadīth of the Forty — see الحديث الأول — النية.

So the ḥadīth binds the outer and the inner together: the طَيِّب Lord accepts the طَيِّب deed, fed by the طَيِّب morsel, bought with the طَيِّب dirham. Break the chain at any link and acceptance is endangered.

أَمَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ Commanded with the Very Command of the Messengers

The Prophet ﷺ then recites two āyāt. To the Messengers, Allah said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الرُّسُلُ كُلُوا مِنَ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَاعْمَلُوا صَالِحًا﴾ — "O Messengers, eat of the good things and do righteousness" (al-Muʼminūn 23:51). And to the believers He said: ﴿يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُلُوا مِنْ طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ﴾ — "O you who believe, eat of the good things We have provided you" (al-Baqarah 2:172).

Ibn Rajab points out the treasure hidden in placing these two āyāt side by side: the believers were commanded with the very command given to the Messengers themselves. This is an honour — you are addressed with the address of the Prophets — and it is a standard: if the Messengers, the purest of creation, were commanded to eat only the طَيِّبَات and to work righteousness, then no believer beneath them is excused from it. Notice also how each āyah joins eating and deeds in one breath — كُلُوا then وَاعْمَلُوا — a subtle sign that the purity of the morsel and the soundness of the deed rise and fall together.

الرجل يُطِيلُ السَّفَرَ — أسباب الإجابة وموانعها The Traveller — Causes of Answered Duʿāʼ, and What Blocks Them

Then the Prophet ﷺ paints a picture the scholars never tire of unpacking. A man on a long journey, أَشْعَث (dishevelled) and أَغْبَر (dust-covered), stretches both hands to the sky crying يَا رَبِّ يَا رَبِّ. Count what he has in his favour — Ibn Rajab and Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn count four causes of answered دعاء, all present at once:

  1. Long travel — the دعاء of the traveller is among those given special hope of response.
  2. Brokenness and humility — dishevelled and dusty, stripped of adornment, in the state of one desperate before his Lord.
  3. Raising the hands — stretching the hands to the sky is itself a cause Allah loves to answer.
  4. Calling on Allah by His lordship — يَا رَبِّ يَا رَبِّ, repeated, pleading by the Name that carries creation, provision, and care.

And yet: وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ وَغُذِيَ بِالْحَرَامِ — his food, his drink, his clothing, his very nourishment, all haram. So the Prophet ﷺ says: فَأَنَّى يُسْتَجَابُ لِذَلِكَ — "so how can he be answered?" The scholars take from this that دعاء has causes that pull the answer near and impediments (موانع) that push it away — and haram sustenance is among the heaviest impediments of all. Four engines of acceptance were running, and one impediment stalled them. The question فَأَنَّى is deeming the answer far-fetched, not declaring it forever impossible — Allah answers whom He wills — but the man has himself thrown a wall between his cry and the sky.

Connect this across your studies: the manners and times of دعاء and ذِكْر are taught in the Islam 101 lesson الأذكار — Daily Remembrance; and the gravest doorway of haram earnings, الرِّبَا, is warned against in the Fiqh study of money and trade — the Qurʼan's decisive word on it, أَحَلَّ اللهُ الْبَيْعَ وَحَرَّمَ الرِّبَا "Allah has permitted trade and forbidden usury" (al-Baqarah 2:275), is taught with its vocabulary in the Arabic lesson المال والتجارة — Money & Trade.

A gem from Ibn Rajab — فائدة

Ibn Rajab draws the practical fruit with both hands: eating halal is itself a cause of answered supplication, and consuming haram is a cause of its rejection. So the one who wants his دعاء answered should begin long before he raises his hands — purify the income, purify the plate, purify the wardrobe — and only then polish the words. Many people labour over beautiful phrases and chosen hours while their earnings are untouched by review; this ḥadīth turns the order right side up. Purify what feeds you, and the shortest cry of يَا رَبِّ from halal flesh travels further than the longest litany from haram.

Memorize & Renew — احفظ وجدّد

إِنَّ اللَّهَ تَعَالَى طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا

"Allah, Most High, is ṭayyib (pure and good) and accepts only what is ṭayyib." Memorize it word-perfect, and let it audit your life from the plate upward: before you raise your hands to ask, ask yourself what fed the hands you are raising.
