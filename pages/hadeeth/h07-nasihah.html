Ḥadīth 7 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَة

The Religion Is Sincere Counsel

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Memorize the ḥadīth of تميم الداري: «الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ» with its question and answer.
  • Understand the root ن-ص-ح — purity and sincerity, like honey purified of its wax — and why "advice" alone falls short.
  • Unpack the five recipients of النصيحة one by one: Allah, His Book, His Messenger ﷺ, the leaders, and the common Muslims.
  • Learn the Salafi manhaj of advising the Muslim leaders: respectful, private, without rebellion.
  • See why some scholars counted this ḥadīth among the few upon which the whole religion turns.

عَنْ أَبِي رُقَيَّةَ تَمِيمِ بْنِ أَوْسٍ الدَّارِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ قَالَ: «الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ» قُلْنَا: لِمَنْ؟ قَالَ: «لِلَّهِ وَلِكِتَابِهِ وَلِرَسُولِهِ وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ» رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ.

On the authority of Abū Ruqayyah Tamīm ibn Aws al-Dārī (may Allah be pleased with him): the Prophet ﷺ said: "The religion is naṣīḥah (sincere counsel)." We said: to whom? He said: "To Allah, and to His Book, and to His Messenger, and to the leaders of the Muslims, and to their common folk." Narrated by Muslim.

Narrated by Tamīm al-Dārī · Muslim

الراوي تميم الداري The Narrator — Tamīm al-Dārī

تميم بن أوس الداري (Tamīm ibn Aws al-Dārī), whose kunyah أبو رقية comes from his daughter رقية, was a Christian before Islam and accepted the religion in the ninth year after the Hijrah. Allah honoured him twice over: he came to the truth after a lifetime in another faith, and once he held it he clung to it with famous devotion — he was known for standing long in night prayer and for his recitation of the Qur'an. It is through him that Muslim narrates the great account of الجساسة concerning الدجال (the Dajjāl, the false messiah) — a rare case where the Prophet ﷺ related a report from a Companion to the people. He later settled in الشام. How fitting that the ḥadīth defining the religion as pure sincerity comes through a man whose own path to Islam was a story of sincere searching.

معنى النصيحة — كلمة جامعة The Meaning of Naṣīḥah — a Comprehensive Word

Do not translate النَّصِيحَة as mere "advice" and move on — the word is far bigger than that. Its root ن-ص-ح carries the meaning of purity and freedom from every impurity. The Arabs said نَصَحَ الْعَسَلَ of honey that has been purified — strained clean of its wax until nothing remains but what benefits. So النصيحة is a relationship purified of deceit, cheating, envy, and hidden motives: you sincerely want good for the one you turn to, outwardly and inwardly, and you act on that wanting. Ibn Rajab in جامع العلوم والحكم relays from the early scholars that النصيحة is كلمة جامعة — a comprehensive word gathering the giving of every kind of good to the one it is due. Advice-giving is only one small branch of it.

Now feel the weight of the opening: الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ — "the religion is naṣīḥah." The Prophet ﷺ did not say the religion contains some sincerity; he made the one stand for the other, the way he ﷺ said «الْحَجُّ عَرَفَة» ("Ḥajj is ʿArafah") — naming the whole by its greatest pillar. Sincerity is not a garnish on the religion; it is its substance. Then the Companions, ever hungry for detail, asked لِمَنْ؟ — "to whom?" — and the answer laid out five recipients in a deliberate order, from the Creator down to the humblest believer. Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn notes in his sharḥ that whoever fulfils these five has fulfilled the religion itself.

النصيحة لله ولكتابه ولرسوله ﷺ Sincerity to Allah, to His Book, and to His Messenger ﷺ

لِلَّهِ — to Allah. This is the head of them all: singling Him out with التوحيد (tawḥīd, pure monotheism), believing in Him without any partner, worshipping Him with الإخلاص (sincerity) so that no share of your worship goes to another, obeying His commands, avoiding what He forbade, loving for His sake and hating for His sake, and being truthful with Him in private exactly as you appear in public. Note carefully — as Ibn Rajab points out — that Allah is in no need of anyone's counsel; the benefit of this النصيحة returns entirely to the servant himself. Allah is الغني (the Self-Sufficient); we are the ones purified by sincerity to Him.

وَلِكِتَابِهِ — to His Book. Sincerity to the Qur'an is to believe that it is the Speech of Allah, revealed not created; to recite it properly and regularly, giving its letters their due; to seek to understand it as the Companions understood it; to act upon its commands and stop at its limits; and to defend it against the distortions of those who twist its meanings. A person who owns a beautiful muṣḥaf he never opens, or recites without ever obeying, has not yet given the Book its نصيحة.

وَلِرَسُولِهِ — to His Messenger ﷺ. In his lifetime this meant believing him, aiding him, and defending him. After his passing it means obeying his command, believing every report he gave, loving him above all creation, sending ṣalāh upon him, honouring his Companions and family, and — the very core — following his Sunnah as it is, without adding to it or subtracting from it. Whoever invents new worship in his ﷺ name has not honoured him; he has implied the Messenger left the religion incomplete. This is why sincerity to the Messenger ﷺ and the rejection of البدعة (innovation in the religion) are two faces of one coin — revisit Ḥadīth 5 — رد البدعة, where the scales of الإخلاص and المتابعة were set. The truest lover of the Prophet ﷺ is the closest follower of his Sunnah, not the loudest claimant.

النصيحة لأئمة المسلمين وعامتهم Sincerity to the Leaders of the Muslims and to Their Common Folk

وَلِأَئِمَّةِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ — to the leaders of the Muslims. The أئمة (leaders) are of two kinds: the scholars, who lead by knowledge, and the rulers, who lead by authority. Sincerity to the scholars is to love them, take knowledge from them, and defend their honour. Sincerity to the rulers is to obey them in what is good, pray behind them, make duʿāʾ for their rectification, help them upon truth, and — when they err — advise them privately and respectfully, not by public denunciation from pulpits and platforms that breeds hatred and chaos. This is the manhaj of the Salaf which Imāms like Ibn Bāz and al-ʿUthaymīn spent their lives teaching: النصيحة is given in secret between you and him, for the aim is his correction, not his humiliation — and rebellion against the Muslim ruler is not نصيحة at all but the very door of bloodshed the Sharīʿah slammed shut. The one who truly wants good for the leaders wants their guidance, not their downfall.

وَعَامَّتِهِمْ — and to their common folk. This is sincerity to every ordinary Muslim: loving for them what you love for yourself, teaching the ignorant among them, commanding the good and forbidding the evil with الرفق (gentleness), concealing their faults, giving honest counsel when they seek it, keeping your dealings with them free of cheating and deception, and holding back your tongue and hand from their honour, wealth, and blood. Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn reminds us that this recipient is the one you meet a hundred times a day — the seller, the neighbour, the brother at the masjid — so it is here that your claim to النصيحة is tested most.

A gem from the scholars — فائدة

The scholars gave this ḥadīth an extraordinary rank. Some counted it among the handful of ḥadīths upon which the fiqh of Islam turns — so that it forms a quarter, or by other countings a third, of the religion — and Ibn Rajab relays from some of the Imāms that the religion in its entirety returns to it, for there is no duty in Islam that is not, at root, sincerity to Allah, His Book, His Messenger ﷺ, or His servants. Test yourself with it: take any act of the religion — prayer, trade, speech, silence — and ask which of the five sincerities it serves. You will not find one outside them.
