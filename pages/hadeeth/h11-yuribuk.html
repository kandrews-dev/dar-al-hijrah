Ḥadīth 11 of 42 · al-Tirmidhī & al-Nasāʼī

دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ

Leave What Makes You Doubt

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Memorize the ḥadīth of الحسن بن علي: leave what makes you doubt for what does not make you doubt.
  • Meet the narrator — the Prophet's ﷺ grandson, a child Companion whose word حَفِظْتُ ("I memorised") makes him a model for every young student of this site.
  • Grasp the rule: certainty is not left for doubt — and see how this ḥadīth is the practical twin of Ḥadīth 6 on the doubtful matters.
  • Unpack الريب — the unease the sound heart feels — and its link to truthfulness, tranquility, and الورع (scrupulousness).
  • Apply the compass move to everyday life: doubtful income, doubtful food, doubtful words — and know the difference between الورع and الوسوسة.

عَنْ أَبِي مُحَمَّدٍ الْحَسَنِ بْنِ عَلِيِّ بْنِ أَبِي طَالِبٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمَا قَالَ: حَفِظْتُ مِنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ»

On the authority of Abū Muḥammad al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (may Allah be pleased with them both) who said: I memorised from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: "Leave what makes you doubt for what does not make you doubt."

Narrated by al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī · al-Tirmidhī & al-Nasāʼī · ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ

الراوي الحسن بن علي The Narrator — the Prophet's ﷺ Grandson

الحسن بن علي بن أبي طالب (al-Ḥasan ibn ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib) is the Prophet's ﷺ own grandson — the son of علي and فاطمة, born in the year 3 after the hijrah. The wording رضي الله عنهما — "may Allah be pleased with them both" — embraces him and his father together, just as it did for النعمان بن بشير in Ḥadīth 6. His grandfather ﷺ loved him openly, carried him on the minbar, and said of him: «إِنَّ ابْنِي هَذَا سَيِّدٌ» — "this son of mine is a سيد (a noble master), and perhaps Allah will make peace through him between two great parties of Muslims" (al-Bukhārī). Allah fulfilled that prophecy in the year 41, when الحسن gave up the caliphate to spare Muslim blood — a year the Ummah named عام الجماعة (the Year of Unity).

Now listen to how the ḥadīth opens: حَفِظْتُ — "I memorised from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ." He was a child of about seven when his grandfather ﷺ passed away, yet these words were already fixed in his young memory, letter for letter, and through him they reached the whole Ummah. The child memoriser is not a footnote in this religion — he is a chain-bearer. And al-Ḥasan lived the meaning he memorised: al-Bukhārī and Muslim relate that as a small boy he once popped a date from the صدقة (charity) dates into his mouth, and the Prophet ﷺ made him spit it out — "do you not know that we do not eat the ṣadaqah?" He grew up tasting, on his own tongue, the discipline of leaving what is not clearly yours. If you are young and memorising on this site: حَفِظْتُ is your word too.

القاعدة: اليقين لا يُترك للشك The Rule — Certainty Is Not Left for Doubt

Four Arabic words — دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ — carry an entire method. Ibn Rajab explains in جامع العلوم والحكم that the ḥadīth commands you, whenever a matter stirs الريب (doubt and unease) in you, to step across to the side you are sure of. Do not stand wobbling on the shaky plank when a firm one lies beside it. Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn puts it as a rule you can hold in one hand: what you are certain about is a foundation; what you merely doubt is not — so build on certainty and leave the doubt behind. From this ḥadīth the jurists derived the famous maxim الْيَقِينُ لَا يَزُولُ بِالشَّكِّ (certainty is not removed by doubt): if you are sure you made wuḍūʼ and only suspect you broke it, your wuḍūʼ stands; if you doubt whether you prayed three rakʿahs or four, you build on the number you are sure of.

See how precisely this completes Ḥadīth 6. There, النعمان بن بشير gave you the map: a clear halal region, a clear haram region, and between them the fog of المشتبهات (the doubtful matters), with the shepherd warned away from the edge of the sanctuary. But a map alone does not move your feet. This ḥadīth is the compass move: when you find yourself standing in the fog, دَعْ — step from the side that unsettles you to the side that does not. Ḥadīth 6 told you the doubtful zone exists and is dangerous; Ḥadīth 11 tells you exactly what to do the moment you meet it. The scholars therefore count this short ḥadīth among the foundations of الورع (scrupulousness) — the map and the compass, memorised together by a grandson and a governor's son, both of them children when they heard.

الريب وطمأنينة القلب What Is al-Rayb? — the Unease of the Sound Heart

الريب (al-rayb) is not plain الشك (doubt). الشك is the mind wavering between two possibilities; الريب is that wavering plus a knot of unease — the low hum of disquiet a sound heart gives off when something is not right. The Qurʼān opens with its negation: ذَٰلِكَ الْكِتَابُ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ — "that Book, there is no doubt in it." Ibn Rajab connects our ḥadīth to the Prophet's ﷺ other wording: الْإِثْمُ مَا حَاكَ فِي النَّفْسِ — "sin is what wavers in the soul and which you would hate people to see." The believing heart, kept alive by إيمان and knowledge, is itself an instrument: it settles upon the pure and clenches at the murky. In al-Tirmidhī's fuller narration of this very ḥadīth the Prophet ﷺ continues: فَإِنَّ الصِّدْقَ طُمَأْنِينَةٌ وَإِنَّ الْكَذِبَ رِيبَةٌ — "for truthfulness is طمأنينة (tranquility) and lying is ريبة (unease)." Truth sits still in the chest; falsehood fidgets.

Two cautions from the people of knowledge. First: the heart is only a reliable gauge when it is sound — a heart deadened by sins feels no knot at all, and a heart empty of knowledge may feel knots in the wrong places. So the compass of the chest works alongside the map of the Book and the Sunnah, never instead of it: when the ruling is known, there is no "but my heart is fine with it." Second: الريب the ḥadīth speaks of is a real unease with a real cause — it is not الوسوسة (obsessive whispering). The one who doubts clearly pure water for the tenth time, or re-washes a clean garment "just in case," is not practising الورع; he is feeding a whisper, and the cure for him is the opposite — ignore it and build on certainty. الورع leaves the genuinely murky; it does not manufacture murk in the clear.

تطبيقات يومية Everyday Applications — the Path with No Knot in It

Doubtful income. Two job offers: one plainly halal, one tangled with interest, deception, or "we're not really sure where that revenue comes from." Two transactions: one clean, one with a clause nobody can quite explain. دَعْ — take the one that leaves no knot in your chest. You learned in Ḥadīth 10 that Allah is طيب (pure) and accepts only the pure, and that haram nourishment blocks duʿāʼ; this ḥadīth is how you choose the pure when the label is smudged. Doubtful food. Unclear slaughter, an ingredient you cannot verify, an invitation whose fare is questionable — and beside it an option you are sure of: step to the sure side. This is exactly what little الحسن was taught with a single ṣadaqah date.

Doubtful words. A story you are not certain is true — leave repeating it. A joke that might be mockery, a "white lie" that might smooth things over — remember: الصدق طمأنينة والكذب ريبة. If you are unsure whether a word is accurate, fair, and safe for your brother's honour, the sure path is silence or better speech. In all three arenas the test is the same question الحسن memorised as a boy: which option leaves my heart at rest? Choose the path with no knot in it — and you will find, as Ibn Rajab says, that what you left for Allah's sake is repaid with a sweetness and a rest that the doubtful thing could never have given.

A gem from the Salaf — فائدة

Ibn Rajab fills pages of جامع العلوم والحكم with the Salaf living this ḥadīth, and the crown of them is أبو بكر الصديق. Al-Bukhārī relates that a servant of his once brought him food and he ate from it — then the servant told him it was earned through fortune-telling done back in الجاهلية (the pre-Islamic days of ignorance). أبو بكر put his hand into his mouth and vomited up everything he had eaten. He did not argue "but I ate it unknowingly" — the moment the ريبة appeared, he expelled it, refusing to let a single doubtful morsel settle in a body devoted to Allah. That is the ḥadīth of الحسن running in the veins: certainty kept, doubt cast out — even when it has already reached the stomach.

Memorize & Renew — احفظ وجدّد

حَفِظْتُ مِنْ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ: «دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لَا يَرِيبُكَ»

"I memorised from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: 'Leave what makes you doubt for what does not make you doubt.'" Four words from a grandson to the whole Ummah — memorise them word-perfect, as he did as a child. Then carry them like a compass: at every fork where one path has a knot in it and one does not, say دَعْ — and take the sure road.
