Ḥadīth 22 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَات

If I Pray the Prescribed Prayers… Shall I Enter Paradise?

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say who narrated this ḥadīth and where it is reported, and read the question word by word — «أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَات».
  • Give the shar‘ī definition of ṣiyām and the force of the qayd «تَعَبُّدًا لِله».
  • Give the two senses of «أَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَال», and when leaving the forbidden becomes an act of worship.
  • Answer the difficulty of the missing zakāh and ḥajj — why the man was still promised Paradise.
  • Explain «نَعَم» as a reiteration of the question, and name the aims of the Companions it reveals.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٣٨–٢٤٣
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 238–243.

عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ اللهِ جَابِرِ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللهِ الأَنْصَارِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّ رَجُلاً سَأَلَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ فَقَالَ: «أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَاتِ، وَصُمْتُ رَمَضَانَ، وَأَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَالَ، وَحَرَّمْتُ الحَرَامَ، وَلَمْ أَزِدْ عَلَى ذَلِكَ شَيْئًا، أَأَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ؟» قَالَ: «نَعَمْ». رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم. وَمَعْنَى «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَامَ» اجْتَنَبْتُهُ، وَمَعْنَى «أَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَالَ» فَعَلْتُهُ.

Abū ʿAbdillāh Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh al-Anṣārī (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that a man asked the Prophet ﷺ, "Tell me: if I pray the prescribed prayers, fast Ramaḍān, treat the lawful as lawful, treat the forbidden as forbidden, and add nothing more to that — shall I enter Paradise?" He said, "Yes." Narrated by Muslim. The meaning of "I treated the forbidden as forbidden" is: I avoided it; and "I treated the lawful as lawful" is: I did it.

Narrated by Jābir ibn ʿAbdillāh al-Anṣārī · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم — Muslim, in his Ṣaḥīḥ (Kitāb al-Īmān, bāb bayān al-īmān), so the ḥadīth is ṣaḥīḥ.

قِرَاءَةُ السُّؤَال Reading the Question

«أَرَأَيْتَ» means “inform me” (أَخْبِرْنِي). The man is asking the Prophet ﷺ to tell him the outcome of a settled, limited practice.

«إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَات» — the prescribed prayers are the five in the day and night, as Allah said; and what is beyond the five is not obligatory except for a cause that calls for it.

إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَوْقُوتًا

Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times.

سُورَةُ النِّسَاء · 4:103

«وَصُمْتُ رَمَضَان» — the fast of Ramaḍān. Ṣiyām in the language is imsāk — to hold back from anything.

In the sharī‘ah it is: to hold back from the things that break the fast, from the rising of dawn (طُلُوعِ الفَجْر) to the setting of the sun (غُرُوبِ الشَّمْس), as worship to Allah (تَعَبُّدًا لِله). The qayd “as worship” excludes one who holds back from food for a diet or his health — that is not a shar‘ī fast.

«أَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَالَ وَحَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام» Doing the Lawful, Leaving the Forbidden

“Aḥalla the thing” carries two senses — to believe it lawful, and to act upon it. So «أَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَال» is: I did the lawful believing it lawful; and «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام» is: I avoided the forbidden believing it forbidden.

And here leaving the forbidden can rise to worship. A man may avoid drinking khamr not because it is ḥarām, but only because his soul has no taste for it — no sin lies on him; yet if he leaves it believing it forbidden, leaving it for Allah, he is rewarded for it. Belief turns a bare abstention into an act of obedience.

A point beyond al-Nawawī’s wording. Al-Nawawī explained «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام» as “I avoided it” and «أَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَال» as “I did it,” without tying it to belief. But one must believe the forbidden is forbidden and the lawful is lawful — for if you do not believe the ḥarām is ḥarām you have not believed the shar‘ī ruling, and likewise the ḥalāl. So al-Nawawī’s explanation carries a touch of shortfall (قُصُور). Wallāhu aʿlam.

إِشْكَالُ الزَّكَاةِ وَالحَجّ The Difficulty: Zakāh and Ḥajj

The man said he added nothing more — yet he named neither zakāh nor ḥajj, two pillars of Islam, and still he was promised Paradise. Zakāh was obligated before fasting, so we cannot say the ḥadīth predates zakāh; ḥajj was obligated only in the ninth or tenth year, so it may predate ḥajj. How is this answered?

الزَّكَاةPerhaps the Prophet ﷺ knew the man was not one of wealth — and that, were he wealthy, he would pay it; for he said «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام», and withholding zakāh is of the ḥarām, so it falls under his words.
الحَجّIt is easy to say this ḥadīth was before ḥajj was made obligatory (the ninth or tenth year). And «وَأَتِمُّوا الحَجَّ وَالعُمْرَةَ لِله» obligates completing the ḥajj, not beginning it; leaving ḥajj, too, is of the ḥarām, so it may fall under «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام».

وَأَتِمُّوا الْحَجَّ وَالْعُمْرَةَ لِلَّهِ

And complete the Ḥajj and ʿUmrah for Allah.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:196

«قَالَ: نَعَم» “Yes” — a Reiteration of the Question

«نَعَم» is a particle of answer that affirms the thing asked about. Its force is to repeat the question: “shall I enter Paradise?” — “yes [you enter Paradise].” This is a rule that runs through the fiqh of contracts and declarations: wherever «نَعَم» answers a question, read it as the question said back in the affirmative.

أَطَلَّقْتَ امْرَأَتَك؟ — نَعَم“Did you divorce your wife?” — “Yes.” She is divorced, for «نَعَم» means “I divorced her.”
زَوَّجْتُكَ ابْنَتِي… أَقَبِلْت؟ — نَعَم“I marry my daughter to you… do you accept?” — “Yes.” It suffices as acceptance of the marriage.
أَوَقَفْتَ بَيْتَك؟ — نَعَم“Did you make your house a waqf?” — “Yes.” The house becomes waqf.
أَبِعْتَ سَيَّارَتَك؟ — نَعَم“Did you sell your car?” — “Yes.” He has affirmed the sale.
غَايَاتُ الصَّحَابَة The Aims of the Companions

First benefit: the loftiness of the Companions’ aims. The end they sought was entering Paradise — not abundance of wealth, nor many children, nor ease in this world. When one of them did the Prophet ﷺ a service and was told, “Ask what you wish,” he said: your companionship in Paradise. He was answered: «أَعِنِّي عَلَى نَفْسِكَ بِكَثْرَةِ السُّجُود» — “then help me for yourself by abundant prostration,” that is, by much prayer. He asked for no coin, no herds, no palace, no field — he asked for Paradise.

And Paradise is the reward of such deeds. It is the abode of bliss Allah prepared for the God-fearing — what no eye has seen, as in the qudsī ḥadīth: «أَعْدَدْتُ لِعِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحِينَ مَا لَا عَيْنٌ رَأَتْ، وَلَا أُذُنٌ سَمِعَتْ، وَلَا خَطَرَ عَلَى قَلْبِ بَشَر» (muttafaq ʿalayh).

Second: restricting oneself to the obligatory prayer brings no blame and does not bar one from Paradise — «أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَات». Imām Aḥmad said of one who leaves the witr that he is a bad man whose witness should not be accepted; but being a bad man does not bar him from Paradise — he is only faulted because the witr is as little as a single rak‘ah, and still he neglects it. And fasting and prayer are among the causes of entering Paradise: whoever fasts Ramaḍān in faith and seeking reward, his past sins are forgiven (al-Bukhārī).

فَلَا تَعْلَمُ نَفْسٌ مَّا أُخْفِيَ لَهُم مِّن قُرَّةِ أَعْيُنٍ جَزَاءً بِمَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

No soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort for the eyes as reward for what they used to do.

سُورَةُ السَّجْدَة · 32:17

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
«أَرَأَيْتَ» فِي الحَدِيثِ بِمَعْنَى أَخْبِرْنِي. «Araʾayta» in the ḥadīth means “inform me.”
المَكْتُوبَاتُ هِيَ الصَّلَوَاتُ الخَمْسُ فِي اليَوْمِ وَاللَّيْلَة. The maktūbāt are the five prayers of the day and night.
مَنْ أَمْسَكَ عَنِ الطَّعَامِ حِمْيَةً لِنَفْسِهِ فَصِيَامُهُ صِيَامٌ شَرْعِيّ. Whoever abstains from food for a diet, his fast is a shar‘ī fast.
تَرْكُ الحَرَامِ مَعَ اعْتِقَادِ تَحْرِيمِهِ لِلهِ يَصِيرُ عِبَادَةً يُثَابُ عَلَيْهَا. Leaving the forbidden while believing it forbidden, for Allah, becomes an act of worship one is rewarded for.
مَنْعُ الزَّكَاةِ مِنَ الحَرَام، فَيَدْخُلُ فِي قَوْلِهِ «حَرَّمْتُ الحَرَام». Withholding zakāh is of the forbidden, so it falls under his words “I treated the forbidden as forbidden.”
«نَعَم» فِي جَوَابِ السُّؤَالِ كَإِعَادَةِ السُّؤَالِ مُثْبَتًا. «Yes» in answer to a question is like the question repeated in the affirmative.
مَنِ اقْتَصَرَ عَلَى الصَّلَاةِ المَكْتُوبَةِ يُحْرَمُ مِنْ دُخُولِ الجَنَّة. Whoever restricts himself to the obligatory prayer is barred from entering Paradise.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَاتِ، وَصُمْتُ رَمَضَانَ، وَأَحْلَلْتُ الحَلَالَ، وَحَرَّمْتُ الحَرَامَ، وَلَمْ أَزِدْ عَلَى ذَلِكَ شَيْئًا، أَأَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ

A man counts up the five prayers, the fast of Ramaḍān, doing the lawful and leaving the forbidden — adds nothing more — and asks whether that is enough for Paradise. The Prophet ﷺ says yes. Around it the Shaykh fixes the shar‘ī meaning of the fast (holding back, dawn to sunset, for Allah), answers how the unnamed zakāh and ḥajj are still accounted for, and makes «naʿam» a question said back in the affirmative. Its heart is the Companions’ aim: not wealth, not ease — but to enter the Garden.

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
