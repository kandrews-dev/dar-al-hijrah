Ḥadīth 17 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ الإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء

Allah Has Prescribed Excellence in All Things

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Explain why the ḥadīth says iḥsān is written عَلَى everything, not toward it — and what that one preposition settles.
  • Give the two examples the Prophet ﷺ struck, and the difference between killing and slaughtering.
  • Distinguish الكِتَابَةُ القَدَرِيَّة from الكِتَابَةُ الشَّرْعِيَّة, and say to what the pronoun in «وَهُوَ كُرْهٌ لَكُم» returns.
  • State what إِحْسَانُ القِتْلَة is on the strongest view, and how that answers the objection about stoning; and name the one exception to killing in the easiest way.
  • List the four conditions of a valid ذَبْح, and say why the tooth and the nail are excluded from the instrument.
  • Answer the two hard questions: the one who forgets the tasmiyah, and the charge that ruling the animal unlawful «wastes people’s wealth».
  • Give the duties of sharpening the blade and giving the animal rest — and how the ḥadīth reaches even the disciplining of one’s family.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٠٩–٢١٨
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 209–218.

عَنْ أَبِي يَعْلَى شَدَّادِ بْنِ أَوْسٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ عَنْ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ الإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء؛ فَإِذَا قَتَلْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا القِتْلَة، وَإِذَا ذَبَحْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا الذِّبْحَة، وَلْيُحِدَّ أَحَدُكُمْ شَفْرَتَه، وَلْيُرِحْ ذَبِيحَتَه». رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم.

Abū Yaʿlā Shaddād ibn Aws (may Allah be pleased with him) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Allah has prescribed excellence (iḥsān) in all things. So when you kill, kill well; and when you slaughter, slaughter well. Let one of you sharpen his blade and let him spare his animal [suffering]."

Narrated by Shaddād ibn Aws · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم — Muslim, Kitāb al-Ṣayd wa-l-Dhabāʾiḥ

«عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء» — لَا «إِلَى» Written “Upon” Everything, Not “Toward” It

The sharḥ begins on a single preposition, and it is not a pedantry. He said «عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء» — upon every thing — and did not say «إِلَى كُلِّ شَيْء», toward every thing.

The meaning: that iḥsān is not particular to one appointed thing of life, but is in the whole of it — «بَلْ هُوَ فِي جَمِيعِ الحَيَاة».

And kataba here means He prescribed it, a firm legislation — «شَرَعَهُ شَرْعًا مُؤَكَّدًا». So the ḥadīth is not describing a virtue that is nice to have; it is reporting a thing Allah has written over every act a person does, the grave and the small alike.

المِثَالَان: القَتْلُ وَالذَّبْح The Two Examples — Killing and Slaughtering

Then he struck two examples — «فَإِذَا قَتَلْتُمْ... وَإِذَا ذَبَحْتُمْ» — and the difference between them is this: that the killed is not made lawful to eat by the killing, while the slaughtered is.

القَتْل
killing		What is killed because it must be — a harmful dog, a snake. It is not eaten; so «أَحْسِنِ القِتْلَة» means: take its life by the easiest means available to you.
الذَّبْح
slaughtering		What is slaughtered to be eaten. «أَحْسِنِ الذِّبْحَة» means: do it by everything in which there is iḥsān — which is why he went on: «وَلْيُحِدَّ أَحَدُكُمْ شَفْرَتَه».

«وَلْيُحِدَّ أَحَدُكُمْ شَفْرَتَه» — let him sharpen his knife: whet it with a file or a stone until it cuts strongly, so the slaughter is quick. And «وَلْيُرِحْ ذَبِيحَتَه» — let him give his animal rest: pass the blade with strength and speed so its dying is brief. Both clauses are about the animal’s ease, not the slaughterer’s convenience.

كِتَابَةٌ قَدَرِيَّةٌ وَكِتَابَةٌ شَرْعِيَّة Two Kinds of “Writing” — Decree and Law

From «إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ» the Shaykh draws that the command and the ruling belong to Allah — and that His “writing” is of two kinds.

قَدَرِيَّة
of the decree		What must come to pass. Its example is the promise of Allah in His Book that the righteous shall inherit the earth — a thing written that will be.
شَرْعِيَّة
of the law		What is legislated, and may be done by the children of Ādam or may not. As «كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ القِتَال» — fighting is written upon you — and «كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَام» — fasting is written upon you.

وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِي الزَّبُورِ مِن بَعْدِ الذِّكْرِ أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ

And We have already written in the Scripture, after the [earlier] mention, that the land shall be inherited by My righteous servants.

سُورَةُ الأَنْبِيَاء · 21:105

كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْقِتَالُ وَهُوَ كُرْهٌ لَّكُمْ

Fighting has been prescribed for you, though it is hateful to you.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:216

And a precision not to be missed in «وَهُوَ كُرْهٌ لَكُم». The pronoun in «وَهُوَ» returns to the fighting, not to the writing — because the Companions رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ could not possibly hate an obligation of Allah; they hated the being-killed, yet fought.

There is a difference between a man hating Allah’s ruling and hating the thing ruled. To dislike the hardship of a command while submitting to it is the believer’s state; to hate the command itself is another matter entirely. The whole of the eighth nullifier’s «بُغْضٌ طَبِيعِيّ» distinction sits inside this one grammatical point.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ

O you who have believed, fasting has been prescribed for you.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:183

إِحْسَانُ القِتْلَة What “Killing Well” Actually Means

The strongest view is exact, and it heads off a famous objection. Killing well, on the rājiḥ view, is following the sharʿ in it — whether that turns out harder or easier.

Why the precision matters: if «killing well» meant simply «by the gentlest physical means», the stoning of the adulterer (رَجْمُ الزَّانِي) would seem to violate the ḥadīth. But once إِحْسَان is defined as conformity to what Allah legislated, the difficulty falls away: the ḥadd prescribed by the sharʿ is the iḥsān in that case. «لَا يَرِدُ عَلَيْنَا».

Otherwise — where the sharʿ has not fixed the manner — one kills what may be killed by the easiest way. A man harmed by a dog may kill it a dozen ways; he takes the lightest. The one exception is qiṣāṣ: there the offender is dealt with as he did — for Allah legislated the like of the crime in the penalty.

شُرُوطُ الذَّبْحِ الأَرْبَعَة The Four Conditions of a Valid Slaughter

Here the short matn opens into fiqh. From «فَأَحْسِنُوا الذِّبْحَة» the Shaykh hangs the conditions of a lawful slaughter — four of them.

١ · الذَّابِح
the slaughterer		He must be a Muslim or a person of the Book. The slaughter of an idolater is not lawful, nor an apostate’s — and so the slaughter of one who abandons the prayer is not lawful, for he is neither Muslim nor of the Book. Proof: «وَطَعَامُ الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الكِتَابَ حِلٌّ لَكُم»; Ibn ʿAbbās said their food is what they slaughter.
٢ · الآلَة
the instrument		Anything that makes the blood flow — iron, silver, gold, stone, reed — «مَا أَنْهَرَ الدَّمَ وَذُكِرَ اسْمُ اللهِ عَلَيْهِ فَكُل». Excluded are two: the tooth and the nail.
٣ · إِنْهَارُ الدَّم
making blood flow		By cutting the two wadajān, the thick vessels around the windpipe; being joined to the heart, their cutting drains the blood fast and the death is quick. The Prophet ﷺ forbade شَرِيطَةَ الشَّيْطَان — the one left to die without its vessels severed.
٤ · التَّسْمِيَة
naming Allah		Saying the name of Allah at the slaughter — «بِسْمِ الله». It is a condition; Allah stressed it: «وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا مِمَّا لَمْ يُذْكَرِ اسْمُ اللهِ عَلَيْهِ».

Why the tooth and the nail? Because the Prophet ﷺ gave the reason: «أَمَّا السِّنُّ فَعَظْمٌ، وَأَمَّا الظُّفُرُ فَمُدَى الحَبَشَة» — as for the tooth, it is a bone; and as for the nail, it is the knife of the Abyssinians. From «it is a bone» some scholars took that no bone at all makes slaughter lawful (the عُمُومُ العِلَّة — the ruling follows the general reason, not the single named thing); and the nail is excluded both as imitation of the non-Arabs and because, being joined to the body, making it a blade runs against the fiṭrah.

وَلَا تَأْكُلُوا مِمَّا لَمْ يُذْكَرِ اسْمُ اللَّهِ عَلَيْهِ وَإِنَّهُ لَفِسْقٌ

And do not eat of that over which the name of Allah has not been mentioned, for indeed it is a grave disobedience.

سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام · 6:121

النِّسْيَانُ وَإِفْسَادُ الأَمْوَال The One Who Forgets, and the Charge of Wasting Wealth

Two hard questions the Shaykh raises against himself.

First — what of one who forgets the tasmiyah? The condition does not drop by forgetfulness — just as a man who prayed in a state of ḥadath, forgetting, has not prayed validly. But is he sinful? No: «رَبَّنَا لَا تُؤَاخِذْنَا إِن نَّسِينَا» is answered by Allah’s «قَدْ فَعَلْت». So he bears no sin for forgetting — yet the animal is still not eaten, because the ruling on the meat is one thing and the sin on the man is another. (And were someone to eat of it in forgetfulness or ignorance, there is nothing upon him.)

Second — does ruling the animal unlawful «waste people’s wealth»? The answer: we did not waste it. A thing left for the command of Allah is not a loss but an obedience — as we give the zakāh and do not call it loss. And ruling it unlawful this once ensures the man «%s» — he will not forget again.

The parallel the Shaykh draws is worth keeping. Some object to cutting the thief’s hand that «half the nation would be one-handed». «You have just admitted half your nation are thieves — and had you cut one thief’s hand, thousands would have stopped.» A penalty that looks harsh in the one case is what protects the many — and the same logic answers the forgotten tasmiyah.

One mercy in the law, though: the animal not under control — a camel that bolts, one fallen in a well beyond reach — is made lawful by a wound in any part of its body. Where the throat cannot be reached, Allah does not burden the slaughterer with reaching it.

حَدُّ الشَّفْرَةِ وَإِرَاحَةُ الذَّبِيحَة Sharpening the Blade, and Giving Rest

Back to the animal’s ease, which is where the matn ends. Sharpening the blade is a duty, because it is easier on the animal; one who slaughters with a dull blade — though the cut be valid and the meat lawful — is sinful for not having sharpened it.

And he must not sharpen the blade before the animal, because the Prophet ﷺ «أَمَرَ أَنْ تُحَدَّ الشِّفَارُ وَأَنْ تُوَارَى عَنِ البَهَائِم» — ordered that blades be sharpened and hidden from the animals; for the animal knows, and has been seen to flee the slaughter in fear when the knife is whetted before it.

Giving the animal rest is by the speed of the slaughter, and by how it is held: leave its legs free and place your foot on the side of its neck so it cannot rise — this is easier on it, and drains the blood more fully, since the movement helps the blood out.

What some do — kneeling on the sheep and pinning its limbs — is a torment to it; and breaking its neck before it dies, to hasten death, is not allowed, for there is severe pain in it and no need. «نَنْتَظِرُ حَتَّى يَخْرُجَ الدَّم».

And the Shaykh ends where the ḥadīth began — «upon every thing». Even the disciplining of one’s family falls under «أَحْسِنُوا»: the Prophet ﷺ said in the Farewell Pilgrimage, of correcting them, «ضَرْبًا غَيْرَ مُبَرِّح» — a striking that is not severe. Discipline with iḥsān, not with violence; and the fuqahāʾ said that one who beats his child to excess and he dies is liable, while ordinary correction without violence carries no liability. The ḥadīth that opened on a sharpened blade closes on a raised hand — and the measure in both is the same.

Because they are not sharp enough to make blood flow

What does إِنْهَارُ الدَّم require, and what is forbidden against it?

Draining all blood before death; nothing is forbidden
Cutting the two wadajān around the windpipe so the blood drains fast; forbidden is شَرِيطَةُ الشَّيْطَان — the animal left to die without its vessels severed
A single shallow cut; deep cuts are forbidden
Breaking the neck first

What is the ruling on one who FORGETS the tasmiyah — on the meat, and on the man?

The meat is lawful and the man sinful
The condition does not drop by forgetfulness, so the animal is not eaten; but the man bears no sin, for the ruling on the meat and the sin on the man are two different things
Both the meat is unlawful and the man is sinful
He must repeat the slaughter on the same animal

How does the Shaykh answer that ruling the animal unlawful «wastes people’s wealth»?

He concedes it is a loss but says it is necessary
A thing left for the command of Allah is not loss but obedience — like the zakāh — and ruling it unlawful once ensures the man never forgets again; as cutting one thief’s hand stops thousands
He allows eating it to avoid waste
He says wealth is unimportant

How does the ḥadīth reach the disciplining of one’s family?

It does not; that is a separate topic
Even correction falls under «أَحْسِنُوا» — the Prophet ﷺ allowed in the Farewell Pilgrimage only «ضَرْبًا غَيْرَ مُبَرِّح», a striking that is not severe; excess that kills brings liability
It permits severe beating for discipline
It forbids all discipline of family
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
الإِحْسَانُ مَكْتُوبٌ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء، لَيْسَ خَاصًّا بِشَيْءٍ مُعَيَّنٍ مِنَ الحَيَاة. Iḥsān is written upon everything, not particular to one appointed thing of life.
الضَّمِيرُ فِي «وَهُوَ كُرْهٌ لَكُمْ» يَعُودُ عَلَى الكِتَابَةِ لَا عَلَى القِتَال. The pronoun in «though it is hateful to you» returns to the writing, not to the fighting.
ذَبِيحَةُ تَارِكِ الصَّلَاةِ لَا تَحِلُّ، لِأَنَّهُ لَيْسَ مُسْلِمًا وَلَا كِتَابِيًّا. The slaughter of one who abandons the prayer is not lawful, for he is neither Muslim nor a Person of the Book.
يَحِلُّ الذَّبْحُ بِالسِّنِّ وَالظُّفُر. Slaughter is lawful with the tooth and the nail.
مَنْ نَسِيَ التَّسْمِيَةَ فَلَا إِثْمَ عَلَيْه، لَكِنْ لَا تُؤْكَلُ الذَّبِيحَة. One who forgets the tasmiyah bears no sin, but the animal is not eaten.
لَا تُحَدُّ الشَّفْرَةُ أَمَامَ الذَّبِيحَة، وَتُوَارَى عَنِ البَهَائِم. The blade is not sharpened before the animal, and is hidden from the beasts.
إِحْسَانُ القِتْلَةِ عَلَى الرَّاجِحِ هُوَ اتِّبَاعُ الشَّرْعِ فِيهَا. Killing well, on the strongest view, is following the sharʿ in it.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ الإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْء

The whole ḥadīth turns on one preposition — «عَلَى» not «إِلَى». Iḥsān is written upon everything, so there is no act a person does that falls outside it: not the killing of a harmful animal, not the slaughter of food, not the sharpening of a blade, not even the disciplining of one’s own family. The ḥadīth that teaches the fiqh of the knife is, underneath, teaching that nothing a believer does is too small for excellence.
