Ḥadīth 9 of 42 · Agreed uponYear 2

مَا نَهَيْتُكُمْ عَنْهُ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ

Avoid What Is Forbidden — Do What You Are Able of What Is Commanded

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Memorize the ḥadīth of أبو هريرة: avoid every prohibition; perform every command according to ability.
  • Learn the qāʿidah the scholars draw from it — prohibitions are left absolutely, commands follow اسْتِطَاعَة (ability) — and its Qur'anic root in سورة التغابن.
  • Trace the fiqh fruits of مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ: the concessions of التيمم (dry ablution), قصر الصلاة (shortening the prayer), and praying seated.
  • Understand how كثرة المسائل (excessive questioning) and differing with the prophets destroyed the nations before us — the story of بنو إسرائيل and the cow.
  • Pair this ḥadīth with Ḥadīth 5 (رد البدعة) as the two guardrails of الاتباع (following).

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنِ صَخْرٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: سَمِعْتُ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ يَقُولُ: «مَا نَهَيْتُكُمْ عَنْهُ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ، وَمَا أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِهِ فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ، فَإِنَّمَا أَهْلَكَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ كَثْرَةُ مَسَائِلِهِمْ وَاخْتِلَافُهُمْ عَلَى أَنْبِيَائِهِمْ» رَوَاهُ الْبُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ.

Abū Hurayrah ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Ṣakhr (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: "What I have forbidden you, avoid it; and what I have commanded you, do of it what you are able; for what destroyed those before you was only the abundance of their questions and their differing with their prophets." Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah · al-Bukhārī & Muslim

الراوي أبو هريرة The Narrator — Abū Hurayrah

أبو هريرة (Abū Hurayrah), whose name is عبد الرحمن بن صخر الدوسي, is the most prolific narrator of ḥadīth among all the Companions — more than five thousand narrations reach us through him. He accepted Islam in the year of خيبر (Khaybar) and accompanied the Prophet ﷺ for barely four years, yet he outstripped Companions of twenty years' standing. His secret was total devotion: while others were occupied with trade and farmland, he stayed in the masjid among أهل الصُّفَّة, hungry and patient, memorizing everything he heard. He once complained to the Prophet ﷺ that he forgot what he heard, so the Prophet ﷺ told him to spread his cloak, made a movement of his hands toward it, and told him to gather it to himself — and he said: "I never forgot anything after that." It is fitting that the ḥadīth about how a student should ask and obey comes to us through the Ummah's greatest student.

النهي يُجتنب كله والأمر بحسب الاستطاعة The Great Asymmetry — Avoid Absolutely, Obey According to Ability

Listen to how precisely the two halves of this ḥadīth are worded. For prohibitions: فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ — "avoid it," with no condition attached. For commands: فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ — "do of it what you are able," with ability written into the very sentence. Ibn Rajab in جامع العلوم والحكم and Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn in his sharḥ both stand at this asymmetry: why is avoidance absolute while performance is measured? Because a prohibition is left by simply not doing — and not doing is within everyone's power, always. No one is ever "unable" to leave الخمر (wine) or الغيبة (backbiting). A command, however, is an action — and actions collide with weakness, illness, travel, and poverty. So the Lawgiver, in His mercy, tied commands to capacity and left prohibitions unqualified.

From this the scholars derive a famous qāʿidah (legal maxim): whoever cannot perform all of a command does what he can of it — الميسور لا يسقط بالمعسور, what is within reach is not dropped because of what is out of reach. And the ḥadīth is the twin of an āyah you have already studied: ﴿فَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ﴾ (al-Taghābun 64:16) — "So fear Allah as much as you are able." The Qur'an and the Sunnah speak with one tongue. To revisit the āyah in its sūrah, see the Quran lesson: سورة التغابن.

ثمرات فقهية — رخص الشريعة Fiqh Fruits — the Concessions You Already Know

Every concession (رخصة) you have met in the Fiqh tab lives under مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ. The sick or waterless worshipper does not abandon purification — he moves to التيمم (dry ablution), because the wudu he can do is owed even when the wudu he cannot do is lifted. The traveller does not abandon ṣalāh — he shortens the four-rakʿah prayers to two, and the one who cannot stand prays sitting, and the one who cannot sit prays on his side, exactly as the Prophet ﷺ taught the sick. Fasting is owed by the able and made up or ransomed by those who cannot. Ḥajj is only obligatory upon مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا — the one able to make the journey. Not one command of the Sharīʿah crushes its servant; the door of "I cannot do all of it" never opens into "so I will do none of it." Browse the Fiqh tab and you will see this single ḥadīth standing quietly behind ruling after ruling.

هلاك من قبلنا بكثرة السؤال والاختلاف What Destroyed the Nations Before Us

The Prophet ﷺ then gives the reason (فَإِنَّمَا — "for only"): the nations before us were destroyed by كَثْرَةُ مَسَائِلِهِمْ (the abundance of their questions) and اخْتِلَافُهُمْ عَلَى أَنْبِيَائِهِمْ (their differing with their prophets). The clearest picture is بنو إسرائيل (the Children of Israel) and the cow in سورة البقرة: Mūsā told them Allah commands you to slaughter a cow — and had they taken any cow, it would have sufficed them. Instead they interrogated: What is it? What colour? Which one exactly? — and with every question the command narrowed and hardened against them, until they barely did it at all. Ibn Rajab notes that this ḥadīth itself was spoken in such a moment: when the Prophet ﷺ announced that Ḥajj had been made obligatory, a man asked, "Every year, O Messenger of Allah?" — and he ﷺ warned that had he said yes, it would have become binding, and they would not have managed it.

Is questioning itself blameworthy? No — revelation came by the Companions' questions, and the scholars say: السؤال مفتاح العلم, asking is the key to knowledge. What this ḥadīth condemns is the question asked to argue, to escape, to split hairs over what Allah left generous and wide. The adab (etiquette) of the student is: ask in order to act, not in order to argue. You have seen this adab living in the study circle in القراءة ٤ — في مجلس العلم: the student asks his shaykh what he needs in order to obey, then rises and obeys.

A gem — الحديثان حارسا الاتباع

Set this ḥadīth beside Ḥadīth 5 — رد البدعة and you hold the two guardrails of the straight path. Ḥadīth 5 guards the religion from addition: whoever invents into it what is not from it, his deed is thrown back. Ḥadīth 9 guards the believer from the two opposite ruins: abandoning the command because he cannot perfect it, and dissolving the command under a flood of questions and objections. Between the two, the path of أهل السنة is simple and light: do not invent, do not interrogate to escape — receive what the Messenger ﷺ brought, leave what he forbade, and do what he commanded as well as you are able.

Memorize & Renew — احفظ وجدّد

مَا نَهَيْتُكُمْ عَنْهُ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ، وَمَا أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِهِ فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ

"What I have forbidden you, avoid it; and what I have commanded you, do of it what you are able." Memorize these two clauses word-perfect — they are the believer's whole method of obedience. When you meet a prohibition, leave it entirely; when you meet a command, give it everything you have — and know that Allah never burdens a soul beyond its capacity.
