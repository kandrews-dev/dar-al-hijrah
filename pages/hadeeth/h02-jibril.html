Ḥadīth 2 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

حديث جبريل

The Ḥadīth of Jibrīl — Islam, Īmān & Iḥsān

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the three levels of the religion: الإسلام · الإيمان · الإحسان.
  • Know the five pillars of Islam and the six articles of faith.
  • Understand iḥsān: "to worship Allah as though you see Him."
  • Learn the manners of seeking knowledge from how Jibrīl asked and the Prophet ﷺ answered.
  • Know that the questioner was the angel Jibrīl, come to teach the religion.

عَنْ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: بَيْنَمَا نَحْنُ جُلُوسٌ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ ﷺ ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ، إِذْ طَلَعَ عَلَيْنَا رَجُلٌ شَدِيدُ بَيَاضِ الثِّيَابِ، شَدِيدُ سَوَادِ الشَّعْرِ، لَا يُرَىٰ عَلَيْهِ أَثَرُ السَّفَرِ، وَلَا يَعْرِفُهُ مِنَّا أَحَدٌ، حَتَّىٰ جَلَسَ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ فَقَالَ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ، أَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِسْلَامِ. فَقَالَ: «الإِسْلَامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ البَيْتَ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا». قَالَ: صَدَقْتَ … قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِيمَانِ. قَالَ: «أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاللَّهِ، وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ، وَكُتُبِهِ، وَرُسُلِهِ، وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ». قَالَ: صَدَقْتَ. قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِحْسَانِ. قَالَ: «أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ» … ثُمَّ انْطَلَقَ، فَقَالَ: «يَا عُمَرُ، أَتَدْرِي مَنِ السَّائِلُ؟» قُلْتُ: اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: «فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ، أَتَاكُمْ يُعَلِّمُكُمْ دِينَكُمْ».

ʿUmar said: One day while we were sitting with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, a man appeared with intensely white clothes and jet-black hair, no trace of travel upon him, and none of us knew him. He sat before the Prophet ﷺ and said, "O Muḥammad, tell me about Islam." He said: "Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish the prayer, to give the zakāh, to fast Ramaḍān, and to make pilgrimage to the House if you are able." He said, "You have spoken the truth… Now tell me about Īmān." He said: "That you believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and that you believe in the Decree — its good and its evil." He said, "You have spoken the truth. Now tell me about Iḥsān." He said: "That you worship Allah as though you see Him, for if you do not see Him, He surely sees you."… Then the man left, and the Prophet ﷺ said, "O ʿUmar, do you know who the questioner was?" I said, "Allah and His Messenger know best." He said: "That was Jibrīl; he came to teach you your religion."

Narrated by ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb · Muslim

مراتب الدين الثلاث The Three Levels of the Religion

Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn explains that this ḥadīth is so comprehensive it is called "the mother of the Sunnah," just as al-Fātiḥah is called the mother of the Qurʾān. It lays out the religion in three ascending levels — every higher level includes the one below it:

  1. الإسلام (Islam) — the outward deeds: the five pillars — the two testimonies, prayer, zakāh, fasting Ramaḍān, and Ḥajj.
  2. الإيمان (Īmān) — the inward beliefs: the six articles — belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and the Decree, its good and its evil.
  3. الإحسان (Iḥsān) — the highest level: perfecting worship through constant awareness of Allah.
الإحسان Iḥsān — Worship as Though You See Him

«أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ» — al-ʿUthaymīn explains that iḥsān has two levels within it. The higher: to worship Allah as though you see Him — worship driven by longing and love, as if beholding Him. And if you cannot reach that, the second: to worship knowing He sees you — worship driven by awe and watchfulness (murāqabah). Either way, the servant never worships as though Allah is absent. This is the soul of sincere, excellent worship.

آداب طلب العلم Manners of Seeking Knowledge

al-ʿUthaymīn draws out how Jibrīl modelled the etiquette of the learner: he came in clean, dignified appearance, sat close and attentively before the teacher, asked clear and important questions, and affirmed the answers. And the Prophet ﷺ modelled the teacher: when he did not know the timing of the Hour he said, "The one asked knows no more than the questioner" — teaching that "I do not know" is part of knowledge. The lesson closes by noting that among the signs of the Hour the Prophet ﷺ mentioned are the slave-girl giving birth to her mistress, and the barefoot, destitute shepherds competing in raising tall buildings — signs the world has since witnessed.
