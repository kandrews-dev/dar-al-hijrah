Ḥadīth 21 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللهِ ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ

Say “I Believe in Allah,” Then Be Steadfast

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say who narrated this ḥadīth and where it is reported, and what kind of statement Sufyān asked for — «قَوْلاً لَا أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ أَحَدًا غَيْرَك».
  • Unfold the two words of the answer — «آمَنْتُ بِالله» (the heart’s affirmation) and «اسْتَقِمْ» (going straight on the sharīʿah).
  • Show how the two words gather the two conditions of worship — ikhlāṣ and mutābaʿah — so that the whole religion stands in two words.
  • Name the three āyāt that witness to «رَبُّنَا اللهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا».
  • Correct the common praise «مُلْتَزِم» to the Qurʾānic «مُسْتَقِيم», and turn istiqāmah into a rule of self-inspection.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٣٥–٢٣٧
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 235–237.

عَنْ أَبِي عَمْرٍو، وَقِيلَ: أَبِي عَمْرَةَ، سُفْيَانَ بْنِ عَبْدِ اللهِ الثَّقَفِيِّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، قُلْ لِي فِي الإِسْلَامِ قَوْلاً لَا أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ أَحَدًا غَيْرَكَ. قَالَ: «قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللهِ ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ». رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم.

Abū ʿAmr — and it is said Abū ʿAmrah — Sufyān ibn ʿAbdillāh al-Thaqafī (may Allah be pleased with him) said: I said, "O Messenger of Allah, tell me a statement about Islam that I will ask no one about besides you." He said: "Say: I believe in Allah, then be steadfast." Narrated by Muslim.

Narrated by Sufyān ibn ʿAbdillāh al-Thaqafī · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم — Muslim, in his Ṣaḥīḥ (Kitāb al-Īmān, bāb jāmiʿ awṣāf al-Islām), so the ḥadīth is ṣaḥīḥ.

«قُلْ لِي فِي الإِسْلَام» What Sufyān Asked For

“Tell me a statement about Islam” — that is, about the sharīʿah. And «قَوْلاً لَا أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ أَحَدًا غَيْرَك» means: a statement that is a decisive boundary (حَدٌّ فَاصِل), comprehensive and preventive (جَامِعٌ مَانِع) — one word he could hold to and need no other. He did not ask for a long list; he asked for the kernel.

An apparent difficulty — «لَا أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ أَحَدًا غَيْرَك». Could a Companion really ask no one but the Messenger ﷺ about the religion?

The answer: yes, a person may ask someone who surpasses him in knowledge — that happens. And the phrase is said even when one would not in fact ask another, in order to make the one questioned give the answer its due weight and care.

الكَلِمَتَان The Two Words

The Prophet ﷺ gave him two words — «آمَنْتُ بِالله» and «اسْتَقِمْ» — and these two gathered the whole religion.

«قُلْ آمَنْتُ»This takes in the saying of the tongue and the saying of the heart together. The people of knowledge say: the saying of the heart is its affirmation and acknowledgement (إِقْرَارُهُ وَاعْتِرَافُه). The locus of īmān is the heart.
«آمَنْتُ بِالله»“I have affirmed Him” — according to what is obligatory upon the servant of belief in His oneness in rubūbiyyah, in ulūhiyyah, and in His names and attributes (تَوْحِيدُ الرُّبُوبِيَّةِ وَالأُلُوهِيَّةِ وَالأَسْمَاءِ وَالصِّفَات).
«ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ»Go straight on the sharīʿah — neither to the right nor to the left (لَا يَمِينًا وَلَا شِمَالًا): no excess and no falling short.
الدِّينُ كُلُّهُ فِي كَلِمَتَيْن The Whole Religion in Two Words

Why is this among the most comprehensive of ḥadīths? Because the two words meet the two conditions on which every act of worship is accepted:

«آمَنْتُ بِالله» ⟵ الإِخْلَاصBelief in Allah entails ikhlāṣ — devoting the worship to Him alone. This is the first condition of worship.
«اسْتَقِمْ» ⟵ المُتَابَعَةIstiqāmah entails mutābaʿah — walking upon His sharīʿah ﷺ. This is the second condition of worship.

So the Prophet ﷺ was given jawāmiʿ al-kalim — speech that gathers much in little — when he placed the entire dīn in two words. And the Qurʾān itself bears this out three times, each joining «قَالُوا رَبُّنَا الله» to «ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا»:

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ قَالُوا رَبُّنَا اللَّهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا فَلَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ

Indeed, those who say, "Our Lord is Allah," and then are steadfast — there will be no fear concerning them, nor will they grieve.

سُورَةُ الأَحْقَاف · 46:13

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ قَالُوا رَبُّنَا اللَّهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا تَتَنَزَّلُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ أَلَّا تَخَافُوا وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا وَأَبْشِرُوا بِالْجَنَّةِ الَّتِي كُنتُمْ تُوعَدُونَ

Indeed, those who say, "Our Lord is Allah," and then are steadfast — the angels descend upon them, [saying], "Fear not, nor grieve, but receive good tidings of the Paradise which you were promised."

سُورَةُ فُصِّلَت · 41:30

فَاسْتَقِمْ كَمَا أُمِرْتَ وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ

So remain on a right course as you have been commanded, [you] and those who have turned back with you.

سُورَةُ هُود · 11:112

اسْتِقَامَةٌ لَا الْتِزَام Say “Upright,” Not “Committed”

A point of language the Shaykh is careful about. People today, when they want to praise someone for holding to the religion, say: “Fulān is multazim (مُلْتَزِم).” The correct thing is to say: “Fulān is mustaqīm (مُسْتَقِيم)” — upright — for that is the word that came in the Qurʾān and the Sunnah. The ḥadīth says «اسْتَقِمْ», and the āyāt say «اسْتَقَامُوا» — so let the praise follow the revealed word.

تَفَقُّدُ النَّفْس Inspect Yourself: Am I Upright?

Istiqāmah is not a label one claims once; it is a line one must keep. Whoever falls short in the obligations has not stayed upright — rather deviation (اِنْحِرَاف) has entered, and its severity is by the measure of what he left of duties or did of forbidden things. So a person should always inspect himself: am I upright or not? If upright, he praises Allah, thanks Him, and asks Him for firmness (الثَّبَات); if not, he must straighten his course back to Allah.

Istiqāmah is a general, sweeping description of all one’s deeds — and so its opposite shows in every field:

أَخَّرَ الصَّلَاةَ عَنْ وَقْتِهَاWhoever delays the prayer past its time is not upright — he has let the prayer slip.
مَنَعَ الزَّكَاةWhoever withholds the zakāh is not upright — he has let the zakāh slip.
اعْتَدَى عَلَى أَعْرَاضِ النَّاسWhoever transgresses against people’s honour is not upright — for doing the forbidden.
غَشَّ وَخَادَعَ فِي البَيْعِ وَالشِّرَاءWhoever cheats and deceives in buying, selling, hiring and the like is not upright.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
«آمَنْتُ بِالله» مَحَلُّ الإِيمَانِ فِيهِ القَلْب. In «I believe in Allah», the locus of faith is the heart.
«قُلْ لِي فِي الإِسْلَام» أَيْ فِي الشَّرِيعَة. «Tell me about Islam» means: about the sharīʿah.
الصَّوَابُ أَنْ يُثْنَى عَلَى المُتَمَسِّكِ بِالدِّينِ بِأَنَّهُ «مُلْتَزِم» لَا «مُسْتَقِيم». The correct praise for one who holds to the religion is “multazim,” not “mustaqīm.”
«اسْتَقِمْ» مَعْنَاهَا التَّمَشِّي عَلَى شَرِيعَةِ اللهِ لَا يَمِينًا وَلَا شِمَالًا. «Be steadfast» means to walk upon the sharīʿah of Allah, neither right nor left.
الإِيمَانُ بِاللهِ يَتَضَمَّنُ الإِخْلَاصَ، وَالاِسْتِقَامَةُ تَتَضَمَّنُ المُتَابَعَة. Belief in Allah entails ikhlāṣ, and istiqāmah entails mutābaʿah.
مَنْ أَخَّرَ الصَّلَاةَ عَنْ وَقْتِهَا فَهُوَ مُسْتَقِيم. Whoever delays the prayer past its time is upright.
لَا يُمْكِنُ لِأَحَدٍ مِنَ الصَّحَابَةِ أَنْ يَسْأَلَ أَحَدًا غَيْرَ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ فِي أُمُورِ الدِّين. It is impossible for any Companion to ask anyone other than the Prophet ﷺ about matters of religion.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللهِ ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ

A Companion asked for one word about Islam he would need no other for, and the Prophet ﷺ gave him the whole religion in two: آمَنْتُ بِالله — the heart’s affirmation of Allah’s oneness, which carries ikhlāṣ; and اسْتَقِمْ — go straight on the sharīʿah, which carries mutābaʿah. Between them stand the two conditions of every accepted deed. The Qurʾān says it three times — «رَبُّنَا اللهُ ثُمَّ اسْتَقَامُوا». Say it of a man and mean the revealed word: not “committed,” but upright — and then keep inspecting whether you still are.
