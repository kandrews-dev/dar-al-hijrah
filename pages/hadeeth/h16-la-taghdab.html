Ḥadīth 16 of 42 · al-BukhārīYear 2

لَا تَغْضَبْ

Do Not Become Angry

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why the sharḥ declines to identify the man who asked — and what that tells you about reading ḥadīth.
  • Define الوَصِيَّة, and say what the Prophet ﷺ compared anger to.
  • Give the two readings of «لَا تَغْضَبْ» and say which one Ibn ʿUthaymīn calls plainly obligatory.
  • State the rule يُخَاطَبُ بِمَا تَقْتَضِيهِ حَالُه, and use it to answer why he ﷺ did not advise this man with taqwā.
  • List the verbal and the practical remedies for anger, with the reason given for each.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٠٥–٢٠٨
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 205–208.

عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَنَّ رَجُلًا قَالَ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ: أَوْصِنِي، قَالَ: «لَا تَغْضَبْ»، فَرَدَّدَ مِرَارًا، قَالَ: «لَا تَغْضَبْ». رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ.

Abū Hurayrah (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated that a man said to the Prophet ﷺ: "Advise me." He said: "Do not become angry." The man repeated his request several times, and he said: "Do not become angry."

Narrated by Abū Hurayrah · رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ — al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Adab, bāb al-ḥadhar min al-ghaḍab

الرَّجُلُ المُبْهَم The Man Who Is Not Named

The sharḥ opens on something easy to walk past: this man is not identified, and this happens a great deal in the ḥadīth — the unnamed one is not named. And Ibn ʿUthaymīn gives the reason, then a verdict:

— because knowing the man's name or his description is not needed.

— And you find some of the scholars wearying themselves greatly in determining who this man was; and what I hold is that there is no need for the trouble, so long as the ruling does not change with this man or that one.

That is a reading rule worth taking from the very first line. A ḥadīth is read for the ruling it carries. Where a detail cannot change the ruling, chasing it is effort spent away from the point — and the Shaykh is willing to say so of scholars who did chase it.

And الوَصِيَّة is an undertaking laid upon a person concerning a weighty matter — as a man leaves a bequest over his third, or over his young child, or the like. So the man is not asking for a pleasantry. He is asking for the one thing worth entrusting to him, and that is what makes the brevity of the answer so striking.

مَا هُوَ الغَضَب؟ What Anger Is

Before the ruling, the thing itself. The Prophet ﷺ made clear that it is a coal which Shayṭān throws into the heart of the son of Ādam — so the heart boils, and for that reason his face reddens, the veins of his neck swell, and sometimes his hair stands on end.

Note that the description is physical throughout. The redness, the swelling, the hair — anger is being described as something that happens to a body, which is precisely why the question that follows is a real one: can a man be commanded not to have it at all?

مَعْنَيَا «لَا تَغْضَبْ» The Two Readings of "Do Not Become Angry"

So is the Messenger's ﷺ intent by «لَا تَغْضَبْ»: that anger should not occur from you — or is the meaning: do not carry out the anger? Ibn ʿUthaymīn takes them in turn, and does not treat them as equal.

الأَوَّل
the natural anger		Controlling that is difficult, because people differ in it a great difference. But there is no objection to saying he meant the natural anger in this sense: that you settle yourself and cool the matter down upon yourself — and restrain yourself when the cause is present, so that you do not become angry.
الثَّانِي
acting on it		that you do not carry out what the anger demands — and this is true, and it is forbidden to do.

The asymmetry is the lesson. The first is a counsel about a disposition, and it is conceded to be hard and to vary from man to man. The second is a prohibition, flat and binding on everyone — because what a man does is within his power in a way that what rises in his chest is not.

So if a man became angry and wanted to divorce his wife, we say to him: be patient, and take your time.

The example is not incidental. It names the thing anger most often destroys that cannot be put back — and the counsel against it is not «do not feel this», which he cannot obey on the spot, but «do not act yet», which he can.

مِنْ فَوَائِدِ الحَدِيث From the Benefits of the Ḥadīth

١ · «أَوْصِنِي» — they asked in order to act. The Companions رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ, when they came to know the truth, did not stop at mere knowledge — they acted. And many people today ask about the ruling and come to know it but do not act by it. As for the Companions, when they asked about the medicine, they took it.

And notice he repeated the request several times — and received the same four letters back each time. The man wanted more and was given the same thing again, which is itself part of the answer: he had already been told what he needed.

٢ · يُخَاطَبُ بِمَا تَقْتَضِيهِ حَالُه — the one addressed is addressed by what his condition calls for. And this is an important rule. Ibn ʿUthaymīn raises the objection against it before answering:

وَلَقَدْ وَصَّيْنَا الَّذِينَ أُوتُوا الْكِتَابَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ وَإِيَّاكُمْ أَنِ اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ

"And We have instructed those given the Scripture before you, and you, to fear Allah."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:131 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

Why did he ﷺ not advise him with the taqwā of Allah عَزَّ وَجَلَّ, as Allah said? The answer: that every person is addressed by what his condition calls for — so it is as though the Prophet ﷺ knew of this man that he was given to anger, and so advised him with that.

And the Shaykh makes the rule portable with two cases of his own: a man comes to you and says «advise me», and you know he keeps bad company — it is right to say: I advise you not to keep company with the wicked. Another comes, and you know he treats his family badly — you say to him: be good in your dealings with your family.

— that a person is advised with what his condition calls for, not with the highest thing that can be advised; because the highest thing that can be advised is other than this.

Which quietly answers a question the student will meet everywhere in the Sunnah: why the same question gets different answers from him ﷺ. It is not inconsistency. The answer is fitted to the man standing in front of him — and the most useful advice is rarely the grandest one available.

And the harm anger does, in the Shaykh's own tally: how many a man became angry and divorced, then came asking; and how many a man became angry and said «by Allah I will not speak to so-and-so», then regretted it and came asking. Both examples are of a man undoing something in a moment that takes years to repair — and in both, what he came back with was a question, not a remedy.

عِلَاجُ الغَضَب The Treatment of Anger

If the cause of anger is present and a man becomes angry, what should he do? The sharḥ answers in two kinds, and gives a reason for each rather than leaving them as bare instructions.

الدَّوَاءُ اللَّفْظِيّ
the verbal remedy		When he feels the anger, let him say: أَعُوذُ بِاللهِ مِنَ الشَّيْطَانِ الرَّجِيم — on the ḥadīth that «a word which, if he said it, what he finds would leave him — were he to say: I seek refuge with Allah from the accursed Shayṭān» (al-Bukhārī and Muslim).
الدَّوَاءُ الفِعْلِيّ
the practical remedy		If he is standing, let him sit; and if he is sitting, let him lie down — because changing his outward state brings about a change in his inward state. And if that does not help, let him make wuḍūʾ: because occupying himself with the wuḍūʾ makes him forget the anger, and because the wuḍūʾ extinguishes its heat.

Read the two reasons given for wuḍūʾ, because they are different in kind. One is about attention — the act occupies him, and anger needs attention to sustain itself. The other is about the coal of the first section: if anger is a heat, water is its opposite. The remedy is matched to the diagnosis.

And is he limited to this? The answer: he is not obliged to stop at it. We may say: if you become angry, leave the place — and many people do this; if one of them becomes angry he goes out of the house so that nothing he dislikes afterwards should happen.

— And forbidding the evils of character entails commanding the excellences of character. So accustom yourself…

That one clause is the turn of the whole lesson. «Do not become angry» looks purely negative — a thing withheld. But a prohibition of a vice is a command of the virtue facing it, so «do not become angry» is also «be forbearing», and the ḥadīth stops being a restraint and becomes a training.

So take to الحِلْم, forbearance, as far as you are able — until your heart is at rest, and you are kept from the illnesses that come on from anger, such as diabetes and blood pressure and the like. وَاللهُ المُسْتَعَان.

And the Shaykh ends on the body again, where he began. Anger entered as something that reddens a face and swells a vein; it leaves as something that damages a man over years. The counsel is not only for his religion.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لَا تَغْضَبْ

Four letters, asked for three times and given back unchanged. He wanted more and was told the same thing — because he had already been given what his own condition called for. And read it the way the sharḥ ends it: a prohibition of a vice is a command of the virtue facing it, so «do not become angry» is also «be forbearing».
