Ḥadīth 4 of 42 · Agreed uponYear 2

خَلْقُ الجَنِينِ والقَدَر

The Creation in the Womb & the Decree

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Memorize the ḥadīth of ابن مسعود on the creation in the womb.
  • Learn the three forty-day stages — نُطْفَة، عَلَقَة، مُضْغَة — and the breathing of the رُوح (soul).
  • Know the four written words and how this ḥadīth grounds belief in القَدَر (the divine decree).
  • Understand الأعمال بالخواتيم — deeds are by their seals — and live between fear and hope.
  • Know that the decree is never an excuse to stop working.

عَنْ أَبِي عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَنِ عَبْدِ اللَّهِ بْنِ مَسْعُودٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ قَالَ: حَدَّثَنَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ وَهُوَ الصَّادِقُ الْمَصْدُوقُ: «إِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ يُجْمَعُ خَلْقُهُ فِي بَطْنِ أُمِّهِ أَرْبَعِينَ يَوْمًا نُطْفَةً، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ عَلَقَةً مِثْلَ ذَلِكَ، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ مُضْغَةً مِثْلَ ذَلِكَ، ثُمَّ يُرْسَلُ إِلَيْهِ الْمَلَكُ فَيَنْفُخُ فِيهِ الرُّوحَ، وَيُؤْمَرُ بِأَرْبَعِ كَلِمَاتٍ: بِكَتْبِ رِزْقِهِ وَأَجَلِهِ وَعَمَلِهِ وَشَقِيٌّ أَوْ سَعِيدٌ. فَوَاللَّهِ الَّذِي لَا إِلَهَ غَيْرُهُ إِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ لَيَعْمَلُ بِعَمَلِ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ حَتَّى مَا يَكُونُ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهَا إِلَّا ذِرَاعٌ فَيَسْبِقُ عَلَيْهِ الْكِتَابُ فَيَعْمَلُ بِعَمَلِ أَهْلِ النَّارِ فَيَدْخُلُهَا، وَإِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ لَيَعْمَلُ بِعَمَلِ أَهْلِ النَّارِ حَتَّى مَا يَكُونُ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهَا إِلَّا ذِرَاعٌ فَيَسْبِقُ عَلَيْهِ الْكِتَابُ فَيَعْمَلُ بِعَمَلِ أَهْلِ الْجَنَّةِ فَيَدْخُلُهَا» — رَوَاهُ الْبُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ.

Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd said: The Messenger of Allah ﷺ — and he is the truthful, the believed — narrated to us: "Verily the creation of each one of you is gathered in his mother's womb for forty days as a drop, then he is a clinging clot for the like of that, then he is a morsel of flesh for the like of that; then the angel is sent to him and breathes the soul into him, and he is commanded with four words: to write his provision, his term, his deeds, and whether he is wretched or happy. So by Allah, besides whom there is no god, one of you will do the deeds of the people of Paradise until there is nothing between him and it but a cubit, then the writ overtakes him and he does the deeds of the people of the Fire and enters it; and one of you will do the deeds of the people of the Fire until there is nothing between him and it but a cubit, then the writ overtakes him and he does the deeds of the people of Paradise and enters it."

Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn Masʿūd · al-Bukhārī & Muslim

الراوي والصادق المصدوق The Narrator, and "the Truthful, the Believed"

ابن مسعود (Ibn Masʿūd) was among the earliest to accept Islam, the first to recite the Qur'an aloud in Makkah after the Prophet ﷺ, and so constantly in the Prophet's company and service that strangers assumed he was of his household. Before relating the ḥadīth he says: حَدَّثَنَا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ ﷺ وَهُوَ الصَّادِقُ الْمَصْدُوقُ — "and he is the truthful, the believed." Ibn Rajab explains why this introduction matters here: everything in this ḥadīth is غَيْب (unseen) — what happens inside the womb, what the angel writes, the endings of people — none of it can be known by observation or reasoning. So Ibn Masʿūd first reminds us that the one reporting it is صادق (truthful in what he says) and مصدوق (confirmed as true by the revelation given to him). Matters of the unseen are received from revelation with full acceptance, exactly as they came.

أطوار الخلق ونفخ الروح The Stages of Creation and the Breathing of the Soul

The ḥadīth lays out three stages of forty days each in the mother's womb:

  1. نُطْفَة (the drop) — the first forty days: the drop of fluid from which the human is gathered and formed.
  2. عَلَقَة (the clinging clot) — the second forty: a clot of blood that clings to the wall of the womb.
  3. مُضْغَة (the morsel of flesh) — the third forty: a small lump of flesh, like a morsel that could be chewed.

After these one hundred and twenty days, the angel is sent and breathes the رُوح (soul) into the child — the point at which it becomes a living human being. Shaykh al-ʿUthaymīn notes that the reality of the رُوح itself is knowledge Allah kept to Himself; our task is to believe what the ḥadīth affirms, not to probe what it left unsaid. These same stages appear in the Qur'an: ﴿ثُمَّ خَلَقْنَا النُّطْفَةَ عَلَقَةً فَخَلَقْنَا الْعَلَقَةَ مُضْغَةً﴾ (al-Muʾminūn 23:14) — the Book and the Sunnah describing one creation with one voice.

الكلمات الأربع والقدر The Four Written Words — and the Decree

While the child is still in the womb, the angel is commanded to write four words: رِزْقُهُ (his provision), أَجَلُهُ (his appointed term), عَمَلُهُ (his deeds), and شَقِيٌّ أَوْ سَعِيدٌ (wretched or happy). Nothing that reaches you of sustenance, no day of your life, no deed of your hands, and no final abode falls outside what Allah already knew and had written.

This is why the scholars count this ḥadīth among the great foundations of belief in القَدَر (the divine decree). al-ʿUthaymīn explains that this womb-writing is one of the writings of the decree: the all-encompassing writing in اللوح المحفوظ (the Preserved Tablet) preceded the creation of the heavens and the earth, and this individual writing at the breathing of the رُوح records the course of one particular servant. The writing follows Allah's knowledge — it does not force anyone; it simply records what Allah knew His servant would freely do. To study the four levels of القدر — knowledge, writing, will, and creation — see the Aqeedah lesson: الإيمان بالقدر — Belief in the Divine Decree.

الأعمال بالخواتيم Deeds Are by Their Seals — Between Fear and Hope

The Prophet ﷺ then swears by Allah that a person may do the deeds of the people of الجنة (Paradise) until only a cubit remains between him and it — then the writ overtakes him, he does the deeds of the people of النار (the Fire), and enters it; and the reverse. The lesson the scholars draw is الأعمال بالخواتيم — deeds are judged by their endings. So the believer walks between two wings: fear, which stops him from ever feeling safe about his own state or looking down on others; and hope, which stops him from despair — for even a lifetime of sin can be sealed with repentance and a good ending.

And here is the balance of Ahl al-Sunnah: the decree is never an excuse to stop working. When the Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ whether they should not simply rely on what is written, he answered: «اعْمَلُوا فَكُلٌّ مُيَسَّرٌ لِمَا خُلِقَ لَهُ» — "Work, for each is eased to that for which he was created." The writing is hidden from you; the command to act is in front of you. You are not asked to read the pen — you are asked to pray, to be honest, to seek the ḥalāl (lawful) provision, and to ask Allah for a good ending.

A gem from Ibn Rajab — فائدة

In جامع العلوم والحكم, Ibn Rajab comforts the fearful heart: the one whose deeds of Paradise are overturned at the last cubit is, in another narration, one whose deeds were only "as they appeared to people" — outwardly sound while concealing a hidden disease within. A bad ending does not ambush a servant whose inside and outside are both upright; and most people die upon what they lived upon. So tend to the deeds of the heart as carefully as the deeds of the limbs, and keep asking Allah for ثَبَات (firmness) until the seal.

Memorize & Renew — احفظ وجدّد

رِزْقُهُ · أَجَلُهُ · عَمَلُهُ · شَقِيٌّ أَوْ سَعِيدٌ

The four words written for you before your birth: your provision, your term, your deeds, and wretched or happy. Recite them until firm — then work, for the pen is no excuse: each of us is eased to that for which he was created. Ask Allah daily for a good ending.
