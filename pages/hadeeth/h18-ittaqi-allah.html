Ḥadīth 18 of 42 · al-Tirmidhī (ḥasan)Year 2

اتَّقِ اللهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْت

Fear Allah Wherever You Are

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define التَّقْوَى as the sharḥ does — a wiqāyah — and say what «حَيْثُمَا كُنْت» adds to the command.
  • Give the grammar of «أَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا» and the meaning it yields.
  • State the scholars’ disagreement over «the good deed», which view is correct, and the two proofs for it.
  • Distinguish الخُلُق from الخَلْق, and give the fourfold definition of good character.
  • Say whether good character is innate or acquired, with the ḥadīth of al-Ashajj ʿAbd al-Qays.
  • Answer which is better — secret or public taqwā — and whether firmness contradicts good character.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢١٩–٢٢٣
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 219–223.

عَنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ جُنْدُبِ بْنِ جُنَادَةَ، وَأَبِي عَبْدِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مُعَاذِ بْنِ جَبَلٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا، عَنْ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ قَالَ: «اتَّقِ اللهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا، وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ». رَوَاهُ التِّرْمِذِيُّ وَقَالَ: حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ، وَفِي بَعْضِ النُّسَخِ: حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ.

Abū Dharr Jundub ibn Junādah and Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān Muʿādh ibn Jabal (may Allah be pleased with them both) reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Fear Allah wherever you are; follow a bad deed with a good one and it will erase it; and deal with people with good character."

Narrated by Abū Dharr & Muʿādh · رَوَاهُ التِّرْمِذِيُّ — al-Tirmidhī, who said: a ḥasan ḥadīth (and in some copies: ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ). The grading named here is al-Tirmidhī’s own.

«اتَّقِ اللهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْت» Fear Allah Wherever You Are

Three commands, and they cover the three relationships a person lives inside: with his Lord, with his own sins, and with other people. The first is taqwā — and the sharḥ defines the word before the command.

«اتَّقِ الله» — take a shield (wiqāyah) from the punishment of Allah; and that is by doing His commands and avoiding His prohibitions.

«حَيْثُمَا كُنْت» is a ẓarf of place — in whatever place you are: in the open or in secret, at home or in the market, whether people are with you or not.

Read what the «wherever» is doing. It is easy to be God-fearing where one is seen; the command reaches precisely to the place no one is watching. Taqwā that switches off in private was never taqwā of Allah — it was taqwā of people.

«وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا» Follow a Bad Deed with a Good One

The second command is for the man who has already slipped — and the grammar carries the meaning. «أَتْبِع» is a verb of command; «السَّيِّئَة» is its first object and «الحَسَنَة» its second; and «تَمْحُهَا» is the jawāb of the command, which is why it is majzūm. The sense: if you do a bad deed, follow it with a good one, and the good deed erases the bad.

Then a real disagreement, which changes how wide the mercy is. Is the «good deed» that erases the sin repentance specifically — as though he said: if you sin, repent — or is it general? The correct view is the second: the good deed erases the sin even if it is not repentance.

وَأَقِمِ الصَّلَاةَ طَرَفَيِ النَّهَارِ وَزُلَفًا مِّنَ اللَّيْلِ إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَاتِ

And establish the prayer at the two ends of the day and in the early hours of the night. Indeed, good deeds drive away bad deeds.

سُورَةُ هُود · 11:114

And the proof from the Sunnah settles it. A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said he had taken from a woman everything a man takes from his wife short of zinā — and he had prayed Fajr with them. The Prophet ﷺ asked: «Did you pray Fajr with us?» He said yes — and the Prophet ﷺ recited to him «إِنَّ الْحَسَنَاتِ يُذْهِبْنَ السَّيِّئَات» (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). The good deed here was the prayer, not a formal repentance — which shows the good deed erases the sin even when it is not itself the repentance.

«وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَن» Deal with People with Good Character

The third command turns outward. «خَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَن» — deal with people with good character. And the Shaykh draws the fine line between two words that sound alike:

الخُلُق
khuluq		the inner trait in a person — the disposition the character flows from.
الخَلْق
khalq		the outer form — the body and appearance. The command is about the first, shown through word and deed.

What is good character, concretely? Some of them said it is four things: كَفُّ الأَذَى — withholding harm from people; بَذْلُ النَّدَى — the giving of kindness; الصَّبْرُ عَلَى الأَذَى — patience over the harm others do, since no one is free of being harmed by people; and الوَجْهُ الطَّلْق — a cheerful, open face.

خُذِ الْعَفْوَ وَأْمُرْ بِالْعُرْفِ وَأَعْرِضْ عَنِ الْجَاهِلِينَ

Take what is easily given, enjoin what is good, and turn away from the ignorant.

سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف · 7:199

Its ḍābiṭ is what Allah named in «خُذِ الْعَفْوَ» — take what people give easily, and do not demand that they come to you as you would wish, for that is impossible; take what comes readily. Good character is not requiring of people a perfection they cannot give.

Is good character innate or acquired? Both: part of it is inborn, and part is won by effort. The Prophet ﷺ said to al-Ashajj ʿAbd al-Qays: «إِنَّ فِيكَ لَخُلُقَيْنِ يُحِبُّهُمَا الله: الحِلْمُ وَالأَنَاة» — in you are two traits Allah loves: forbearance and deliberateness. He asked: did I acquire them, or did Allah fashion me upon them? He said: «Allah fashioned you upon them» (Muslim).

So one may be given a fine character from the outset — and another may train himself to it until it becomes his. And the wonder, the Shaykh notes, is that good character earns the one who has it rest and peace of mind, because he is at ease with himself in dealing with others.

مِنْ فَوَائِدِ الحَدِيث From the Benefits

١ · Which is better — taqwā in secret or in the open? There is a detail here, not a flat rule. If showing it brings about emulation and following — people taking after you in the good — then making it public is better, which is why Allah praised those who spend «سِرًّا وَعَلَانِيَة», and why the Prophet ﷺ said «مَنْ سَنَّ فِي الإِسْلَامِ سُنَّةً حَسَنَةً». But where showing it brings no benefit, secrecy is better — as in the man whom Allah shades, who gave charity so secretly «حَتَّى لَا تَعْلَمَ شِمَالُهُ مَا تُنْفِقُ يَمِينُه».

٢ · Does «wherever you are» mean performing acts of worship even in unfit places — a washroom and the like? No: do not perform the acts there; but intend in your heart that you are obedient to Allah, complying with His command and avoiding His prohibition. The command reaches the heart in every place, not the ritual act in every place.

٣ · Does dealing firmly with people contradict «good character»? It does not — «لِكُلِّ مَقَامٍ مَقَال», to every situation its own speech. Where the benefit lies in firmness, be firm; where it lies the other way, be gentle; and when the two are even, incline to gentleness, for the Prophet ﷺ said «إِنَّ اللهَ رَفِيقٌ يُحِبُّ الرِّفْقَ فِي الأَمْرِ كُلِّه».

The sharḥ’s own illustration: a Jew passed the Prophet ﷺ and said «al-sām ʿalayk» — death be upon you. ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا answered him back with worse. The Prophet ﷺ restrained her and said: «Allah is gentle and loves gentleness in all things» — and «when the People of the Book greet you, say: wa-ʿalaykum» (al-Bukhārī and Muslim). Even provoked, the answer measured to the moment was the gentle one.

إِظْهَارُ التَّقْوَى أَفْضَلُ إِذَا حَصَلَ بِهِ التَّأَسِّي وَالاتِّبَاع. Showing taqwā openly is better when emulation and following result from it.
إِذَا دَارَ الأَمْرُ بَيْنَ اللِّينِ وَالشِّدَّةِ عَلَى حَدٍّ سَوَاءٍ فَالأَفْضَلُ اللِّينُ وَالرِّفْق. When the matter is even between gentleness and firmness, the better course is gentleness and kindness.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

اتَّقِ اللهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا، وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ

Three commands for the three relationships of a life. With your Lord — fear Him in the place no one sees, for taqwā that stops in private was taqwā of people. With your own sins — do not despair; follow the slip with a good deed and it wipes it, even when it is not a formal repentance. With other people — a beautiful character, which is to withhold harm, give kindness, bear what they do, and meet them with an open face. Between a man and his Lord, his record, and his neighbour — the ḥadīth leaves no relation untended.
