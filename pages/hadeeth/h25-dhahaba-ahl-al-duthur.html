Ḥadīth 25 of 42 · MuslimYear 2

ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالأُجُور

The People of Wealth Have Gone Off With the Rewards

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say who narrated this ḥadīth and where it is reported, and what the poor Companions meant by «ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالأُجُور».
  • List the doors of ṣadaqah the Prophet ﷺ opened that cost no wealth.
  • Distinguish ṣadaqah that is obligatory from what is not, and the qāṣir from the mutaʿaddin.
  • State the conditions of amr bi-l-maʿrūf and nahy ʿan al-munkar, and the four outcomes of forbidding an evil.
  • Explain «وَفِي بُضْعِ أَحَدِكُمْ صَدَقَة» by qiyās al-ʿaks — how a lawful desire earns reward.

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ عُثَيْمِين · ص ٢٧٧–٢٨٥
Sharḥ al-Arbaʿīn al-Nawawiyyah, Ibn ʿUthaymīn — pp. 277–285.

عَنْ أَبِي ذَرٍّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ أَيْضًا أَنَّ أُنَاسًا مِنْ أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ قَالُوا لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالأُجُورِ؛ يُصَلُّونَ كَمَا نُصَلِّي، وَيَصُومُونَ كَمَا نَصُومُ، وَيَتَصَدَّقُونَ بِفُضُولِ أَمْوَالِهِمْ. قَالَ: «أَوَلَيْسَ قَدْ جَعَلَ اللهُ لَكُمْ مَا تَصَّدَّقُونَ بِهِ؟ إِنَّ بِكُلِّ تَسْبِيحَةٍ صَدَقَةً، وَكُلِّ تَكْبِيرَةٍ صَدَقَةً، وَكُلِّ تَحْمِيدَةٍ صَدَقَةً، وَكُلِّ تَهْلِيلَةٍ صَدَقَةً، وَأَمْرٌ بِالمَعْرُوفِ صَدَقَةٌ، وَنَهْيٌ عَنْ مُنْكَرٍ صَدَقَةٌ، وَفِي بُضْعِ أَحَدِكُمْ صَدَقَةٌ». قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، أَيَأْتِي أَحَدُنَا شَهْوَتَهُ وَيَكُونُ لَهُ فِيهَا أَجْرٌ؟ قَالَ: «أَرَأَيْتُمْ لَوْ وَضَعَهَا فِي حَرَامٍ أَكَانَ عَلَيْهِ وِزْرٌ؟ فَكَذَلِكَ إِذَا وَضَعَهَا فِي الحَلَالِ كَانَ لَهُ أَجْرٌ». رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم.

Abū Dharr (may Allah be pleased with him) also reported that some of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said to the Prophet ﷺ: "O Messenger of Allah, the people of wealth have gone off with the rewards: they pray as we pray, they fast as we fast, and they give charity from the surplus of their wealth." He said: "Has Allah not made for you what you can give as charity? In every tasbīḥah is a charity, every takbīrah a charity, every taḥmīdah a charity, every tahlīlah a charity; enjoining good is a charity, forbidding evil is a charity, and in the intimacy of one of you with his wife there is a charity." They said: "O Messenger of Allah, does one of us fulfil his desire and have a reward in it?" He said: "Do you not see that were he to place it in the forbidden there would be a sin upon him? So likewise, when he places it in the lawful, he has a reward." Narrated by Muslim.

Narrated by Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِم — Muslim, in his Ṣaḥīḥ (Kitāb al-Zakāh, bāb anna ism al-ṣadaqah yaqaʿu ʿalā kull maʿrūf), so the ḥadīth is ṣaḥīḥ.

«ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالأُجُور» The Poor Companions’ Complaint

“Ahl al-duthūr” are the people of abundant wealth; «bi-l-ujūr» — they carried off the rewards. The poor Companions did not mean envy, nor an objection to Allah’s decree. They prayed and fasted as the rich did, but had nothing to give in charity — so they came hoping the Prophet ﷺ would open them a door by which to catch the race.

أَبْوَابٌ فَتَحَهَا لَهُم The Doors He Opened

“Has Allah not made for you what you can give as charity?” He affirmed them with what they could not deny (taqrīr), then named the deeds — every one a ṣadaqah, and none of them costing a dirham:

«تَسْبِيحَة / تَكْبِيرَة»To say سُبْحَانَ الله is a charity; to say اللهُ أَكْبَر is a charity.
«تَحْمِيدَة / تَهْلِيلَة»To say الحَمْدُ لِله is a charity; to say لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا الله is a charity.
«أَمْرٌ بِالمَعْرُوف»To command one you see falling short in an act of obedience is a charity.
«نَهْيٌ عَنْ مُنْكَر»To forbid one you see upon an evil is a charity.
«بُضْعِ أَحَدِكُم»And in a man’s intimacy with his wife there is a charity.

Bodily worship is shared by rich and poor alike. “They pray as we pray and fast as we fast” — and the poor man’s performance may be more complete than the rich man’s. The rich may not give charity every day, and when they do they do not fill the day with it; but you can fill every hour with tasbīḥ, takbīr, tahlīl and taḥmīd.

لَيْسَتِ الصَّدَقَاتُ سَوَاء Not Every Charity Is Equal

All he named is ṣadaqah — but it is of two sorts twice over.

وَاجِب / غَيْر وَاجِبSome is obligatory, some not. The takbīr of the prayer is wājib; the dhikr after it is mustaḥabb — and so for the rest. Enjoining good and forbidding evil are of the obligatory.
قَاصِر / مُتَعَدٍّSome is confined to the doer (قَاصِر) — like the dhikr; some reaches others (مُتَعَدٍّ) — like enjoining good and forbidding evil. The mutaʿaddin benefits the one commanded as well as the commander.
شُرُوطُ الأَمْرِ وَالنَّهْي The Conditions of Commanding Good and Forbidding Evil

وَلْتَكُن مِّنكُمْ أُمَّةٌ يَدْعُونَ إِلَى الْخَيْرِ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ

And let there be among you a community calling to good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong.

سُورَةُ آلِ عِمْرَان · 3:104

Two conditions for commanding good: (1) that the commander knows it is a maʿrūf — for if he commands out of ignorance he has spoken about Allah what he does not know; (2) that he knows the person has left that maʿrūf, or else he asks first. So when a man entered during the Friday khuṭbah and sat down, the Prophet ﷺ asked, “Did you pray?” — “No.” — “Stand and pray two rakʿahs.” He did not command until he had asked.

Three conditions for forbidding an evil:

١ · أَنْ يَكُونَ مُنْكَرًا بِالدَّلِيلThat you know it is munkar by the sharī‘ah’s proof — not by taste, custom, jealousy, or emotion.
٢ · أَنْ يَكُونَ قَدْ وَقَعَ فِيهThat you know this person has actually fallen into it — do not rebuke a man eating in Ramaḍān until you ask whether he is a traveller.
٣ · أَلَّا يَؤُولَ إِلَى مَا هُوَ أَعْظَمThat forbidding it not lead to something worse.

Four outcomes of forbidding an evil: (1) it is removed entirely → forbidding is wājib; (2) it is lessened → wājib, for lessening evil is a duty; (3) it turns into a like evil → a place of consideration (naẓar); (4) it turns into a greater evil → forbidding is ḥarām.

The proof of the last is «وَلَا تَسُبُّوا الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِنْ دُونِ الله» — do not revile their false gods, lest they revile Allah in enmity and ignorance. Ibn Taymiyyah passed Tatars drinking wine and did not forbid them: had he done so they would have risen to plunder people’s homes and violate their honour — a greater evil. See the fiqh in the religion of Allah.

وَلَا تَسُبُّوا الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ فَيَسُبُّوا اللَّهَ عَدْوًا بِغَيْرِ عِلْمٍ

And do not revile those they invoke besides Allah, lest they revile Allah in enmity without knowledge.

سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام · 6:108

«وَفِي بُضْعِ أَحَدِكُمْ صَدَقَة» A Lawful Desire — and Qiyās al-ʿAks

“Does one of us fulfil his desire and have a reward in it?” They asked to understand, not to object. The Prophet ﷺ answered by an analogy of opposites (قِيَاسُ العَكْس): were he to place that desire in the forbidden, there would be a sin upon him — and all agree there would — so, by the opposite, placing it in the lawful earns him a reward.

And it is a charity even for mere desire — not only to fend off the ḥarām — for two reasons:

(1) A person is commanded not to deny his soul its lawful desire: «إِنَّ لِنَفْسِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَقًّا» (al-Bukhārī). (2) In coming to his wife he does good to her, for she has of desire what he has. Sufficing with the lawful in place of the forbidden makes the lawful itself a nearness and a charity.

مَا لَمْ يَسْأَلْ عَنْهُ الصَّحَابَة What the Companions Did Not Ask

The Companions left no difficulty unasked — “does one of us fulfil his desire and have reward in it?” By this we learn that a matter of religion the Companions did not ask about — asking about it now is an innovation: had it been of the religion, Allah would have raised someone to ask. When the Prophet ﷺ spoke of the Dajjāl’s first day being like a year, and they asked whether one prayer would suffice that day, he said: «اقْدُرُوا لَهُ قَدْرَهُ» — estimate its measure. What people need in their religion either came from him ﷺ at the start or in answer to a question; what did neither, and touches the religion, is a bidʿah to raise.

وَتَعَاوَنُوا عَلَى الْبِرِّ وَالتَّقْوَىٰ

And cooperate in righteousness and piety.

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:2

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
قَصْدُ الفُقَرَاءِ بِقَوْلِهِمْ «ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ» الحَسَدُ وَالاِعْتِرَاضُ عَلَى القَدَر. The poor Companions’ aim in saying “the people of wealth have gone off…” was envy and objection to the decree.
مِنَ الصَّدَقَةِ قَوْلُ سُبْحَانَ اللهِ وَالحَمْدُ لِلهِ وَاللهُ أَكْبَر. Saying subḥānallāh, al-ḥamdu lillāh and Allāhu akbar is of the charity.
الأَمْرُ بِالمَعْرُوفِ وَالنَّهْيُ عَنِ المُنْكَرِ صَدَقَةٌ مُتَعَدِّيَةٌ تَنْفَعُ الغَيْر. Enjoining good and forbidding evil is a charity that reaches others.
يَجُوزُ أَنْ تَأْمُرَ بِمَعْرُوفٍ وَأَنْتَ جَاهِلٌ بِكَوْنِهِ مَعْرُوفًا. It is permitted to enjoin a maʿrūf while you are ignorant of its being a maʿrūf.
إِذَا كَانَ إِنْكَارُ المُنْكَرِ يَؤُولُ إِلَى مُنْكَرٍ أَعْظَمَ فَالإِنْكَارُ حَرَام. If forbidding an evil would lead to a greater evil, then forbidding it is ḥarām.
أَثْبَتَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ الأَجْرَ فِي الشَّهْوَةِ الحَلَالِ بِقِيَاسِ العَكْس. The Prophet ﷺ established the reward in a lawful desire by the analogy of opposites.
كُلُّ مَا لَمْ يَسْأَلْ عَنْهُ الصَّحَابَةُ مِنْ أُمُورِ الدِّينِ فَالسُّؤَالُ عَنْهُ سُنَّةٌ مُسْتَحَبَّة. Everything of religion the Companions did not ask about — asking about it is a recommended sunnah.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَوَلَيْسَ قَدْ جَعَلَ اللهُ لَكُمْ مَا تَصَّدَّقُونَ بِهِ؟ إِنَّ بِكُلِّ تَسْبِيحَةٍ صَدَقَةً… وَفِي بُضْعِ أَحَدِكُمْ صَدَقَة

The poor Companions feared the rich had outrun them in reward through charity. So the Prophet ﷺ opened doors that cost nothing: every tasbīḥ, takbīr, taḥmīd and tahlīl a charity; enjoining good and forbidding evil a charity — each with its conditions; and even a man’s intimacy with his wife a charity. When they wondered how a desire could be rewarded, he answered by qiyās al-ʿaks: if it were a sin in the forbidden, it is a reward in the lawful. Worship is not priced in wealth — the tongue, the hand, and the lawful life are all roads of ṣadaqah.

دَارُ الهِجْرَة — المَدْرَسَةُ السَّلَفِيَّةُ التَّأْسِيسِيَّةُ لِلْعُلُومِ الشَّرْعِيَّة

al-Arbaʿūn al-Nawawiyyah · sharḥ of Shaykh Muḥammad ibn Ṣāliḥ al-ʿUthaymīn · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
