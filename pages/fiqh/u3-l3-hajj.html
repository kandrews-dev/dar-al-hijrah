Ḥajj — The Fifth Pillar

Year 2+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • Explain the meaning of Ḥajj and that it is the fifth pillar
  • State the conditions that make Ḥajj obligatory

معنى الحج ومكانتهThe Meaning & Status of Ḥajj

Ḥajj (حج) is the fifth pillar of Islam — the pilgrimage to the Sacred House (the Kaʿbah) in Makkah at a specific time, to perform specific rites. The word (root ح-ج-ج) means to intend and to set out toward a venerated place. It is obligatory once in a lifetime upon whoever is able.

وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا

"Pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by people — whoever is able to find a way to it."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:97 · سورة آل عمران

Its reward is immense: "Whoever performs Ḥajj and does not commit obscenity or sin returns [free of sin] as the day his mother bore him" (Bukhārī & Muslim), and "an accepted Ḥajj has no reward but Paradise."

شروط وجوب الحجWhen Ḥajj Becomes Obligatory

الإسلام

Being Muslim.

العقل

Sound mind (sanity).

البلوغ

Adulthood (a child's Ḥajj is valid but does not discharge the obligation).

الحرية

Being free.

الاستطاعة

Ability — physical health, sufficient lawful provision, and a safe route (for a woman, a maḥram per many scholars).

Istiṭāʿah (ability) is the key condition. One who is physically unable but wealthy may appoint someone to perform Ḥajj on their behalf (al-niyābah). One who lacks the means is simply not obligated until they have it.

المواقيت المكانيةThe Mawāqīt — Boundary Stations for Iḥrām

The Prophet ﷺ fixed five mawāqīt — locations around Makkah beyond which no pilgrim may pass (heading to Makkah for Ḥajj/ʿUmrah) without entering iḥrām. Each serves the people who pass through it and those beyond:

MīqātFor the people of...
ذو الحُلَيْفَة Dhul-Ḥulayfah (Abyār ʿAlī)Madinah
الجُحْفَة al-Juḥfah (near Rābigh)al-Shām, Egypt, the West
قَرْن المَنازِل Qarn al-Manāzil (al-Sayl)Najd & the East (incl. those flying via the Gulf)
يَلَمْلَم YalamlamYemen
ذات عِرْق Dhāt ʿIrqIraq

Whoever lives nearer than a mīqāt (e.g. the people of Makkah) enters iḥrām from where they are. Air travellers enter iḥrām when they reach the level of their mīqāt — often announced on the flight.

الإحرام والتلبيةEntering Iḥrām & the Talbiyah

Iḥrām is the state of consecration the pilgrim enters with the intention to begin the rites. It is recommended to bathe, perfume the body (not the garments), and for men to wear two white unstitched sheets; women wear ordinary modest clothing. Then one declares the intention and begins the talbiyah:

📣 The Talbiyah — التلبية

لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ

"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am, You have no partner, here I am. Indeed all praise, grace, and dominion are Yours; You have no partner."

In iḥrām, certain things become prohibited (maḥẓūrāt) until one exits it:

  • ✗ Cutting hair or nails  ·  ✗ Using perfume  ·  ✗ Hunting game
  • ✗ Marriage contracts  ·  ✗ Marital relations  ·  ✗ (men) sewn/fitted clothing & covering the head
  • ✗ (women) covering the face with the niqāb or wearing gloves (she covers in other ways before men)

Notice how iḥrām strips away worldly distinctions — rich and poor stand identical before Allah in two simple sheets. (We follow the rites themselves in L.4.)
