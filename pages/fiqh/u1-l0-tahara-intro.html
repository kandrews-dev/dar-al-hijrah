مَا هِيَ الطَّهَارَة؟

What Is Ṭahārah (Purification)?

Year 1+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • Define ṭahārah and explain why it matters — تعريف الطهارة
  • Distinguish pure water (ṭahūr) from impure water (najis)
  • Explain what najāsah is and how it is removed
  • Distinguish minor ḥadath (removed by wudūʾ) from major ḥadath (removed by ghusl)
مَعْنَى الطَّهَارَةِ وَمَكَانَتُهَا The Meaning & Status of Purification

Welcome to Fiqh — the study of how to worship Allah correctly. We begin exactly where worship itself begins: with ṭahārah (طهارة), purification. Before a Muslim can stand in prayer, they must be in a state of purity. That is why every fiqh book opens with this chapter.

In the Arabic language, ṭahārah (root ط-ه-ر) means cleanliness and freedom from filth. In the Sharīʿah, it means removing what prevents prayer — both physical filth (najāsah) and ritual impurity (ḥadath).

«الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الإِيمَانِ»

"Purification is half of faith."

Narrated by Muslim · رواه مسلم

Purification has two branches that this unit will cover: purification from najāsah (removing filth from the body, clothes, and place of prayer) and purification from ḥadath (the ritual state removed by wudūʾ, ghusl, or tayammum).

المِيَاهُ وَأَقْسَامُهَا Water — the Default Means of Purification

The primary means of purification is water. The foundational principle, taught by the Prophet ﷺ, is that water is pure by default — rain, sea, river, well, and spring water are all طَهُور (ṭahūr): pure in itself and purifying for other things.

وَأَنزَلْنَا مِنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً طَهُورًا

"And We sent down from the sky pure water."

Sūrat al-Furqān 25:48 · سورة الفرقان

طَهُور

Ṭahūr — Pure & purifying

Water in its natural state. You may drink it and purify with it. This is the default for all water.

نَجِس

Najis — Impure

Water becomes impure only if a najāsah changes its colour, taste, or smell. Then it may not be used.

«إِنَّ المَاءَ طَهُورٌ لَا يُنَجِّسُهُ شَيْءٌ»

"Indeed water is pure; nothing makes it impure."

Narrated by Abū Dāwūd & al-Tirmidhī — ṣaḥīḥ · حديث بئر بضاعة

The simple rule: treat any water as pure and usable unless you are certain a najāsah has changed one of its three qualities — colour, taste, or smell. This is the merciful and evidence-based principle. (Classical books also mention a third category, ṭāhir, used water — a point of scholarly difference.)

النَّجَاسَةُ وَإِزَالَتُهَا Najāsah (Filth) & How to Remove It

Najāsah is physical filth that the Sharīʿah commands us to remove from our body, clothing, and place of prayer. Common examples include urine and stool, flowing blood, and the like. The default for everything else is purity — we do not declare things filthy without evidence.

Removing najāsah simply means washing the affected spot until the filth (its colour and smell) is gone. For example, when a bedouin urinated in the mosque, the Prophet ﷺ ordered a bucket of water to be poured over the spot — showing how easy purification is.

وَثِيَابَكَ فَطَهِّرْ

"And purify your garments."

Sūrat al-Muddaththir 74:4 · سورة المدثر

الحَدَثُ الأَصْغَرُ وَالأَكْبَر The Two Kinds of Ḥadath

Ḥadath is not visible filth — it is a ritual state that prevents prayer until it is lifted. It comes in two levels, and knowing the difference is the key that unlocks this entire unit:

الحَدَث الأصْغَر

Minor ḥadath

Caused by things like using the toilet or passing wind. Removed by wudūʾ (L.1) — or by tayammum when there is no water (L.4).

الحَدَث الأكْبَر

Major ḥadath

Caused by things like janābah and the end of menses. Removed by ghusl (L.3) — or by tayammum when there is no water.

The map of this unit: minor ḥadath → Wudūʾ (and wiping over the Khuffs); major ḥadath → Ghusl; and when water is absent → Tayammum stands in for both. That is exactly the order of the next four lessons.
