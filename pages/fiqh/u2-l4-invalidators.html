مُبْطِلَاتُ الصَّلَاةِ وَسُجُودُ السَّهْو

What Invalidates Prayer & the Prostration of Forgetfulness

Year 1+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • List the main things that invalidate the prayer
  • Explain the three cases of sujūd al-sahw and when it comes before or after the salām
  • Apply the sahw rules to real situations
  • Explain khushūʿ as the soul of the prayer
مُبْطِلَاتُ الصَّلَاةWhat Invalidates the Prayer

Certain actions break the prayer and make it invalid, requiring it to be repeated. The main ones:

  • 1. Deliberate speech that is not part of the prayer — "this prayer of ours is not fit for any human speech" (Muslim).
  • 2. Eating or drinking deliberately.
  • 3. Much continuous movement unrelated to the prayer (small necessary movements are excused).
  • 4. Losing a condition — breaking wudūʾ, exposing the ʿawrah, turning the chest away from the qiblah (from L.1).
  • 5. Deliberately leaving a pillar or condition (from L.3).
  • 6. Laughing out loud (qahqahah). Note: a minor smile does not break it.
سُجُودُ السَّهْوSujūd al-Sahw — the Forgetfulness Prostration

We are human, and we forget in prayer. Allah's mercy gave us a remedy: two prostrations that mend a mistake. They apply in three cases. The Prophet ﷺ said: "If one of you doubts in his prayer and does not know how much he has prayed, let him strive for what is correct, then complete it, then prostrate twice" (Bukhārī & Muslim).

CASE 1

الزيادة — Addition

You added something by mistake — e.g. an extra rakʿah, rukūʿ, or sujūd. → Prostrate twice after the salām. (As the Prophet ﷺ did in the ḥadīth of Dhū al-Yadayn, when he prayed and added/shortened.)

CASE 2

النقص — Omission of a Wājib

You left out an obligation (wājib) by mistake — e.g. forgot the first tashahhud and already stood up. → Prostrate twice before the salām. (You do not go back for it once you've stood.)

CASE 3

الشك — Doubt

You are unsure how many rakʿahs you prayed. → Build on what you are certain of (the lesser number), complete the prayer, and prostrate twice before the salām.

Memory aid: Added something → sajdah after salām. Missed a wājib or in doubt → sajdah before salām. The two prostrations of sahw are done exactly like normal prostrations (with "Subḥāna Rabbiya al-Aʿlā"), followed by the salām.

طَبِّقْ — Apply the Sahw RulesBefore or After the Salām?

For each case, tap the correct response. Feedback appears after you choose.

1. During Ẓuhr, a man absentmindedly stands up for a fifth rakʿah, then realises and sits back. What does he do?

Sajdah after salām. This is an addition (Case 1). He completes the tashahhud, makes the salām, then prostrates twice and gives salām again.

2. In Maghrib, a woman forgets the first tashahhud and has already stood for the third rakʿah. What does she do?

Sajdah before salām. She left a wājib (Case 2) and should not return once standing — she continues and prostrates twice before the salām.

3. A man is unsure whether he is in the third or fourth rakʿah of ʿIshāʾ, with no strong inclination either way.

Build on certainty (three), then sajdah before salām. This is doubt (Case 3): take the lesser certain number, pray the remaining rakʿah, then prostrate twice before the salām.

4. A woman forgets to say the opening duʿāʾ (istiftāḥ) and goes straight to al-Fātiḥah.

Nothing is owed. The istiftāḥ is only a sunnah (from L.3) — leaving it requires no sujūd al-sahw at all.

الخُشُوعُ — رُوحُ الصَّلَاةKhushūʿ — the Soul of the Prayer

We have learned the body of the prayer — its conditions, pillars, and how to mend its mistakes. But a prayer can be outwardly perfect yet empty. Its soul is الخُشُوع (khushūʿ) — the presence and humility of the heart, knowing you stand before Allah (recall al-Raqīb and iḥsān from Aqeedah).

قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۝ الَّذِينَ هُمْ فِي صَلَاتِهِمْ خَاشِعُونَ

"Successful indeed are the believers — those who are humbly submissive in their prayer."

Sūrat al-Muʾminūn 23:1–2 · سورة المؤمنون

Strive to understand what you recite, pray slowly with ṭumaʾnīnah, and remember that this may be your last prayer. A person is rewarded only for the part of the prayer in which they were truly present.
