الإعراب والأقسام الأربعة

Iʿrāb & the Four Cases

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand what iʿrāb means.
  • Learn the four cases: rafʿ, naṣb, khafḍ and jazm.
  • Know which cases belong to nouns and which to verbs.

In Lesson 1 you learned the three kinds of word. Now we meet the idea that makes Arabic so precise: iʿrāb — the way a word's ending changes to show its job in the sentence.

ما هو الإعراب؟What is Iʿrāb?

Iʿrāb is the change that comes over the end of a word because of the different words that govern it. Look at one word doing three different jobs:

SentenceالمثالThe word "house"
The house is bigالبيتُ كبيرٌالبيتُ — ends in -u
I saw the houseرأيتُ البيتَالبيتَ — ends in -a
I passed by the houseمررتُ بالبيتِالبيتِ — ends in -i

Same word — three different endings, because it is doing three different jobs. That changing ending is iʿrāb. (A word whose ending never changes is called mabnī, "fixed.")

الأقسام الأربعةThe Four Cases
الرَّفْعar-rafʿ · nominative

The "doer / subject" state — e.g. البيتُ.

النَّصْبan-naṣb · accusative

The "object" state — e.g. البيتَ.

الخَفْض (الجَرّ)al-khafḍ / al-jarr · genitive

After a preposition or in possession — e.g. بالبيتِ.

الجَزْمal-jazm · jussive

A "cut-off" ending — for verbs only — e.g. لم يذهبْ.

The key rule — قاعدة

Nouns take rafʿ, naṣb and khafḍ — but never jazm. Verbs (the muḍāriʿ) take rafʿ, naṣb and jazm — but never khafḍ. So khafḍ is special to nouns, and jazm is special to verbs.
