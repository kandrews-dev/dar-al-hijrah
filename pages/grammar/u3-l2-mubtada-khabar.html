المُبْتَدَأُ وَالخَبَر

Subject & Predicate

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Recognize a nominal sentence (jumla ismiyya).
  • Identify the mubtadaʾ (subject) and the khabar (predicate).
  • Know that both are marfūʿ.

Last lesson, the sentence began with a verb. The second kind begins with a noun — the nominal sentence — and it has two parts working together.

الجملة الاسميةThe Nominal Sentence

A nominal sentence opens with a noun and is built from two parts:

المبتدأal-mubtadaʾ · the subject

The noun we begin with — what the sentence is about. It is marfūʿ.

الخبرal-khabar · the predicate

The information we give about the subject — it completes the meaning. It too is marfūʿ.

SentenceالمثالSubject / Predicate
The mosque is bigالمسجدُ كبيرٌالمسجدُ mubtadaʾ · كبيرٌ khabar
Allah is our Lordاللهُ ربُّنااللهُ mubtadaʾ · ربُّنا khabar
The believers are successfulالمؤمنونَ مفلحونَالمؤمنونَ · مفلحونَ

Both are marfūʿ — القاعدة

In المسجدُ كبيرٌ notice that both words end in a ḍammah — the subject and the predicate are both marfūʿ. And how do you tell the two sentence types apart? Look at the first word: a verb → verbal sentence; a noun → nominal sentence.

Memorize — احفظ

"The subject and the predicate are both marfūʿ." You can now read both sentence types of Arabic — verbal and nominal — and name their marfūʿ parts.
