الكلمة وعلاماتها

The Word and Its Signs

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Meet ابن هشام (Ibn Hishām) and his primer قطر الندى — the text of the Year 3 naḥw ladder.
  • Define الكلمة (the word) precisely: قولٌ مفردٌ — a single meaningful utterance.
  • Know the diagnostic signs (علامات) that test whether a word is اسم، فعل or حرف.
  • Apply the tests to real words — from the Qur'an and everyday speech.
Where we are: al-Ājurrūmiyyah (Lesson 1) told you that every word is اسمٌ أو فعلٌ أو حرفٌ — and you believed it. Now ابن هشام (Ibn Hishām, d. 761 AH), one of the greatest grammarians of Egypt, teaches you to prove it: each type of word carries signs, like fingerprints. This is where the Year 3 ladder begins — the same ground, at greater depth, exactly as the institutes climb from al-Ājurrūmiyyah to Qaṭr al-Nadā.
أولاً: تعريف الكلمة Part One — Defining the Word

الكلمةُ قولٌ مفردٌ

This is how Ibn Hishām opens Qaṭr al-Nadā: a word is a single meaningful utterance. قول (utterance) — it is spoken sound that carries meaning, not a cough or a knock; مفرد (single) — it is one unit, not a compound like a full sentence. So زَيْد is a كلمة; قَامَ زَيْدٌ is two of them joined into كلام (speech). And every كلمة, without exception, is one of three: اسم (noun), فعل (verb), or حرف (particle).

But how do you know which of the three a given word is? Not by guessing — by testing. Each type accepts certain signs and rejects others. Hold any word up to the tests below and it will confess what it is.

ثانياً: علامات الاسم Part Two — Signs of the Noun

The grammarians give the اسم (noun) five fingerprints. If a word passes any one of these tests, it is an اسم:

العلامة The test مثال
الجرIt can take kasrah — the jarr case sits on itبِزَيْدٍ
التنوينIt can carry tanwīn (the doubled ending)زَيْدٌ
دخول ألIt accepts أل ("the") on its frontالرَّجُل
حرف الجر قبلهA preposition can sit before itفِي البَيْتِ
الإسناد إليهYou can predicate of it — say something about itزَيْدٌ قَائِمٌ
ثالثاً: علامات الفعل Part Three — Signs of the Verb

The فعل (verb) has its own fingerprints — signs that only ever attach to verbs:

العلامة The test مثال
قَدْقَدْ can come before itقَدْ أَفْلَحَ
السين / سَوْفَThe future markers سَـ / سَوْفَ can precede itسَيَقُولُ · سَوْفَ تَعْلَمُونَ
تاء التأنيث الساكنةThe quiet tāʾ of femininity can end itقَالَتْ
تاء الفاعلThe tāʾ of the doer ("I did") can attach to itكَتَبْتُ
رابعاً: الحرف Part Four — The Particle

الحرف ما لا يقبل شيئاً من علامات الاسم ولا علامات الفعل

The حرف (particle) is known by having no signs — it rejects every test. Try مِنْ، هَلْ، لَمْ: no أل will sit on them, no tanwīn, no قَدْ, no سَوْفَ, no tāʾ. Ibn Hishām's elegant point: the sign of the حرف is the absence of signs. When a word refuses every fingerprint of the noun and every fingerprint of the verb, that refusal itself identifies it.

أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

قَدْ أَفْلَحَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ

Successful indeed are the believers. (al-Muʼminūn 23:1)

قَدْ proves أَفْلَحَ is a فعل · الْمُؤْمِنُونَ wears أل and the rafʿ-wāw — an اسم twice over

سَيَقُولُ السُّفَهَاءُ مِنَ النَّاسِ

The foolish among the people will say… (al-Baqarah 2:142)

The س of the future proves يَقُولُ a فعل · مِنَ sits before النَّاسِ, proving it an اسم · and مِنْ itself passes no test — a حرف

Memorize — احفظ

علامات الاسم: الجر والتنوين وأل وحرف الجر والإسناد · وعلامات الفعل: قد والسين وسوف والتاءان

The signs of the noun: jarr, tanwīn, أل, the preposition, and predication. The signs of the verb: قد, the sīn, sawfa, and the two tāʾs. Take any āyah, hold each word up to the tests, and watch it confess: noun, verb, or particle.
