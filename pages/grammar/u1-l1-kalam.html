الكلام وأقسامه

Speech & its Three Parts

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know what al-kalām (speech) means in grammar.
  • Learn its three parts: the noun, the verb, and the particle.
  • Be able to tell which part any Arabic word belongs to.

Grammar (naḥw) begins here. Every sentence in the Qur'an and the Sunnah is built from just three kinds of words. Once you can name them, you have taken your first real step toward reading Arabic on your own. We build this on al-Ājurrūmiyyah.

ما هو الكلام؟ What is al-Kalām?

In grammar, al-kalām is not just any sound — it is a complete, meaningful sentence in Arabic. For example, قَامَ زَيْدٌ ("Zayd stood up") is kalām: it is composed of words and gives a full, useful meaning. But a single word like زَيْد ("Zayd") on its own, or a meaningless string, is not kalām — it does not complete a thought.

And every kalām is built from three parts of speech (aqsām al-kalām), no more: the ism (noun), the fiʿl (verb), and the ḥarf (particle).

الأقسام الثلاثة The Three Parts
الاسمal-ism · the noun

A word that names a person, place, thing or idea, with no sense of time attached to it. Examples: رَجُل (man), مَسْجِد (mosque), كِتَاب (book), مَكَّة (Makkah), صَبْر (patience).

الفعلal-fiʿl · the verb

A word that shows an action joined to a time — past, present or future. Examples: كَتَبَ (he wrote — past), يَكْتُبُ (he writes — present), اُكْتُبْ (write! — command).

الحرفal-ḥarf · the particle

A small word that has no clear meaning on its own — it only gives meaning when joined to a noun or a verb. Examples: مِنْ (from), إِلَى (to), فِي (in), هَلْ (is…?), لَمْ (did not).

علامات كل قسم How to Recognize Each

Arabic gives you signs (ʿalāmāt) so you can test any word:

PartالقسمA simple test
NounاسمYou can put الـ ("the") on it (الكِتَاب), or a tanwīn (كِتَابٌ), or a preposition before it (فِي البَيْت).
VerbفعلYou can put قَدْ, سَـ / سَوْفَ (will) before it, or end it with the quiet tāʾ (كَتَبَتْ).
ParticleحرفIt accepts none of the signs above — that absence is itself the sign of a ḥarf.

Try it together — مثال

ذَهَبَ المُسْلِمُ إِلَى المَسْجِدِ

"The Muslim went to the mosque."

ذَهَبَ = a verb (an action in the past). المُسْلِم = a noun (it carries الـ). إِلَى = a particle (it only links). المَسْجِد = a noun (الـ, and a preposition came before it). Four words — and you just parsed your first Arabic sentence.

"Speech is three parts: a noun, a verb, and a particle." Memorize this with the children today — then, reading any āyah, point to a word and ask: noun, verb, or particle?
