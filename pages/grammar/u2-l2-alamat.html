علامات الإعراب

The Signs of the Cases

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Learn the sign (vowel) that shows each of the four cases.
  • Read an ending and name its case.
  • Recognize these signs in the Qur'an.

Last lesson you met the four cases. But how do you see a case on a word? By its sign (ʿalāmah) — the little vowel on the last letter. Each case has its own.

العلامات الأصليةThe Four Main Signs
CaseالعلامةSignمثال
Rafʿـُḍammah (-u)جاءَ المسلمُ
Naṣbـَfatḥah (-a)رأيتُ المسلمَ
Khafḍـِkasrah (-i)مررتُ بالمسلمِ
Jazmـْsukūn (no vowel)لم يذهبْ

Read the three sentences about "the Muslim": المسلمُ (came — ḍammah, rafʿ), المسلمَ (I saw — fatḥah, naṣb), المسلمِ (I passed by — kasrah, khafḍ). The job changed, so the sign changed.

Good to know — فائدة

These four are the original signs, for the ordinary single word. Some words (duals, sound plurals, the five nouns, the five verbs) show their case with a letter instead — wāw, alif, yāʾ, or a dropped nūn. Those "sub-signs" come in a later lesson; master these four first.

Memorize — احفظ

الرفعُ ضمةٌ، والنصبُ فتحةٌ، والخفضُ كسرةٌ، والجزمُ سكونٌ

"Rafʿ is a ḍammah, naṣb is a fatḥah, khafḍ is a kasrah, and jazm is a sukūn." Open any āyah and read the last vowel of a word — you can now name its case.
