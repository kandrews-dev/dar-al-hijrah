الأفعال الثلاثة

The Three Verbs

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Learn the three kinds of verb by their time.
  • Recognize each by a simple sign.
  • Know which verb changes its ending (takes iʿrāb).

In Lesson 1 the verb was one of the three parts of speech. The verb itself comes in three kinds, sorted by time.

الأقسام الثلاثةThe Three Kinds
الماضيal-māḍī · the past

An action that already happened: كَتَبَ (he wrote), صَلَّى (he prayed). Its ending is fixed (mabnī).

المضارعal-muḍāriʿ · the present/future

An action happening now or to come: يَكْتُبُ (he writes / will write). It begins with one of the four letters أ · ن · ي · ت ("a-na-ya-ta"). This is the only verb whose ending changes (muʿrab).

الأمرal-amr · the command

An order to do something: اُكْتُبْ (write!), اِقْرَأْ (read!). Its ending is fixed (mabnī), usually on sukūn.

علامة كل فعلA Sign for Each
VerbالفعلRecognize it by…
Pastماضٍthe quiet tāʾ of femininity (كتبَتْ — "she wrote")
Presentمضارعstarts with أ/ن/ي/ت; accepts قد، السين، سوف، لم
Commandأمرit orders, and accepts the yāʾ of the feminine addressee (اكتبي — "write!")

Tie it back — ربط

Remember from Lesson 2: only the muḍāriʿ takes iʿrāb (rafʿ, naṣb, jazm). The past and the command are mabnī — fixed. So when you study the cases of the verb, you are always studying the muḍāriʿ.

اِقْرَأْ (read!) is which kind of verb?

Which verb is muʿrab — its ending changes (takes iʿrāb)?

Memorize — احفظ

الفعلُ ثلاثةٌ: ماضٍ، ومضارعٌ، وأمرٌ

"The verb is three: past, present, and command." Only the muḍāriʿ changes its ending. You now hold the whole frame of Arabic: three words, four cases, four signs, three verbs.
