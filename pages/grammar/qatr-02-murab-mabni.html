المعرب والمبني

The Declinable and the Indeclinable

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Split every word in the language into مُعْرَب (declinable — its ending changes) and مَبْنِيّ (indeclinable — its ending is fixed).
  • Know the classical rules: which verbs are مبني and on what, and when the مضارع loses its iʿrāb.
  • Recognise the closed lists of مبني nouns — pronouns, demonstratives, relatives and their sisters.
  • Name the four building-vowels and use the formula مبنيٌّ على — في محلِّ — in a real iʿrāb line.
Where we are: Lesson 1 taught you to test any word and prove it an اسم، فعل or حرف. Now ابن هشام makes the second great cut, the one every iʿrāb line you will ever recite depends on: does this word's ending move, or is it frozen? We explain his matn in our own words, leaning on the sharḥ tradition — including ابن عثيمين's commentary on the Qaṭr.
أولاً: القسمة الكبرى Part One — The Great Divide

الكلمةُ إمَّا مُعْرَبَةٌ وإمَّا مَبْنِيَّةٌ

Every كلمة, without exception, lives on one side of this line. A word is مُعْرَب (muʿrab — declinable) when its ending changes with its position in the sentence: جَاءَ زَيْدٌ · رَأَيْتُ زَيْدًا · مَرَرْتُ بِزَيْدٍ — one word, three endings, because it plays three different roles. A word is مَبْنِيّ (mabnī — indeclinable, "built") when its ending is fixed forever: مِنْ ends in sukūn wherever it sits, in every sentence ever spoken, and nothing can move it.

Now map this divide onto Lesson 1's three word-types. The question for each type is the same: does its ending obey the sentence, or ignore it? The حرف answers in one word. The فعل answers tense by tense. The اسم answers with a default and a short list of rebels. Take them in turn.

ثانياً: الحرف — كلُّه مبني Part Two — The Particle: All of It Is Mabnī

الحُرُوفُ كُلُّهَا مَبْنِيَّةٌ، لا اسْتِثْنَاءَ

The simplest rule in the whole chapter: every particle is mabnī — no exceptions, no footnotes. مِنْ never changes no matter where it sits; neither do هَلْ، لَمْ، إِنَّ، عَلَى. And since particles play no case-role in the sentence, we add: لا مَحَلَّ لَهُ مِنَ الإِعْرَابِ — it holds no iʿrāb position. You have already recited this line for every preposition in the drills.

ثالثاً: الأفعال الثلاثة Part Three — The Three Verbs

1 · الفعل الماضي (the past-tense verb) is always mabnī — but the vowel it is built on shifts with what attaches to its tail:

Built on When مثال
الفتحThe default — nothing rafʿ-bearing attachedذَهَبَ
السكونWhen تاء الفاعل or نَا attachesذَهَبْتُ · ذَهَبْنَا
الضمWhen واو الجماعة attachesذَهَبُوا

You have already parsed this without the theory: in تدريب الإعراب ٢ you recited فعلٌ ماضٍ مبنيٌّ على السكونِ لاتصالِهِ بتاءِ الفاعلِ for ذَهَبْتُ. Today you learn why that line says what it says — this lesson is the theory behind that drill, so we will not re-parse those sentences here.

2 · فعل الأمر (the command verb) is always mabnī — and here is Ibn Hishām's elegant rule: it is built on whatever its muḍāriʿ would show in jazm. Whatever لَمْ would do to the present-tense form, the command is built on:

Built on الأمر قارِن بالمجزوم
Sukūnاُكْتُبْلَمْ يَكْتُبْ
Dropping the weak letterاِرْمِلَمْ يَرْمِ
Dropping the nūnاُكْتُبُوالَمْ يَكْتُبُوا

3 · الفعل المضارع (the present-tense verb) is the family's one muʿrab member — يَكْتُبُ · لَنْ يَكْتُبَ · لَمْ يَكْتُبْ, its ending obeying whatever governs it. It goes mabnī in exactly two situations:

When نُونُ النِّسْوَةِ attaches (nūn al-niswah — the feminine-plural nūn) — mabnī on sukūn: يَكْتُبْنَ ("they — the women — write"). Test it: لَمْ يَكْتُبْنَ · لَنْ يَكْتُبْنَ — the ending refuses to budge.
When نُونُ التَّوْكِيدِ attaches directly (nūn al-tawkīd — the nūn of emphasis) — mabnī on fatḥah: لَيَكْتُبَنَّ ("he will most surely write"). Directly matters: the nūn must touch the verb itself with nothing between them, or the verb stays muʿrab.

رابعاً: الاسم — الأصل الإعراب Part Four — The Noun: Muʿrab by Default

The اسم reverses the verbs' picture: its default is iʿrāb — زَيْدٌ، الكِتَابُ، المَسْجِدُ and the overwhelming majority of nouns all decline. The mabnī nouns are closed lists: small, countable families you can memorise once and own forever. No new members are ever admitted:

القائمة The family أمثلة
الضمائرThe pronouns — separate and attachedأَنَا · هُوَ · إِيَّاكَ · التاء في ذَهَبْتُ
أسماء الإشارةThe demonstratives ("this / these")هَذَا · هَذِهِ · هَؤُلَاءِ
الأسماء الموصولةThe relatives ("who / which")الَّذِي · الَّتِي · الَّذِينَ
أسماء الاستفهامThe interrogatives ("who? where? when?")مَنْ · أَيْنَ · مَتَى · كَمْ
أسماء الشرطThe conditionals ("whoever… / whenever…")مَنْ · مَا · مَتَى · مَهْمَا
بعض الظروفSome adverbs of time and placeحَيْثُ · أَمْسِ · الآنَ
خامساً: حركات البناء الأربع Part Five — The Four Building-Vowels

A mabnī word is "built on" (مبنيٌّ على) whatever fixed ending it carries — and there are only four possibilities:

البناء The fixed ending مثال
مبني على الفتحA frozen fatḥahأَيْنَ
مبني على الضمA frozen ḍammahحَيْثُ
مبني على الكسرA frozen kasrahأَمْسِ
مبني على السكونNo vowel at allكَمْ

Careful with أَمْسِ ("yesterday"): that kasrah looks like jarr, but no preposition put it there and nothing can take it away — it is bināʼ wearing jarr's clothing. The test is never which vowel a word ends in, but whether that vowel can change.

سادساً: مبنيٌّ على — في محلِّ — Part Six — The Formula: Built on X, in the Position of Y

Here is the payoff. A mabnī noun still plays a role in its sentence — it can be a mubtadaʼ, a doer, an object — it simply cannot show the case vowel on its frozen ending. So the iʿrāb line splits into two halves: what the word is built on, then the position it occupies: مبنيٌّ على — في محلِّ —.

هَذَا كِتَابٌ

هَذَا — اسمُ إشارةٍ مبنيٌّ على السكونِ في محلِّ رفعِ مبتدأٍ (a demonstrative, built on sukūn, in the position of rafʿ as the mubtadaʼ). No ḍammah will ever appear on it — the rafʿ lives in its position, not on its ending.
كِتَابٌ — خبرٌ مرفوعٌ وعلامةُ رفعِهِ الضمةُ الظاهرةُ (the khabar, marfūʿ by the visible ḍammah — a plain muʿrab noun doing what muʿrab nouns do).
"This is a book." One sentence, both halves of today's divide sitting side by side.

You have been using this formula since the drills: تدريب الإعراب ٢ and تدريب الإعراب ٣ said في محلِّ رفعِ فاعلٍ for the تاء and في محلِّ نصبِ مفعولٍ به for نَا. Now you know the rule behind the phrase: pronouns are mabnī nouns, so they take positions, not endings.

أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

هَٰذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي

This is from the favour of my Lord. (al-Naml 27:40)

هَٰذَا — mabnī on sukūn, في محلِّ رفعِ مبتدأٍ · مِنْ — a حرف, mabnī, لا محلَّ له · فَضْلِ and رَبِّي — muʿrab, wearing the kasrah of jarr

إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ

You alone we worship. (al-Fātiḥah 1:5)

إِيَّاكَ — ضميرٌ منفصلٌ mabnī, في محلِّ نصبِ مفعولٍ به مقدَّمٍ (the object brought forward for exclusivity) · نَعْبُدُ — a مضارع, muʿrab, marfūʿ by the ḍammah: both halves of the divide in two words you recite in every rakʿah

Exercise A · True or False صحيح أم خاطئ
الحروفُ كلُّها مبنيةٌ بلا استثناءٍ. All particles are mabnī, without a single exception.
الفعلُ المضارعُ مبنيٌّ دائمًا. The present-tense verb (المضارع) is always mabnī.
ذَهَبُوا فعلٌ ماضٍ مبنيٌّ على الضمِّ لاتصالِهِ بواوِ الجماعةِ. ذَهَبُوا is a past verb mabnī on ḍammah because the wāw of the group is attached.
الأصلُ في الأسماءِ البناءُ. The default state of nouns is bināʼ (being indeclinable).
يَكْتُبْنَ فعلٌ مضارعٌ مبنيٌّ على السكونِ لاتصالِهِ بنونِ النسوةِ. يَكْتُبْنَ is a muḍāriʿ verb mabnī on sukūn because nūn al-niswah is attached.
