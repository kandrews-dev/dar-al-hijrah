Unit 3 · Lesson 7

كان وإنّ وأخواتهما

Kāna & Inna — the Abrogators

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand what the abrogators (al-nawāsikh) do.
  • Learn how kāna changes the predicate to manṣūb.
  • Learn how inna changes the subject to manṣūb.
  • See the beautiful symmetry between them.

You know the nominal sentence: mubtadaʾ + khabar, both marfūʿ (Lesson 6). Now meet the words that "enter" on it and change its cases — the abrogators (al-nawāsikh). There are two families.

كان وأخواتها1. Kāna & its Sisters (verbs)

Kāna ("was / used to be") and its sisters — صارَ (became), أصبحَ, ليسَ (is not), ما زالَ (still) — are verbs. When kāna enters: it keeps the subject marfūʿ (now ism kāna) and makes the predicate manṣūb (now khabar kāna).

وَكَانَ اللَّهُ غَفُورًا رَّحِيمًا

"And Allah is ever Forgiving, Merciful." — اللَّهُ ism kāna (marfūʿ, ḍammah) · غَفُورًا khabar kāna (manṣūb, fatḥah).

al-Nisāʾ 4:96

إنّ وأخواتها2. Inna & its Sisters (particles)

Inna ("indeed") and its sisters — أنَّ (that), كأنَّ (as if), لكنَّ (but), لعلَّ (perhaps) — are particles. They do the opposite: they make the subject manṣūb (now ism inna) and keep the predicate marfūʿ (now khabar inna).

إِنَّ اللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ

"Indeed Allah is Forgiving, Merciful." — اللَّهَ ism inna (manṣūb, fatḥah) · غَفُورٌ khabar inna (marfūʿ, ḍammah).

al-Baqarah 2:173

The mirror — التقابل

Memorize — احفظ

كانَ ترفعُ الاسمَ وتنصبُ الخبرَ ، وإنَّ تنصبُ الاسمَ وترفعُ الخبرَ

"Kāna raises the noun and accusatives the predicate; inna accusatives the noun and raises the predicate." That completes the nominatives — you can now read the nominal sentence in all its states.
