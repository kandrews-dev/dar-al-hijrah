الخِلَافَاتُ الزَّوْجِيَّة

Marital Disagreements

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of family conflict and its resolution — from خِلَافٌ to اسْتِقْرَارٌ.
  • Read the real Book 3 passage on why disagreement must be kept away from children, and how it protects their نَفْسِيَّة.
  • Learn six agreed principles spouses can set for handling disagreement well.
  • Know three authentic hadith that guard a calm, stable household.

أَمْنُ الأُسْرَة (the family's security) and its اسْتِقْرَار (stability) are among the most important matters for the happiness of its members — the greatest hope for supplying the ummah with a righteous believer. This lesson reads what Book 3 itself teaches about one thing that threatens that stability if handled carelessly: خِلَافٌ بَيْنَ الأَبَوَيْن, disagreement between the two parents — and, more importantly, how it can be handled well.

Notice how many of these words are doer-verbs on doors you already know: عَاتَبَ is door III (فَاعَلَ — an action between two people, exactly what reproach is), تَحَمَّلَ is door V (تَفَعَّلَ — the reflexive, taking a burden onto yourself), and اعْتَرَفَ is door VIII (اِفْتَعَلَ). The grammar of self-control turns out to be the same grammar you have been building all along.

حماية الأطفال من الخلاف Protecting the Children

Book 3's own passage makes one point before any other: many parents assume their children don't understand or notice disagreement between them — and this is a serious mistake. Even a child too young to speak understands much of what is said, and it leaves the deepest marks in his نَفْسِيَّة (psyche). No married life, however happy, is entirely free of خلاف — no two minds are identical, and no two spouses are either — but this خلاف must be resolved quickly, and if its causes must be discussed, that discussion belongs in private, away from the children. Keeping it away from them protects:

  • The children's psychological wellbeing, and their innocence, kept intact.
  • Both parents' good image in their children's eyes, and the children's lasting love for them.
  • A faster path to resolution — each spouse wanting the other to come around, they naturally lower their voices and keep the matter quiet once no outside ears are involved.
ستة أسس للتعامل مع الخلاف Six Agreed Principles

The source text recommends that spouses set these foundations between them, in a calm moment, before anger ever rises:

  • Each spouse bears with (يَتَحَمَّلُ) the other.
  • Neither meets the other's outburst with the same outburst.
  • Whoever errs acknowledges (يَعْتَرِفُ بِخَطَئِهِ) the mistake, without arrogance.
  • Disagreement never lasts long — no estrangement (هَجْر) beyond a day and a night.
  • Reproach (العِتَاب) over one matter is not repeated except rarely.
  • Neither one brings in a third party — no relative, no friend, and not even a child.

ثلاثة أحاديث — Three Hadith That Guard a Home

«لَا تَغْضَبْ»

"Do not become angry" — the Prophet ﷺ repeated this same answer to a man who kept asking him for advice (al-Bukhārī, from Abū Hurayrah).

«مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَلْيَقُلْ خَيْرًا أَوْ لِيَصْمُتْ»

"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him speak good or remain silent" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah).

«لَا يَفْرَكْ مُؤْمِنٌ مُؤْمِنَةً؛ إِنْ كَرِهَ مِنْهَا خُلُقًا رَضِيَ مِنْهَا آخَرَ»

"A believing man must not detest a believing woman; if he dislikes a trait in her, he will be pleased with another" (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah).

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Parents: read the six agreed principles together, privately, and discuss which is easiest and which is hardest for your own household. Then teach the three hadith to the whole family as a shared standard for everyone's disagreements — siblings included: لا تغضب، قل خيرًا أو اصمت، and look for the good trait, not only the disliked one.
