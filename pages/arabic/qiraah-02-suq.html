فِي السُّوقِ

At the Market

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Read a full Arabic dialogue — voices, not just narration.
  • Follow question and answer as the line passes between speakers.
  • Reuse the market, numbers, and colors vocabulary in live conversation.
  • Meet the fatherly sunnah of the market duʿāʼ (optional note only).

أين نحن — Where we are

Reading 1 was a story about Ahmad — every sentence told you what he does. Reading 2 lets you hear him speak. This is a dialogue in the market between Ahmad, his father, and the seller, and it is built almost entirely from three lessons you already own: Vocab 13 (shopping), Vocab 16 (numbers), and Vocab 17 (colors) — plus their friends from the greetings and clothes lessons. When a written line has a voice, Arabic stops being a page and starts being a conversation.

الحِوَار The Dialogue — read it aloud

يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ يَذْهَبُ أَحْمَدُ مَعَ أَبِيهِ إِلَى السُّوقِ.

الأَب: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ.

البَائِع: وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ وَرَحْمَةُ اللهِ.

أَحْمَد: مَا هَذَا؟

البَائِع: هَذَا مَوْزٌ.

الأَب: بِكَمْ هَذَا؟

البَائِع: بِخَمْسَةِ دَرَاهِمَ.

الأَب: أُرِيدُ كِيلُو مَوْزٍ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ.

البَائِع: نَعَمْ، هَذَا كِيلُو مَوْزٍ.

أَحْمَد: أُرِيدُ قَمِيصًا أَزْرَقَ مِنْ فَضْلِكَ.

البَائِع: عِنْدِي قَمِيصَانِ: قَمِيصٌ أَبْيَضُ وَقَمِيصٌ أَزْرَقُ.

أَحْمَد: أُرِيدُ القَمِيصَ الأَزْرَقَ.

الأَب: وَبِكَمْ هَذَا القَمِيصُ؟

البَائِع: بِعَشَرَةِ دَرَاهِمَ.

الأَب: جَزَاكَ اللهُ خَيْرًا. مَعَ السَّلَامَةِ.

البَائِع: مَعَ السَّلَامَةِ.

Notice how the whole conversation is carried by three little questions you already know — مَا هَذَا؟ from your very first lessons, بِكَمْ هَذَا؟ from the shopping lesson, and the price answers built on خَمْسَة and عَشَرَة from the numbers lesson. And قَمِيصٌ أَبْيَضُ وَقَمِيصٌ أَزْرَقُ is the colors lesson doing exactly what it promised: the color sitting after its noun, like a نعت.

Translation — التَّرْجَمَة · read the Arabic first!

On Saturday, Ahmad goes with his father to the market.

The father: Peace be upon you.

The seller: And upon you be peace, and the mercy of Allah.

Ahmad: What is this?

The seller: This is a banana.

The father: How much is this?

The seller: For five dirhams.

The father: I want a kilo of bananas, please.

The seller: Yes — here is a kilo of bananas.

Ahmad: I want a blue shirt, please.

The seller: I have two shirts: a white shirt and a blue shirt.

Ahmad: I want the blue shirt.

The father: And how much is this shirt?

The seller: For ten dirhams.

The father: May Allah reward you with good. Goodbye.

The seller: Goodbye.

Three glosses — everything else is yours already: السُّوق and مَوْز and بِكَمْ هَذَا؟ and أُرِيدُ from the shopping lesson, خَمْسَة and عَشَرَة and عِنْدِي from the numbers lesson, أَبْيَضُ and أَزْرَقُ from the colors lesson, يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ from the days lesson, and the greetings from your very first week. One shopping trip, stitched together out of lessons you have already finished.

سنة السوق — the market duʿāʼ · optional note

A father entering the market with his son carries a sunnah with him. The Prophet ﷺ taught a duʿāʼ for whoever enters the market — reported in Sunan al-Tirmidhī and graded ḥasan by al-Albānī:

لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، لَهُ المُلْكُ وَلَهُ الحَمْدُ، يُحْيِي وَيُمِيتُ وَهُوَ حَيٌّ لَا يَمُوتُ، بِيَدِهِ الخَيْرُ وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

"None has the right to be worshipped but Allah alone, without partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise; He gives life and gives death, and He is Ever-Living and does not die; in His Hand is all good, and He is able to do all things." This is an optional extra for this reading lesson — no need to memorize it yet; just know that even a shopping trip has a sunnah.

Comprehension · MCQ أسئلة الفهم

On which day do Ahmad and his father go to the market?

How much does the banana cost?

What does Ahmad ask the seller for?

The seller has two shirts. Which color do Ahmad and his father choose?

How does the father say "please"?

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

This dialogue was written to be acted, not just read. Take three readers — one is الأَب, one is أَحْمَد, one is البَائِع — and perform it aloud with every ḥarakah in place. Then swap roles and run it again, and this time swap the item being bought: instead of مَوْز, try خُبْز or حَلِيب; instead of قَمِيص أَزْرَق, ask for قَمِيص أَبْيَض — and change the price too. The frame stays; the words move. That is exactly how conversation works.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

مَا هَذَا؟ · بِكَمْ هَذَا؟ · أُرِيدُ... مِنْ فَضْلِكَ · جَزَاكَ اللهُ خَيْرًا

Memorize the four market phrases — what is this, how much is this, I want… please, and may Allah reward you with good — until they leave your tongue without thinking. With these four you can walk into any Arabic market and hold your own.

Further reading — optional aids

These are optional supports only. The lesson is complete on-page. If any line felt shaky, revisit its home lesson on the Lugha track — shopping (Vocab 13) for السوق and the prices, numbers (Vocab 16) for خَمْسَة and عَشَرَة, colors (Vocab 17) for أَبْيَضُ and أَزْرَقُ, and clothes (Vocab 18) for القَمِيص. Then come back and act the dialogue once more without glancing at the translation — the moment the voices carry their own meaning, the qirāʼah track has done its work.
