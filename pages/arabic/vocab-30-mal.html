المال والتجارة

Money & Trade

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the money and trade vocabulary — from مَالٌ and تِجَارَةٌ to رِبْحٌ and خَسَارَةٌ.
  • Handle buying, selling, profit and loss in Arabic with بَاعَ and اِشْتَرَى.
  • Know the āyah that permits البيع and forbids الرِّبَا — al-Baqarah 2:275, letter by letter.
  • Meet the rank of the honest merchant in the hadith of al-Tirmidhī.

Back in Vocab 13 you learned to walk the سُوق, ask بِكَمْ هَذَا؟ and talk about the سِعْر. This lesson hands you the trade itself: the مَال that changes hands, the تَاجِر who stands behind the scale, the رِبْح he hopes for and the خَسَارَة he fears, and the two verbs the whole market runs on — بَاعَ and اِشْتَرَى. And above every stall in every market sits one āyah of five words that splits all earnings into حَلَال and حَرَام.

Ten words at the Book 3 pace — and three of them are ṣarf lessons in disguise. بَاعَ is hollow (أَجْوَف) like قَالَ: its middle letter is a hidden yāʼ (root ب ي ع) that resurfaces in the present tense — يَبِيعُ. اِشْتَرَى is two lessons in one word: the door اِفْتَعَلَ from the ṣarf track and a weak last letter. And غَالٍ is a manqūṣ noun like عَالٍ: say هَذَا غَالٍ and the yāʼ hides; add ال — الثَّوْبُ الْغَالِي — and it comes out of hiding.

آية التجارة The Āyah of Trade — al-Baqarah 2:275

وَأَحَلَّ اللَّهُ الْبَيْعَ وَحَرَّمَ الرِّبَا

"And Allah has permitted trade and forbidden ribā."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:275 · سورة البقرة

Five words that rule every market on earth. And two ṣarf gems sit inside them: أَحَلَّ is door IV — أَفْعَلَ, "he made lawful" — and حَرَّمَ is door II — فَعَّلَ, "he made forbidden." The two causative doors you built on the ṣarf track carry the two rulings of the āyah: one door opens the market, the other locks the door of الرِّبَا. And الْبَيْع is the maṣdar of بَاعَ from your table — the āyah blesses the very verb you just learned.

سنة — The honest merchant's rank

«التَّاجِرُ الصَّدُوقُ الأَمِينُ مَعَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ»

"The truthful, trustworthy merchant is with the prophets, the ṣiddīqīn and the martyrs" (al-Tirmidhī; graded ḥasan).

Look closely at الصَّدُوق — not صَادِق (truthful once) but the intensive فَعُول pattern: truthful again and again, in every sale, until truthfulness becomes his trade-mark. And الأَمِين — before a single āyah came down, Makkah already called the Prophet ﷺ by this very name, الأَمِين, the trustworthy one; the Seerah tab tells that story. The merchant who carries these two names through the market walks, by this hadith, toward the highest company of the Day of Judgement.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

عَمُّ أَحْمَدَ تَاجِرٌ فِي السُّوقِ. يَبِيعُ الْبِضَاعَةَ بِأَسْعَارٍ عَادِلَةٍ. لَا يَكْذِبُ وَلَا يَكْتُمُ الْعُيُوبَ، وَيُوفِي الْكَيْلَ. النَّاسُ يُحِبُّونَ الشِّرَاءَ مِنْهُ. وَقَدْ وَعَدَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ التَّاجِرَ الصَّدُوقَ الأَمِينَ بِصُحْبَةِ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: Ahmad's uncle is a merchant in the market. He sells the goods at fair prices. He does not lie, he does not hide defects, and he gives full measure. People love buying from him. And the Prophet ﷺ promised the truthful, trustworthy merchant the company of the prophets, the ṣiddīqīn and the martyrs. Notice the Vocab 13 words coming back to work — السُّوق, الْبِضَاعَة, أَسْعَار — and notice الشِّرَاء in the fourth sentence: the maṣdar of اِشْتَرَى, just as الْبَيْع in the āyah is the maṣdar of بَاعَ. The passage ends where the sunnah box began: honesty at the scale is not shop policy — it is a promise of the best company there is.

البيع والشراء Buying & Selling — phrases
  • اِشْتَرَيْتُ كِتَابًا مِنَ الْمَتْجَرِ. — I bought a book from the store.
  • بَاعَ التَّاجِرُ كُلَّ الْبِضَاعَةِ. — The merchant sold all the goods.
  • رَبِحَ التَّاجِرُ فِي تِجَارَتِهِ. — The merchant profited in his trade.
  • هَذَا رَخِيصٌ وَذَاكَ غَالٍ. — This is cheap and that is expensive.

Four phrases cover the whole exchange: buying, selling, profit, and judging the price. The last one shows the manqūṣ غَالٍ in rafʿ — the yāʼ in hiding, exactly as the table promised.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

At the next shop visit, turn the aisles into a quiet drill: point at things and judge them in Arabic — هَذَا رَخِيصٌ، وَذَاكَ غَالٍ — until both words come without thinking. Then, standing at the till, recall together the hadith of the honest merchant: «التَّاجِرُ الصَّدُوقُ الأَمِينُ مَعَ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَالشُّهَدَاءِ» — and let it set the family's standard for every buying and selling to come.
