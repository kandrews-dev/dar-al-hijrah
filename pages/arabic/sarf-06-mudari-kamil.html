تصريف المضارع الكامل

The Full Present Conjugation — All Fourteen Forms

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Complete the muḍāriʿ of كَتَبَ to all fourteen forms — the present-tense twin of Lesson 5's table.
  • Keep the same three groups: الغائب (the absent) · المخاطب (the addressed) · المتكلم (the speaker).
  • Recognise the endings ـَانِ (dual) · ـُونَ (masc. plural) · ـِينَ (you f.) · ـْنَ (fem. plural).
  • Spot the look-alike pairs and tell them apart by prefix and context.
Where we are in the ṣarf track: Lesson 3 gave you the four core prefixes أ · ن · ي · ت and four core forms. Lesson 5 completed the past to all fourteen. Today we complete the present the same way — and with that, you hold both full tables. Recited back to back, they are the daily wird of every ṣarf student in every madrasah: fourteen of the past, fourteen of the present, morning after morning until they run like water.
تصريف «يَكْتُبُ» الكامل Part One — The Full Fourteen

تصريفُ المضارعِ الكاملُ أربعَ عشْرةَ صيغةً أيضًا: ستٌّ للغائب، وستٌّ للمخاطَب، واثنتان للمتكلِّم

The complete muḍāriʿ conjugation is also fourteen forms — six, six, two, in the same three groups. Remember the logic from Lesson 3: the prefix announces the doer before you reach the root — أ for I, ن for we, ي for he/they, ت for she/you. What is new today are the endings that finish the job: the prefix opens the word, the ending closes it, and between them every doer has a seat.

Here is the whole table. The four core forms of Lesson 3 are all inside it — what is new are the duals (ending ـَانِ), the masculine plurals (ending ـُونَ), the "you (f.)" form تَكْتُبِينَ (ending ـِينَ), and the feminine plurals (ending ـْنَ). Read each row aloud: pronoun, then verb.

الضمير الفعل Meaning Ending

المخاطب — the addressed · six forms
أَنْتَتَكْتُبُyou (m.) write — same word as "she writes"—
أَنْتُمَاتَكْتُبَانِyou two (m.) writeـَانِ
أَنْتُمْتَكْتُبُونَyou (m. pl.) writeـُونَ
أَنْتِتَكْتُبِينَyou (f.) writeـِينَ

أَنْتُنَّتَكْتُبْنَyou (f. pl.) writeـْنَ
المتكلم — the speaker · two forms

نَحْنُنَكْتُبُwe write—

Count them: six, six, two — fourteen. Watch the ḥarakāt closely: the dual nūn takes a kasrah (يَكْتُبَانِ), while the nūn of the plural ـُونَ and of the feminine-singular ـِينَ takes a fatḥah (يَكْتُبُونَ · تَكْتُبِينَ), and the feminine plurals put a sukūn on the ب before the نَ — هُنَّ يَكْتُبْنَ with ي، أَنْتُنَّ تَكْتُبْنَ with ت. And notice: تَكْتُبَانِ sits in three seats and تَكْتُبُ in two, so the fourteen forms are carried by only eleven distinct words. Swap the root and every prefix and ending stays put: يَدْرُسُ، يَدْرُسَانِ، يَدْرُسُونَ… same mould.

فرِّق بين المتشابهات Part Two — Look-Alikes to Tell Apart

1 · One word, two seats. تَكْتُبُ is both she writes (هي) and you write (أنتَ) — identical letter for letter. The sentence around it decides, exactly as كَتَبْتُمَا served two seats in Lesson 5. Lesson 3 already warned you: the ت prefix is the busiest of the four.

2 · Only the prefix differs. يَكْتُبْنَ is they (f. pl.) write — هُنَّ, with the ي of الغائب. تَكْتُبْنَ is you (f. pl.) write — أَنْتُنَّ, with the ت of المخاطب. Same body, same sukūn on the ب, same نَ of the women; the first letter alone tells you whether the women are spoken about or spoken to.

3 · The ـِينَ ending belongs to one seat only. تَكْتُبِينَ is you (feminine singular) write — أنتِ. Do not let its length fool you into reading a plural: the yāʼ after a kasrah is the mark of the single woman addressed, not of a group.

الأفعال الخمسة Part Three — A Seed for the Naḥw Track
Look back at the table and count the forms that end in a nūn after a long vowel: يَكْتُبَانِ · تَكْتُبَانِ · يَكْتُبُونَ · تَكْتُبُونَ · تَكْتُبِينَ — five. The scholars call them الأفعال الخمسة (the five verbs), and that nūn is not fixed forever: the naḥw track will later show you how it is dropped when a governor puts the verb in naṣb or jazm. For now, simply know the five by name — the seed is planted.
أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

فَوَيْلٌ لِّلَّذِينَ يَكْتُبُونَ الْكِتَابَ بِأَيْدِيهِمْ

So woe to those who write the Book with their own hands. (al-Baqarah 2:79)

يَكْتُبُونَ — the هُمْ form of the very verb of our table: ي of الغائب at the front, ـُونَ of the masculine plural at the end. The ayah continues by condemning those who then say "this is from Allah" to buy a small price with it — the People of the Book who forged scripture. The same fourteen-seat mould you drilled today carries, in the Qur'an, the weight of warning.

قُلْ هَلْ يَسْتَوِي الَّذِينَ يَعْلَمُونَ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

Say: are those who know equal to those who know not? (al-Zumar 39:9)

يَعْلَمُونَ — twice in one ayah, the ـُونَ form of the root ع ل م. Here the same ending carries the honour of knowledge: the people of ʿilm and the people without it are declared unequal. One of the five verbs you just met, reciting itself in your ears every time this ayah is read.
