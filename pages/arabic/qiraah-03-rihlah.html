Qirāʼah Track · Reading 3 · Year 2Year 2

رِحْلَةٌ إِلَى مَكَّةَ

A Journey to Makkah

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Read a longer travel narrative — the track's first full story told start to finish.
  • Reuse the travel, months, and nature vocabulary inside connected Arabic.
  • Meet Makkah, the Kaʿbah, and the ḥajj months on the page, not just in a list.
  • Follow past-tense narration — the Year-1 conjugations finally at work in a real story.

أين نحن — Where we are

Readings 1 and 2 lived in the present tense — what Ahmad does every day, what he says in the market. Reading 3 turns around and looks back: it tells a journey that is already complete. That is the job of the past tense you drilled in Ṣarf 2 and conjugated in full in Ṣarf 5 — فَعَلَ for he did, فَعَلُوا for they did. Until now those endings sat in a table; in this reading they carry a real story, sentence after sentence, all the way from the airport to the Kaʿbah.

النَّصّ The Passage — read it aloud

فِي شَهْرِ ذِي الحِجَّةِ سَافَرَ أَحْمَدُ مَعَ أُسْرَتِهِ إِلَى مَكَّةَ.

ذَهَبُوا إِلَى المَطَارِ فِي الصَّبَاحِ وَرَكِبُوا الطَّائِرَةَ.

نَظَرَ أَحْمَدُ مِنَ النَّافِذَةِ فَرَأَى الجِبَالَ وَالبَحْرَ.

وَصَلُوا إِلَى مَكَّةَ فِي المَسَاءِ.

ذَهَبُوا إِلَى المَسْجِدِ الحَرَامِ.

رَأَى أَحْمَدُ الكَعْبَةَ فَفَرِحَ فَرَحًا شَدِيدًا.

طَافُوا حَوْلَ الكَعْبَةِ وَشَرِبُوا مِنْ مَاءِ زَمْزَمَ.

قَالَ أَحْمَدُ: الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي بَلَّغَنَا هَذَا اليَوْمَ.

ثُمَّ صَلَّوْا جَمِيعًا فِي المَسْجِدِ الحَرَامِ.

وَدَعَوْا رَبَّهُمْ وَشَكَرُوهُ عَلَى هَذِهِ الرِّحْلَةِ المُبَارَكَةِ.

Two small treasures inside the passage. First, الطَّائِرَة (airplane) — its root طَارَ (he flew) is the very root behind طَائِر, the bird from Vocab 22: the airplane is simply "the flying one." Second, فَرِحَ فَرَحًا شَدِيدًا — the action named twice: the verb فَرِحَ followed by its own maṣdar فَرَحًا as a مفعول مطلق, which is Arabic's way of saying he rejoiced intensely. A light note only — you will meet it properly on the naḥw track.

Translation — التَّرْجَمَة · read the Arabic first!

In the month of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, Ahmad travelled with his family to Makkah.

They went to the airport in the morning and boarded the airplane.

Ahmad looked out of the window and saw the mountains and the sea.

They arrived in Makkah in the evening.

They went to al-Masjid al-Ḥarām.

Ahmad saw the Kaʿbah and rejoiced with intense joy.

They circled around the Kaʿbah and drank from the water of Zamzam.

Ahmad said: "All praise is for Allah, who has caused us to reach this day."

Then they all prayed in al-Masjid al-Ḥarām.

And they called upon their Lord and thanked Him for this blessed journey.

كلمات جديدة New Words — glossed on the spot
الكلمة English Note
الطَّائِرَةairplanefrom the root of طَارَ (he flew) — the same root as طَائِر, the bird from Vocab 22; the airplane is literally "the flying one"
وَصَلَhe arrivedthe مثال verb from Ṣarf 12 — its first letter is و, and in the past tense the و holds firm: وَصَلَ، وَصَلُوا. It only slips away in the present (يَصِلُ)
طَافَhe circled — made ṭawāfa hollow (أجوف) verb from Ṣarf 12, built exactly like قَالَ — the middle و has melted into an alif; from it comes the word الطَّوَاف, circling the Kaʿbah
زَمْزَمZamzamthe well beside the Kaʿbah that Allah brought forth for Hājar and her son Ismāʿīl — its water is still drunk by every pilgrim today

Four glosses — the rest of the passage is already yours: رِحْلَة and المَطَار and رَكِبَ from the travel lesson (Vocab 10), شَهْر and ذُو الحِجَّة from the months lesson (Vocab 21), الجِبَال and البَحْر from the nature lesson (Vocab 23), الصَّبَاح and المَسَاء from the time lessons, and every verb ending straight out of the ṣarf track's past-tense table. One journey, stitched together out of lessons you have already finished.

أدب الرحلة — the month and the ḥamd · optional note

The story opens in ذُو الحِجَّة for a reason: it is month 12 from Vocab 21 — a sacred month, and the month of ḥajj. The ḥajj is performed in the months Allah appointed for it (شَوَّال, ذُو القَعْدَة, and the first days of ذُو الحِجَّة) — so a family flying to Makkah in this month is stepping into the very season the calendar lesson taught you to name.

And look closely at one word of Ahmad's ḥamd: بَلَّغَنَا. The root is بَلَغَ (to reach). Doubling the middle letter puts it through door II — بَلَّغَ, he caused to reach — and the attached ـنَا is the object pronoun us. "He caused us to reach this day." The vocab track gave the root, the ṣarf track gave the door, and the naḥw track attached the pronoun — three tracks meeting in a single word of praise.

Comprehension · MCQ أسئلة الفهم

In which month did Ahmad and his family travel to Makkah?

How did the family travel to Makkah?

What did Ahmad see from the window?

What did the family do around the Kaʿbah?

What did they drink?

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Close the book and retell the journey in the past tense, one sentence per person, going around the family: the first says سَافَرَ أَحْمَدُ إِلَى مَكَّةَ, the next says what happened next — رَكِبُوا الطَّائِرَةَ — and so on until the family reaches the duʿāʼ at the end. Every verb must stay in the past. Then, if anyone at the table has made ʿumrah or ḥajj, let them share one memory in simple Arabic — even a single sentence like رَأَيْتُ الكَعْبَةَ وَفَرِحْتُ — so the passage on the page meets a journey that really happened.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي بَلَّغَنَا هَذَا اليَوْمَ

Memorize Ahmad's closing ḥamd — "All praise is for Allah, who has caused us to reach this day" — until it rises by itself whenever Allah brings you to something you hoped for. One sentence, and inside it a whole habit of gratitude.
