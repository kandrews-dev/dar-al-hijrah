نَوَادِرُ وَظُرَف

Anecdotes & Witty Tales

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of classical Arabic humour — from نَادِرَةٌ to ذَكَاءٌ.
  • Meet three real anecdotes from the Arabic adab tradition, told about real characters remembered across the centuries.
  • Recognise a fresh manqūṣ noun — غَاوٍ — and a broken plural — حِمَار ← حَمِير.
  • Connect a wife's clever remark to a real hadith about patience and gratitude in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

Arabic literature keeps a whole shelf of نَوَادِر — short, memorable anecdotes passed down for their wit as much as their wisdom. Three characters appear again and again in this shelf: أَشْعَب, famous as a proverb for greed ("أَطْمَعُ مِنْ أَشْعَب" — greedier than Ashʿab); عُثْمَان بْن رَوَاح, a proverb for laziness; and جُحَا, whose stories hide real cleverness (ذَكَاء) under a mask of foolishness. This lesson tells you three of the shelf's best-loved tales.

غَاوٍ joins the manqūṣ family your ear should now recognise on sight: its yāʼ hides in the bare form and only returns once ال is attached — الغَاوِي. And حَمِير is another جَمْعُ تَكْسِير like عُقُول from Vocab 30 — the singular's shape breaks and rebuilds, this time on the pattern فَعِيل, and there is no shortcut to knowing it beyond meeting the word.

هل تعرف هذه الشخصيات؟ Do You Know These Characters? — from the source text's own introduction

أَشْعَب — a man who lived in al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah, remembered so strongly for greed (الطَّمَع) that Arabic still says of an extremely greedy person: «أَطْمَعُ مِنْ أَشْعَب» — "greedier than Ashʿab."

عُثْمَان بْن رَوَاح — a man made into a proverb for laziness (الكَسَل).

جُحَا — a man to whom humorous stories are attributed across the Arabic tradition; his anecdotes point to real ذَكَاء hidden under a mask of foolishness (البَلَاهَة).

النَّصّ Three Anecdotes — retold in plain Arabic

١ — امرأة عِمْرَان

كَانَ عِمْرَانُ قَبِيحَ الْوَجْهِ، وَكَانَتِ امْرَأَتُهُ جَمِيلَةً جِدًّا. نَظَرَ إِلَيْهَا يَوْمًا طَوِيلًا، فَقَالَتْ: لِمَاذَا تُدِيمُ النَّظَرَ هَكَذَا؟ قَالَ: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ، لَقَدْ أَصْبَحْتُ جَمِيلًا! فَقَالَتْ: إِذَنْ نَحْنُ الِاثْنَانِ فِي الْجَنَّةِ! قَالَ: وَمِنْ أَيْنَ عَلِمْتِ ذَلِكَ؟ قَالَتْ: لِأَنَّنِي أُعْطِيتُ جَمَالًا فَشَكَرْتُ، وَأَنْتَ أُعْطِيتَ مِثْلِي فَصَبَرْتَ، وَالصَّابِرُ وَالشَّاكِرُ كِلَاهُمَا فِي الْجَنَّةِ.

ʿImrān had a plain face, and his wife was very beautiful. One day he looked at her for a long time, so she said: "Why do you keep staring like that?" He said: "Praise be to Allah, I have become beautiful!" She said: "Then the two of us are in Paradise!" He said: "And how do you know that?" She said: "Because I was given beauty and I was thankful, and you were given someone like me and you were patient — and the patient one and the thankful one are both in Paradise."

حديث — the anecdote's real foundation

«عَجَبًا لِأَمْرِ الْمُؤْمِنِ، إِنَّ أَمْرَهُ كُلَّهُ خَيْرٌ، وَلَيْسَ ذَاكَ لِأَحَدٍ إِلَّا لِلْمُؤْمِنِ: إِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ سَرَّاءُ شَكَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ، وَإِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ ضَرَّاءُ صَبَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ»

"How wonderful is the affair of the believer — all of his affair is good, and this belongs to no one except the believer: if ease befalls him, he is thankful, and that is good for him; and if hardship befalls him, he is patient, and that is good for him" (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, from Ṣuhayb ibn Sinān). ʿImrān's wife was not just being clever — her line is this hadith in miniature: شَكَرَ and صَبَرَ, the same two verbs from your table, are exactly the two roads the hadith names to Paradise.

٢ — الطُّفَيْلِيّ وَالشُّعَرَاء

رَأَى طُفَيْلِيٌّ قَوْمًا ذَاهِبِينَ، فَظَنَّ أَنَّهُمْ إِلَى وَلِيمَةٍ، فَتَبِعَهُمْ. وَإِذَا هُمْ شُعَرَاءُ جَاءُوا بِمَدَائِحَ لِلْأَمِيرِ. أَنْشَدَ كُلُّ شَاعِرٍ قَصِيدَتَهُ وَأَخَذَ جَائِزَتَهُ، حَتَّى لَمْ يَبْقَ إِلَّا الطُّفَيْلِيُّ جَالِسًا لَا يَتَكَلَّمُ. فَقِيلَ لَهُ: أَنْشِدْ شِعْرَكَ. فَقَالَ: لَسْتُ شَاعِرًا. قِيلَ: فَمَنْ أَنْتَ؟ فَقَالَ: أَنَا مِنَ الْغَاوِينَ الَّذِينَ قَالَ اللهُ فِيهِمْ: ﴿وَالشُّعَرَاءُ يَتَّبِعُهُمُ الْغَاوُونَ﴾. فَضَحِكَ الْأَمِيرُ مِنْ جَوَابِهِ، وَأَمَرَ لَهُ بِجَائِزَةٍ.

An uninvited guest saw a group of people going somewhere, thought they were headed to a banquet, and followed them. It turned out they were poets who had come with praise-poems for the amīr. Each poet recited his poem and took his prize, until only the uninvited guest remained, sitting, saying nothing. He was told: "Recite your poetry." He said: "I am not a poet." He was asked: "Then who are you?" He said: "I am one of the misguided ones Allah spoke of: 'And the poets — [only] those who stray follow them' (al-Shuʿarāʼ 26:224)." The amīr laughed at his answer and ordered a prize for him too.

٣ — جُحَا وَالْعَشَرَة الْحَمِير

اشْتَرَى جُحَا عَشَرَةَ حَمِيرٍ، وَرَكِبَ وَاحِدًا مِنْهَا. بَدَأَ يَعُدُّ الْحَمِيرَ فَوَجَدَهَا تِسْعَةً فَقَطْ، فَنَزَلَ خَائِفًا. فَلَمَّا عَدَّهَا وَهُوَ وَاقِفٌ، وَجَدَهَا عَشَرَةً! فَقَالَ: الْمَشْيُ خَيْرٌ مِنْ أَنْ أَرْكَبَ وَأَخْسَرَ حِمَارًا.

Juḥā bought ten donkeys and rode one of them. He began counting the donkeys and found only nine, so he got down, worried. But when he counted them standing on the ground, he found ten! He said: "Walking is better than riding and losing a donkey." Juḥā, of course, had simply forgotten to count the one he was sitting on — the joke's whole point, and the reason his stories are proverbs for hidden ذَكَاء: the fool's mistake is often exactly this obvious once someone else points it out.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Retell Juḥā's donkey story around the table, in Arabic if you can, English if you can't yet — then ask each person: what is one thing you were thankful for today (شَكَرْتُ), and one thing you were patient with (صَبَرْتُ)? Close by reciting the hadith of Ṣuhayb together: «عَجَبًا لِأَمْرِ الْمُؤْمِنِ، إِنَّ أَمْرَهُ كُلَّهُ خَيْرٌ».

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

إِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ سَرَّاءُ شَكَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ، وَإِنْ أَصَابَتْهُ ضَرَّاءُ صَبَرَ فَكَانَ خَيْرًا لَهُ

Memorize this line until it is part of you — "If ease befalls him, he is thankful, and that is good for him; and if hardship befalls him, he is patient, and that is good for him" (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, from Ṣuhayb ibn Sinān).
