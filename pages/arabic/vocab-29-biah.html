البيئة والنظافة

Environment & Cleanliness

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the environment and cleanliness words — from بِيئَةٌ and نَظَافَةٌ to قُمَامَةٌ and حَدِيقَةٌ.
  • Tell شَجَرَةٌ (one tree) from شَجَرٌ (trees, the collective) — one ة is doing all the work.
  • Learn the planting hadith: every creature fed from your tree is written for you as ṣadaqah.
  • Connect نَظَافَة to the dīn's care for purity — a religion of wuḍūʼ has no room for mess.

In Vocab 23 you walked through nature — the شَجَرَة, the بَحْر, the جَبَل. This lesson asks a bigger question: who looks after all of it? The Muslim does. The بِيئَة is not scenery; it is an أمانة (trust) in your hands, and نَظَافَة is not a chore; it belongs to a dīn that begins every prayer with washing and calls purification half of īmān — «الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الْإِيمَانِ» (Muslim). Ten words, one connected passage, and a hadith that turns a garden spade into an instrument of charity.

Stop at شَجَرٌ before anything else. In Vocab 23 you learned شَجَرَةٌ — one tree. Take the ة away and you get شَجَر — trees, the whole stand of them at once. Arabic calls this the collective: the bare word means the mass, and one ة carves a single unit out of it. You have already met the trick without noticing — طَيْر (birds) beside طَائِر in Vocab 22. And give سَقَى a second look too: its last root letter is weak, which makes it a ناقص verb from Ṣarf 12 — it behaves exactly like دَعَا, hiding and showing its ending as the sentence demands.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

تَقْضِي الْأُسْرَةُ يَوْمَ السَّبْتِ فِي الْحَدِيقَةِ. يَجْمَعُونَ الْقُمَامَةَ وَيَسْقُونَ الشَّجَرَ. يَزْرَعُ أَحْمَدُ شَجَرَةً صَغِيرَةً. يَتَذَكَّرُ الْأَبُ حَدِيثَ الْغَرْسِ وَيَبْتَسِمُ. الْهَوَاءُ نَظِيفٌ، فَيَشْكُرُونَ اللهَ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: The family spends Saturday at the park. They collect the rubbish and water the trees. Ahmad plants a small tree. The father remembers the planting hadith and smiles. The air is clean, so they thank Allah. Watch two things go past. First, يَسْقُونَ — the ناقص verb سَقَى tucks its weak letter away in the plural, exactly as Ṣarf 12 promised. Second, أَحْمَد plants شَجَرَةً صَغِيرَةً — one small tree, wearing its ة — while the family waters الشَّجَرَ, the collective, wearing none. The grammar of the table is alive in the story.

سنة — The planting hadith

«مَا مِنْ مُسْلِمٍ يَغْرِسُ غَرْسًا أَوْ يَزْرَعُ زَرْعًا فَيَأْكُلُ مِنْهُ طَيْرٌ أَوْ إِنْسَانٌ أَوْ بَهِيمَةٌ إِلَّا كَانَ لَهُ بِهِ صَدَقَةٌ»

The Prophet ﷺ said: "No Muslim plants a plant or sows a crop, then a bird or a person or a beast eats from it, except that it counts as charity for him" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).

Your vocabulary is all over this matn. طَيْرٌ is the collective of طَائِر from Vocab 22 — the same peel-the-unit trick as شَجَر and شَجَرَة. And look at the doubled pairs يَغْرِسُ غَرْسًا and يَزْرَعُ زَرْعًا — each verb followed by its own maṣdar, the المفعول المطلق pattern you brushed past in Reading 3: Arabic stamping the action with its own name. The tree keeps paying you ṣadaqah long after you have washed your hands. And the dīn does not stop at planting: the Prophet ﷺ taught that īmān has many branches, and among them is إِمَاطَةُ الْأَذَى عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ — removing harm from the path (Muslim). Picking up a piece of قُمَامَة is not a chore. It is a branch of īmān.

في الحديقة In the Garden — phrases
  • هَذِهِ الْحَدِيقَةُ نَظِيفَةٌ. — This park is clean.
  • لَا تَرْمِ الْقُمَامَةَ فِي الطَّرِيقِ. — Do not throw rubbish on the path.
  • مَنْ سَقَى الشَّجَرَ الْيَوْمَ؟ — Who watered the trees today?
  • الْهَوَاءُ هُنَا نَظِيفٌ، الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. — The air here is clean, al-ḥamdu lillāh.

The second phrase hides a ناقص verb in its jussive: رَمَى loses its weak letter after لَا — hence لَا تَرْمِ, not لَا تَرْمِي. Ṣarf 12 keeps appearing whether you invite it or not.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Choose one family cleanliness act this week and do it together: tidy a bedroom until it is truly نَظِيف, sweep the front of your street, or straighten the shoe area at the masjid. Do it with the hadith in mind — say the matn quietly as you work, and let the children name what they are handling in Arabic: هَذِهِ قُمَامَةٌ، الْآنَ الْمَكَانُ نَظِيفٌ. A religion whose Prophet ﷺ counted removing harm from the path as a branch of īmān has made your bin bag an act of worship.
