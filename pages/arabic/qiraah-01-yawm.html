يَوْمٌ فِي حَيَاةِ مُسْلِمٍ

A Day in the Life of a Muslim

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Read a connected Arabic passage built from Year-1 vocabulary.
  • Follow a story across sentences — not word lists, but words working in order.
  • Answer comprehension questions from the text itself.
  • Feel the Year-1 words working together — كَلِمَات becoming كَلَام.

أين نحن — Where we are

Year 1 gave you twenty-four vocab lessons of words — greetings, family, food, prayer, days, feelings. Year 2's reading track is where those words stop living in tables and start living in stories. This first passage is built only from words you met in Vocab 1–24, plus a small handful glossed for you on the spot below. If a word looks familiar, that is because it is — you have already earned every sentence on this page.

النَّصّ The Passage — read it aloud

يَسْتَيْقِظُ أَحْمَدُ فِي الصَّبَاحِ. يُصَلِّي الفَجْرَ فِي المَسْجِدِ. ثُمَّ يَأْكُلُ الخُبْزَ وَيَشْرَبُ الحَلِيبَ. يَذْهَبُ إِلَى المَدْرَسَةِ. يَدْرُسُ القُرْآنَ وَالعَرَبِيَّةَ. فِي المَسَاءِ يَلْعَبُ كُرَةَ القَدَمِ مَعَ أَخِيهِ. يُصَلِّي العِشَاءَ ثُمَّ يَنَامُ. يَوْمَ الجُمُعَةِ يَذْهَبُ مَعَ أَبِيهِ إِلَى المَسْجِدِ. أَحْمَدُ سَعِيدٌ، وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

Every verb here is a he-form: the يـ prefix from the ṣarf track's muḍāriʿ table. They are the very verbs you learnt as "I" forms in the daily-life lesson — أَسْتَيْقِظُ، أُصَلِّي، آكُلُ، أَذْهَبُ، أَدْرُسُ، أَنَامُ — now wearing يـ instead of أ, telling someone else's story.

Translation — التَّرْجَمَة · read the Arabic first!

Ahmad wakes up in the morning. He prays Fajr in the mosque. Then he eats bread and drinks milk. He goes to school. He studies the Qur'an and Arabic. In the evening he plays football with his brother. He prays ʿIshāʼ, then he sleeps. On Friday he goes with his father to the mosque. Ahmad is happy — and all praise is for Allah.

كلمات جديدة New Words — glossed on the spot
الكلمة English Note
يَشْرَبُhe drinksthe he-form of the drinking verb — same يـ pattern as every other verb in the passage
مَعَwithyou have already heard it inside إِنَّ اللهَ مَعَنَا — Allah is with us
أَخِيهِ · أَبِيهِhis brother · his fatherأَخ and أَب from the family lesson, wearing the attached pronoun ـهِ (his) — Ṣarf 9 teaches the attached pronouns properly; for now just recognise the family word inside

Three glosses — everything else in the passage is yours already: الصَّبَاح and المَسَاء from the daily-life lesson, الفَجْر and العِشَاء and المَسْجِد from the prayer lesson, الخُبْز and الحَلِيب from the food lesson, يَوْمَ الجُمُعَةِ from the days lesson, and سَعِيدٌ from the feelings lesson. One boy's day, stitched together out of your whole first year.

Comprehension · MCQ أسئلة الفهم

Where does Ahmad pray Fajr?

What does Ahmad eat and drink in the morning?

What does Ahmad study at school?

What does Ahmad do in the evening?

Where does Ahmad go on Friday, and with whom?

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Read the passage aloud twice: once slowly, giving every ḥarakah its full sound, and once smoothly, as if telling the story to a friend. Then close the page and go around the family — each person retells one sentence from memory, in order, until the whole of Ahmad's day is rebuilt: يَسْتَيْقِظُ… يُصَلِّي… ثُمَّ يَأْكُلُ… The first family to reach يَوْمَ الجُمُعَةِ without opening the book wins.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

يَسْتَيْقِظُ أَحْمَدُ فِي الصَّبَاحِ. يُصَلِّي الفَجْرَ فِي المَسْجِدِ.

Memorize the passage's first two sentences — Ahmad wakes up in the morning; he prays Fajr in the mosque — until they run off the tongue with every ḥarakah in place. Your first two sentences of connected Arabic prose, owned for life.

Further reading — optional aids

These are optional supports only. The lesson is complete on-page. If any word in the passage felt shaky, revisit its home lesson on the Lugha track — daily life for the verbs, prayer for the masjid words, food for the breakfast, days for الجُمُعَة, feelings for سَعِيدٌ. Then come back and reread the passage until you no longer glance at the translation at all — that moment, when the Arabic carries its own meaning, is the whole point of the qirāʼah track.
