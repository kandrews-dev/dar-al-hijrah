أعضاء الجسم

Parts of the Body

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the main words for the parts of the body, from رَأْسٌ (head) to قَلْبٌ (heart).
  • Use the dual forms عَيْنَانِ · يَدَانِ you built in the ṣarf track.
  • Connect the body words to الوضوء (wudū) — the limbs you wash five times a day.
  • Know the hadith of the heart — the مُضْغَة upon which the whole body depends.

Allah shaped your body and gave each limb a name — and you already meet these names every single day. Every time you make الوضوء (wudū) you wash them; every time you look, listen, or walk you use them. Eight small words in this lesson, and behind them sits one word greater than all the rest: القَلْب (the heart), the king of the limbs.

أعضاء الجسم The Eight Words
الكلمة English Note
رَأْسٌheadwiped — not washed — in الوضوء
وَجْهٌfacethe first limb washed in الوضوء
عَيْنٌeyedual: عَيْنَانِ — two eyes
أُذُنٌeardual: أُذُنَانِ — wiped with the head
فَمٌmouthrinsed in المضمضة (rinsing the mouth)
يَدٌhanddual: يَدَانِ — two hands
رِجْلٌfoot / legdual: رِجْلَانِ — two feet
قَلْبٌheartthe king of the limbs — see the hadith below

Notice how many of these limbs come in pairs — and Arabic has a special form just for “two of something”: the dual you met in Ṣarf 5–6. Add ـَانِ and عَيْنٌ (eye) becomes عَيْنَانِ (two eyes), يَدٌ (hand) becomes يَدَانِ (two hands). No word for “two” needed — the ending says it all.

الوضوء — The wudū connection

In الوضوء (wudū) we wash الوَجْه (the face), then اليَدَيْنِ (the two hands and forearms), wipe الرَّأْس (the head), then wash الرِّجْلَيْنِ (the two feet) — the full order and proofs are in the Fiqh wudū lesson.

Look closely at يَدَيْنِ and رِجْلَيْنِ — they are DUALS too. The ـَيْنِ ending is the majrūr/manṣūb twin of the ـَانِ you met in Ṣarf 5–6: the same two hands, but because the word is not marfūʿ here, the dual wears its other face.

جُمَل Phrases
  • هَذِهِ يَدِي. — This is my hand.
  • لِي عَيْنَانِ وَأُذُنَانِ. — I have two eyes and two ears.
  • أَغْسِلُ وَجْهِي فِي الوُضُوءِ. — I wash my face in wudū.
  • القَلْبُ مَلِكُ الأَعْضَاءِ. — The heart is the king of the limbs.

In لِي عَيْنَانِ the duals stand marfūʿ, so they wear ـَانِ — compare that with اليَدَيْنِ in the wudū box above.

سنة — The greatest limb of all

«أَلَا وَإِنَّ فِي الْجَسَدِ مُضْغَةً إِذَا صَلَحَتْ صَلَحَ الْجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، وَإِذَا فَسَدَتْ فَسَدَ الْجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، أَلَا وَهِيَ الْقَلْبُ»

The Prophet ﷺ said: “Truly, in the body there is a morsel of flesh: when it is sound, the whole body is sound, and when it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt — truly, it is the heart” (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).

And that word مُضْغَة (a morsel of flesh) is the very word you learned in Nawawī Hadith 4, describing the stage of our creation in the womb. Eyes, ears, hands and feet all obey their king: keep القَلْب sound, and every limb follows.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Play the pointing game: one person points to a limb — the head, an ear, a foot — and everyone names it in Arabic: هَذَا رَأْسٌ، هَذِهِ أُذُنٌ. Then, before الوضوء, name aloud what you are about to wash: أَغْسِلُ وَجْهِي، أَغْسِلُ يَدَيَّ — eight words, practised five times a day at the sink.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

رَأْس · وَجْه · عَيْن · أُذُن · فَم · يَد · رِجْل · قَلْب

Recite the eight words out loud while pointing to each limb, until the whole body answers in Arabic — and end with القَلْب, the king of them all.
