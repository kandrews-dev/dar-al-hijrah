وَصِيَّةُ أَب

A Father's Advice

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of realistic expectations in marriage — from مَسْؤُولِيَّةٌ to تَافِهٌ.
  • Read a real father's wedding-night advice to his daughter, on what married life actually requires.
  • Understand why unrealistic pictures of married life lead to real divorce, per the source text.
  • Know the authentic hadith on kindness within the home: the best of you are the best to their families.

This lesson reads a father's advice to his daughter on her wedding night — real advice from the same source as Vocab 36's lesson on marital disagreements. His core message: مَسْؤُولِيَّة (responsibility) answered with مَسْؤُولِيَّة, not a picture of endless ease — and the small, daily kindnesses that actually sustain a home.

١ — الزواج مسؤولية 1 — Marriage Is a Responsibility

A father advised his daughter on her wedding night: "Marriage, my daughter, is not an outing — it is a great مَسْؤُولِيَّة, the responsibility of establishing a complete family. It begins with caring for your life-partner, but does not stop there — it extends to children and grandchildren. Raising the sons and daughters of the ummah is in your hands, and home upbringing plays a great role in giving the ummah its identity and preserving its very being."

٢ — الحرية الحقيقية 2 — True Freedom

Some girls marry to escape their father's قُيُود, imagining married life free of restriction — "a very mistaken assumption," the father says, "because fathers' restrictions are not against their daughters' own interest and happiness — this is the general case, not the exception. There is no life anywhere entirely free of قُيُود; absolute, unrestrained freedom is ruin. A daughter should learn that she is more free in her father's house than she will be once she moves to her husband's."

٣ — مملكة الزوجة 3 — The Wife's Kingdom

"Marriage, my daughter, is not rest and continuous sleep — it is work and planning. The successful wife works twelve hours a day in her house: she is her household's finance minister, managing the مِيزَانِيَّة with her husband; its internal-affairs minister, guarding its safety; its minister of upbringing, raising and educating the children; and its minister of provisions, managing food and clothing. She cannot set aside a single one of these roles."

٤ — دروس من حوادث الطلاق 4 — Lessons from Real Divorce Cases

"Take a useful lesson, my daughter, from what we see and hear. We hear of many طَلَاق cases among young women — one of them married believing marriage meant nightly outings, a lavish dinner every week, travel abroad every year, and constant leisure. And you find one of them, after a while, discovering that marriage is instead continuous work: enduring life's problems, being صَبْر through hard circumstances, and تَكَيُّف with a new life whose unfamiliar sides can bring despair — and married life collapses between one evening and the next morning."

٥ — كلمة طيبة ووجه مشرق 5 — A Good Word and a Radiant Face

"Know, my daughter, that a person may endure an hour walking through a dusty, littered street, yet cannot bear a single dusty chair in his own home; he may eat a tasteless meal at a friend's table, yet cannot bear that at his own. A spouse returns from work tired, hoping to be met with a sweet اِبْتِسَامَة, a good word, and a radiant face — not a repeated frown." The text's own proverb: if you want a long, happy life together, meet each other with a smile — it costs nothing, and it is worth more than anything money can buy.

٦ — السعادة ليست في الأشياء 6 — Happiness Isn't in Things

"Know, my daughter, that today's concern with تَافِه (trivial) matters — the apartment, the furniture, the newest television — while neglecting the essentials of married life, only diminishes happiness. A big house, the latest devices — none of these provide سعادة on their own. Rather, a big heart, warm feelings, deep understanding, great تَقْدِير (appreciation), and true love: these are what achieve happiness, and everything else follows from them."

حديث — Best to one's family

«خَيْرُكُمْ خَيْرُكُمْ لِأَهْلِهِ، وَأَنَا خَيْرُكُمْ لِأَهْلِي»

"The best of you are the best of you to their families, and I am the best of you to my family" (Sunan al-Tirmidhī; graded ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albānī). The Prophet ﷺ measured a person's character by the very thing this lesson's father describes: the small, daily kindness shown at home — the good word, the radiant face — not grand gestures, and not things money can buy.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Practice the text's own proverb tonight: whoever in the house is tired or has had a hard day, greet them with an اِبْتِسَامَة and a good word before anything else. Then discuss as a family: what is one "trivial" thing people sometimes chase, expecting it to bring happiness it can't actually give?
