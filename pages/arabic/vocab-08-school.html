المدرسة والدرس

School & the Lesson

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the basic words for the school and the classroom.
  • Ask and answer أين؟ and ماذا؟ in simple sentences.
  • Use words like مدرسة · فصل · معلم · طالب · كتاب · قلم.
  • Say what you study: أدرس العربية and أقرأ.
  • Build a small school-day vocabulary that feels useful right away.

Learning Arabic is not only about the home and the city. It is also about the place where we study, read, and grow. This lesson gives you the words for the school world so you can talk about learning in simple, everyday Arabic.

أين؟ · ماذا؟ Where? · What?
  • أين المدرسةُ؟ — Where is the school?
  • أين الفصلُ؟ — Where is the classroom?
  • ماذا في الفصلِ؟ — What is in the classroom?
  • أدرس العربيةَ — I study Arabic.
  • أقرأ الكتابَ — I read the book.

The words are simple, but they give you a real way to talk about learning and the places where it happens.

جذر ك-ت-ب — one root, a whole classroom

Arabic builds whole families of words from a single three-letter root, and the classroom is the clearest place to see it. From ك-ت-ب (the idea of writing) grows almost everything in the room: كَتَبَ (he wrote) → كِتَاب (book) → مَكْتَبَة (library) → مَكْتَب (desk) → كَاتِب (writer) → مَكْتُوب (written). Learn one root and you can already guess a dozen words.

The root ع-ل-م does exactly the same for knowledge: عَلِمَ (he knew) → عِلْم (knowledge) → عَالِم (scholar) → مُعَلِّم (teacher) → تَعَلَّمَ (he learned). This was the very first thing taught to آدم (Ādam): «وَعَلَّمَ آدَمَ الْأَسْمَاءَ كُلَّهَا» (al-Baqarah 2:31) — and He taught Ādam all the names.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

In the family, point to each object and say its name in Arabic: هذا كتابٌ, هذا قلمٌ, and ask أين الفصلُ؟. Then say what you study: أدرس العربيةَ.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

مَدْرَسَةٌ · فَصْلٌ · كِتَابٌ · قَلَمٌ · أَدْرُسُ

Keep these school words close. Say them before you begin your study session, and let your Arabic begin with your daily work.
