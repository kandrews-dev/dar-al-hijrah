Health & Medicine — Advanced

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Deepen the health vocabulary you began in Vocab 12 — from طَبِيبٌ and دَوَاءٌ to the full clinic.
  • Handle a clinic visit in Arabic: مَوْعِدٌ (appointment), فَحْصٌ (examination), صَيْدَلِيَّةٌ (pharmacy), وَصْفَةٌ (prescription).
  • Read a short connected passage about a doctor's visit — the new skill of Book 3.
  • Know the sunnah duʿāʼ for the sick, word by word.

Welcome to Book 3. Back in Vocab 12 you learned to say أَنَا مَرِيضٌ and أَحْتَاجُ دَوَاءً — enough to ask for help. This lesson gives you the whole journey: booking the مَوْعِد, sitting through the فَحْص, carrying the وَصْفَة to the صَيْدَلِيَّة. The tables are a little bigger now, and — the real upgrade — the words stop standing alone and start walking together in a connected passage. And above all of it sits the word that only Allah can grant: الشِّفَاء.

Ten words instead of eight — that is the Book 3 pace. And look at مُمَرِّضٌ before moving on: it shares its root م-ر-ض with مَرِيضٌ (sick) from Vocab 12. The sick one and the one who tends the sick, cut from the same three letters — Arabic never wastes a root. The word sits on the pattern مُفَعِّل: the doer of the doubled-middle door II you met on the ṣarf track — the one who makes the caring happen, again and again.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

أَصَابَتْ أَحْمَدَ حُمَّى شَدِيدَةٌ وَسُعَالٌ. حَجَزَ أَبُوهُ مَوْعِدًا فِي الْعِيَادَةِ. فَحَصَ الطَّبِيبُ أَحْمَدَ وَكَتَبَ لَهُ وَصْفَةً. أَخَذُوا الدَّوَاءَ مِنَ الصَّيْدَلِيَّةِ. وَبَعْدَ أَيَّامٍ شَفَاهُ اللهُ، فَقَالَ أَحْمَدُ: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: A strong fever and a cough struck Ahmad. His father booked an appointment at the clinic. The doctor examined Ahmad and wrote him a prescription. They took the medicine from the pharmacy. And after some days Allah cured him, so Ahmad said: al-ḥamdu lillāh. Five sentences, seven of the ten table words — this is what Book 3 asks of you: not word lists, but stories. Notice the last sentence gives the cure to Allah, not to the pill: شَفَاهُ اللهُ. The medicine is the means; the شِفَاء is His.

سنة — The duʿāʼ for the sick

«أَذْهِبِ الْبَاسَ رَبَّ النَّاسِ، اشْفِ وَأَنْتَ الشَّافِي، لَا شِفَاءَ إِلَّا شِفَاؤُكَ، شِفَاءً لَا يُغَادِرُ سَقَمًا»

When the Prophet ﷺ visited a sick person he would say: "Remove the harm, Lord of mankind; cure — and You are the Curer; there is no cure but Your cure — a cure that leaves no sickness behind" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).

Count the word شِفَاء — it appears three times: لَا شِفَاءَ إِلَّا شِفَاؤُكَ، شِفَاءً… — the very maṣdar sitting in your table, and beside it الشَّافِي, the Curer Himself. And the opening word أَذْهِبِ is a command (amr) from door IV — the أَفْعَلَ door you built in Ṣarf 7 with the amr-forms of Ṣarf 4: ذَهَبَ is "he went"; أَذْهَبَ is "he made it go away"; أَذْهِبِ is "make it go away!" Your vocabulary and your ṣarf meet inside a duʿāʼ the Prophet ﷺ actually said at the bedside.

في العيادة At the Clinic — phrases
  • عِنْدِي مَوْعِدٌ مَعَ الطَّبِيبِ. — I have an appointment with the doctor.
  • عِنْدِي حُمَّى وَسُعَالٌ. — I have a fever and a cough.
  • أَيْنَ الصَّيْدَلِيَّةُ؟ — Where is the pharmacy?
  • هَذِهِ وَصْفَةٌ مِنَ الطَّبِيبِ. — This is a prescription from the doctor.

Four phrases carry you through the whole visit: the reception desk, the doctor's question, the walk to the pharmacy, the counter. Say them in order and you have role-played the entire journey.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Role-play the clinic tonight: one of you is the patient (عِنْدِي حُمَّى وَسُعَالٌ), one is the parent booking the visit (عِنْدِي مَوْعِدٌ مَعَ الطَّبِيبِ), one is the doctor doing the فَحْص and handing over the وَصْفَة — then swap roles until everyone has played all three. And carry the best part of this lesson beyond the game: whenever anyone in the family is truly unwell, be the one who puts a hand on them and recites the Prophet's ﷺ duʿāʼ — أَذْهِبِ الْبَاسَ رَبَّ النَّاسِ…

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

أَذْهِبِ الْبَاسَ رَبَّ النَّاسِ، اشْفِ وَأَنْتَ الشَّافِي، لَا شِفَاءَ إِلَّا شِفَاؤُكَ، شِفَاءً لَا يُغَادِرُ سَقَمًا

Memorize the duʿāʼ for the sick until it comes without thinking — remove the harm, Lord of mankind; cure, and You are the Curer; there is no cure but Your cure — a cure that leaves no sickness behind (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).
