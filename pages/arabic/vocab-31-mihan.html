المهن الشرعية

The Professions of the Dīn

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the people who serve the masjid and the dīn by name — from الإِمَام to الحَافِظ.
  • Recognise the ṣarf patterns carrying each title — فَاعِل, مُفَعِّل, فَعِيل, and the hiding-yāʼ manqūṣ.
  • Connect each role to what you have already seen with your own eyes at the masjid on a Friday.
  • Complete the professions picture begun in Vocab 11 — the jobs of the dunyā, now joined by the ranks of the dīn.

Back in Vocab 11 you learned the professions of the dunyā — the طَبِيب, the مُهَنْدِس, the مُعَلِّم — and how to ask أَيْنَ تَعْمَلُ؟ This lesson completes the picture with the professions of the dīn: the people you meet every Friday without ever being introduced. You already hear the أَذَان of Vocab 6 — now meet the man who calls it. You already stand behind the إِمَام — now the title itself is explained. And behind them stand the quieter ranks: the قَاضٍ who judges, the مُفْتٍ who answers, the عَالِم who teaches, and the حَافِظ who carries the Qur'an in his chest.

Ten words, and nearly every one is a ṣarf lesson wearing a turban. مُؤَذِّن is مُفَعِّل — the doer-noun of door II: أَذَّنَ (he called the adhān) gives مُؤَذِّن exactly as عَلَّمَ gives مُعَلِّم from Vocab 11. عَالِم and حَافِظ are plain فَاعِل — the doers of عَلِمَ and حَفِظَ. قَاضٍ and مُفْتٍ are the manqūṣ pair — like غَالٍ from last lesson, the yāʼ hides in rafʿ and jarr and steps out with ال: القَاضِي، المُفْتِي. And طَالِبُ عِلْمٍ is an iḍāfah: the first noun drops its tanwīn, the second takes jarr — one title made of two words.

أدب — The fatwā belongs to the people of knowledge

هَذَا الْمَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي؛ لِلْفَتَاوَى يُرْجَعُ إِلَى الْعُلَمَاءِ

You have read that line before — it closes every page of this site. Now you can finally read it as a student of Arabic: يُفْتِي is the very verb of the مُفْتِي, and الفَتَاوَى is the plural of فَتْوَى. The فَتْوَى belongs to the people of knowledge — not to websites, not to students, not to whoever speaks loudest.

In this lesson you learn these words — and with the words, respect for the ranks they name. Nobody appoints himself قَاضٍ or مُفْتٍ; these titles are earned by decades under the scholars. To see how knowledge is actually carried — checked, chained, and passed hand to hand across the generations — read the science of hadith lesson on the Hadeeth tab.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — Friday at the masjid

يَوْمَ الْجُمُعَةِ يَذْهَبُ أَحْمَدُ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ مَعَ أَبِيهِ. يُؤَذِّنُ الْمُؤَذِّنُ لِصَلَاةِ الْجُمُعَةِ فَيَدْخُلُ النَّاسُ الْمَسْجِدَ. يَخْطُبُ الْخَطِيبُ خُطْبَةَ الْجُمُعَةِ ثُمَّ يُصَلِّي الإِمَامُ بِالنَّاسِ. وَبَعْدَ الصَّلَاةِ يُعَلِّمُ عَالِمٌ النَّاسَ دَرْسًا. يَحْلُمُ أَحْمَدُ أَنْ يَكُونَ حَافِظًا لِلْقُرْآنِ وَطَالِبَ عِلْمٍ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: On Friday, Ahmad goes to the masjid with his father. The muʼadhdhin calls the adhān for the Friday prayer, and the people enter the masjid. The khaṭīb delivers the Friday khuṭbah, then the imām leads the people in prayer. After the prayer, a scholar teaches the people a lesson. Ahmad dreams of becoming a ḥāfiẓ of the Qur'an and a student of knowledge. Notice how the verbs and their doers walk in pairs — يُؤَذِّنُ الْمُؤَذِّنُ, يَخْطُبُ الْخَطِيبُ خُطْبَةَ — and how يُصَلِّي بِالنَّاسِ with بِـ means "leads the people in prayer." In the last sentence, يَكُونَ pushes its khabar into naṣb: حَافِظًا… وَطَالِبَ عِلْمٍ — the iḍāfah changes only its first word.

في المسجد At the Masjid — phrases
  • مَنِ الإِمَامُ فِي مَسْجِدِكُمْ؟ — Who is the imām in your masjid?
  • يُؤَذِّنُ الْمُؤَذِّنُ لِصَلَاةِ الْفَجْرِ. — The muʼadhdhin calls the adhān for Fajr prayer.
  • اِسْتَمَعْنَا إِلَى خُطْبَةِ الْجُمُعَةِ. — We listened to the Friday khuṭbah.
  • أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَكُونَ طَالِبَ عِلْمٍ. — I want to be a student of knowledge.

Watch the first phrase: مَنْ takes a helping kasrah before ال — مَنِ الإِمَامُ. And the last phrase puts the iḍāfah into naṣb after أَكُونَ: طَالِبَ عِلْمٍ — the first word bends, the second stays in jarr.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

At the next Friday prayer, name each role you see in Arabic — quietly, with respect. The one who calls is الْمُؤَذِّنُ; the one on the minbar is الْخَطِيبُ; the one you follow in ṣalāh is الإِمَامُ. Whisper the names to each other on the way home, not in the rows — these are titles of service, and the adab of the masjid comes before the vocabulary drill.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

الإِمَام · المُؤَذِّن · الخَطِيب · العَالِم · طَالِب العِلْم

Carry the five names of service in the masjid until they come without thinking — and let طَالِب العِلْم be the first title you work to deserve.
