المشاعر

Feelings

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the main feeling words, from سَعِيدٌ (happy) to مَسْرُورٌ (delighted).
  • Ask and answer كَيْفَ تَشْعُرُ؟ — how do you feel?
  • Notice that most feeling words sit on the فَاعِل doer-pattern you met on the ṣarf track.
  • Meet the Qur'an's لَا تَحْزَنْ — the believer's answer to sadness and fear.

Every day carries feelings through it — you wake up happy, you get hungry by midday, something frightens you, something delights you. Arabic has a precise word for each one, and — here is the surprise of this lesson — most of those words are built on a pattern you already own from the ṣarf track. Eight words, one question, and one āyah that answers sadness and fear forever.

المشاعر والصرف The Ṣarf Hiding in the Feelings

لطيفة — The ṣarf track walking around in daily life

Look at خَائِفٌ، غَاضِبٌ، جَائِعٌ — all three sit on the doer-pattern فَاعِل from Ṣarf 1: the one doing the fearing, the being-angry, the hungering. Then flip it: مَسْرُورٌ is مَفْعُول — not the one doing, but the one made happy, the one delight was done to. The feelings vocabulary is the ṣarf track walking around in daily life. One word plays by different rules: عَطْشَانُ is on the pattern فَعْلَان and is a diptote — no tanwīn, just a single ḍammah at the end.

لَا تَحْزَنْ Do Not Grieve

«إِذْ يَقُولُ لِصَاحِبِهِ لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا»

When he said to his companion, "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us."

سورة التوبة — al-Tawbah 9:40

You know this moment. The Prophet ﷺ and Abū Bakr are hiding in the cave during the hijrah, the pursuers standing so close that a glance downward would have found them — the very scene you studied on the Seerah tab's hijrah stage. Abū Bakr felt what this lesson's words name: حَزِين and خَائِف. And the Prophet ﷺ gave him the believer's answer to both, in five words: لَا تَحْزَنْ، إِنَّ اللهَ مَعَنَا — do not grieve, Allah is with us. Sadness and fear are real feelings; the Qur'an does not pretend otherwise. It answers them.

كَيْفَ تَشْعُرُ؟ How Do You Feel?
  • كَيْفَ تَشْعُرُ؟ — How do you feel?
  • أَنَا سَعِيدٌ اليَوْمَ. — I am happy today.
  • أَنَا جَائِعٌ. — I am hungry.
  • لَا تَحْزَنْ، إِنَّ اللهَ مَعَنَا. — Do not grieve — Allah is with us.

A grammar taste for later: لَا تَحْزَنْ is the forbidden-form of the muḍāriʿ — the لَا of prohibition cuts the verb's ending down to a sukūn (jazm). The naḥw track will formalize this; for now, just hear that clipped ending and know it means "do not…".

من الصفة إلى المصدر — from the adjective to the noun

This unit taught the adjective-states — خَائِف، حَزِين، غَاضِب — the person feeling. But to read the Qur'an you almost always need the noun of the emotion, the maṣdar: خَائِف → خَوْف, حَزِين → حُزْن, غَاضِب → غَضَب. The Book rarely says a man is afraid; it names the fear itself — «لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ».

And it names whole spiritual states this unit had left out. خَوْف (fear) and رَجَاء (hope) — the two wings of worship, on which the believer flies to Allah, neither one alone. صَبْر (patience) in hardship and شُكْر (gratitude) in ease — between them lies the whole of the believer's affair. And above all سَكِينَة — the tranquillity Allah sends down, which sits inside this very lesson's own cave āyah «فَأَنزَلَ اللَّهُ سَكِينَتَهُ عَلَيْهِ» (al-Tawbah 9:40). Learn the noun beside every adjective, and the emotional vocabulary of the Qur'an opens to you.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

At dinner tonight, go around the table with كَيْفَ تَشْعُرُ؟ — and everyone answers honestly with a feeling word: أَنَا مُتْعَبٌ، أَنَا جَائِعٌ، أَنَا مَسْرُورٌ. No repeats allowed, so the last person has to dig for the rarer words. And keep the best line of the lesson ready for real life: when someone in the family is down, be the one who says لَا تَحْزَنْ، إِنَّ اللهَ مَعَنَا.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

سَعِيدٌ · حَزِينٌ · خَائِفٌ · غَاضِبٌ · مُتْعَبٌ · جَائِعٌ · عَطْشَانُ · مَسْرُورٌ — لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللهَ مَعَنَا

Recite the eight feelings until they come without stopping — happy, sad, afraid, angry, tired, hungry, thirsty, delighted — and seal them with the cave's answer: do not grieve, Allah is with us.
