طَلَبُ الْعِلْمِ

Seeking Knowledge — the Capstone

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of طَلَبُ الْعِلْمِ — from عِلْمٌ and حَلْقَةٌ to إِجَازَةٌ.
  • Read the famous path-to-Paradise hadith with full understanding, letter by letter.
  • See how many of its words you now own — and name the door of every verb in it.
  • Close the 36-lesson journey, and know the road ahead.

Thirty-five tables ago you learned to say السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ. Since then the words of the home, the masjid, the market, the seasons, the hajj and the whole Muslim world have passed through your hands. One table remains — and it is about the very thing you have been doing all along. Every time you opened a lesson, flipped a card, memorized a hadith, you were performing an act the scholars have a whole vocabulary for: طَلَبُ الْعِلْمِ, the seeking of knowledge. Your last vocabulary lesson names the road you have been walking — and the hadith at its centre tells you where that road leads.

Look down the first column: your last table is almost entirely maṣdars — and you can weigh every one. عِلْمٌ، حِفْظٌ، فَهْمٌ، طَلَبٌ are bare-door maṣdars, the compact "act-nouns" you first met on the ṣarf track. مُرَاجَعَةٌ wears the door-III dress مُفَاعَلَة — the door of back-and-forth, and what is revision but going back and forth over what you learned? And طَلَبٌ itself has been hiding inside your ṣarf track all along: Ṣarf 11 named door X اِسْتَفْعَلَ بَابَ الطَّلَبِ, the door of seeking — اِسْتَغْفَرَ, "he sought forgiveness." Then two words from the other tracks join the table: دَفْتَرُ الْمُلَاحَظَاتِ is a naḥw-track iḍāfah, two nouns hand in hand, and إِجَازَةٌ comes straight from the hadith sciences: the scholar's license saying "what you took from me, you may now pass on" — the chain of knowledge running unbroken from teacher to student since the Companions.

سنة — The path to Paradise

«وَمَنْ سَلَكَ طَرِيقًا يَلْتَمِسُ فِيهِ عِلْمًا سَهَّلَ اللَّهُ لَهُ بِهِ طَرِيقًا إِلَى الْجَنَّةِ»

"And whoever travels a path seeking knowledge in it, Allah eases for him by it a path to Paradise" (Muslim).

Now read it again slowly, because this hadith is a ṣarf feast — and you are, at last, a guest who can name every dish. يَلْتَمِسُ — "he seeks out" — is door VIII اِفْتَعَلَ, the تاء tucked inside the root. سَهَّلَ — "He eased" — is door II فَعَّلَ with its doubled middle letter, built on the same root as the سَهْلًا of your very first greeting: Allah makes the ground easy under the seeker's feet. And عِلْمًا is the maṣdar itself standing as the object, its fatḥah-tanwīn announcing the naṣb you learned to hear on the naḥw track. Three years ago you could not have read this sentence. Today you can take it apart word by word — and the hadith is about the very act by which you learned to do so.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the capstone: this one is about you

بَدَأْتَ بِـ«السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ» فِي الدَّرْسِ الْأَوَّلِ. تَعَلَّمْتَ كَلِمَاتِ الْبَيْتِ وَالْمَسْجِدِ وَالسُّوقِ وَالْعَالَمِ. قَرَأْتَ الْقِصَصَ وَأَعْرَبْتَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ. وَالْيَوْمَ تُتِمُّ الدَّرْسَ السَّادِسَ وَالثَّلَاثِينَ. وَهَذَا لَيْسَ نِهَايَةَ الطَّرِيقِ — بَلْ هُوَ بِدَايَةُ طَلَبِ الْعِلْمِ.

For thirty-five lessons the passages were about Ahmad, his family, his city, his travels. The last one is about you: You began with al-salāmu ʿalaykum in the first lesson. You learned the words of the home, the masjid, the market and the world. You read the stories and parsed the Book of Allah. Today you finish the thirty-sixth lesson. And this is not the end of the road — it is the beginning of seeking knowledge. Even here the grammar keeps teaching: تُتِمُّ is door IV of the doubled root تَمَّ — "you bring to completion"; وَالثَّلَاثِينَ carries the ياء ending of the sound masculine plural in naṣb, exactly as the naḥw track promised; and the final words are a three-noun iḍāfah chain, بِدَايَةُ طَلَبِ الْعِلْمِ — the beginning, of the seeking, of knowledge. Read the passage aloud once more, slowly. Every word in it, you own.

عبارات طالب العلم The Seeker's Phrases
  • عِنْدِي سُؤَالٌ يَا أُسْتَاذُ. — I have a question, teacher.
  • حَفِظْتُ الدَّرْسَ ثُمَّ رَاجَعْتُهُ. — I memorised the lesson, then revised it.
  • أَكْتُبُ الْفَوَائِدَ فِي دَفْتَرِ الْمُلَاحَظَاتِ. — I write the benefits in the notebook.
  • جَلَسْنَا فِي حَلْقَةِ الْعِلْمِ بَعْدَ الْمَغْرِبِ. — We sat in the study-circle after Maghrib.

Four phrases for a lifetime in the حَلْقَة: the raised hand, the nightly routine, the notebook habit, and the seat in the masjid. The second one hides the verb behind your table's مُرَاجَعَة — رَاجَعْتُهُ, door III at work — and the last two each carry an iḍāfah you can now hear without being told.

🎉 الرحلة تمت — the lugha spine is complete

36 lessons · around 350 words · 5 exams — from السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ at the door of Book 1 to the hadith of the path at the close of Book 3. Every table, every passage, every phrase-box is now behind you, and the words are yours. And the road ahead is open: the qirāʼah readings, then قطر الندى on the naḥw track, and then — the goal of it all — the books of the scholars themselves, read in their own tongue, إِنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

This week, close the year the way the حَلْقَة closes: go around the table, and let each person tell the family, in Arabic, one thing they learned this year — a word, an ayah, a hadith, a rule. Simple sentences are enough: تَعَلَّمْتُ… — "I learned…" Let the youngest go first and the eldest go last, and when the circle is complete, seal it together with one line: الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي عَلَّمَنَا — praise be to Allah who taught us.
