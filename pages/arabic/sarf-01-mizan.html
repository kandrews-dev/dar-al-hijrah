الجَذْرُ وَالمِيزَانُ الصَّرْفِيّ

Roots & the Scale

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Know what الصرف (morphology) studies, and how it differs from النحو.
  • Understand the three-letter root (الجذر) that most Arabic words grow from.
  • Weigh words on المِيزَانُ الصَّرْفِيّ — the scale of ف ع ل.
  • Recognise the patterns فَاعِل (doer), مَفْعُول (done-to) and مَفْعَل (place).
  • See root-families in words you already know — كِتَاب، مَسْجِد، مَطْبَخ.
A new track opens: On the naḥw track you learned how word endings change — الرفع والنصب والخفض. Ṣarf looks inside the word: how its very shape is built and reshaped to give new meanings. Naḥw governs the end of the word; ṣarf governs its body. Together they are the two wings of Arabic grammar.
مَا هُوَ الصَّرْف؟ Part One — What is Ṣarf?

الصرف علمٌ يُعرَف به بناءُ الكلمة وتغيُّرُ صيغتها لمعانٍ مقصودة

Ṣarf is the science of how a word is built, and how its shape is changed on purpose to produce new meanings. From one small root Arabic grows a whole family: a verb, a doer, a thing done, a place, a tool. Once you can see the root inside a word, an unknown word stops being a stranger — you can often guess what it means before you open a dictionary.

الجَذْرُ الثُّلَاثِيّ Part Two — The Three-Letter Root

Most Arabic words grow from a root of three letters. Watch what the root ك ت ب (the idea of writing) produces — you already know half of these words from the vocab track:

الكلمة Meaning How the root works
كَتَبَhe wrotethe base verb
كَاتِبٌwriterthe one who does it
مَكْتُوبٌwrittenthe thing it was done to
كِتَابٌbookthe written thing (Vocab 2)
مَكْتَبٌdesk / officethe place of writing (Vocab 2)
مَكْتَبَةٌlibrarythe place of books (Vocab 7)
المِيزَانُ الصَّرْفِيّ Part Three — The Scale of ف ع ل

To talk about word-shapes, the scholars invented a scale. The three root letters are matched, in order, to the letters ف · ع · ل. Any extra letters stay as they are. The result is the word's وزن (pattern):

الكلمة الوزن What the pattern means
كَتَبَفَعَلَthe base past verb
كَاتِبٌفَاعِلٌthe doer — writer, and likewise عَالِمٌ (knower)
مَكْتُوبٌمَفْعُولٌthe done-to — written, and likewise مَعْلُومٌ (known)
مَسْجِدٌمَفْعِلٌplace-noun — the place of سُجُود (Vocab 6)
مَطْبَخٌمَفْعَلٌplace-noun — the place of cooking (Vocab 7)

Read a pattern like a mould: pour any root into فَاعِل and you get its doer; pour it into مَفْعَل and you get its place. This is why the scholars call ṣarf the mother of the sciences of Arabic — one scale unlocks thousands of words.

مِثَالٌ قُرْآنِيّ An Example from the Qur'an

بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ

In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Bestower of Mercy. (al-Fātiḥah 1:1)

الرَّحْمَٰن and الرَّحِيم grow from one root — ر ح م (mercy) — on two different patterns. The scholars of tafsīr, such as Ibn ʿUthaymīn and al-Saʿdī, explain that الرَّحْمَٰن describes the vastness of Allah's mercy as His attribute, while الرَّحِيم points to His mercy reaching His creation. Two shapes from one root — this is ṣarf serving ʿaqīdah and tafsīr.

Memorize — اِحْفَظْ

الميزان: ف ع ل · فَاعِلٌ للفاعل · مَفْعُولٌ للمفعول · مَفْعَلٌ للمكان

The scale is ف ع ل. Fāʿil for the doer, mafʿūl for the done-to, mafʿal for the place. This week, take any word you meet and try to find its three root letters.
