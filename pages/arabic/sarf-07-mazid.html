Ṣarf Track · Lesson 7 · المزيدYear 2

الفعل المزيد

The Increased Forms — Families of Meaning

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the difference between المجرد (the bare three-letter verb) and المزيد (the verb with added letters).
  • Recognise the three most common increased doors: فَعَّلَ · فَاعَلَ · أَفْعَلَ.
  • Feel how each added letter adds meaning — the shape of the word carries part of its sense.
  • Find these forms hiding inside words you already know — مُعَلِّم، سَافَرَ، الإِسْلَام.
Where we are in the ṣarf track: Lessons 1–6 taught you to conjugate the bare verb — كَتَبَ and يَكْتُبُ turned through all fourteen seats. But open any real text and you will find that most verbs carry extra letters beyond their three-letter root — and each addition opens a new door of meaning. The Year-1 goal today is modest and clear: recognise forms II, III and IV when you see them — not master all ten doors. Recognition first; mastery comes later on the ladder.
المجرد والمزيد Part One — The Bare Verb and the Increased Verb

المجرد ما كانت حروفه كلها أصلية، والمزيد ما زيد فيه حرف أو أكثر

The bare verb is the one whose letters are all root letters and nothing more: كَتَبَ — three letters, all of them the root ك ت ب. The increased verb has one letter or more added to the root: عَلَّمَ — the root is still ع ل م, but the middle letter has been doubled, and that shaddah is an addition. Put them on the scale of Lesson 1 and the difference shows at once: كَتَبَ weighs فَعَلَ, while عَلَّمَ weighs فَعَّلَ — the scale itself gains the extra letter. Nothing in Arabic is added for decoration: every added letter adds meaning.

الأبواب الثلاثة الأشهر Part Two — The Three Most Common Doors

The scholars call each increased pattern a باب (door) — walk a root through a door and it comes out with a new shade of meaning. There are ten well-known doors; this year you need only the three you will meet most often. Each is shown with a word you already know:

الباب الوزن مثال Meaning shift
فَعَّلَ — doubling the middle letter · often intensive or causative
الثانيفَعَّلَعَلِمَ ← عَلَّمَعَلِمَ he knew → عَلَّمَ he TAUGHT — he made someone else know. And مُعَلِّم (teacher, Vocab 11) is exactly this door's doer-pattern مُفَعِّل — the word finally explains itself.
فَاعَلَ — an alif after the first letter · doing WITH someone
الثالثفَاعَلَسَافَرَسَافَرَ he travelled — the door of shared, directed action; from the same root as السَّفَر (travel, Vocab 10). Likewise جَاهَدَ، قَاتَلَ — actions done with or towards another.
أَفْعَلَ — a hamzah in front · often causative
الرابعأَفْعَلَسَلِمَ ← أَسْلَمَسَلِمَ to be safe → أَسْلَمَ he submitted, surrendered himself — he gave himself over into safety and obedience. Its maṣdar is الإِسْلَام itself: the very name of the dīn is a form-IV word.

Read the three doors aloud as a chant — فَعَّلَ · فَاعَلَ · أَفْعَلَ — and hear what changed: a shaddah in the middle, an alif after the first letter, a hamzah at the front. The root ف ع ل never moves; only the additions move around it. That is the whole trick of the mazīd: find the three root letters first, and whatever is left over is the addition telling you which door you are standing in.

نَزَّلَ وأَنْزَلَ Part Three — Two Doors for One Sending Down
Here is something beautiful: the Qur'an uses both of the causative doors for the sending down of revelation — نَزَّلَ (form II) and أَنْزَلَ (form IV), both grown from the one root ن ز ل (coming down). The scholars of tafsīr — as Ibn ʿUthaymīn and others discuss — note the shades between the two: نَزَّلَ often carries the sense of a gradual, repeated sending down, ayah after ayah over twenty-three years, while أَنْزَلَ can point to the sending down taken as a whole. Hold the observation lightly — the mufassirūn weigh each ayah in its own context — but see what it proves: the difference between two doors of ṣarf is a difference the Qur'an itself uses with precision. This is why we learn the mazīd.
أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

الرَّحْمَٰنُ ۝ عَلَّمَ الْقُرْآنَ

The Most Merciful — He taught the Qur'an. (al-Raḥmān 55:1–2)

عَلَّمَ — form II, the very verb of our table: the middle ل doubled, and the meaning turned causative — Allah made known, He taught. The teaching of the Qur'an is announced in a form-II verb, and every مُعَلِّم who passes it on stands in the doer-pattern of that same door.

إِنَّا أَنزَلْنَاهُ فِي لَيْلَةِ الْقَدْرِ

Indeed, We sent it down in the Night of Decree. (al-Qadr 97:1)

أَنزَلْنَاهُ — form IV أَنْزَلَ wearing the ـْنَا ending you learned in Ṣarf 5: We sent down. Strip the ending and the added hamzah, and the root ن ز ل remains. One word, and you can already read three layers in it: the root, the door, and the doer — this is ṣarf paying its wages.
