العالم الإسلامي

The Muslim World

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the geography words — بَلَدٌ (country), عَاصِمَةٌ (capital), قَارَّةٌ (continent), and أُمَّةٌ (ummah).
  • Name the sacred cities — مَكَّةُ المُكَرَّمَةُ and المَدِينَةُ المُنَوَّرَةُ — and some lands of the Muslim world in Arabic.
  • Ask and answer "where are you from?" — مِنْ أَيِّ بَلَدٍ أَنْتَ؟ — at Book-3 depth.
  • Feel the oneness of the ummah across every border — one dīn, one qiblah, one ummah.

Since your very first Fātiḥah you have said رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ — Lord of the worlds — and in the naḥw drills you long ago parsed that عَالَمِين. This lesson opens the map underneath the word. Book 3 hands you the vocabulary of the عَالَم itself: its قَارَّات, its بِلَاد and their عَوَاصِم, its شُعُوب and their لُغَات — and then the one word that leaps over every line on the map: أُمَّة. From المغرب at one edge to إندونيسيا at the other, the borders change the stamps in the passport, not the qiblah in the chest.

Ten words, and the ṣarf track has walked this map before you. عَاصِمَة is فَاعِلَة from عَصَمَ (to protect) — the capital as "the protecting one," the city that shelters the land's affairs. Now read the two sacred names slowly. مُكَرَّمَة is the done-to of door II كَرَّمَ — "the honoured one" — and مُنَوَّرَة is its twin from نَوَّرَ — "the illuminated one": the same مُفَعَّلَة pattern stamped on both cities, one honoured by Allah's House, one lit by the one who emigrated to it ﷺ. And keep أُمَّة close to your ear: it rises from the same root as أُمّ, the mother — one mother-community, born not of one land or one لُغَة but of one dīn.

آية — One ummah

«إِنَّ هَٰذِهِ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَأَنَا رَبُّكُمْ فَاعْبُدُونِ»

"Indeed this ummah of yours is one ummah, and I am your Lord, so worship Me" (al-Anbiyāʼ 21:92).

Two small treasures sit in this āyah. First, hear the double fatḥah-tanwīn of أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً — manṣūb, describing the state: "this ummah of yours, being one ummah." Take that lightly for now; the naḥw track will name it properly in its own time. Second, weigh فَاعْبُدُونِ — three of your attached-pronoun lessons packed into one word: the amr اعْبُدُوا, then نون الوقاية, then a hidden ي — "so worship Me" — with only the kasrah on the نون left pointing to the vanished ي. The āyah names the ummah one, then turns at once to the reason it is one: one Lord, worshipped alone.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

اَلْعَالَمُ الْإِسْلَامِيُّ يَمْتَدُّ عَبْرَ الْقَارَّاتِ، مِنَ الْمَغْرِبِ إِلَى إِنْدُونِيسِيَا. تَخْتَلِفُ لُغَاتُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ، وَلَكِنَّ الْأُمَّةَ وَاحِدَةٌ. يُصَلُّونَ جَمِيعًا إِلَى قِبْلَةٍ وَاحِدَةٍ فِي مَكَّةَ الْمُكَرَّمَةِ. أَصْدِقَاءُ أَحْمَدَ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ مِنْ بِلَادٍ كَثِيرَةٍ. دِينٌ وَاحِدٌ، وَقِبْلَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ، وَأُمَّةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: The Muslim world stretches across the continents, from Morocco to Indonesia. The Muslims' languages differ, but the ummah is one. They all pray toward one qiblah in Makkah al-Mukarramah. Ahmad's friends at the masjid are from many countries. One dīn, one qiblah, one ummah. A word on إِنْدُونِيسِيَا — a borrowed name wearing Arabic letters, so do not go hunting for a three-letter root; the map is full of such loan-words. But يَمْتَدُّ in the first sentence is pure ṣarf — a door-VIII verb from مَدَّ, "it stretches itself out." And watch وَلَكِنَّ الْأُمَّةَ وَاحِدَةٌ in the second: لَكِنَّ is a sister of إِنَّ, so الْأُمَّةَ takes fatḥah after it — the very structure of the āyah above, echoed in the passage. The last sentence has no verb at all: three nouns, three times وَاحِد, and the whole lesson said in six words.

من أي بلد أنت؟ Where Are You From? — phrases
  • مِنْ أَيِّ بَلَدٍ أَنْتَ؟ — Which country are you from?
  • أَنَا مِنَ الْمَغْرِبِ، وَعَاصِمَتُهُ الرِّبَاطُ. — I am from Morocco, and its capital is Rabat.
  • فِي الْعَالَمِ الْإِسْلَامِيِّ شُعُوبٌ وَلُغَاتٌ كَثِيرَةٌ. — In the Muslim world there are many peoples and languages.
  • الْأُمَّةُ وَاحِدَةٌ عَبْرَ الْحُدُودِ. — The ummah is one across the borders.

This is the Book-3 depth of a question you met in Book 1. Back then the answer was one word; now look at the question itself — أَيِّ wears a kasrah after مِنْ and leans on بَلَدٍ as its muḍāf ilayh — and the answer has grown a second half: name the بَلَد, then offer its عَاصِمَة. The last phrase is the lesson's banner, ready to hang.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Spread a world map on the table. Let the children find three Muslim lands and name each one in Arabic — هَذَا بَلَدٌ مُسْلِمٌ — and point out its عَاصِمَة if the map shows it. Then play the passport game around the table: each person picks a بَلَد and asks the next — مِنْ أَيِّ بَلَدٍ أَنْتَ؟ — answering with أَنَا مِنْ… and the capital, exactly as the phrases box taught. Finish at the two cities no Muslim child ever needs labelled on a map — مَكَّةُ الْمُكَرَّمَةُ and الْمَدِينَةُ الْمُنَوَّرَةُ — and recall together that whatever the answers around the table were, إِنَّ هَٰذِهِ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

إِنَّ هَٰذِهِ أُمَّتُكُمْ أُمَّةً وَاحِدَةً وَأَنَا رَبُّكُمْ فَاعْبُدُونِ

Memorize the āyah of the one ummah — "Indeed this ummah of yours is one ummah, and I am your Lord, so worship Me" (al-Anbiyāʼ 21:92) — until every border on the map looks thinner than it did yesterday.
