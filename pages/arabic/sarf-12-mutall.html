الصحيح والمعتل

Sound and Weak Verbs

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the three weak letters — ا · و · ي — the حروف العلة.
  • Tell the sound verb (الصحيح) from the weak verb (المعتل) at a glance.
  • Recognise the hollow verb (أجوف — قَالَ)، the defective (ناقص — دَعَا) and the assimilated (مثال — وَصَلَ).
  • Finally understand why قُلْ and كُلْ were marked "memorise as is" back in Ṣarf 4.
Where we are in the ṣarf track: back in Ṣarf 4, two little words sat at the bottom of the amr table with a warning label: قُلْ and كُلْ — "special — memorise as is." Today the mystery finally resolves. Some verbs carry a weak letter in their very root, and weak letters bend, melt or vanish under pressure. This lesson only opens the door — you learn to name what you see. Full mastery of the bending rules belongs to Year 3's rung of the ladder.
الصحيح والمعتل Part One — The Sound and the Weak

الصحيحُ ما سَلِمَتْ حروفُه الأصليةُ من أحرفِ العلة، والمعتلُّ ما كان أحدُ أصولِه حرفَ علة

The sound verb is the one whose root letters are all safe — no weak letter among them: كَتَبَ، دَرَسَ، جَلَسَ. Every verb you conjugated in Year 1 was sound, and that is why the tables always behaved. The weak verb carries ا، و or ي — a حرف علة — somewhere in its root. These three letters are soft: they stretch into long vowels, swap into each other, or drop out entirely. Where the weak letter sits decides which kind of weak verb you are holding.

أنواع المعتل الثلاثة Part Two — The Three Famous Kinds
النوع Where the weakness sits مثال Watch it bend
المِثَالfirst root letterوَصَلَhe arrived → muḍāriʿ يَصِلُ — the و drops clean away
الأَجْوَفmiddle root letterقَالَhe said — root ق و ل: the middle و became alif; in the amr it collapses entirely — قُلْ
النَّاقِصlast root letterدَعَاhe called on — root د ع و: the final و became alif; its maṣdar is الدُّعَاء — a word you already own

Look at the hollow row and Ṣarf 4's mystery dissolves: قُلْ was never random — the weak middle letter of قَالَ simply cannot survive the squeeze of the amr, so it falls out and leaves two letters standing. As for كُلْ from أَكَلَ — that one is strictly a different, smaller quirk: its first letter is a hamzah, not a weak letter, and the hamzah was dropped for smoothness. A cousin of the weak family, not a member — but the same rescue applies: take it as a whole word.

Do not panic — the weak verbs follow rules, not chaos. Every bend, swap and disappearance happens by fixed laws, and the scholars have mapped them all. But those laws come later on the ladder — Year 3, with شذا العرف. Your Year-2 goal is smaller and completely within reach: when you meet a weak verb, name its kind — and stop being surprised by قَالَ، كَانَ، دَعَا، صَلَّى. They are not broken. They are weak — and now you know what that means.
أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

قَالَ رَبِّ اشْرَحْ لِي صَدْرِي

He said: My Lord, expand for me my chest. (Ṭā-Hā 20:25)

The very first word is the hollow verb itself — قَالَ, root ق و ل with its middle و worn down to alif — opening the duʿāʼ of Mūsā عليه السلام before he faced Firʿawn. And notice لِي: the attached ـي of Ṣarf 9 riding on the preposition — "for me".

ادْعُونِي أَسْتَجِبْ لَكُمْ

Call upon Me; I will respond to you. (Ghāfir 40:60)

ادْعُونِي is the defective دَعَا in full dress: the plural amr, then نون الوقاية, then the attached ـي — call on Me. And أَسْتَجِبْ is door اِسْتَفْعَلَ from Ṣarf 11 meeting jazm — I will answer you. Two lessons of ṣarf inside one promise from Allah.

Further reading — optional aids

These are optional supports only. The lesson is complete on-page. The full laws of the weak verb — how each kind conjugates through all fourteen forms, the إعلال substitutions, and the doubled and hamzated verbs beside them — are the meat of Year 3's rung of the ladder, traditionally studied with شذا العرف في فن الصرف (Shadhā al-ʿArf). What you carry out of Year 2 is the map: sound or weak, and if weak — مثال، أجوف or ناقص. That map is exactly what the Year-3 text assumes you already hold.
