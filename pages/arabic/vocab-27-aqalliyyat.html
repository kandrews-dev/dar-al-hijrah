أَقَلِّيَّاتُنَا فِي العَالَم

Our Minorities in the World

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know why Muslims historically left their homelands — ghurbah for knowledge, sustenance, or daʿwah.
  • Learn the vocabulary of Muslim minority life: worship, personal-status law, and education.
  • Understand real challenges Muslim minorities navigate — with wisdom, not grievance.
  • Read a connected passage using the unit's vocabulary.

Many Muslims have left their homelands throughout history — seeking knowledge, seeking a livelihood, or carrying the daʿwah itself. That غُرْبَة (being a stranger abroad) spread Islam across much of the world. In the modern era, many Muslims and their children now live as أَقَلِّيَّات — minorities — in lands where Islam is not the majority religion. This unit teaches the vocabulary of their real, everyday challenges: finding a place to pray, raising children on the دِين, and living by Islamic law in matters of marriage and inheritance.

التَّحَدِّيَات Four Real Challenges

The unit names four areas Muslim minorities commonly navigate:

  1. Worship (العِبَادَة) — sometimes no مُصَلَّى nearby, or workplaces that make it hard to step away for ṣalāh.
  2. Personal-status law (الأَحْوَالُ الشَّخْصِيَّة) — marriage, divorce, and مِيرَاث handled by civil law rather than the Sharīʿah.
  3. Education (التَّعْلِيم) — children struggling to learn Arabic, حِفْظ القرآن, and the ḥadīth of the Prophet ﷺ when the surrounding language and schools are not Arabic.
  4. Social life (الحَيَاة الاجْتِمَاعِيَّة) — societies that make الحِجَاب difficult, and the search for حلال food and proper burial according to Islamic rites.

None of this is cause for despair. Muslim communities abroad have built masājid, weekend Arabic and Qurʾān schools, and halal networks precisely to answer these challenges — patiently, generation after generation, the same way the earliest emigrants for daʿwah built Islam's presence across the world in the first place.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage

اغْتَرَبَ أَبِي طَلَبًا لِلْعِلْمِ، فَأَصْبَحْنَا أَقَلِّيَّةً فِي بَلَدٍ جَدِيدٍ. كَانَ أَصْعَبُ شَيْءٍ إِيجَادَ مُصَلًّى قَرِيبٍ. حَرَصَ أَبِي عَلَى تَعْلِيمِنَا حِفْظَ القُرْآنِ فِي عُطْلَةِ الأُسْبُوعِ. وَبِفَضْلِ اللَّهِ، وَجَدْنَا مَسْجِدًا، وَوَجَدْنَا إِخْوَةً صَالِحِينَ.

Read it aloud twice, then check your understanding.

peek — English translationMy father went abroad seeking knowledge, so we became a minority in a new country. The hardest thing was finding a nearby prayer place. My father was keen to teach us the memorization of the Qurʾān on the weekend. And by Allah's grace, we found a mosque, and we found righteous brothers.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Talk together about your own community: where is your nearest مُصَلَّى or masjid? How does your family make time for حِفْظ القرآن each week? Naming the blessings Allah has already provided is itself a form of gratitude.

تَعْبِير

فَرِغَ قَلْبُهَا

المَعْنَى

صَارَ قَلْبُهَا خَالِيًا مِنْ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا مِنَ القَلَقِ عَلَى وَلَدِهَا — لَمْ تَعُدْ تُفَكِّرُ فِي شَيْءٍ آخَرَ سِوَاهُ.

peek — English

Her heart became empty of everything except anxiety for her child — she could no longer think of anything else at all.

الصُّورَة

القَلْبُ "الفَارِغُ" هُنَا لَيْسَ خَالِيًا مِنَ الشُّعُورِ، بَلْ العَكْسُ: شَيْءٌ وَاحِدٌ فَقَطْ مَلَأَهُ حَتَّى أَخْرَجَ كُلَّ مَا سِوَاهُ — كَإِنَاءٍ فِيهِ شَيْءٌ وَاحِدٌ فَقَطْ فَلَا مَكَانَ لِغَيْرِهِ.

peek — English

The "empty" heart here is not empty of feeling — it is the opposite: one single thing filled it so completely that it pushed everything else out, like a vessel holding only one thing, with no room left for anything more.

فِي القُرْآن

«وَأَصْبَحَ فُؤَادُ أُمِّ مُوسَىٰ فَارِغًا إِن كَادَتْ لَتُبْدِي بِهِ لَوْلَا أَن رَّبَطْنَا عَلَىٰ قَلْبِهَا»

Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ 28:10 · سورة القصص

peek — English

"And the heart of Mūsā's mother became empty [of all else] — she was about to reveal his identity had We not fastened her heart [with patience]." She had just placed her infant son in the river.

عَائِلَةُ الجَذْر — ف ر غ
  • «فَإِذَا فَرَغْتَ فَانصَبْ» — when you finish [one task], turn fully to another (الشرح 94:7)
  • «سَنَفْرُغُ لَكُمْ أَيُّهَ الثَّقَلَانِ» — We will attend fully to you (الرحمن 55:31)
peek — English

One root, one idea: being cleared for a single focus. A heart "emptied" for one worry, a task finished so you turn fully to the next, a Lord who will attend fully to His creation — each time, everything else is set aside for the one thing at hand.

إِذَا لَمْ يَسْتَقِمِ المَعْنَى الحَرْفِيّ، فَهُوَ تَعْبِير — اُنْظُرْ إِلَى الجَذْرِ وَالصُّورَةِ وَالسِّيَاق.
