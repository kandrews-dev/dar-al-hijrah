الأخلاق والصفات

Character & Traits

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the words of good character and their ugly opposites — from صَادِقٌ to مُتَكَبِّرٌ.
  • Describe a person's traits in Arabic — أَخِي صَادِقٌ وَأَمِينٌ.
  • See the ṣarf patterns the trait-words sit on — فَعِيل and the intensive فَعُول.
  • Connect this vocabulary to Islam 101's character unit — the Arabic of حُسْنُ الخُلُقِ.

In Vocab 33 you learned what a good person does — opens the door, spreads the مَائِدَة, honours the ضَيْف. This lesson names what a good person is. On the Islam 101 tab you weighed حُسْنُ الخُلُقِ on the scale of the Day of Judgement and found nothing heavier; now Arabic hands you the words themselves — صَادِق and its ugly shadow كَاذِب, كَرِيم and the closed hand of بَخِيل, مُتَوَاضِع and the swollen مُتَكَبِّر. Ten words, five of them ṣarf patterns you already own, and one hadith that turns the best character into the nearest seat.

Look at the shapes before the meanings. أَمِينٌ، كَرِيمٌ، بَخِيلٌ all sit on فَعِيل — the pattern Arabic reaches for when a quality has settled into a person, not a passing act but what he is. صَبُورٌ goes one step further: فَعُول is the intensive — not merely patient once, but patient again and again, trial after trial, exactly as الصَّدُوق in Vocab 30 was truthful sale after sale. And the last pair are old friends from Ṣarf 11 wearing their doer-shapes: مُتَوَاضِعٌ is the doer of تَوَاضَعَ — the one who lowers himself, and Allah raises him — while مُتَكَبِّرٌ is the doer of تَكَبَّرَ, the one who makes himself great. Notice the table moves in pairs: every noble trait stands beside its ugly opposite, so that learning the word teaches you what to be — and what to flee.

سنة — The nearest seat on the Day of Judgement

«إِنَّ مِنْ أَحَبِّكُمْ إِلَيَّ وَأَقْرَبِكُمْ مِنِّي مَجْلِسًا يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ أَحَاسِنَكُمْ أَخْلَاقًا»

"Indeed among the most beloved of you to me, and the nearest of you to me in seating on the Day of Judgement, are the best of you in character" (al-Tirmidhī; graded ḥasan/ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albānī).

Stop at مَجْلِسًا — this is an اسم مكان from جَلَسَ (he sat), on the pattern مَفْعِل you built in Ṣarf 13: the place of sitting, the seat. The hadith is measuring distance — whose seat is placed nearest the Prophet ﷺ on the Day every human being longs to be near him — and the measure is not wealth, lineage, or eloquence but أَحَاسِنَكُمْ أَخْلَاقًا, the best of you in character. This is the same scale you met in Islam 101's character unit — nothing heavier on the believer's scale than حُسْنُ الخُلُقِ — now paid out as nearness: the trait-words of this lesson's table are the very things that seat a person beside him ﷺ.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

جَدُّ أَحْمَدَ رَجُلٌ صَالِحٌ. هُوَ صَادِقٌ فِي كَلَامِهِ، كَرِيمٌ مَعَ الضُّيُوفِ. وَهُوَ صَبُورٌ فِي الشِّدَّةِ، لَا يَغْضَبُ وَلَا يَشْكُو. وَهُوَ مُتَوَاضِعٌ مَعَ عِلْمِهِ الْكَثِيرِ. النَّاسُ يُحِبُّونَهُ لِحُسْنِ خُلُقِهِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: Ahmad's grandfather is a righteous man. He is truthful in his speech, generous with guests. He is patient in hardship — he does not get angry and does not complain. And he is humble despite his great knowledge. People love him for his good character. Watch the trait-words at work: each one follows هُوَ and simply describes — this is how Arabic paints a person. The second sentence seats Vocab 33 at this lesson's table: كَرِيمٌ مَعَ الضُّيُوفِ — generous with the very guests you learned to honour. And the last sentence hides the crown of the table in an iḍāfah chain: لِحُسْنِ خُلُقِهِ — "for the goodness of his character" — the لام of reason pulling حُسْن into jarr, and خُلُقِهِ leaning on it. The most humble line is the fourth: his knowledge is كَثِير, and precisely there he is مُتَوَاضِع — knowledge that lowers its carrier is knowledge that raised him.

وصف الناس Describing a Person — phrases
  • أَخِي صَادِقٌ وَأَمِينٌ. — My brother is truthful and trustworthy.
  • جَدِّي كَرِيمٌ مَعَ الضُّيُوفِ، صَبُورٌ فِي الشِّدَّةِ. — My grandfather is generous with guests, patient in hardship.
  • الْمُسْلِمُ لَيْسَ كَاذِبًا وَلَا مُتَكَبِّرًا. — The Muslim is not a liar nor arrogant.
  • هُوَ حَسَنُ الْخُلُقِ، وَالنَّاسُ يُحِبُّونَهُ. — He is of good character, and people love him.

A naḥw taste in the third phrase: لَيْسَ flips its predicate into naṣb — كَاذِبًا، مُتَكَبِّرًا — which is why the tanwīn turns to fatḥatān. And the last phrase carries the iḍāfah from the table: a person is not "good-charactered" in Arabic — he is good of character, حَسَنُ الْخُلُقِ.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Tonight, go around the family and let each person name — in Arabic — one trait from the table they want to grow this month: أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَكُونَ صَبُورًا (I want to be patient), أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَكُونَ كَرِيمًا. Write the chosen words where everyone can see them, and then hold one another to them kindly — a gentle reminder in the moment, not a scoreboard. When the month closes, ask together: did the word settle? The فَعِيل pattern taught you the answer to aim for — not a single generous act, but generosity that has become who you are.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

إِنَّ مِنْ أَحَبِّكُمْ إِلَيَّ أَحَاسِنَكُمْ أَخْلَاقًا — صَادِقٌ / كَاذِبٌ · كَرِيمٌ / بَخِيلٌ · مُتَوَاضِعٌ / مُتَكَبِّرٌ

Memorize the short form of the hadith — "indeed among the most beloved of you to me are the best of you in character" (al-Tirmidhī) — and drill the three pairs until each word summons its opposite: truthful and lying, generous and stingy, humble and arrogant.
