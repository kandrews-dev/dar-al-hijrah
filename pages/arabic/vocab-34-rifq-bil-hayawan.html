الرِّفْقُ بِالحَيَوان

Kindness to Animals

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of animal welfare in Islam — from رِفْقٌ to رَحْمَةٌ.
  • Meet جَمَل ← جِمَال, a fifth broken-plural pattern across this book's units.
  • Know four verified hadith on kindness to animals — the thirsty dog, the complaining camel, sharpening the blade, and the ban on using animals as targets.
  • See that الرِّفْق in Islam is not sentiment but a matter of reward and sin.

الشَّرِيعَة does not leave animals outside its concern. It forbids overworking them past their strength, forbids using them for target practice, forbids branding their faces, and even shapes how they are fed, watered, and slaughtered. This lesson reads four hadith that show what رِفْقٌ بِالحَيَوَان actually looked like in the Prophet's ﷺ own dealings and teaching.

جِمَال gives you a fifth broken-plural shape this book has now taught: عُقُول (فُعُول), حَمِير (فَعِيل), حُدُود (فُعُول again — proof one pattern can carry more than one word), أَحْرَار (أَفْعَال), and now جِمَال on فِعَال. Five plurals, five reminders that جَمْعُ التَّكْسِير is learned noun by noun, never guessed from a rule.

١ — الرجل والكلب العطشان Hadith 1 — the man and the thirsty dog

«بَيْنَمَا رَجُلٌ يَمْشِي بِطَرِيقٍ اشْتَدَّ عَلَيْهِ الْعَطَشُ، فَوَجَدَ بِئْرًا فَنَزَلَ فِيهَا فَشَرِبَ، ثُمَّ خَرَجَ فَإِذَا كَلْبٌ يَلْهَثُ يَأْكُلُ الثَّرَى مِنَ الْعَطَشِ، فَقَالَ الرَّجُلُ: لَقَدْ بَلَغَ هَذَا الْكَلْبَ مِنَ الْعَطَشِ مِثْلُ الَّذِي كَانَ بَلَغَ بِي، فَنَزَلَ الْبِئْرَ فَمَلَأَ خُفَّهُ مَاءً، ثُمَّ أَمْسَكَهُ بِفِيهِ حَتَّى رَقِيَ فَسَقَى الْكَلْبَ، فَشَكَرَ اللَّهُ لَهُ فَغَفَرَ لَهُ»

"While a man was walking on a road, he became very thirsty. He found a well, climbed down, and drank. When he came out, he saw a dog panting and eating the wet earth out of thirst. The man said: 'This dog has reached the same thirst that reached me.' So he climbed back down the well, filled his shoe with water, held it in his mouth as he climbed up, and gave the dog water. So Allah thanked him for it and forgave him" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah). In another wording of the same hadith: «فِي كُلِّ ذَاتِ كَبِدٍ رَطْبَةٍ أَجْرٌ» — "there is a reward in [being good to] every living creature" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).

٢ — الجمل الذي شكا إلى النبي ﷺ Hadith 2 — the camel that complained to the Prophet ﷺ

«فَدَخَلَ حَائِطًا لِرَجُلٍ مِنَ الْأَنْصَارِ فَإِذَا جَمَلٌ، فَلَمَّا رَأَى النَّبِيَّ ﷺ حَنَّ وَذَرَفَتْ عَيْنَاهُ، فَأَتَاهُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ فَمَسَحَ ذِفْرَاهُ فَسَكَتَ، فَقَالَ: مَنْ رَبُّ هَذَا الْجَمَلِ؟ فَجَاءَ فَتًى مِنَ الْأَنْصَارِ فَقَالَ: لِي يَا رَسُولَ اللَّهِ. فَقَالَ: أَفَلَا تَتَّقِي اللَّهَ فِي هَذِهِ الْبَهِيمَةِ الَّتِي مَلَّكَكَ اللَّهُ إِيَّاهَا؟ فَإِنَّهُ شَكَا إِلَيَّ أَنَّكَ تُجِيعُهُ وَتُدْئِبُهُ»

"He [the Prophet ﷺ] entered an orchard belonging to a man of the Anṣār, and there was a camel. When it saw the Prophet ﷺ, it moaned and its eyes flowed with tears. The Prophet ﷺ came to it, wiped behind its ear, and it fell silent. He said: 'Who owns this camel?' A young man of the Anṣār came and said: 'It is mine, O Messenger of Allah.' He said: 'Do you not fear Allah regarding this animal that Allah has placed in your possession? It has complained to me that you starve it and overwork it (تُدْئِبُهُ)'" (Sunan Abī Dāwūd, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaʿfar; graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albānī). Even the animal's owner answers for its حَقّ — the same root ح ق ق behind حَقّ that this book's earlier lessons already carried.

٣ — أحسنوا القِتلة، أحسنوا الذَّبح Hadith 3 — the command of Iḥsān

«إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَتَبَ الْإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ، فَإِذَا قَتَلْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا الْقِتْلَةَ، وَإِذَا ذَبَحْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا الذَّبْحَ، وَلْيُحِدَّ أَحَدُكُمْ شَفْرَتَهُ، فَلْيُرِحْ ذَبِيحَتَهُ»

"Indeed Allah has decreed Iḥsān (excellence) in all things. So if you kill, kill well, and if you slaughter, slaughter well; let one of you sharpen his blade, and let him give his slaughtered animal rest [i.e. spare it unnecessary suffering]" (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, from Shaddād ibn Aws). أَحْسِنُوا is door IV, amr plural, from the same root as إِحْسَان — the door that turns a quality into an action toward someone else, exactly as أَكْرَمَ did in Vocab 33. Even the last, unavoidable moment of an animal's life is placed under this command.

٤ — تحريم اتخاذ الحيوان هدفاً Hadith 4 — the ban on using a living creature as a target

«إِنَّ رَسُولَ اللَّهِ ﷺ لَعَنَ مَنِ اتَّخَذَ شَيْئًا فِيهِ الرُّوحُ غَرَضًا»

"Indeed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ cursed whoever takes a living creature as a target [for shooting practice]" (Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim, from ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿUmar — who himself rebuked a group of young men he found doing exactly this to a bird). Notice لَعَنَ from your table: the same verb the sources use for branding an animal's face with fire — cruelty for sport or for marking carries the same weight of blame in both hadith.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

If your family keeps any animal — a pet, a bird, even fish — check together tonight that its food, water, and space are truly sufficient, and say aloud why: «فِي كُلِّ ذَاتِ كَبِدٍ رَطْبَةٍ أَجْرٌ». If you keep none, discuss what you would do differently if you passed a thirsty animal on the road, the way the man in Hadith 1 did.
