الأسرة

The Family

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Name the members of the الأسرة (family / household) in Arabic.
  • Use the possessive endings: ـي (my), ـكَ / ـكِ (your), ـهُ (his).
  • Say هذا أبي · هذه أمي — "this is my father / mother."
  • Ask and answer مَن هذا؟ / مَن هذه؟ — "who is this?"
  • Describe your own family in a few simple sentences.

The family is the first world every child knows — and in Islam, honouring parents and kin (صلة الرحم) is a great act of worship. Learning these words lets a child speak about the people they love most in the language of the Qurʾān.

ياء الملكية "My / Your / His" — Possessive Endings

In Arabic you show "my / your / his" by attaching a small ending to the word — not a separate word. Watch اِبْن (son) take each ending:

  • ـِي (my) → اِبْني = my son
  • ـكَ / ـكِ (your, m/f) → اِبْنُكَ / اِبْنُكِ = your son
  • ـهُ (his) → اِبْنُهُ = his son

The two dearest words come out as أبي (abī — my father) and أمّي (ummī — my mother). Learn those two first.

صلة الرحم — the whole tree has names

English collapses four different relatives into one word, "cousin." Arabic never does — it always tells you the side and the sex: اِبْنُ العَمِّ (father's-brother's son), بِنْتُ العَمِّ (his daughter), اِبْنُ الخالِ (mother's-brother's son), بِنْتُ الخالِ (her daughter). The same precision runs through the aunts (عَمّة vs خالة) and the nephews and nieces (اِبْنُ الأخِ vs اِبْنُ الأختِ). Allah Himself lists them side by side when naming who a believer may marry — بَنَاتِ عَمِّكَ وَبَنَاتِ عَمَّاتِكَ وَبَنَاتِ خَالِكَ وَبَنَاتِ خَالَاتِكَ (al-Aḥzāb 33:50) — so learning the full tree is learning to read the āyah.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Draw a simple family tree together and label each person in Arabic: جدّي · جدّتي · أبي · أمّي · أخي · أختي. Then take turns pointing and asking مَن هذا؟ / مَن هذه؟, answering in full: هذا عمّي · هذه خالتي.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

هذا أبي · هذه أمي · وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

Learn to introduce your parents in Arabic — and remember the āyah beside it: "and to parents, good treatment." Naming them in the language of the Qurʾān is a small way of honouring them.
