طَابَ نَوْمُكُم... طَابَ يَوْمُكُم

May Your Sleep Be Good... May Your Day Be Good

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of sleep and its benefits and dangers — from حِرْمَانٌ to الأَرَق.
  • Learn Arabic's فعل + حرف جر pairs — verbs that lock onto a specific preposition, like يَتَرَاوَحُ بَيْنَ and يُحْرَمُ مِنْ.
  • Meet Āyat al-Kursī's negation of sleep from Allah, and the āyāt that name sleep a kind of death.
  • Know the hadith on washing the hands after waking, and why sleep nullifies wuḍūʼ.

Every night, without fail, you do something the Qurʾān says Allah Himself never does. This lesson's reading — adapted from Majallat al-Fayṣal — walks through what sleep actually is: a mercy the body cannot live without, a nightly return that resembles death, and a habit the Sunnah shapes from the moment you close your eyes to the moment you open them again.

يَشْكُو مِنْhe complains ofفعل + حرف جر — locked to مِنْ, like يُحْرَمُ مِنْ

The last three rows teach one of Arabic's quiet traps: a فِعْل and its حَرْف جَرّ (preposition) are married — you cannot swap the partner. English "complain of," "complain about," and "complain to" are all fine; Arabic يَشْكُو takes only مِنْ. يَتَرَاوَحُ (it ranges/varies) always takes بَيْنَ. يُحْرَمُ (he is deprived) always takes مِنْ. These pairs are not guessed from English — they are simply learned, word by word, the way this table teaches them.

آيات عن النَّوم Sleep in the Qurʾān

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

"Allah — there is no deity except Him, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of existence. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:255 (Āyat al-Kursī) · سورة البقرة

Every created thing that lives needs sleep — even the smallest, curling into itself like a ball. Sleep is negated from Allah alone, because He alone needs nothing. And sleep itself is described as a kind of death: «وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَتَوَفَّاكُم بِاللَّيْلِ» — "It is He who takes your souls by night" (al-Anʿām 6:60). This is why the Prophet's ﷺ waking duʿāʼ from Vocab 26 — الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَحْيَانَا بَعْدَ مَا أَمَاتَنَا وَإِلَيْهِ النُّشُورُ — makes sense word for word: waking up is a small return to life.

وَجَعَلْنَا نَوْمَكُمْ سُبَاتًا * وَجَعَلْنَا اللَّيْلَ لِبَاسًا * وَجَعَلْنَا النَّهَارَ مَعَاشًا

"And We made your sleep [a means for] rest, and We made the night as clothing, and We made the day for livelihood."

Sūrat al-Nabaʼ 78:9-11 · سورة النبأ

Night for rest, day for work — the passage below calls this the body's natural rhythm, and warns what happens to those who reverse it.

حديث — Wash your hand before you know where it slept

«إِذَا اسْتَيْقَظَ أَحَدُكُمْ مِنْ نَوْمِهِ فَلَا يَغْمِسْ يَدَهُ فِي الْإِنَاءِ حَتَّى يَغْسِلَهَا ثَلَاثًا، فَإِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ لَا يَدْرِي أَيْنَ بَاتَتْ يَدُهُ»

"When one of you wakes from his sleep, let him not dip his hand into the vessel until he washes it three times, for he does not know where his hand spent the night" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim, muttafaq ʿalayh — from Abū Hurayrah).

The sleeper loses control of his own will — he cannot choose what his hand touches or what his eyes dream. This is exactly why sleep is a نَاقِضٌ لِلْوُضوء: not because sleeping is impure, but because the one asleep cannot vouch for himself. And the ummah's blessing was placed at the opposite end of the night, in the بُكُور: «اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لِأُمَّتِي فِي بُكُورِهَا» — "O Allah, bless my ummah in its early mornings" (Abū Dāwūd, al-Tirmidhī & Ibn Mājah, from Ṣakhr al-Ghāmidī; graded ḥasan by al-Albānī).

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the body's need for sleep

النَّوْمُ ضَرُورَةٌ لِكُلِّ الْكَائِنَاتِ الْحَيَّةِ، وَحِرْمَانُ الْجَسَدِ مِنْهُ يُسَبِّبُ كَثِيرًا مِنَ الْأَخْطَارِ. وَعَدَدُ سَاعَاتِ النَّوْمِ الَّتِي يَحْتَاجُهَا الْإِنْسَانُ يَتَرَاوَحُ بَيْنَ سَبْعِ وَثَمَانِ سَاعَاتٍ فِي كُلِّ لَيْلَةٍ. وَبَعْضُ النَّاسِ يَشْكُو مِنَ الْأَرَقِ، وَقِلَّةُ النَّوْمِ الْمُسْتَمِرَّةُ تَجْعَلُ الْإِنْسَانَ أَسْرَعَ انْفِعَالًا وَأَضْعَفَ تَآلُفًا مَعَ الْآخَرِينَ. وَلِهَذَا يَنْصَحُ الْأَطِبَّاءُ بِالنَّوْمِ فِي مَكَانٍ هَادِئٍ، وَالْمُحَافَظَةِ عَلَى مَوَاعِيدَ ثَابِتَةٍ لِلنَّوْمِ وَالِاسْتِيقَاظِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: Sleep is a necessity for all living creatures, and depriving the body of it causes many dangers. The number of hours of sleep a person needs ranges between seven and eight hours each night. Some people complain of insomnia, and continuous lack of sleep makes a person quicker to react emotionally and weaker in bonding with others. For this reason, doctors advise sleeping in a quiet place, and keeping fixed times for sleeping and waking. Notice يَتَرَاوَحُ بَيْنَ and يَشْكُو مِنَ doing exactly the job your vocabulary table promised — each verb walking arm in arm with its one fixed preposition.

فعل وحرف جر Verb + Preposition — phrases
  • يُؤَدِّي إِلَى — leads to
  • يُسَاعِدُ عَلَى — helps toward
  • يَبْعُدُ عَنْ — stays away from
  • يُحْرَمُ مِنْ — is deprived of

Four more pairs from the same lesson. Say them together, verb and preposition as one unit — that is how a native speaker's ear expects to hear them, and how yours should learn to expect them too.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

As a family, check your own sleep schedule against the passage's advice — a quiet dark room, fixed sleep and wake times even on weekends, no late-day القَيْلُولَة. Then practise the waking chain together: say the wash-the-hand hadith, the waking duʿāʼ from Vocab 26, and «اللَّهُمَّ بَارِكْ لِأُمَّتِي فِي بُكُورِهَا» — three sunnah acts, one after another, every morning.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ

Memorize this line of Āyat al-Kursī until it is part of you — "Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep" (al-Baqarah 2:255).
