المصدر

The Verbal Noun — the Name of the Action Itself

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know what المصدر is — the name of the action itself, with no tense and no doer.
  • Recognise maṣdars you already own as vocabulary — قِرَاءَة، سِبَاحَة، سَفَر، عَمَل.
  • Know the regular maṣdar patterns of doors II–IV: تَفْعِيل · مُفَاعَلَة / فِعَال · إِفْعَال.
  • See why الإِسْلَام and الإِحْسَان are themselves maṣdars.
Where we are in the ṣarf track: Year 1 gave you the verb and its people — the doer who does it, and last lesson, the done-to it lands on. But underneath doer and done-to there is the action itself: not "he wrote" or "he wrote it", but simply writing. Today's lesson names that action, stripped of time and stripped of doer — the maṣdar, the mother of the whole word-family. Every verb, every doer-noun, every done-to-noun grows out of it.
تعريف المصدر Part One — What the Maṣdar Is

المصدرُ اسمٌ يدلُّ على الحدثِ مُجَرَّدًا عن الزمانِ

The maṣdar is a noun that names the event — the action itself — with no tense and no doer. كَتَبَ is "he wrote" — past tense, with a doer inside it. كِتَابَة is simply writing: nobody did it, and it happened at no time — it is the pure act, held still so you can look at it. Likewise قِرَاءَة is reading itself. For the bare three-letter verb, the maṣdar follows many different patterns and is learned by hearing — the scholars call it سَمَاعِيّ — which sounds like bad news until you notice that you have been collecting them for two years without knowing it:

Every one of these was taught to you as "a noun meaning reading / swimming / travel". Today they change rank: each one is the maṣdar of a verb you conjugated in Year 1 — the name of the act itself, standing free of any doer and any tense. You were never learning random words; you were collecting the mothers of the word-families.

مصادر الأبواب المزيدة Part Two — The Increased Doors Have Regular Maṣdars

مصادرُ الأفعالِ المزيدةِ قِيَاسِيَّةٌ تجري على أوزانٍ معلومةٍ

Here is the relief after the bad news. The bare verb's maṣdar you must hear and memorise — but the increased doors you met in Ṣarf 7 stamp their maṣdars out of a machine. One door, one pattern, no exceptions worth your worry at this stage:

الباب وزن المصدر مثال The example means
فَعَّلَتَفْعِيلعَلَّمَ ← تَعْلِيمhe taught → teaching
فَاعَلَمُفَاعَلَة أو فِعَالجَاهَدَ ← جِهَادhe strove → striving
أَفْعَلَإِفْعَالأَسْلَمَ ← إِسْلَام · أَحْسَنَ ← إِحْسَانhe submitted → submission · he did excellently → excellence
Now the payoff. In the hadith of Jibrīl you memorised the three levels of the dīn: الإِسْلَام والإِيمَان والإِحْسَان. All three are maṣdars — the names of acts, not of persons or things — and two of them, إِسْلَام and إِحْسَان, wear the door-IV pattern إِفْعَال you just learned: أَسْلَمَ — he submitted — gives إِسْلَام, submission itself; أَحْسَنَ — he did excellently — gives إِحْسَان, excellence itself. The very name of your religion is a piece of ṣarf: not "the submitters" and not "he submitted", but the act of submitting, timeless, asked of every doer.
أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

وَقُل رَّبِّ زِدْنِي عِلْمًا

And say: My Lord, increase me in knowledge. (Ṭā-Hā 20:114)

عِلْمًا is a maṣdar — knowledge itself, the pure act of knowing — standing as the object of the verb. And look at زِدْنِي: an amr with the attached ـنِي of Ṣarf 9 riding on its end — increase me. The tracks keep meeting: last lesson's pronoun and this lesson's maṣdar sit side by side in a duʿāʼ the Prophet ﷺ was commanded to make.

وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا

And to parents, excellence. (al-Isrāʼ 17:23)

إِحْسَانًا is the door-IV maṣdar of أَحْسَنَ on the pattern إِفْعَال — and Allah commands the act itself toward parents: not "be one who is excellent someday" but excellence, pure and timeless. Notice too that الْوَالِدَيْنِ wears the dual ending ـَيْنِ — the two parents, both of them, neither left out.
