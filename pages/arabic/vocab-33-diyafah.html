الضيافة والكرم

Hospitality & Generosity

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the hospitality vocabulary — from ضَيْفٌ and مُضِيفٌ to وَلِيمَةٌ and مَائِدَةٌ.
  • Welcome and honour a guest in Arabic with أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا and تَفَضَّلْ.
  • Know the hadith — whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his guest — letter by letter.
  • Taste the adab of the host and the adab of the guest, and carry both home.

Back in Vocab 5 you set the table: الطعام, الشراب, بِسْمِ الله before and الْحَمْدُ لِلَّه after. This lesson seats someone at it. The ضَيْف who knocks at the door, the مُضِيف who rises to receive him, the دَعْوَة that brought him, the مَائِدَة spread between them — Arabic has a whole vocabulary for the moment a stranger becomes an honoured guest. And above the doorway of every Muslim home hangs one hadith that makes this welcome a matter of īmān itself.

Ten words, and half the ṣarf track sits at this table with you. مُضِيف is the doer of door IV أَضَافَ — a hollow root like بَاعَ from Vocab 30, its weak middle letter in hiding. أَكْرَمَ is door IV built on كَرُمَ: "he was generous" becomes "he honoured someone" — the door that turns a quality into a gift. تَفَضَّلْ is a door-V command, تَفَعَّلَ wearing its amr form. And the oldest word here you have known since your first lesson: أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا — the Arabs' full sentence folded into two words: you have come to family (أَهْل), and your feet have found easy ground (سَهْل).

سنة — The guest and the Last Day

«مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ ضَيْفَهُ»

"Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his guest" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).

Stop at فَلْيُكْرِمْ — three tracks meet in one word. The naḥw track gave you the لام of command; the ṣarf track gave you door IV يُكْرِمُ; and the لام puts it in the jussive, dropping the final ḍammah to a sukūn: فَـ + لْـ + يُكْرِمْ — "then let him honour." And weigh what the hadith hangs this command on: not etiquette, not culture — يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ. Honouring the guest is placed on the scale of īmān itself: the one who truly believes in Allah and the Day he will stand before Him opens his door, spreads his table, and gives his guest the best of what he has.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the new words walking together

وَصَلَ الضُّيُوفُ إِلَى بَيْتِ أَحْمَدَ. قَالَتِ الْأُسْرَةُ: أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا بِكُمْ! وَأَعَدَّتِ الْأُمُّ الْمَائِدَةَ، وَقَالَ الْأَبُ: تَفَضَّلُوا. فَأَكَلُوا جَمِيعًا وَحَمِدُوا اللَّهَ. وَخَرَجَ الضُّيُوفُ وَهُمْ يَدْعُونَ لِأَهْلِ الْبَيْتِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: The guests arrived at Ahmad's house. The family said: welcome! The mother prepared the dining table, and the father said: please, go ahead. So they ate together and praised Allah. And the guests left making duʿāʼ for the people of the house. Notice تَفَضَّلُوا in the third sentence — the same door-V amr from your table, now addressed to a group: the وَاو of the plural on the end. And the last sentence closes the circle of the دَعْوَة: the guests answered a "calling" to the table, and they leave with a "calling" to Allah — يَدْعُونَ, the same root دَعَا doing both jobs, exactly as the table promised. The adab of the guest is the mirror of the adab of the host: he is honoured, and he answers with duʿāʼ.

عبارات الضيافة Welcoming the Guest — phrases
  • أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا، تَفَضَّلْ بِالدُّخُولِ. — Welcome — please, come in.
  • دَعَانِي صَدِيقِي إِلَى وَلِيمَةٍ. — My friend invited me to a feast.
  • أَكْرَمَ الْمُضِيفُ ضُيُوفَهُ. — The host honoured his guests.
  • جَزَاكُمُ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا عَلَى الضِّيَافَةِ. — May Allah reward you with good for the hospitality.

Four phrases carry the whole visit: the welcome at the door, the invitation that started it, the honouring at the table, and the guest's thanks on leaving. The second one hides دَعَا at work — دَعَانِي, "he called me" — and the last is the guest's duʿāʼ from the passage, ready-made.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

At the next guest visit, hand the welcome to the children: let them stand at the door and give the أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا, and let them carry the dishes to the مَائِدَة and give the تَفَضَّلُوا. Before the doorbell rings, recall the hadith together — «مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ ضَيْفَهُ» — so the children learn that the welcome they are giving is not politeness but īmān at work.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ ضَيْفَهُ

Memorize the hadith of the guest until it opens the door before you do — "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his guest" (al-Bukhārī & Muslim).
