التصريف الكامل للماضي

The Complete Past Conjugation — All Fourteen Forms

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Complete the past verb كَتَبَ to all fourteen forms — التصريف الكامل.
  • Know the three groups: الغائب (the absent — he/she/they) · المخاطب (the addressed — you) · المتكلم (the speaker — I/we).
  • Recognise the dual endings ـَا / ـْتُمَا and the feminine plurals كَتَبْنَ / كَتَبْتُنَّ.
  • Recite the full table from memory, the way the institutes do.
Where we are in the ṣarf track: Lesson 2 gave you seven core forms of the past verb and promised the rest. The institutes do not stop at seven — they memorise all fourteen. Today we complete the table: the same table recited aloud, row by row, in every madrasah that walks the ladder from البناء والأساس (al-Bināʾ wa-l-Asās) onward. Nothing new in method — only the remaining seats at the table.
الغائب والمخاطب والمتكلم Part One — The Three Groups

تصريفُ الماضي الكاملُ أربعَ عشْرةَ صيغةً: ستٌّ للغائب، وستٌّ للمخاطَب، واثنتان للمتكلِّم

The complete conjugation of the past verb has fourteen forms: six for الغائب, six for المخاطب, and two for المتكلم. The scholars of ṣarf order every verb table by who is doing the action — not "first, second, third person" as English books say, but three plain descriptions of where the doer stands relative to the speech.

تصريف «كَتَبَ» الكامل Part Two — The Full Fourteen

Here is the whole table. The seven forms of Lesson 2 are all inside it — what is new are the duals (two people, ending ـَا · ـَتَا · ـْتُمَا), the feminine plurals (ending ـْنَ or ـْتُنَّ), and the "you all" form كَتَبْتُمْ (ending ـْتُمْ). Read each row aloud: pronoun, then verb.

Count them: six, six, two — fourteen. Notice the order inside الغائب and المخاطب: masculine singular, dual, plural — then feminine singular, dual, plural. المتكلم has no masculine/feminine split: أَنَا and نَحْنُ serve everyone. Swap the root and every ending stays put: دَرَسَ، دَرَسَا، دَرَسُوا… all fourteen, same mould.

فرِّق بين المتشابهات Part Three — Two Look-Alikes to Tell Apart

1 · كَتَبْنَ vs كَتَبْنَا. One alif apart, worlds apart in meaning. كَتَبْنَ ends in the نَ called نون النسوة (the nūn of the women): they — feminine plural — wrote. كَتَبْنَا ends in نَا: we wrote. In a past verb whose last root letter carries a sukūn, the stretched نَا is the doer "we" — نَزَّلْنَا، ظَلَمْنَا. The same نَا after a fatḥah is the object "us" (خَلَقَنَا — He created us), and on a noun it is "our" (رَبَّنَا — our Lord). And with no alif at all — كَتَبْنَ — the women are the doers.

2 · One word, two seats. The المخاطب dual كَتَبْتُمَا serves both masculine and feminine — "you two wrote" whether you address two men or two women. It is the only form in the table that occupies two rows at once, which is why the fourteen forms are carried by only thirteen distinct words.

أمثلة قرآنية Examples from the Qur'an

وَقَالُوا سَمِعْنَا وَأَطَعْنَا

And they say: We hear and we obey. (al-Baqarah 2:285)

From the closing of آمَنَ الرَّسُولُ — the last two ayahs of al-Baqarah. Three table-forms in one short phrase: قَالُوا is the "they" form (ـُوا of الغائب), while سَمِعْنَا and أَطَعْنَا both carry the ـْنَا of "we" — the believers speaking as one. The believers said (they-form) words in the we-form.

رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنْفُسَنَا

Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves. (al-Aʿrāf 7:23)

The duʿāʼ of آدم and حواء after the slip in the Garden. ظَلَمْنَا — we wronged — is the we-form of this lesson's table. And the ayah opens with قَالَا, "they two said": the very dual ending ـَا you just learned, because the two of them made this duʿāʼ together.
