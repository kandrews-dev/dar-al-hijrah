الحج والعمرة

Ḥajj & ʿUmrah

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the vocabulary of الحَجّ and العُمْرَة in Arabic — the rites by their real names.
  • Recognise the ṣarf hiding inside the rites' names — إِحْرَام، طَوَاف، سَعْي.
  • Reconnect with Reading 3's journey to Makkah and the Fiqh tab's rites lesson.
  • Recite the talbiyah with understanding — every word parsed and owned.

In Reading 3 you flew with Ahmad's family to مَكَّة, watched them circle the الكَعْبَة and drink from زَمْزَم — but the story named the actions in passing. This lesson hands you the rites as vocabulary: the إِحْرَام that opens the sacred state, the طَوَاف around the House, the سَعْي between the two hills, the day of عَرَفَة, and the cry of the تَلْبِيَة that fills the road to Makkah. Three of these words are ṣarf lessons wearing iḥrām — and one of them is a sentence the naḥw track has already parsed.

Look at what the ṣarf track has done to this table. إِحْرَام is the door-IV maṣdar إِفْعَال — from أَحْرَمَ, "he entered the sacred state," exactly as إِسْلَام comes from أَسْلَمَ. طَوَاف is the maṣdar of the hollow طَافَ you read in Reading 3 — طَافُوا حَوْلَ الكَعْبَةِ named the action; طَوَاف names the rite. سَعْي is the barest maṣdar there is — from سَعَى, to walk with purpose. And the two doers: حَاجّ is فَاعِل of the doubled حَجَّ — the two jīms of حَاجِج collapse under one shaddah — while مُعْتَمِر is the doer of door اِفْتَعَلَ, from اِعْتَمَرَ, built exactly like اِشْتَرَى gave مُشْتَرٍ. Name the rite, and you have named its grammar.

التلبية The Talbiyah — the pilgrim's cry

سنة — The talbiyah of the Prophet ﷺ

«لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ، لَبَّيْكَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ لَبَّيْكَ، إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ وَالنِّعْمَةَ لَكَ وَالْمُلْكَ، لَا شَرِيكَ لَكَ»

"Here I am, O Allah, here I am. Here I am — You have no partner — here I am. Indeed all praise and favour are Yours, and the dominion. You have no partner." (al-Bukhārī & Muslim)

And a gift for the naḥw student: إِنَّ الْحَمْدَ is the very construction the naḥw track parsed — إِنَّ enters the sentence and makes its ism manṣūb: الْحَمْدَ with a fatḥah, and وَالنِّعْمَةَ joined to it, likewise manṣūb. Millions of pilgrims recite a naḥw lesson on the road to Makkah without knowing it — you will recite it knowing exactly why that fatḥah is there.

النَّصّ Mini-Passage — the family performs ʿumrah

وَصَلَتْ أُسْرَةُ أَحْمَدَ إِلَى الْمِيقَاتِ فَأَحْرَمُوا لِلْعُمْرَةِ. وَفِي الطَّرِيقِ إِلَى مَكَّةَ رَفَعُوا أَصْوَاتَهُمْ بِالتَّلْبِيَةِ. طَافُوا حَوْلَ الْكَعْبَةِ سَبْعَةَ أَشْوَاطٍ. ثُمَّ سَعَوْا بَيْنَ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةِ. وَقَصَّرُوا مِنْ شُعُورِهِمْ، فَتَمَّتِ الْعُمْرَةُ بِفَضْلِ اللَّهِ.

Read it aloud twice before peeking: Ahmad's family arrived at the mīqāt and entered iḥrām for ʿumrah. On the road to Makkah they raised their voices with the talbiyah. They circled the Kaʿbah seven circuits. Then they walked the saʿy between al-Ṣafā and al-Marwah. And they shortened their hair — and the ʿumrah was complete, by the favour of Allah. This is Reading 3's journey, finished: that story ended at the ṭawāf and the Zamzam; this passage carries the family through every rite to the trimming of the hair. Notice سَعَوْا — the weak-last سَعَى in the plural, its alif giving way to the wāw exactly as دَعَوْا did at the end of Reading 3 — and قَصَّرُوا, door II doing its work one last time.

الفقه — where the rulings live

This lesson gives you the words; the rulings live on the Fiqh tab. The order of the rites, the three types of ḥajj, the four pillars, and the day-by-day timeline from the 8th of ذُو الحِجَّة to the farewell ṭawāf are all taught in Fiqh Unit 3 · Lesson 4 — مناسك الحج والعمرة. Learn the vocabulary here, then read the fiqh there — and the rites will meet you twice: once as language, once as law.

في الطريق إلى مكة On the Road to Makkah — phrases
  • ذَهَبَ الْحَاجُّ إِلَى مَكَّةَ فِي ذِي الْحِجَّةِ. — The pilgrim went to Makkah in Dhū al-Ḥijjah.
  • أَحْرَمَ الْمُعْتَمِرُ مِنَ الْمِيقَاتِ. — The ʿumrah-performer entered iḥrām at the mīqāt.
  • وَقَفَ الْحُجَّاجُ بِعَرَفَةَ يَدْعُونَ اللَّهَ. — The pilgrims stood at ʿArafah calling upon Allah.
  • طَافَ الْمُعْتَمِرُ وَسَعَى ثُمَّ قَصَّرَ. — The ʿumrah-performer made ṭawāf and saʿy, then trimmed his hair.

Four phrases walk the whole road: setting out, entering iḥrām, the standing, and the rites of the House. The third shows the diptote from the table at work — بِعَرَفَةَ takes a fatḥah where an ordinary noun would take kasrah, because عَرَفَةُ never accepts tanwīn or kasrah.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Memorise the talbiyah together, out loud, the way pilgrims say it — one person leads with لَبَّيْكَ اللَّهُمَّ لَبَّيْكَ and the family completes it, phrase by phrase, until all four lines rise without the page. Then, if anyone at the table has made ḥajj or ʿumrah, let them describe one rite in simple Arabic — even a single sentence like طُفْتُ حَوْلَ الْكَعْبَةِ or سَعَيْتُ بَيْنَ الصَّفَا وَالْمَرْوَةِ — so the words of this lesson meet a journey that really happened.
