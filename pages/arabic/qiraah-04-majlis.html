Qirāʼah Track · Reading 4 · Year 3

مَجْلِسُ الْعِلْمِ

The Gathering of Knowledge

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Read the track's first mixed-tense narrative — past narration with a present-tense scene alive inside it.
  • Follow an embedded dialogue — a hand raised, a question asked, an answer given, all inside the story.
  • Reuse the masjid professions (Vocab 31) and seeking-knowledge (Vocab 36) vocabulary in connected Arabic.
  • Meet the study-circle hadith from Muslim and weigh its verb يَتَدَارَسُونَ on the ṣarf scale.

أين نحن — Where we are

Reading 1 lived in the present, Reading 2 gave the present a voice, and Reading 3 told a finished journey in the past. Reading 4 does what real Arabic books do all the time: it mixes the tenses. The narration walks in the past — اغْتَسَلَ، مَشَى، جَلَسَ — then one line steps into the present to paint the scene around you — الْأُسْتَاذُ يَقْرَأُ وَالطُّلَّابُ يَسْتَمِعُونَ — and then a dialogue opens inside the story, exactly as in the books of the scholars. Every word of it is harvested from lessons you already own: the masjid professions of Vocab 31, the seeker's toolkit of Vocab 36, and the doors of Ṣarf 11.

النَّصّ The Passage — read it aloud

يَوْمُ الْجُمُعَةِ أَحَبُّ الْأَيَّامِ إِلَى أَحْمَدَ.

فِي الصَّبَاحِ اغْتَسَلَ وَلَبِسَ أَجْمَلَ ثِيَابِهِ ثُمَّ مَشَى إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ.

اِسْتَمَعَ إِلَى خُطْبَةِ الْجُمُعَةِ ثُمَّ صَلَّى مَعَ النَّاسِ خَلْفَ الْإِمَامِ.

وَبَعْدَ صَلَاةِ الْمَغْرِبِ رَجَعَ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ، لِأَنَّ فِيهِ حَلْقَةَ عِلْمٍ كُلَّ جُمُعَةٍ.

جَلَسَ أَحْمَدُ فِي الْحَلْقَةِ وَفَتَحَ دَفْتَرَ الْمُلَاحَظَاتِ.

الْأُسْتَاذُ يَقْرَأُ وَالطُّلَّابُ يَسْتَمِعُونَ وَيَكْتُبُونَ.

قَرَأَ الْأُسْتَاذُ حَدِيثًا فِيهِ كَلِمَةُ «يَتَدَارَسُونَهُ»، فَرَفَعَ أَحْمَدُ يَدَهُ.

أَحْمَد: عِنْدِي سُؤَالٌ يَا أُسْتَاذُ، مَا أَصْلُ هَذِهِ الْكَلِمَةِ؟

الْأُسْتَاذ: أَصْلُهَا «دَرَسَ»، وَمَعْنَاهَا: يَدْرُسُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ مَعًا.

أَحْمَد: فَهِمْتُ الْآنَ، جَزَاكَ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا.

الْأُسْتَاذ: أَحْسَنْتَ يَا أَحْمَدُ، السُّؤَالُ مِفْتَاحُ الْعِلْمِ.

كَتَبَ أَحْمَدُ الْجَوَابَ فِي دَفْتَرِهِ، وَهُوَ يُرَاجِعُ دُرُوسَهُ فِي بَيْتِهِ كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ.

رَجَعَ إِلَى بَيْتِهِ فَرِحًا وَقَالَ: أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَجْلِسَ فِي الْحَلْقَةِ كُلَّ جُمُعَةٍ.

Two small treasures inside the passage. First, watch the tense change on purpose: the story walks in the past — جَلَسَ، فَتَحَ — then one line steps into the present, الْأُسْتَاذُ يَقْرَأُ وَالطُّلَّابُ يَسْتَمِعُونَ, so that you are no longer being told about the circle — you are sitting in it. Arabic narration does this constantly. Second, اغْتَسَلَ is not غَسَلَ: the door-VIII dress turns "he washed" into "he washed himself" — the full-body ghusl that is a beloved sunnah of Friday.

Translation — التَّرْجَمَة · read the Arabic first!

Friday is the most beloved of days to Ahmad.

In the morning he performed ghusl and put on the most beautiful of his clothes, then walked to the masjid.

He listened to the Friday khuṭbah, then prayed with the people behind the imām.

And after the Maghrib prayer he returned to the masjid, because in it there is a study-circle of knowledge every Friday.

Ahmad sat in the circle and opened the notebook.

The teacher reads, and the students listen and write.

The teacher read a hadith in which is the word «يَتَدَارَسُونَهُ», so Ahmad raised his hand.

Ahmad: I have a question, teacher — what is the root of this word?

The teacher: Its root is «دَرَسَ», and its meaning is: they study the Book of Allah together.

Ahmad: I understand now — may Allah reward you with good.

The teacher: Well done, Ahmad — the question is the key of knowledge.

Ahmad wrote the answer in his notebook — and he revises his lessons at his home every night.

He returned to his home joyful, and said: I want to sit in the circle every Friday.

نظرة اللغوي Read It Again like a Linguist — five words under the lens
الكلمة Root · Door Why it looks the way it does
اغْتَسَلَغ س ل · door VIII اِفْتَعَلَthe door's تاء tucked inside the root, exactly as Ṣarf 11 promised — washing done to yourself, the full-body ghusl. The bare fatḥah ending is the past-tense "he": the ماضي needs no prefix at all
يَسْتَمِعُونَس م ع · door VIII اِفْتَعَلَthe present of اِسْتَمَعَ — more than سَمِعَ (he heard): leaning in, listening with attention. The ـُونَ ending is the present-tense "they," and its standing نون announces rafʿ — one of the five verbs the naḥw track taught you to watch
حَلْقَةَ عِلْمٍح ل ق · nouna حَلْقَة is a ring — students seated in a ring around the teacher. Why the fatḥah? It is the noun of لِأَنَّ, and أَنَّ gives its noun naṣb even when that noun comes after its khabar (فِيهِ). Then عِلْمٍ hangs on it in jarr — an iḍāfah: a circle of knowledge
يُرَاجِعُر ج ع · door III فَاعَلَرَاجَعَ — "he revised" — is literally kept returning to the lesson; its maṣdar is the مُرَاجَعَة of Vocab 36. The ḍammah on the يـ prefix is the fingerprint of doors II–IV in the present, and the final ḍammah is rafʿ — no naṣb or jazm tool stands before it
فَرِحًاف ر ح · ḥālthe same root Reading 3 doubled into فَرِحَ فَرَحًا شَدِيدًا — here it stands once, as a حال: "he returned joyful." The fatḥah-tanwīn is naṣb, describing his state at the very moment of returning — a naḥw-track guest inside a qirāʼah story

And the rest of the passage is already yours: الْإِمَام and خُطْبَةُ الْجُمُعَةِ from the sacred-professions lesson (Vocab 31), حَلْقَة and سُؤَال and دَفْتَرُ الْمُلَاحَظَاتِ from the seeking-knowledge lesson (Vocab 36), the أُسْتَاذ from the university lesson (Vocab 26), and every past-tense ending straight out of the ṣarf track's tables. One Friday, stitched together out of lessons you have already finished.

حديث الحلقة The Hadith the Teacher Read

«مَا اجْتَمَعَ قَوْمٌ فِي بَيْتٍ مِنْ بُيُوتِ اللَّهِ يَتْلُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ وَيَتَدَارَسُونَهُ بَيْنَهُمْ إِلَّا نَزَلَتْ عَلَيْهِمُ السَّكِينَةُ وَغَشِيَتْهُمُ الرَّحْمَةُ وَحَفَّتْهُمُ الْمَلَائِكَةُ وَذَكَرَهُمُ اللَّهُ فِيمَنْ عِنْدَهُ»

"No people gather in a house among the houses of Allah, reciting the Book of Allah and studying it among themselves, except that tranquility descends upon them, mercy covers them, the angels surround them, and Allah mentions them to those who are with Him." (Muslim)

the very hadith Ahmad heard in the حَلْقَة — and the home of the word he asked about

Now weigh the word Ahmad asked about. يَتَدَارَسُونَ — root دَرَسَ (he studied), lifted through door VI تَفَاعَلَ — the door of mutual action, the very door Ṣarf 11 taught you with تَعَاوَنَ, cooperating with one another. دَرَسَ is one student studying alone; تَدَارَسَ is the whole circle studying together — each one reading to the other, each one checking the other. And count what answers it: four gifts, four past-tense verbs — نَزَلَتْ (tranquility descends), غَشِيَتْ (mercy covers), حَفَّتْ (the angels surround), ذَكَرَ (Allah mentions them to those who are with Him). The ustādh's answer to Ahmad — يَدْرُسُونَ كِتَابَ اللَّهِ مَعًا, they study the Book of Allah together — is door VI unpacked into a sentence.

تمرين أسري — Family Practice

Hold a ḥalqah at your own table. One person is the أُسْتَاذ and reads the passage aloud; the others are the طُلَّاب. Each student must raise a hand once and ask, exactly as Ahmad did — عِنْدِي سُؤَالٌ يَا أُسْتَاذُ، مَا أَصْلُ هَذِهِ الْكَلِمَةِ؟ — pointing at any word in the passage, and the teacher answers with أَصْلُهَا… and the three root letters. Then close the book and retell Ahmad's Friday in order, keeping every verb in its right tense: the narration in the past, the scene inside the circle in the present.
