الأَلْوَان

Colors

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Know the main colors from أَحْمَرُ (red) to أَسْوَدُ (black).
  • Ask what color something is with مَا لَوْنُ...؟.
  • Describe the things around you with color adjectives.
  • Know that a color follows the noun it describes, just like a نعت.

You can already name the things around you and count them. This lesson lets you describe them: the red car, the green book, the white milk. One question — مَا لَوْنُ...؟ (what is the color of…?) — and a handful of adjectives open up a whole layer of everyday speech.

الأَلْوَان The Colors
Swatch الكلمة English
لَوْنٌcolor
أَحْمَرُred
أَخْضَرُgreen
أَزْرَقُblue
أَصْفَرُyellow
أَبْيَضُwhite
أَسْوَدُblack
بُنِّيٌّbrown

Colors are a نعت

A color adjective comes after the noun it describes and agrees with it — exactly like the نعت from Naḥw L12: قَلَمٌ أَزْرَقُ (a blue pen), السَّيَّارَةُ الحَمْرَاءُ (the red car).

The six main colors have feminine forms on the pattern فَعْلَاء: حَمْرَاءُ، خَضْرَاءُ، زَرْقَاءُ، صَفْرَاءُ، بَيْضَاءُ، سَوْدَاءُ. So a pen is أَزْرَقُ, but a car is زَرْقَاءُ.

ما لون...؟ What color is…?
  • مَا لَوْنُ السَّيَّارَةِ؟ — What color is the car?
  • السَّيَّارَةُ حَمْرَاءُ. — The car is red.
  • القَلَمُ أَزْرَقُ. — The pen is blue.
  • الحَلِيبُ أَبْيَضُ. — Milk is white.

These answers are the مبتدأ–خبر sentences from the naḥw track — a noun to start, and the color as its news.

تَمْرِين أسري — Family Practice

Walk around the house pointing at things and ask each other “مَا لَوْنُ هَذَا؟” — the cup, the door, the rug, the sky out the window. Answering about real objects fixes the colors far faster than reciting the list.

تَعْبِير

أَبْيَضُ / أَسْوَدُ

المَعْنَى

الأَلْوَانُ لَا تَصِفُ فَقَطِ الأَشْيَاءَ الخَارِجِيَّةَ، بَلْ تُسْتَعْمَلُ لِوَصْفِ حَالِ النَّفْسِ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ — فَ"بَيَاضُ الوَجْهِ" يَعْنِي الفَرَحَ وَالكَرَامَةَ، وَ"سَوَادُ الوَجْهِ" يَعْنِي الخِزْيَ وَالحُزْنَ.

peek — English

Colors do not only describe external objects — they are used to describe the state of the soul on the Day of Judgement. A face "turning white" means joy and honour; a face "turning black" means disgrace and grief.

الصُّورَة

يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ يُشْرِقُ وَجْهُ المُؤْمِنِ بِالنُّورِ وَالسُّرُورِ فَيُوصَفُ بِالبَيَاضِ، وَيُظْلِمُ وَجْهُ الكَافِرِ بِالحَسْرَةِ وَالخِزْيِ فَيُوصَفُ بِالسَّوَادِ — تَمَامًا كَمَا يُشْرِقُ وَجْهُ الفَرْحَانِ وَيَعْبَسُ وَجْهُ الحَزِينِ فِي الدُّنْيَا.

peek — English

On the Day of Judgement, the believer's face lights up with joy and is described as "white," while the disbeliever's face darkens with regret and is described as "black" — just as a happy face brightens and a grieving face darkens in this world.

فِي القُرْآن

«يَوْمَ تَبْيَضُّ وُجُوهٌ وَتَسْوَدُّ وُجُوهٌ»

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:106 · سورة آل عمران

peek — English

"On the Day [some] faces will turn white and [some] faces will turn black" — the very colors you just learned, describing the two outcomes of the Hereafter.

عَائِلَةُ الجَذْر — ب ي ض / س و د
  • «حَتَّىٰ يَتَبَيَّنَ لَكُمُ الْخَيْطُ الْأَبْيَضُ مِنَ الْخَيْطِ الْأَسْوَدِ مِنَ الْفَجْرِ» — the literal white and black threads of dawn, marking when the fast begins (البقرة 2:187)
peek — English

The very same two colors used literally to mark the start of a fasting day (2:187) are used metaphorically to mark the two eternal outcomes on the Last Day (3:106) — one root pair, two very different mornings.

إِذَا لَمْ يَسْتَقِمِ المَعْنَى الحَرْفِيّ، فَهُوَ تَعْبِير — اُنْظُرْ إِلَى الجَذْرِ وَالصُّورَةِ وَالسِّيَاق. If the literal meaning doesn't stand, it's a تعبير — look at the root, the image, and the context.

Daily Commitment — التزام يومي

أَحْمَرُ · أَخْضَرُ · أَزْرَقُ · أَصْفَرُ · أَبْيَضُ · أَسْوَدُ

Run the six main colors out loud until they come without thinking — then once more with their feminine forms.
