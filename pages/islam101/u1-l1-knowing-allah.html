مَعْرِفَةُ اللَّه

The First Principle: Knowing Your Lord

Year 1+

After this lesson you will be able to · بَعْدَ هَذَا الدَّرْسِ سَتَتَمَكَّنُ مِنْ

  • Answer the question "Who is your Lord?" correctly
  • Explain how we come to know Allah through His signs and His creation
  • State the purpose for which Allah created us
  • Name the fourteen types of worship the matn lists, with proof for each
  • Explain the ḥadīth of Muʿādh on the right of Allah upon His servants
مَنْ رَبُّكَ؟"Who Is Your Lord?"

The first question, and the first principle: Who is your Lord? (مَن رَبُّك؟). The answer:

رَبِّيَ اللهُ"My Lord is Allah"

رَبِّيَ اللهُ الَّذِي رَبَّانِي وَرَبَّى جَمِيعَ العَالَمِينَ بِنِعَمِهِ

"My Lord is Allah, who nurtured me and all the worlds with His blessings."

al-Rabb (الرَّبّ) — the Lord — is the One who creates, owns, provides, and nurtures everything that exists. He is the only one I worship; I have no other lord besides Him.

الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"All praise is for Allah, Lord of all the worlds."

Sūrat al-Fātiḥah 1:2 · سُورَةُ الفَاتِحَة

نَعْرِفُ اللَّهَ بِآيَاتِهِ وَمَخْلُوقَاتِهWe Know Him Through His Signs and Creation

The matn asks: if it is said to you, "How did you come to know your Lord?" — say: "By His signs (آيَاتُهُ) and His creation (مَخْلُوقَاتُهُ)." Of His signs: the night and the day, the sun and the moon. Of His creation: the seven heavens and all within them, the seven earths and all within them, and everything between them.

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ اللَّيْلُ وَالنَّهَارُ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ ۚ لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ الَّذِي خَلَقَهُنَّ إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

"Among His signs are the night and the day, the sun and the moon. Do not prostrate to the sun or the moon, but prostrate to Allah who created them, if it is truly Him you worship."

Sūrat Fuṣṣilat 41:37 · سورة فصلت

إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَىٰ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ ۖ يُغْشِي اللَّيْلَ النَّهَارَ يَطْلُبُهُ حَثِيثًا وَالشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ وَالنُّجُومَ مُسَخَّرَاتٍ بِأَمْرِهِ ۗ أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ ۗ تَبَارَكَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"Indeed your Lord is Allah, who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then rose above the Throne. He covers the day with the night, each pursuing the other urgently. And the sun, the moon, and the stars are subjected by His command. Unquestionably, His is the creation and the command. Blessed is Allah, Lord of all the worlds."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:54 · سورة الأعراف

The lesson is clear: the signs are not to be worshipped — they are pointers to the One who made them. The great commentator Ibn Kathīr explains this verse: the One who alone creates and commands is the One who alone deserves to be worshipped — Lordship (rubūbiyyah) demands worship (ʿibādah) in return. We look at the creation and our hearts say: "Glory to the One who created all this!" This is built into our very nature (the fiṭrah).

لِمَاذَا خَلَقَنَا اللَّه؟Why Did Allah Create Us?

Allah did not create us without purpose, nor to leave us to play. He created us for one great reason:

وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ

"I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me."

Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt 51:56 · سورة الذاريات

(You may recognise this verse — it is the foundation of tawhid in the Aqeedah tab.) Our purpose is worship — and worship means far more than just prayer. It is everything Allah loves: prayer, charity, kindness, honesty, gratitude, and turning the whole heart to Him.

حَقُّ اللَّهِ عَلَى العِبَادThe Right of Allah upon His Servants

The Prophet ﷺ once asked his companion Muʿādh ibn Jabal a question that goes to the heart of this whole principle:

يَا مُعَاذُ، هَلْ تَدْرِي مَا حَقُّ اللَّهِ عَلَى العِبَادِ، وَمَا حَقُّ العِبَادِ عَلَى اللَّهِ؟ ... حَقُّ اللَّهِ عَلَى العِبَادِ أَنْ يَعْبُدُوهُ وَلَا يُشْرِكُوا بِهِ شَيْئًا

"O Muʿādh, do you know the right of Allah upon His servants, and the right of the servants upon Allah? ... The right of Allah upon His servants is that they worship Him and not associate anything with Him."

al-Bukhārī & Muslim

Everything Allah asks of us comes back to this one right — that every act of worship, of every kind, be for Him and Him alone.

العِبَادَةُ لِلَّهِ وَحْدَهWorship Belongs to Allah Alone — the Full List from the Matn

Since Allah alone is our Lord, worship — in every one of its forms — belongs to Him alone. Directing any of it to other than Him is the great sin of shirk. al-Uṣūl al-Thalāthah does not leave this vague — it names fourteen specific acts of worship, each with its own proof. This is the complete list, as Ibn ʿUthaymīn explains it in his sharḥ of the matn:

العِبَادَة Worship type Proof
الدُّعَاءُSupplication (duʿāʾ)"Call upon Me, I will answer you" (40:60); "Duʿāʾ is worship itself" — ḥadīth, Tirmidhī
الخَوْفُFear (khawf)"Do not fear them, but fear Me, if you are believers" (3:175)
الرَّجَاءُHope (rajāʾ)"Whoever hopes to meet his Lord, let him do righteous deeds and associate none in the worship of his Lord" (18:110)
التَّوَكُّلُReliance (tawakkul)"Upon Allah rely, if you are believers" (5:23); "Whoever relies upon Allah — He is sufficient for him" (65:3)
الرَّغْبَةُ وَالرَّهْبَةُ وَالخُشُوعُYearning, dread & humility"They used to hasten to good deeds, calling upon Us in yearning and in dread, and were humbly submissive to Us" (21:90)
الخَشْيَةُAwe / reverent fear (khashyah)"So do not fear them, but fear Me" (2:150)
الإِنَابَةُTurning back to Him (inābah)"And turn back to your Lord, and submit to Him" (39:54)
الِاسْتِعَانَةُSeeking help (istiʿānah)"It is You we worship and You we ask for help" (1:5); "If you ask for help, ask Allah" — ḥadīth, Tirmidhī
الِاسْتِعَاذَةُSeeking refuge (istiʿādhah)"Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of daybreak" (113:1); "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind" (114:1)
الِاسْتِغَاثَةُSeeking rescue (istighāthah)"When you sought rescue from your Lord and He answered you" (8:9)
الذَّبْحُSacrifice (dhabḥ)"Say: my prayer, my sacrifice, my life, my death, are all for Allah, Lord of all the worlds" (6:162); "Allah's curse is on whoever sacrifices for other than Allah" — ḥadīth, Muslim
النَّذْرُThe vow (nadhr)"They fulfil their vows" (76:7) — a description of the righteous

وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا

"And the places of worship are for Allah, so do not call upon anyone alongside Allah."

Sūrat al-Jinn 72:18 · سُورَةُ الجِنّ

Each one of these fourteen — and everything else Allah loves and is pleased with — is worship (عِبَادَة). Directing any single one of them to a grave, a saint, a star, or anything other than Allah is shirk — even if the person still prays and fasts. This is precisely why the matn opens its next principle with tawḥīd before anything else.

مِنَ المَتْنِ · الحَنِيفِيَّةُ وَالتَّوْحِيد The Ḥanīfiyyah and Tawḥīd — in the Author’s Words

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

اعْلَمْ أَرْشَدَكَ اللهُ لِطَاعَتِهِ أَنَّ الحَنِيفِيَّةَ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللهَ مُخْلِصًا لَهُ الدِّينَ، وَبِذَلِكَ أَمَرَ اللهُ جَمِيعَ النَّاسِ وَخَلَقَهُمْ لَهَا. وَأَعْظَمُ مَا أَمَرَ اللهُ بِهِ التَّوْحِيدُ، وَهُوَ: إِفْرَادُ اللهِ بِالعِبَادَةِ. وَأَعْظَمُ مَا نَهَى عَنْهُ الشِّرْكُ، وَهُوَ: دَعْوَةُ غَيْرِهِ مَعَهُ.

Two superlatives in one breath: the greatest thing commanded and the greatest thing forbidden. Everything else in the religion is arranged beneath those two.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn ʿUthaymīn glosses the words before explaining the sense. الرُّشْد is uprightness upon the path of truth. And الحَنِيفِيَّة is the way that inclines away from shirk, built upon sincerity to Allah — so the word contains both a turning-away and a turning-toward.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

What is العِبَادَة? He gives it twice — once in its general sense, once in its detail.

المَعْنَى العَامّ · the general sense

التَّذَلُّلُ لِلَّهِ مَحَبَّةً وَتَعْظِيمًا، بِفِعْلِ أَوَامِرِهِ وَاجْتِنَابِ نَوَاهِيهِ، عَلَى الوَجْهِ الَّذِي جَاءَتْ بِهِ شَرَائِعُهُ

“Humbling yourself to Allah out of love and reverence, by doing what He commands and avoiding what He forbids — in the manner His revealed laws brought.”

Read the three parts of that. Love and reverence together: love without awe becomes presumption, awe without love becomes dread. Doing and avoiding: worship is not only acts performed but things left. And in the manner His laws brought: sincerity alone is not enough — the form must be his ﷺ. Those are the two conditions of an accepted deed again, inside a single definition.

Then the detailed sense, from Ibn Taymiyyah: worship is اسْمٌ جَامِعٌ لِكُلِّ مَا يُحِبُّهُ اللهُ وَيَرْضَاهُ — “a comprehensive name for everything Allah loves and is pleased with, of statements and of deeds, outward and inward” — and he lists them side by side: fear, awe, reliance, prayer, zakāh, fasting, and the rest of the laws of Islam. Notice that fear and reliance, which happen inside a person where no one can see, stand in the same list as prayer and zakāh.

And الإِخْلَاص, he says, is purification — that a person intend by his worship the Face of Allah and the reaching of His abode of honour, worshipping none besides Him: neither a near angel nor a sent prophet.

عِبَادَةٌ كَوْنِيَّةٌ وَعِبَادَةٌ شَرْعِيَّة Two Kinds of Servitude — Universal and Legislated

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here Ibn ʿUthaymīn makes a distinction that resolves a question students often raise: if everything in creation is Allah's servant, in what sense is the disbeliever not a worshipper?

عِبَادَةٌ كَوْنِيَّةعِبَادَةٌ شَرْعِيَّة
What it isSubmission to Allah’s universal decreeSubmission to Allah’s legislated command
Who it coversAll of creation — no one escapes it: believer and disbeliever, righteous and corruptOnly those who obey Allah and follow what the messengers brought
Its āyahإِنْ كُلُّ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ إِلَّا آتِي الرَّحْمَٰنِ عَبْدًا (Maryam 19:93)وَعِبَادُ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الَّذِينَ يَمْشُونَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ هَوْنًا (al-Furqān 25:63)
Is one praised for it?No — it is not of his own doingYes

One refinement he adds, and it is a kind one: although no one is praised for the first, a person may be praised for what comes out of it — gratitude when at ease, and patience under trial. The two responses from the opening lesson reappear here, now as the only part of universal servitude a man can earn credit for.

مَا هُوَ التَّوْحِيد؟ What Tawḥīd Actually Means

الشَّرْح · the explanation

In the language, التَّوْحِيد is the verbal noun of to make a thing one — and Ibn ʿUthaymīn points out that this cannot be achieved except by نَفْيٍ وَإِثْبَاتٍ: negating the ruling from everything other than the one singled out, and affirming it for him alone.

Which is why a person's tawḥīd is not complete until he says لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللهُ — denying divinity to all besides Allah, and affirming it for Him alone. The kalimah is not a statement with a negative preamble; the negation is half the creed. (You met the same two movements in Ibrāhīm's own words in the ʿAqīdah unit on al-Qawāʿid al-Arbaʿ.)

In the technical sense, the author defines it in four words: إِفْرَادُ اللهِ بِالعِبَادَةِ — singling Allah out in worship. Ibn ʿUthaymīn expands the list of who is excluded, and the list is deliberately exhausting: not a sent prophet, not a near angel, not a chief, not a king, not anyone of creation. Rather you single Him out alone in worship مَحَبَّةً وَتَعْظِيمًا وَرَغْبَةً وَرَهْبَةً — in love, in reverence, in longing and in awe.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he sets out the three types of tawḥīd, each with a one-line definition worth memorising as it stands:

Typeإِفْرَادُ اللهِ بِـ…
تَوْحِيدُ الرُّبُوبِيَّةcreating, owning and governing — الخَلْقِ وَالمُلْكِ وَالتَّدْبِيرِ
تَوْحِيدُ الأُلُوهِيَّةworship: that a person take no one alongside Allah whom he worships and draws near to as he worships Allah and draws near to Him
تَوْحِيدُ الأَسْمَاءِ وَالصِّفَاتwhat He named and described Himself with in His Book or on the tongue of His Messenger ﷺ — affirming what He affirmed and negating what He negated, مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍ وَلَا تَعْطِيلٍ، وَمِنْ غَيْرِ تَكْيِيفٍ وَلَا تَمْثِيلٍ

Those four words at the end of the third — no distorting, no denying, no specifying a how, no likening — are the four forbidden doors you study on the ʿAqīdah track. Here they arrive as part of a definition.

Proofs for the first that Ibn ʿUthaymīn brings: اللَّهُ خَالِقُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ (al-Zumar 39:62) · هَلْ مِنْ خَالِقٍ غَيْرُ اللَّهِ (Fāṭir 35:3) · تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ الْمُلْكُ (al-Mulk 67:1) · and أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ (al-Aʿrāf 7:54).

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Which of the three does the author mean here? Ibn ʿUthaymīn answers plainly: tawḥīd al-ulūhiyyah — the one the mushrikīn went astray in, whom the Prophet ﷺ fought; and most of what the messengers had to treat in their peoples was this type. وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ — “and We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying] worship Allah” (al-Naḥl 16:36).

And then the consequence, stated without softening: whoever falls short in this tawḥīd is a mushrik and a disbeliever even if he affirms tawḥīd of Lordship and of the names and attributes. Suppose a man made a complete and correct affirmation of both of those — and then went to a grave and worshipped its occupant, or vowed an offering to draw near to him. The two affirmations do not rescue the act.

If that sounds familiar, it should: it is the first of the four foundations you studied on the ʿAqīdah track, arriving here from the other direction.
