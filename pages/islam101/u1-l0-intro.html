لِمَاذَا نَطْلُبُ العِلْم؟

Why We Seek Knowledge — The Four Matters

Year 1+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • Explain why every Muslim must seek knowledge
  • Recite Sūrat al-ʿAṣr and explain its four matters
  • Name the three great questions of the grave
  • Understand how this unit (the Three Principles) maps the whole religion
  • Recite the author’s own wording of the four matters, and say why بِالأَدِلَّةِ matters.
  • State the الثَّلَاث مَسَائِل and give a سَمْعِي and a عَقْلِي proof for the first of them.
  • Give Ibn ʿUthaymīn’s rational argument that Allah created us, in its four steps.
  • Explain why Allah forbidding a thing is itself proof He is not pleased with it.
العِلْمُ قَبْلَ القَوْلِ وَالعَمَلKnowledge Comes Before Speech & Action

Welcome to Islam 101 — the foundation that the whole school is built upon. Before a person can worship Allah correctly, they must first know: know their Lord, know their religion, and know their Prophet ﷺ. That is why the scholars say: العِلْمُ قَبْلَ القَوْلِ وَالعَمَلِ — "Knowledge comes before speech and action." Allah Himself ordered the Prophet ﷺ to first know, then act:

فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَاسْتَغْفِرْ لِذَنبِكَ

"So know that there is no god but Allah, and seek forgiveness for your sin."

Sūrat Muhammad 47:19 · سورة محمد

Notice the order: "know" (فَاعْلَمْ) comes before the action of seeking forgiveness. This entire unit is built on a small but mighty book — al-Uṣūl al-Thalātha (The Three Fundamental Principles).

سُورَةُ العَصْرA Whole Roadmap in One Short Sūrah

Imām al-Shāfiʿī said: "If Allah had revealed no proof to creation except this sūrah, it would have sufficed them." In just three verses, Sūrat al-ʿAṣr lays out the path of success and the path of loss:

🌙 Sūrat al-ʿAṣr — سُورَةُ العَصْر

وَالْعَصْرِ ۝ إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ ۝ إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ

"By time! Indeed, mankind is in loss — except those who believe, do righteous deeds, advise one another to truth, and advise one another to patience." — Sūrat al-ʿAṣr 103

المَسَائِلُ الأَرْبَعThe Four Matters We Must Learn

From this sūrah, the scholars draw four matters every Muslim must learn — and they map exactly onto the three verses:

1

العِلْم

Knowledge — knowing Allah, His Prophet ﷺ, and the religion of Islam with its evidences. (From "those who believe.")

2

العَمَل بِه

Acting upon it — knowledge is meant to be lived, not just memorised. (From "and do righteous deeds.")

3

الدَّعْوَة إِلَيْه

Calling others to it — sharing the good with kindness and wisdom. (From "advise one another to truth.")

4

الصَّبْر عَلَى الأَذَى فِيه

Patience upon the harm that comes along the way. (From "advise one another to patience.")

This is the order of a complete Muslim life: learn → live it → share it → stay patient. Our school follows the same order — we begin, always, with knowledge.

الأَسْئِلَةُ الثَّلَاثَةThe Three Great Questions

The "Three Fundamental Principles" are the answers to three questions every single person will be asked in the grave (you met these in Aqeedah — the questioning of the grave). Knowing them well, here, is preparing your answer for then. The rest of this unit takes each one in turn:

مَن رَبُّك؟

Who is your Lord?

→ L.1: Knowing Allah

مَا دِينُك؟

What is your religion?

→ L.2: Knowing Islam

مَن نَبِيُّك؟

Who is your Prophet?

→ L.3: Knowing the Prophet ﷺ

مِنَ المَتْنِ · المَسَائِلُ الأَرْبَع The Four Matters — in the Author’s Words

You have just met the four matters as the scholars draw them from Sūrat al-ʿAṣr. This is how Imām مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ عَبْدِ الوَهَّاب himself opens ثَلَاثَةُ الأُصُول — the small book this whole unit is built on. Read his Arabic first; the explanation follows.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

اعْلَمْ رَحِمَكَ اللهُ أَنَّهُ يَجِبُ عَلَيْنَا تَعَلُّمُ أَرْبَعِ مَسَائِلَ: الأُولَى: العِلْمُ، وَهُوَ مَعْرِفَةُ اللهِ، وَمَعْرِفَةُ نَبِيِّهِ، وَمَعْرِفَةُ دِينِ الإِسْلَامِ بِالأَدِلَّةِ. الثَّانِيَةُ: العَمَلُ بِهِ. الثَّالِثَةُ: الدَّعْوَةُ إِلَيْهِ. الرَّابِعَةُ: الصَّبْرُ عَلَى الأَذَى فِيهِ.

Notice that the first matter is not simply “knowledge” but knowledge of three things — Allah, His Prophet ﷺ, and the religion of Islam — and all three بِالأَدِلَّةِ, with their evidences. That phrase is the whole method of this school.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn ʿUthaymīn draws out a precision about al-Shāfiʿī's famous remark that is easy to overstate. When he said that this sūrah alone would have sufficed creation, he did not mean it suffices for the whole of the Sharīʿah. He meant it suffices in urging people to hold fast to Allah's religion — because a person of sense who hears it must set about rescuing himself from loss, and the only way out it names is these four: īmān, righteous action, mutual counsel to the truth, and mutual counsel to patience.

And the heading the unit opened with — بَابٌ: العِلْمُ قَبْلَ القَوْلِ وَالعَمَلِ — is not a general saying of “the scholars.” It is al-Bukhārī's own chapter title in his Ṣaḥīḥ, and the proof he cited for it is the āyah of Sūrat Muḥammad you read above: Allah said know, then seek forgiveness.

That is a textual proof. Ibn ʿUthaymīn adds a rational one: no word or deed is sound and accepted unless it agrees with the Sharīʿah — and a person cannot know that his deed agrees with the Sharīʿah except by knowledge. He then qualifies it fairly: some things a person knows by his fiṭrah, such as that Allah is One God, and these need no great labour to learn. It is the detailed particular rulings that require study and sustained effort.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim maps the same four onto the struggle against one's own soul — جِهَادُ النَّفْسِ — in four ascending levels:

  1. To strive with it to learn the guidance and the religion of truth, without which it has no success in this life or the next.
  2. To strive with it to act on that, once it knows.
  3. To strive with it to call to it, and teach those who do not know.
  4. To strive with it to bear patiently the hardships of calling to Allah and the harm of people — bearing all of it for Allah.

And whoever completes these four, he says, becomes one of the الرَّبَّانِيِّين — the truly learned and devoted. Which is to say: the four matters are not four topics on a syllabus. They are four stages of a life.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn ʿUthaymīn also defines each of the four attributes the sūrah names, and the second definition quietly contains the two conditions of every accepted deed:

In the sūrahWhat it takes in
الإِيمَانAll that draws a person near to Allah of sound belief and beneficial knowledge
العَمَلُ الصَّالِحEvery word or deed that draws near to Allah — its doer being sincere to Allah, and following Muḥammad ﷺ
التَّوَاصِي بِالحَقّCounselling one another to do good, urging it and encouraging it
التَّوَاصِي بِالصَّبْرCounselling one another to patience in doing what Allah commands, leaving what He forbids, and bearing what He decrees

And he notes what the last two amount to together: mutual counsel to truth and to patience comprise الأَمْرَ بِالمَعْرُوفِ وَالنَّهْيَ عَنِ المُنْكَرِ — enjoining good and forbidding evil — by which the ummah stands, is set right, is aided, and attains its honour.

كُنْتُمْ خَيْرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخْرِجَتْ لِلنَّاسِ تَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَتَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ

"You are the best nation brought forth for mankind: you enjoin what is right, forbid what is wrong, and believe in Allah."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:110 · سورة آل عمران

الشَّرْح · the explanation

On the fourth matter — patience upon the harm — Ibn ʿUthaymīn notes that the one who calls must himself be patient, and that patience over Allah's decrees covers two things: what servants have no hand in at all, and what Allah brings about by the hands of some servants, in the form of injury and aggression. The second is the harder, and the Prophet ﷺ met it like this, with his own blood on his face:

اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِقَوْمِي فَإِنَّهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"O Allah, forgive my people, for they do not know."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ · al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الثَّلَاثُ مَسَائِل The Three Matters Every Muslim Must Learn

After the four matters, the author sets down three more — and these are the spine of the book. They were not on this page before. Each one, as Ibn ʿUthaymīn explains it, carries two kinds of proof: a سَمْعِي proof, from revelation, and a عَقْلِي proof, from reason. Watch how both are brought for each.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

اعْلَمْ رَحِمَكَ اللهُ أَنَّهُ يَجِبُ عَلَى كُلِّ مُسْلِمٍ وَمُسْلِمَةٍ تَعَلُّمُ هَذِهِ الثَّلَاثِ المَسَائِلِ وَالعَمَلُ بِهِنَّ: الأُولَى: أَنَّ اللهَ خَلَقَنَا وَرَزَقَنَا وَلَمْ يَتْرُكْنَا هَمَلًا، بَلْ أَرْسَلَ إِلَيْنَا رَسُولًا، فَمَنْ أَطَاعَهُ دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ، وَمَنْ عَصَاهُ دَخَلَ النَّارَ.

إِنَّا أَرْسَلْنَا إِلَيْكُمْ رَسُولًا شَاهِدًا عَلَيْكُمْ كَمَا أَرْسَلْنَا إِلَىٰ فِرْعَوْنَ رَسُولًا فَعَصَىٰ فِرْعَوْنُ الرَّسُولَ فَأَخَذْنَاهُ أَخْذًا وَبِيلًا

"Indeed We have sent to you a Messenger as a witness over you, just as We sent a messenger to Firʿawn. But Firʿawn disobeyed the messenger, so We seized him with a ruinous seizing."

Sūrat al-Muzzammil 73:15–16 · سورة المزمل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَنَّ اللهَ خَلَقَنَا — the textual proof. Ibn ʿUthaymīn lists many; one is enough to see the pattern: وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنْسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ — “and I did not create jinn and mankind except to worship Me” (al-Dhāriyāt 51:56); and اللَّهُ خَالِقُ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ — “Allah is the Creator of all things” (al-Zumar 39:62).

And the rational proof — which is worth reading slowly, because it is a complete argument in four steps:

  1. A man did not create himself. Before he existed he was nothing — and what is nothing brings nothing into being.
  2. His father and mother did not create him, nor did anyone else of creation.
  3. He did not come about by chance, without any originator — because every originated thing must have something that originated it.
  4. And chance is ruled out by the order itself. What exists by chance has no order in its very origin — so how could it be ordered through its continuance and its development? The marvellous arrangement and harmony of these creatures forbids it absolutely.

What remains is that the Creator is Allah alone: أَلَا لَهُ الْخَلْقُ وَالْأَمْرُ — “unquestionably, His is the creation and the command” (al-Aʿrāf 7:54).

Ibn ʿUthaymīn then observes something historical: no one of creation is known to have denied Allah's Lordship except out of sheer obstinacy — as happened with Firʿawn. The argument is not hard to see. It is hard to accept.

أَمْ خُلِقُوا مِنْ غَيْرِ شَيْءٍ أَمْ هُمُ الْخَالِقُونَ أَمْ خَلَقُوا السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ بَلْ لَا يُوقِنُونَ

"Or were they created from nothing? Or are they the creators? Or did they create the heavens and the earth? No — they are not certain."

Sūrat al-Ṭūr 52:35–36 · سورة الطور

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ attaches a moment to that āyah. جُبَيْرُ بْنُ مُطْعِمٍ heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reciting Sūrat al-Ṭūr, and he reached these verses. He was a mushrik at the time. He said: “My heart nearly flew — and that was the first time faith settled in my heart.” (al-Bukhārī, Kitāb al-Tafsīr.) Three rhetorical questions, and a man's resistance gave way.

وَرَزَقَنَا — the proofs. From revelation: إِنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الرَّزَّاقُ ذُو الْقُوَّةِ الْمَتِينُ (al-Dhāriyāt 51:58) and قُلْ مَنْ يَرْزُقُكُمْ مِنَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ قُلِ اللَّهُ (Sabaʾ 34:24). From the Sunnah: the ḥadīth of the child in the womb, to whom an angel is sent and commanded with four words — his provision, his lifespan, his deeds, and whether he is wretched or blessed. And rationally: we live only by food and drink, and food and drink are Allah's creation, not ours.

أَفَرَأَيْتُمُ الْمَاءَ الَّذِي تَشْرَبُونَ أَأَنْتُمْ أَنْزَلْتُمُوهُ مِنَ الْمُزْنِ أَمْ نَحْنُ الْمُنْزِلُونَ

"Have you considered the water you drink? Is it you who sent it down from the clouds, or are We the One who sends it down?"

Sūrat al-Wāqiʿah 56:68–69 · سورة الواقعة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَلَمْ يَتْرُكْنَا هَمَلًا — “and He did not leave us without purpose.” The word هَمَلًا is used of camels left to wander loose with no herdsman. From revelation: أَفَحَسِبْتُمْ أَنَّمَا خَلَقْنَاكُمْ عَبَثًا وَأَنَّكُمْ إِلَيْنَا لَا تُرْجَعُونَ (al-Muʾminūn 23:115) and أَيَحْسَبُ الْإِنْسَانُ أَنْ يُتْرَكَ سُدًى (al-Qiyāmah 75:36).

And rationally: that this human race should exist, live, enjoy itself as the cattle enjoy themselves, then die with no resurrection and no reckoning — this does not befit the wisdom of Allah; it would be pure futility.

Which is why He sent messengers. وَإِنْ مِنْ أُمَّةٍ إِلَّا خَلَا فِيهَا نَذِيرٌ — “there is no nation but that a warner passed among it” (Fāṭir 35:24) — and the purpose is stated outright: لِئَلَّا يَكُونَ لِلنَّاسِ عَلَى اللَّهِ حُجَّةٌ بَعْدَ الرُّسُلِ, “so that mankind would have no argument against Allah after the messengers.” (al-Nisāʾ 4:165.)

كُلُّ أُمَّتِي يَدْخُلُونَ الجَنَّةَ إِلَّا مَنْ أَبَى. قَالُوا: وَمَنْ يَأْبَى يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ؟ قَالَ: مَنْ أَطَاعَنِي دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ، وَمَنْ عَصَانِي فَقَدْ أَبَى

"All of my ummah will enter Paradise except whoever refuses." They said: "And who refuses, O Messenger of Allah?" He said: "Whoever obeys me enters Paradise, and whoever disobeys me has refused."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī

That ḥadīth is the first matter in the Prophet's ﷺ own words — and note how he recasts disobedience. It is not merely a failure. It is a refusal of an offer already made.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الثَّانِيَةُ: أَنَّ اللهَ لَا يَرْضَى أَنْ يُشْرَكَ مَعَهُ أَحَدٌ فِي عِبَادَتِهِ، لَا مَلَكٌ وَلَا نَبِيٌّ.

وَأَنَّ الْمَسَاجِدَ لِلَّهِ فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا

"And the places of prostration belong to Allah, so do not call upon anyone alongside Allah."

Sūrat al-Jinn 72:18 · سورة الجن

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn ʿUthaymīn draws a general rule out of this one that is worth keeping: Allah forbids nothing except that He is not pleased with it. So the prohibition in this āyah is itself the evidence of His displeasure.

And He says so plainly: وَلَا يَرْضَىٰ لِعِبَادِهِ الْكُفْرَ — “and He is not pleased with disbelief for His servants” (al-Zumar 39:7). The gravity is stated in two more places: إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَنْ يُشْرَكَ بِهِ (al-Nisāʾ 4:48), and إِنَّهُ مَنْ يُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ فَقَدْ حَرَّمَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ الْجَنَّةَ — “whoever associates others with Allah, Allah has forbidden him Paradise” (al-Māʾidah 5:72).

And then he turns it on the student, which is the point of the matter: if Allah is not pleased with kufr and shirk, then the believer must not be pleased with them either — because the believer's pleasure and anger follow Allah's pleasure and anger. He is angered by what angers Allah and pleased by what pleases Him.

مَنْ لَقِيَ اللهَ لَا يُشْرِكُ بِهِ شَيْئًا دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ، وَمَنْ لَقِيَهُ يُشْرِكُ بِهِ شَيْئًا دَخَلَ النَّارَ

"Whoever meets Allah associating nothing with Him enters Paradise; and whoever meets Him associating anything with Him enters the Fire."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ · al-Bukhārī and Muslim

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الثَّالِثَةُ: أَنَّ مَنْ أَطَاعَ الرَّسُولَ وَوَحَّدَ اللهَ لَا يَجُوزُ لَهُ مُوَالَاةُ مَنْ حَادَّ اللهَ وَرَسُولَهُ، وَلَوْ كَانَ أَقْرَبَ قَرِيبٍ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This third matter is الوَلَاءُ وَالبَرَاء — allegiance and disavowal — which you will study in its own right on the ʿAqīdah track. Note here only what the author's wording does: وَلَوْ كَانَ أَقْرَبَ قَرِيبٍ, “even if he were the closest of relatives.” He closes the one exit a person would reach for first.

And see how the three matters are ordered. The first establishes why you owe Allah anything at all — He created you, provided for you, and did not abandon you. The second establishes that the debt is His alone. The third establishes what follows in your loyalties once the first two are settled. Nothing in the book is out of order.
