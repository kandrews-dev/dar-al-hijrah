النَّاقِضُ السَّابِع · حُكْمُ السَّاحِر

The Seventh Nullifier, Part 2 — The Ruling on the Magician

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the two grounds on which Ibn Bāz holds the magician is killed, and the comparison he reasons from.
  • State the تَفْصِيل he grants on al-Shāfiʿī’s view — and what remains the أَصْل.
  • Say whether the magician is killed as a ḥadd or as a disbeliever, and what follows for washing, shrouding, the prayer and burial.
  • State when the magician’s repentance is refused and when it is accepted — and the one fact the whole answer turns on.
  • Give the grading of «حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْف» and name at least three of the scholars who gave it.
  • Say what does carry the ruling, how its chain is graded, and the one precision about al-Bukhārī’s wording.
  • State the rule on acting without the ruler’s permission, and what Ibn Bāz nevertheless said of Jundub.
  • Refute قَلْبُ الأَعْيَان — and give the argument the compiler rejects on his own side.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٦٦–١٧٩
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 166–179, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Part 1 established what siḥr is. These folios rule on the man. And two things here are not what the clause’s reputation suggests.

One: Ibn Bāz states the ruling at its strongest and then, pressed, says «فِيهِ التَّفْصِيل» — there is a distinction in it — and grants that al-Shāfiʿī’s position is «الصَّحِيحُ مِنْ حَيْثُ المَعْنَى إِذَا عُرِف».
Two: the one ḥadīth everybody quotes for the ḥadd of the magician is graded weak on these very folios — by seven named scholars, Ibn Bāz among them. What actually carries the ruling is something else, and the page shows which.

قَتْلُ السَّاحِر The Ruling, and Its Two Grounds

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«The correct position regarding the magician is that he is a disbeliever, and that he is killed — because he does not practise magic except by way of worship of the devils, worship of the jinn and drawing near to them by what they love; and when he has drawn near to them by what they love, they teach him things by which he bewitches people and terrifies people and bewitches their eyes. So for this his killing became obligatory, for two reasons:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الأَوَّل«his disbelief, and his misguidance, and his apostasy — if he was a Muslim»
الثَّانِي«because he is of the people of corruption, of those who spread corruption in the earth; and whoever is of the people of corruption, it is obligatory to relieve the people of his evil»

And the second ground he argues by comparison, from the verse on killing a soul — «except for a soul, or for corruption in the earth»:

«And for this the killing of the highway robbers became obligatory when the interest requires their killing — and Allah gave a choice in that, so if the one in authority sees that killing them is more beneficial than crucifying them and amputating, he kills them, to protect the people from their evil. And the magician is above that and worse than that — because he practises worship of other than Allah; he practises shirk… so for this the majority of the people of knowledge held that he is killed.»

مَن قَتَلَ نَفْسًا بِغَيْرِ نَفْسٍ أَوْ فَسَادٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ

…whoever kills a soul, other than for a soul or for corruption in the earth…

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:32

Keep the structure of the two grounds, because they do different work. The first is about what he has become — an apostate. The second is about what he does to others — and it is a public-order ground, reasoned from the muḥārib, and therefore belongs to the one in authority by its very form. Neither ground is available to a private person; the second one explicitly names the ruler making a judgement of interest.

فِيهِ التَّفْصِيل Then He Is Pressed, and Distinguishes

Having stated it that strongly, he states al-Shāfiʿī’s position himself — and does not dismiss it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«As for his disbelief and his misguidance — what is known with the people of knowledge is that he is a disbeliever, for the noble verse. But al-Shāfiʿī — may Allah have mercy on him — and a group said: his magic is examined. So if his magic is of what is practised with potions and fumigation, without drawing near to the jinn and without worship of the jinn — then this one is disciplined, or killed, upon the disagreement in that. But if his magic is of what is practised with worship of the jinn and drawing near to them — then this one is a disbeliever; and this meaning ought not to be a place of disagreement.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — On al-Shāfiʿī’s view it emerges that magic is not disbelief in itself, but by what attaches to the deed?

Ibn Bāz — «According to the deed of the one doing it.»

Q — And that is the correct position, Shaykh?

Ibn Bāz — «And this is the correct position from the standpoint of the meaning — if it is known, if this is established. Otherwise, the default is that he is a disbeliever; this is the default… so the default concerning him is that he is killed and that he is a disbeliever — unless it is known by established means, by the route of the trustworthy, that he is not of those who practise worship of the jinn.»

Q — So magic is not disbelief in itself, but for what follows from it?

Ibn Bāz — «فِيهِ التَّفْصِيل — there is a distinction in it: it may be disbelief in itself, and it may be [disbelief] for what follows from it of corruption. So if his magic is by drawing near to the devils and the jinn and worshipping them and slaughtering for them and vowing to them — it becomes greater disbelief, and for this Allah named it disbelief… But if he described it and it became clear that it is something that harms people yet is not a drawing near to other than Allah, and there is in it no disbelief in Allah nor any claim that necessitates disbelief — such as his holding that they dispose of the universe apart from Allah, or that they change things by their knowledge — that is the greater disbelief, from the standpoint of their shirk in the lordship.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

his craftthe rulingthe burden
Worship of the jinn — slaughter, vows, drawing nearكُفْرٌ أَكْبَر, and «this meaning ought not to be a place of disagreement»—
Potions, fumigation, harm — no worship of the jinn, no claim that they dispose of the universeDisciplined, or killed upon the disagreement — not declared a disbelieverOn whoever claims it. «The default is that he is a disbeliever… unless it is known by established means, by the route of the trustworthy»
Holding that they dispose of the universe, or change things by their own knowledgeالكُفْرُ الأَكْبَر — and he names the reason: shirk in the RUBŪBIYYAH—

So the clause has a distinction in it and the burden of proof sits on the exception. That is a different thing from a flat rule, and a different thing again from a loose one. Ibn Bāz holds the default where the verse put it, and will move off it — but only on establishment «by the route of the trustworthy», which is not a thing a bystander can supply.

يُقْتَلُ كُفْرًا Killed As a Disbeliever, Not As a Ḥadd

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Is the magician killed as a ḥadd, or as a disbeliever?

Ibn Bāz — «He is killed as a disbeliever — because he does not practise it except by shirk and worship of the jinn.»

Q — And the ruling on praying over the magician, and burying him in the graveyards of the Muslims, after he is killed?

Ibn Bāz — «If he is killed, he is not prayed over, and not buried in the graveyards of the Muslims; he is buried in the graveyards of the disbelievers, and he is not washed and not shrouded.»

That answer is where the abstraction stops being abstract. The classification is not rhetorical — it governs the washing, the shroud, the janāzah and the ground. Which is also why the distinction above matters so much: a man disciplined for a harmful craft is buried as a Muslim, and a man killed for shirk is not.

تَوْبَةُ السَّاحِر His Repentance — and the One Fact It Turns On

This is the most carefully balanced answer on the folios, and it separates two things that are constantly run together: whether the worldly sentence is lifted, and whether Allah accepts him.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And it has been established from ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه that he wrote to his governors — I mean his commanders — with the killing of the magicians, to prevent their corruption in the earth and their harming of the Muslims and their bringing of harm upon the people. So whenever they are known, it is obligatory upon the guardians of the Muslims’ affairs to kill them — even if they say: we have repented; because they are not believed.»

«But if they are truthful in the repentance, that benefits them with Allah — for the generality of His saying…»

وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَقْبَلُ التَّوْبَةَ عَنْ عِبَادِهِ وَيَعْفُو عَنِ السَّيِّئَاتِ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا تَفْعَلُونَ

And it is He who accepts repentance from His servants and pardons misdeeds, and He knows what you do.

سُورَةُ الشُّورَى · 42:25

الشَّرْح · the explanation

With it he cites the Prophet’s ﷺ words «التَّوْبَةُ تَهْدِمُ مَا كَانَ قَبْلَهَا» — repentance demolishes what came before it. The sharḥ gives no grading for that wording at this place, so none is supplied here.

«As for one who comes to the guardians of affairs without having been seized, informing of his repentance, and that he had done such-and-such in time past and has repented to Allah, and good has appeared from him — this one’s repentance is accepted; because he came by choice, seeking good, announcing his repentance, without anyone having seized him or anyone having brought a claim against him.»

«And the point is that if he comes in a form in which there is no stratagem and no deceit, then the like of this one’s repentance is accepted; because he came repentant, regretful — like others of the disbelievers who have an evil past, and then Allah favours them with repentance without coercion and without a claim against them from anyone.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

he was SEIZEDhe CAME of his own accord
the worldly sentenceCarried out — «even if they say: we have repented; because they are not believed»Lifted — «this one’s repentance is accepted»
with AllahAccepted if he is truthful — «that benefits them with Allah», on al-Shūrā 42:25Accepted
the reasonCredibility: a claim of repentance under arrest is «a stratagem»«He came by choice… without anyone having seized him»

Read the second row. Even the man who is executed is not pronounced upon before Allah. The refusal is a judgement about credibility in a court, not about Allah’s mercy — and Ibn Bāz says so in the same breath as the refusal. And the magicians of Pharaoh, from part 1, are the Qurʾān’s own case of the door in the second row standing open.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — And the killing of a woman magician?

Ibn Bāz — «This is the correct position; her ruling is the ruling of the man — and for this Ḥafṣah killed the slave girl who had bewitched her.»

And the compiler handles the obvious objection rather than ignoring it. ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا, in a report of ʿAbd al-Razzāq, sold the slave girl who had bewitched her rather than killing her. His answer: it is borne on the slave girl not having worked the magic herself, but having asked a magician to work it for her — «for had she been the one who bewitched her herself, she would not have sold her to a bedouin; rather she would have killed her», since a slave girl’s knowledge of magic is a defect for which a sale is returned, and she might have bewitched the bedouin who bought her or another.

حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْف The One Ḥadīth Everybody Quotes — and Its Grading

This is the most instructive page in the clause, and it is a footnote. The report is quoted everywhere, by everyone, as the ḥadd of the magician. The compiler takes it apart, and the names he quotes are not obscure ones.

حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْف

“The ḥadd of the magician is a stroke of the sword.”

ضَعِيفٌ مَرْفُوعًا · the correct is that it is the word of Jundub رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه — see the gradings below

الشَّرْح · the explanation

the chainAll of them from Ismāʿīl ibn Muslim al-Makkī from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī from Jundub al-Khayr from the Prophet ﷺ — narrated by al-Tirmidhī, Ibn Abī ʿĀṣim, al-Ṭabarānī, al-Dāraquṭnī, Ibn ʿAdī, al-Bayhaqī and others
the defect«a weak ḥadīth: Ismāʿīl ibn Muslim was inconsistent in it, and in him is weakness — so once he narrated it connected, and once he narrated it from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī mursal». The one corroborator, Khālid al-ʿAbdī, is «very weak, not to be argued with»
البُخَارِيّasked about it by al-Tirmidhī, he said: «هَذَا لَا شَيْء» — “this is nothing” — «and Ismāʿīl is very weak»
التِّرْمِذِيّ«and the correct is: from al-Ḥasan, from Jundub, mawqūf» — and Ibn Rajab affirmed that in Jāmiʿ al-ʿUlūm wa-l-Ḥikam
الذَّهَبِيّin al-Kabāʾir: «the correct is that it is from the statement of Jundub»
ابْنُ حَجَرin al-Fatḥ: «in its chain is weakness»
ابْنُ حَزْمweakened it in al-Muḥallā; and Ibn al-Mundhir in al-Iqnāʿ: «in its chain there is a criticism»
ابْنُ بَاز«and the marfūʿ aḥādīth — in them is weakness; and the preponderant view is its being mawqūf upon Jundub al-Khayr»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Seven named scholars, and the Shaykh of this very sharḥ among them. So the single most-quoted proof for the magician’s death penalty is not, as a prophetic ḥadīth, something this book will rest the ruling on.

And yet the ruling stands — which is the lesson. The ruling does not fall because one proof for it falls. It rests on the verse, on the reasoning about corruption, and on what follows.

أَثَرُ عُمَر What Does Carry It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

the atharFrom Bajālah: «ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb wrote: kill every magician and sorceress» — he said: so we killed three sorceresses». Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, Aḥmad, ʿAbd al-Razzāq, al-Bayhaqī, and Ibn Ḥazm in al-Muḥallā
its grading«وَإِسْنَادُهُ صَحِيح» — the compiler’s own grading — «and Ibn Ḥazm authenticated it»
and the ijmāʿIbn Qudāmah in al-Mughnī: «and this became well known and was not objected to, so it was a consensus». Likewise Sulaymān ibn ʿAbdillāh in al-Taysīr: «and the people acted on it in his caliphate without objection, so it was a consensus»; and al-Qannawjī in al-Rawḍah al-Nadiyyah: «the Rightly-Guided Caliphs acted upon the killing of the magicians, and that became widespread and no one objected to it»
the precision«and al-Bukhārī narrated in his Ṣaḥīḥ the kernel of the report abridged, WITHOUT the command to kill the sorceresses». Which is exactly the kind of sentence that separates a careful book from a loose one — the Ṣaḥīḥ is cited for what it contains and not for what one would like it to contain.
and JundubIbn Bāz: «it is established of Jundub al-Khayr al-Azdī, one of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ, that he killed some of the magicians» — he came upon one performing before al-Walīd, playing with his own head and restoring it, «so he came at him from a direction he did not know and killed him, and said: restore your head, if you are truthful»

So the structure of the proof is worth naming. A weak marfūʿ report; a ṣaḥīḥ athar of ʿUmar; a reported consensus built on that athar’s having gone unopposed; and a Companion’s deed. The ruling is as strong as that stack — and no stronger than it, which is why the stack is shown rather than summarised.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the compiler faces the obvious question squarely: if the ḥadd of the magician is death, why did the Prophet ﷺ not kill Labīd, who bewitched him? He gives four answers, and asserts none of them:

قِيلLabīd may not have worked the magic himself — another made it for him, so he was not the magician
قِيلHe may have left killing him for the treaty that was between him and the Jews
قِيلLabīd was a hypocrite outwardly professing Islām, so killing him was left «lest people say that Muḥammad kills his companions»
قِيلIt may have been before it was established that the ḥadd of the magician is death; and «he left him for fear of the Jews’ mischief, for they were people of force until Allah destroyed them»

Four «قِيل»s and no verdict. That is the honest shape of an answer to a question the texts do not settle, and it is worth noticing in a book that is perfectly willing to be decisive elsewhere.

بِغَيْرِ إِذْنِ وَلِيِّ الأَمْر Acting Without Permission

And then the question Jundub’s deed raises, put to him by his student Shaykh ʿUmar al-ʿĪd: whoever does what Jundub did when he sees a magician — is it said that it is permitted, or that the matter is referred to the one in authority?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«In any case this is a place of ijtihād. If he fears that the one in authority will not permit him, and he wants to nullify his plot and to aid the truth — then this is like what Jundub did, fine. But if he hopes that the one in authority will agree and will assist him in this matter, then there is no harm. The matter differs according to the strength of the man in his faith and his vigour and his boldness; so if he seeks permission so as not to harm himself, there is no harm; and if he goes forward and does not mind, as Jundub went forward, there is no harm — because it is of the chapter of aiding the truth.

«But the rule in this is: that these matters are not undertaken except by the permission of the guardians of affairs — so that no corruption occurs, and so that he does not harm himself.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — And if he is killed [for it]?

Ibn Bāz — «Even if he is killed — the matter is easy.»

Read the whole answer and not the half of it. He allows the case and then states the rule against it in the same paragraph — «لَكِنَّ القَاعِدَةَ فِي هَذَا: أَنَّ هَذِهِ الأُمُورَ لَا يُقْدَمُ عَلَيْهَا إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ وُلَاةِ الأُمُور» — with two reasons: lest corruption occur, and lest he harm himself.

And notice what the permitted case actually is in his description: a man in the presence of the ruler, where the ruler knows the man is a corrupter, who puts himself forward to make the falsehood clear to the people and does not mind if he is imprisoned or beaten. The thing being praised is a man accepting cost to expose a fraud — not a man taking a sentence into his own hands in secret.

قَلْبُ الأَعْيَان Can a Magician Turn a Man Into a Donkey?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz, replying to al-Rāzī’s claim that the magician can turn a man into a donkey:

«Then his saying — “that by it he attains to changing the physical essences” — is also an error. Rather what is attained by it is the seeming, and the hurt, and the harm; and as for the essences, the magicians do not attain to that… As for changing the essences — no. All of this is false, and great ignorance.»

His proof is the two verses from part 1 — «they bewitched the eyes of the people» and «it was made to seem to him» — plus «and they could not harm anyone by it except by Allah’s permission». Seeming, and harm. Not creation.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The compiler then stacks the arguments, and the best of them is Ibn Jarīr’s, which is a reductio about truth itself:

«They said: had it been within the magicians’ power to originate bodies and to overturn the realities of essences from the forms they are in, there would be no distinction between falsehood and truth, and it would be possible that all perceptible things were of what the magicians bewitched and whose essences they overturned.»

And he adds ʿĀʾishah’s report of the Prophet ﷺ having been bewitched — «it was made to SEEM to him that he was doing a thing when he was not doing it» — as «the clearest indication of the falsity of the claim of those who claim that the magician originates the essences of things».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And here is the thing worth most on this page: the compiler rejects one of the arguments made on his own side.

Two writers he quotes argue: if that were possible, the magicians would be the richest of people and the kings of the world — and no court file in all the ages records a claim against a magician for turning a brother into a donkey.

His comment: «وَفِي هَذَا الإِلْزَامِ الأَخِيرِ نَظَر» — and in this last binding argument there is something to be examined — because we all say that the magician can, by Allah’s permission, bewitch the eyes of the gold-sellers by giving them blank papers which they see as thousands; and with that they were not the richest of people.» And his explanation of why: «because Allah — blessed and exalted — nullifies most of their magic by which they harm people. Then too, magic is limited: the magician cannot bewitch all people, nor everyone he wishes to bewitch, nor is he able to perform magic at every time.»

That is a scholar refusing a bad argument for a conclusion he holds, and it is worth more as a model than the conclusion is. He then gives the argument he does accept, and it is a tawḥīd argument: overturning essences into other essences is a kind of creating; the magician works only through the jinn; «and the jinn are not able to create, and whoever says they are able to create has disbelieved in Allah» — so if the jinn cannot create, their helpers among the magicians of mankind cannot either.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the athar of Dūmat al-Jandal — the woman who claimed she commanded wheat to sprout and ripen and grind in an instant, which Ibn Kathīr called «a strange report and an astonishing account» and which some used to prove that essences can be overturned — is answered three ways:

١The woman is unknown — «and how is reliance placed on a woman with whom the devils had toyed, and who claimed that her faith had departed from her?»
٢Even granting her truthfulness, it is borne on her having been bewitched with the magic of seeming — she supposed she had sown and nothing was sown
٣The report is not sound, for the weakness of its chain. Ibn Bāz: «in its chain is ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Abī al-Zinād, and in him is criticism, and this is of his oddities and his singularities… so it is not relied upon and not turned to… the upshot is that this is of the strange reports: it is either a concealed defect, or of ʿAbd al-Raḥmān’s errors, or of this old woman’s lying»

And Ibn Bāz adds a substantive objection too: the report contains nothing of what the magicians actually teach — «they teach them shirk with Allah and worship of the jinn besides Allah» — «and there is mentioned here nothing but mere sowing and reaping and grinding, and this is nothing».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Does the magician fly in the air, in reality?

Ibn Bāz — «It is said that the jinn carry him — not of himself; like how they used to eavesdrop, some of them mounting above others until they reached the lowest heaven and received what was said there… So Allah gave them things of strength, and gave them things of swift movement, for a far-reaching wisdom — like what He gave the angels: the angel descends from the heaven and ascends in an instant.»

And asked whether it is established that they carry a person: «هَذَا يُقَالُ — يَقُولُهُ الَّذِينَ يَتَعَاطَوْنَ أَخْبَارَهُم» — “this is said; those who deal in reports of them say it” — and he attributes the mention to Shaykh al-Islām and others. Note the register: he reports who says it and does not adopt it.

وَأَنَّا كُنَّا نَقْعُدُ مِنْهَا مَقَاعِدَ لِلسَّمْعِ فَمَن يَسْتَمِعِ الْآنَ يَجِدْ لَهُ شِهَابًا رَّصَدًا

سُؤَالُ العَرَّافِ وَتَصْدِيقُه Asking Them, and Believing Them

The last block on these folios is about the client, not the magician — and it is the part most people will actually need.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«So the upshot is: these people teach the people what harms them and does not benefit them, so that they may consume people’s wealth wrongfully and live among people by falsehood, in consuming their wealth and lying to them. So whoever believes them, of those who ask them, in the claim of knowledge of the unseen — has disbelieved as they have. And as for mere asking — it is not permitted, because it is a means to believing them.»

مَنْ أَتَى عَرَّافًا فَسَأَلَهُ عَنْ شَيْءٍ لَمْ تُقْبَلْ لَهُ صَلَاةُ أَرْبَعِينَ يَوْمًا

“Whoever comes to a fortune-teller and asks him about something — no prayer of his is accepted for forty days.”

يَنْسِبُهُ الشَّيْخُ إِلَى صَحِيحِ مُسْلِم، عَنْ بَعْضِ أَزْوَاجِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ · reported as the sharḥ places it

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And now a piece of textual criticism aimed at a printed book of this very school. Some copies of Kitāb al-Tawḥīd carry the ḥadīth with the extra words «فَصَدَّقَه» — “and believes him”.

Ibn Bāz: «and as for what is in some copies of Kitāb al-Tawḥīd, “fa-ṣaddaqahu” — this is not preserved; the narration is only “and asks him about something”, thus did Muslim narrate it»; and elsewhere: «by this it is known that what occurred in some copies of al-Tawḥīd, “fa-ṣaddaqahu”, is a mistake — it is not in Muslim, rather a mistake from some of the copyists who added “fa-ṣaddaqahu”.»

And the correction is not a softening — it sharpens the ruling. Because if the forty-day threat is on asking alone, then asking alone carries it; and «when the believing comes, the threat becomes severer — and that is disbelief».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also explains why asking alone carries so heavy a threat, and the reason is social rather than personal: «because it is a means to believing, and a means to making his affair appear and spreading his reputation until people seek him out» — and in the Fatāwā: «because coming to them and asking them raises their standing, and causes their affair to become widespread in the lands, and people’s believing of them».

He quotes the Prophet ﷺ on the kāhins — «لَا تَأْتُوهُم» and «لَيْسُوا بِشَيْء» — both given here without attribution or grading, so none is supplied.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And a footnote guards the sentence «none knows the unseen but Allah» from being over-read. It does not contradict that some of the awliyāʾ may have something disclosed to them — «not because they are described with knowledge of the unseen or part of it, but they are inspired at some time; and then this inspiration is a preponderant supposition, not knowledge».

Ibn Bāz, asked about المُكَاشَفَات: «what appears is that they divide into two kinds — a sound kind and a corrupt kind: a kind belonging to the false Ṣūfīs, and a kind belonging to the people of good and faith», like what came that they are inspired — «إِنْ كَانَ فِي أُمَّتِي مُحَدَّثُونَ فَعُمَر» — «as in the story of Sāriyah of the mountain… and it is called فِرَاسَة and called كَرَامَة»; whereas the others «have no insight — rather the devils convey to them slight hidden things, and they inform people of them, claiming they are unseen matters disclosed to them».

That wording of the Prophet ﷺ is quoted here loosely, with «مِثْلُ مَا جَاءَ» and no grading, so the page supplies none.

تَعَلَّمُوا السِّحْرَ وَلَا تَعْمَلُوا بِه A Fabrication, Named

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — What is the soundness of a ḥadīth I heard from the Prophet ﷺ: «learn magic but do not act upon it»?

Ibn Bāz — «هَذَا الحَدِيثُ بَاطِلٌ لَا أَصْلَ لَه — this ḥadīth is false; it has no basis. And it is not permitted to learn magic, nor to act upon it; and that is an abomination — rather disbelief and misguidance… because learning magic, all of it, is disbelief; and for this He informed of the two angels that they do not teach people magic until they say to the learner: «إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ فِتْنَةٌ فَلَا تَكْفُر» — so it is known that it is disbelief and misguidance.»

Two fabrications named on one clause — this, and the weak marfūʿ of the ḥadd. And the second rule-8 lesson of these folios: a report’s being universally repeated is no part of its grading.

Part 3 takes printed folios ١٧٩–١٩٣ and closes the longest clause in the book: whether the Prophet ﷺ was bewitched and how that sits with «وَاللهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاس», النُّشْرَة and what is permitted in treating magic, telling the bewitched from the insane, and Hārūt and Mārūt.

What is the rule-8 lesson of these folios?

That weak reports may be used for virtues
That a report’s being universally repeated is no part of its grading — two of the most-quoted reports in this clause are weak and fabricated respectively
That gradings are a matter of ijtihād
That only al-Bukhārī and Muslim may be cited
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
قَالَ ابْنُ بَازٍ إِنَّ السِّحْرَ كُفْرٌ فِي ذَاتِهِ بِلَا تَفْصِيل. Ibn Bāz said magic is disbelief in itself with no distinction.
الأَصْلُ فِي السَّاحِرِ أَنَّهُ كَافِرٌ حَتَّى يُعْرَفَ خِلَافُ ذَلِكَ بِطَرِيقِ الثِّقَات. The default concerning the magician is that he is a disbeliever until the contrary is known by the route of the trustworthy.
السَّاحِرُ يُقْتَلُ حَدًّا لَا كُفْرًا. The magician is killed as a ḥadd, not as a disbeliever.
حَدِيثُ «حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْفِ» صَحِيحٌ مَرْفُوعًا. The ḥadīth «the ḥadd of the magician is a stroke of the sword» is authentic as a marfūʿ report.
إِسْنَادُ أَثَرِ عُمَرَ فِي قَتْلِ السَّحَرَةِ صَحِيح. The chain of ʿUmar’s report on killing the magicians is authentic.
مَنْ تَابَ مِنَ السِّحْرِ قَبْلَ أَنْ يُقْبَضَ عَلَيْهِ تُقْبَلُ تَوْبَتُه. Whoever repents of magic before being seized — his repentance is accepted.
مُجَرَّدُ سُؤَالِ العَرَّافِ كُفْرٌ أَكْبَر. Merely asking a fortune-teller is greater disbelief.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَفِي هَذَا الإِلْزَامِ الأَخِيرِ نَظَر

The compiler — about an argument offered in support of a conclusion he himself holds. Two writers argued that if magicians could overturn essences they would be the richest men alive; he agrees with their conclusion and takes their argument apart anyway, then supplies a better one from tawḥīd. A bad argument for a true conclusion is still a bad argument, and these folios show the same discipline twice more — on «حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْف», which is quoted everywhere and graded weak here by seven named scholars, and on «تَعَلَّمُوا السِّحْر», which Ibn Bāz calls «بَاطِلٌ لَا أَصْلَ لَه». And the ruling still stands, resting on what actually carries it.
