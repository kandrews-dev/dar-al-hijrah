نَشْرُ الدَّوَاوِينِ وَالحِسَاب

The Registers Spread Open, and the Reckoning

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why the registers are described as being spread open, and when.
  • Reconcile the two descriptions of how the disbeliever takes his record.
  • Define الحِسَاب as al-Fawzān defines it, and distinguish العَرْض from المُنَاقَشَة.
  • Explain in what sense the disbelievers are reckoned with, given that they have no good deeds.
٤ · وَتُنْشَرُ الدَّوَاوِين Fourth — And the Registers Are Spread Open

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَتُنْشَرُ الدَّوَاوِينُ — وَهِيَ صَحَائِفُ الأَعْمَالِ — فَآخِذٌ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ، وَآخِذٌ كِتَابَهُ بِشِمَالِهِ أَوْ مِنْ وَرَاءِ ظَهْرِهِ، كَمَا قَالَ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى: ﴿وَكُلَّ إِنْسَانٍ أَلْزَمْنَاهُ طَائِرَهُ فِي عُنُقِهِ وَنُخْرِجُ لَهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كِتَابًا يَلْقَاهُ مَنْشُورًا ۝ اقْرَأْ كِتَابَكَ كَفَى بِنَفْسِكَ الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الدَّوَاوِين — that is: the pages in which the deeds of the servants were written — the deeds they did in this world, which the recording angels wrote against them.

And al-Fawzān explains the verb, which is doing real work: because they are folded up at death, and spread open — that is, at the Reckoning — so that every human being may stand upon his own page and know what is in it.

So the word nashr carries a whole sequence: written during the life, closed at the death, opened at the Reckoning. Nothing is being compiled on the Day; the record was complete before the man was buried.

وَكُلَّ إِنْسَانٍ أَلْزَمْنَاهُ طَائِرَهُ فِي عُنُقِهِ وَنُخْرِجُ لَهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كِتَابًا يَلْقَاهُ مَنْشُورًا اقْرَأْ كِتَابَكَ كَفَى بِنَفْسِكَ الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا

"And every man — We have fastened his record to his neck; and We shall bring out for him, on the Day of Rising, a book which he will find spread open. Read your book! You suffice today as a reckoner against yourself."

Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:13–14 · سُورَةُ الإِسْرَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

طَائِرَه — is what flew off from him of his deed, of good and of evil. فِي عُنُقِهِ — that is: he is bound to it and recompensed by it; there is no turning away from it for him — so it clings to him as a necklace clings to the neck.

وَنُخْرِجُ لَهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ كِتَابًا — that is: we gather for him his whole deed in a book he is given on the Day of Rising — either in his right hand if he is of the happy, or in his left if he is of the wretched. مَنْشُورًا — that is: opened; he reads it and so does someone else.

And why spread open rather than merely handed over? al-Fawzān: and He said يَلْقَاهُ مَنْشُورًا only to intensify the glad tidings at the good deed, and the rebuke at the evil one. A sealed record would spare its owner the worst part of it.

اقْرَأْ كِتَابَكَ — that is: we say that to him; and note whom it is said to: before he reads that book — both the one who was a reader and the one who was not. The illiterate man reads his own record on that Day.

حَسِيبًا — that is: as a reckoner; and it is manṣūb as التَّمْيِيز. And this is the utmost of justice, in that He made him the reckoner of his own self, so that he might see all of his deeds — and the shāhid of the āyah is that every human being is given the page of his deed on the Day of Rising, which he reads himself and comes to know himself, not by means of anyone else.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And on the two ways of taking the record, al-Fawzān resolves what looks like a conflict between the āyāt: this explains the manner of the people's taking of their pages, and it is of two kinds: one who takes his book in his right hand — and he is the believer; and one who takes his book in his left hand, or from behind his back — and he is the disbeliever.

— and there is no conflict between the two, because the disbeliever’s right hand is shackled to his neck and his left is put behind his back, so he takes his book with it — by his left hand being twisted round from behind his back and his book given to him in it, as the āyāt came with this and with that.

One posture, described from two angles. The two sets of āyāt were reporting the same thing and there was nothing to choose between them — the resolution being, again, that the texts were never in competition.

٥ · وَيُحَاسِبُ اللهُ الخَلَائِق Fifth — And Allah Reckons with the Creation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَيُحَاسِبُ اللهُ الخَلَائِقَ، وَيَخْلُو بِعَبْدِهِ المُؤْمِنِ فَيُقَرِّرُهُ بِذُنُوبِهِ، كَمَا وُصِفَ ذَلِكَ فِي الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ. وَأَمَّا الكُفَّارُ فَلَا يُحَاسَبُونَ مُحَاسَبَةَ مَنْ تُوزَنُ حَسَنَاتُهُ وَسَيِّئَاتُهُ، فَإِنَّهُ لَا حَسَنَاتِ لَهُمْ؛ وَلَكِنْ تُعَدُّ أَعْمَالُهُمْ فَتُحْصَى، فَيُوقَفُونَ عَلَيْهَا وَيُقَرَّرُونَ بِهَا وَيُجْزَوْنَ بِهَا.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الحِسَاب — and al-Fawzān gives the definition twice, which is worth having both times: al-ḥisāb is Allah's acquainting the creation with the measures of the recompense for their deeds, and His reminding them of what they had forgotten of that; or in another expression: Allah's halting His servants, before their departure from the place of gathering, upon their deeds — whether they were good or evil.

Note what the first definition says about the purpose. It is not an investigation; it is an informing. Allah is not finding out what was done — He is acquainting the servant with it, and reminding him of what he had himself forgotten. Which is why the record must be read by its owner.

Then the Shaykh mentioned that the Reckoning is of two kinds.

النَّوْعُ الأَوَّل · حِسَابُ المُؤْمِن The First Kind — the Reckoning of the Believer

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author's words: وَيَخْلُو بِعَبْدِهِ المُؤْمِنِ فَيُقَرِّرُهُ بِذُنُوبِهِ — as Allah تَعَالَى said:

فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ فَسَوْفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا

"So as for him who is given his record in his right hand, he will be reckoned with an easy reckoning."

Sūrat al-Inshiqāq 84:7–8 · سُورَةُ الانْشِقَاق

إِنَّ اللهَ يُدْنِي المُؤْمِنَ فَيَضَعُ عَلَيْهِ كَنَفَهُ وَيَسْتُرُهُ مِنَ النَّاسِ، وَيُقَرِّرُهُ بِذُنُوبِهِ فَيَقُولُ لَهُ: أَتَعْرِفُ ذَنْبَ كَذَا؟ أَتَعْرِفُ ذَنْبَ كَذَا؟ حَتَّى إِذَا قَرَّرَهُ بِذُنُوبِهِ وَرَأَى فِي نَفْسِهِ أَنْ قَدْ هَلَكَ، قَالَ: فَإِنِّي قَدْ سَتَرْتُهَا عَلَيْكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَأَنَا أَغْفِرُهَا لَكَ اليَوْمَ. ثُمَّ يُعْطَى كِتَابَ حَسَنَاتِهِ

"Allah brings the believer near and places His covering over him and screens him from the people, and makes him acknowledge his sins, saying: do you know of such a sin? Do you know of such a sin? Until, when He has made him acknowledge his sins and he sees within himself that he is ruined, He says: I concealed them for you in the world, and I forgive them for you today. Then he is given the record of his good deeds."

فِي الصَّحِيحَيْنِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim, from Ibn ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the meaning of يُقَرِّرُهُ بِذُنُوبِهِ is that He makes him acknowledge them — that is, confess them, as in this ḥadīth.

Read the shape of it, because the whole mercy of the ḥadīth is in the order. The screening comes first — before a single sin is named, he is brought near and covered from the people. Then he is made to see the full account, to the point of believing himself destroyed. And only then is he forgiven.

He is not spared the knowledge of what he did; he is spared the exposure of it, and then the consequence. And the reason given is the thing to carry out of the lesson: I concealed them for you in the world — the covering on that Day is the continuation of a covering that was already happening, unnoticed, for a lifetime.

And of the believers there are those who enter the Garden without a reckoning — as is authentic in the ḥadīth of the seventy thousand who enter the Garden without reckoning and without punishment.

العَرْضُ وَالمُنَاقَشَة The Presentation, and the Interrogation

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Reckoning differs: of it is the easy, and that is العَرْض — the presentation; and of it is المُنَاقَشَة — the interrogation. And the distinction is not al-Fawzān's own — it is ʿĀʾishah's question and the Prophet's ﷺ answer:

In the two Ṣaḥīḥs, from ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: «no one is reckoned with on the Day of Rising but he is ruined». She said: O Messenger of Allah, did Allah تَعَالَى not say فَأَمَّا مَنْ أُوتِيَ كِتَابَهُ بِيَمِينِهِ فَسَوْفَ يُحَاسَبُ حِسَابًا يَسِيرًا? So the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

إِنَّمَا ذَلِكَ العَرْضُ؛ وَلَيْسَ أَحَدٌ يُنَاقَشُ الحِسَابَ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ إِلَّا عُذِّبَ

— that is only the presentation. And no one is interrogated in the Reckoning on the Day of Rising but he is punished. — فِي الصَّحِيحَيْنِ

That exchange is a model of how an apparent conflict between two texts is settled, and the student should notice who settles it. ʿĀʾishah heard a ḥadīth, found an āyah that seemed to contradict it, and asked. The answer distinguishes two things the one word ḥisāb was covering — the easy reckoning of the āyah is a presentation, and the ruinous reckoning of the ḥadīth is an interrogation. Al-ḥaqqu lā yatanāqaḍ, demonstrated in a conversation.

النَّوْعُ الثَّانِي · حِسَابُ الكُفَّار The Second Kind — the Reckoning of the Disbelievers

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author explained it with his words: وَأَمَّا الكُفَّارُ فَلَا يُحَاسَبُونَ مُحَاسَبَةَ مَنْ تُوزَنُ حَسَنَاتُهُ وَسَيِّئَاتُهُ، فَإِنَّهُ لَا حَسَنَاتِ لَهُمْ — that is: they have no good deeds to be weighed against their evil deeds, because their deeds were nullified by the disbelief, so there remains for them in the Hereafter nothing but evil deeds.

So their reckoning means that تُعَدُّ أَعْمَالُهُمْ فَتُحْصَى، فَيُوقَفُونَ عَلَيْهَا وَيُقَرَّرُونَ بِهَا وَيُجْزَوْنَ بِهَا — that is: they are informed of their deeds of disbelief, and they acknowledge them, and then they are recompensed for them.

So the word reckoning is being used in two senses, and the author is careful to say so. For the believer it is a weighing, with an outcome in doubt. For the disbeliever there is nothing to weigh — only an enumeration, an acknowledgement and a recompense. The scales are not used where there is nothing in one pan.

And al-Fawzān brings three āyāt for the enumeration and the acknowledgement:

فَلَنُنَبِّئَنَّ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا بِمَا عَمِلُوا وَلَنُذِيقَنَّهُمْ مِنْ عَذَابٍ غَلِيظٍ

"We shall surely inform those who disbelieved of what they did, and We shall surely make them taste a harsh punishment."

Sūrat Fuṣṣilat 41:50 · سُورَةُ فُصِّلَت

وَشَهِدُوا عَلَى أَنْفُسِهِمْ أَنَّهُمْ كَانُوا كَافِرِينَ

"And they will bear witness against themselves that they were disbelievers."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:37 · سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف

فَاعْتَرَفُوا بِذَنْبِهِمْ فَسُحْقًا لِأَصْحَابِ السَّعِيرِ

"So they will acknowledge their sin — then away with the companions of the Blaze!"

Sūrat al-Mulk 67:11 · سُورَةُ المُلْك

Notice that in all three the disbelievers testify against themselves. Nobody needs to be convinced of anything; the record is read, and they confirm it. Which is the same justice as كَفَى بِنَفْسِكَ الْيَوْمَ عَلَيْكَ حَسِيبًا in the āyah of al-Isrāʾ above — the man is made the witness in his own case, and in neither case does he dispute the evidence.

He confesses, then is punished, then forgiven
He is forgiven first, then shown the record

How did the Prophet ﷺ settle ʿĀʾishah’s question?

By saying the āyah concerns the prophets only
By distinguishing two things the one word ḥisāb covered — the easy reckoning of the āyah is the ʿarḍ, while no one interrogated in the Reckoning escapes punishment
By saying the ḥadīth was abrogated
By saying the reckoning differs by nation

In what sense are the disbelievers reckoned with?

Their good deeds are weighed but found light
There is nothing to weigh, since their deeds were nullified by disbelief — so their deeds are enumerated, they are halted upon them, they acknowledge them and are recompensed
They are not reckoned with at all
They are reckoned with by their nations rather than individually

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَإِنِّي قَدْ سَتَرْتُهَا عَلَيْكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا، وَأَنَا أَغْفِرُهَا لَكَ اليَوْمَ

The end of the believer's private reckoning, after he has seen the whole account and given himself up for lost. Note the reason He gives: I concealed them for you in the world. The covering on that Day is the continuation of a covering that has been going on, unnoticed, for your whole life.
