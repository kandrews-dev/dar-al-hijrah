النَّاقِضُ الرَّابِع · التَّفْصِيل

The Fourth Nullifier, Part 2 — The Distinction

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the two states of one who judges by other than what Allah sent down, and which of them is كُفْرٌ أَكْبَر.
  • Quote Ibn ʿAbbās’s «كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر» and say what it describes.
  • Identify the one thing that divides the two states — and name the exception the compiler adds to it.
  • Apply the rule «لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم» to the argument that a man who codifies «must» be deeming it lawful.
  • Say what Ibn Bāz holds about قَرَائِنُ الأَحْوَال, and what he says is obligatory where no declaration has been made.
  • State Ibn Bāz’s recorded تَوَقُّف on Shaykh Muḥammad ibn Ibrāhīm’s risālah, and why.
  • Say who fights, on what condition, and what the ruling is where there is no capacity.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٢٠–١٣١
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 120–131, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the half of the fourth nullifier that decides how the first half is applied. Part 1 established the ruling on preferring other than Allah’s judgement. These twelve folios are almost entirely restraint — and that is not a reading imposed on them. It is Ibn Bāz’s own first word on the subject:

«الأَصْلُ التَّفْصِيل، هَذَا هُوَ الأَصْل.»
“The default is making the distinction. This is the default.”

Before the distinctions, the severity is not softened either. Ibn Bāz calls referring judgement to «man-made codes and human systems and the customs of forefathers and the rulings of soothsayers, sorcerers and astrologers» «among the ugliest of evil deeds and the greatest of abominations», and «of the greatest hypocrisy, and among the largest of the emblems of disbelief and oppression and corruption». The تَفْصِيل that follows is not a softening of that. It is the answer to a different question: not «how bad is this?» but «what may be said about a particular man?»

طَاغُوتٌ أَكْبَرُ وَطَاغُوتٌ أَصْغَر The Division, in His Own Words

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He opens the detail with a sentence about the word الطَّاغُوت itself: «the ṭāghūt is sometimes a lesser ṭāghūt and sometimes a greater ṭāghūt.» Then the division:

«Whoever judges by other than what Allah sent down is a ṭāghūt. If it is along with his acknowledging and confessing that he is disobeying — then he is a ṭāghūt with a lesser tyranny. And if he deems it lawful and deems it permissible — he becomes a greater ṭāghūt, and a disbeliever, astray. And if he judged by other than what Allah sent down for a bribe, or for kinship, or for enmity, or for other causes, and he knows that he is disobeying — he becomes a lesser ṭāghūt, and becomes an oppressor, a transgressor, a sinner — but he does not become a disbeliever with that greater disbelief.»

his statethe rulingwhat he is
he deems it lawful / deems it permissibleكُفْرٌ أَكْبَر — «a disbeliever, apostate»a greater ṭāghūt
he judges wrongly for a bribe, kinship or enmity, knowing he is disobeyingكُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر — «as Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid said»a lesser ṭāghūt: ظَالِم, فَاجِر, عَاصٍ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he states the same division a second time in different words, which is worth having because it names the motive: «the one who contravenes the ruling of Allah is upon two states: if he contravened the ruling of Allah deeming that lawful, he is a disbeliever, an apostate; and if he contravened it out of covetousness and desire, and he knows that he is wrong and that he is disobeying, but he contravened it for a bribe or for kinship or the like of that — he becomes by that an oppressor, a sinner, a disbeliever with a disbelief short of disbelief, as Ibn ʿAbbās and Mujāhid said.»

وَمَن لَّمْ يَحْكُم بِمَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الكَافِرُونَ

And whoever does not judge by what Allah has sent down — those are the disbelievers.

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:44

This is the verse Ibn ʿAbbās was asked about, and «كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر» is his own tafsīr of it. So the qualification is not a later concession made under pressure; it is as old as the verse’s earliest explanation, from a Companion, and Mujāhid with him. The sharḥ cites it from a lesson of Ibn Bāz’s and from his tafsīr of Sūrat al-Māʾidah.

مَا هُوَ الفَارِق The Divider Is the Man’s Own Verdict on Himself

Read the two states again and notice that the act can be identical. What differs is what the man holds about what he is doing. The sharḥ’s footnote is unusually thorough here: it collects six separate wordings of that condition from six separate places, and they are worth seeing together, because the repetition is the argument.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

wording ١«he knows that he is disobeying, and that he has done a grave abomination»
wording ٢«he knows that he is an oppressor, and that he is wrong, and that it is a means to disbelief… but so long as he knows that he is wrong and that he is an oppressor, he does not become a disbeliever — he is a sinner, and in it is a kind of disbelief, but it does not become a greater disbelief, because he believes that he is wrong, an oppressor, a sinner, and that the ruling of Allah is what must be followed»
wording ٣«he knows that he is disobeying and that he has contravened the truth»
wording ٤«he believes that he is… mistaken and at fault in that, and that he is disobedient to his Lord»
wording ٥«and he knows that he is disobeying by it, and that he is a criminal by it»
wording ٦«he knows that he is disobeying» — the bare form, in the main text

Every one of the six is a verdict the man passes on himself. Not a verdict we pass on him. The nullifier turns on whether he still holds that Allah’s ruling is the one that must be followed and that he himself is in the wrong — and nobody but he has direct access to that.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the compiler adds one precise exception, which closes the obvious hole. On the word «ظَالِم» in wording ٢ he says: «his saying “an oppressor” excludes the one who declares openly that he is just, and that his ṭāghūtī rulings are justice and equity.»

That is exactly right, and it is the hinge. The excuse is available to a man who concedes he is in the wrong. It is not available to a man who argues that what he has done is the better justice — because that is no longer a sin he admits; it is the preference the matn named in the first place.

القَوَانِينُ الَّتِي لَا تُخَالِفُ الشَّرْع A Code Is Not the Problem

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Before anything else is concluded, Ibn Bāz removes a whole category from the discussion:

«The codes, if they do not contravene the Sharīʿah, there is no harm in them — they are called codes, and they are called regulations. So every code that benefits the Muslims and does not contravene the law of Allah, there is no harm in it — in traffic, or in the judiciary, or in the government departments, or in any place… because Allah made His law ordering everything, and it is from the law of Allah that affairs be regulated, and that they not be neglected… so that people proceed upon something clear.»

So the word قَانُون settles nothing by itself. Traffic regulations, court procedure, the administration of a ministry — the sharḥ names these three — are not a rival to the Sharīʿah; regulating affairs is itself something the Sharīʿah asks for. The question is only ever whether a thing contravenes it.

لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم The Rule That Answers the Hardest Objection

Now the strongest argument on the other side, and the sharḥ puts it fully. The questioner relays the reasoning of those who do declare the people of the codes to be disbelievers:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«They see a difference between one who commits a disobedience — so we rule that he is a sinful Muslim, or deficient in faith — and one who makes the disobedience a binding code for people. Because they say: it cannot be conceived of one who set the Sharīʿah aside — at the furthest remove — and put in its place a binding code, even if he says he does not deem it lawful, except that he deems it lawful, or holds that it is better for the people, or the like. And that he differs from the one who judged a single particular case for kinship or for a bribe.»

That is a serious objection and it is honestly stated. Ibn Bāz’s answer is a single rule of uṣūl:

«بَسِ القَاعِدَة: لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم.»

“Only the principle: the necessary implication of a ruling is not itself a ruling.”

And he shows it works both ways, which is what makes it an answer rather than an assertion: «it may be said of the one who judged for his own desire or for his relative that he is deeming it lawful — it follows necessarily from him. This is not a ruling — this is between him and Allah.»

Read what that does to the objection. The objection says: a man who does this on that scale cannot be conceived except as deeming it lawful. The answer does not deny the inference is reasonable. It denies that an inference is a ruling. And it points out that the same inference could be pressed on the petty judge taking a bribe — who is, by consensus of the two positions, not an apostate. A principle that would convict him too is not doing the work it claims.

وَالحُكْمُ بِغَيْرِ الظَّنّ A Ruling Is Not Made on Supposition

This is the most careful passage in the clause, and it is a series of questions put to him and answered one by one. Notice that he concedes the appearance every single time, and still declines the ruling.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Is that the supposition about them?

Ibn Bāz — «There is no doubt — that is the supposition about them. But the ruling is not by supposition… the supposition about them is evil and disbelief, but a person restrains himself from saying “disbeliever” unless he knows that he deemed it lawful.»

Q — Are they like the merely sinful rulers, or is this apparent disbelief?

Ibn Bāz — «This there is no doubt about: corruption, and the lesser disbelief — this there is no doubt about. The discussion is only: did they disbelieve with a greater disbelief? Are they apostates or not? This is a place of consideration. If they have not declared openly that it is permitted… the default is binding: that they not be declared disbelievers until it is known that they deemed it permissible and lawful.»

Q — Do the circumstantial indications not suffice?

Ibn Bāz — «The circumstantial indications do entail that they deem it permissible — but the ruling of apostasy is a matter beyond that.»

And in his own statement, not in answer to a question: «the apparent state of the countries today, but few, their apparent state is disbelief in Allah and deeming lawful the ruling by other than what Allah sent down — but being decisive about that is a place of consideration if he claims that he does not permit it, and that he holds judging by the Sharīʿah to be binding and the implementing of the codes to be false, but such-and-such drove him to it. This is a place of consideration, and Allah knows best.»

«And if they do not declare it openly, التَّوَقُّف is obligatory — it may be desire, and regard for their own interests…»

Three times he grants that the outward appearance points one way, and three times he refuses to convert the appearance into a verdict. That is not evasion; it is a distinction between two different questions. «What does this look like?» is answered: it looks like istiḥlāl. «What may be ruled?» is answered: not that, until he says it. And he says plainly what is not in doubt — the fisq and the lesser kufr are established; only the greater is withheld.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also declines to shut the question on anyone else, which is itself instructive: «this discussion does not prevent the other discussion. I say: each one exerts himself in the research and may find what sets his heart at rest — because these are dangerous matters; these are not easy; they are weighty matters.»

And the limiting case, which disposes of the simplest version of the argument: «otherwise, merely that he judged by other than what Allah sent down, or ordered that — he does not disbelieve by that, like the one who ordered judgement against so-and-so, or the killing of so-and-so — he does not disbelieve by that until he deems it lawful.»

مَوْضِعُ الخِلَاف Where Two Imāms of This School Differed

The sharḥ does not hide that the matter was disputed inside this school, and the student is better served knowing it than not. Shaykh مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ إِبْرَاهِيم, in his risālah تَحْكِيمُ القَوَانِين, drew the line in a different place:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«As for the one of whom it was said “a disbelief short of disbelief”: if he referred judgement to other than Allah while believing that he is disobeying and that the ruling of Allah is the truth — this is the one from whom the single instance and the like of it issues. But the one who made codes, arranged and instituted — that is disbelief, even if they said: we erred, and the ruling of the Sharīʿah is more just. So there is a difference between the one who affirms and establishes [Allah’s law, and disobeys it in an instance] and the one who made other than the law of Allah the REFERENCE — they made it the reference, and this is a disbelief that removes from the religion.»

That is a real distinction and not a careless one: a single ruling given wrongly, against a law one still acknowledges, is not the same act as installing a different law as the authority. His case is that the second is a change of reference, not an instance of disobedience — and he held it so even where the men concede the Sharīʿah is more just.

And Ibn Bāz, asked about that very risālah, recorded a reservation. He confirms he read it and states its position accurately — «he holds their outward state to be disbelief; meaning, the one who judges by the codes, that indicates contentment and istiḥlāl» — and then:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«قَرَأْتُ رِسَالَتَه… لَكِنْ أَنَا عِنْدِي فِيهِ تَوَقُّف؛ لِأَنَّ الظَّاهِرَ مَا يَكْفِي، مَا يَكْفِي حَتَّى يُعْرَفَ أَنَّهُ [يَسْتَحِلُّهُ].»

“I read his risālah… but I have a reservation about it, because the outward is not enough — it is not enough until it is known that he deems it lawful.”

«The outward is not enough» is the whole of Ibn Bāz’s position in four words, and it is said here against a named scholar he otherwise honours — note he says it while asking Allah to forgive him, «اللهُ يَغْفِرُ لَهُ». This is what disagreement among the imāms of this school actually looks like: the position stated accurately, the reservation stated plainly, and the man’s honour intact.

For completeness: the compiler’s own footnote pushes the other way. It carries Shaykh صَالِحٌ الفَوْزَان rejecting a claim that Ibn Ibrāhīm had retracted his takfīr — «this is from lying and fabrication… the Shaykh did not retract, and what he said is the truth… he was not a hasty man; he used to deliberate and would not issue a fatwā until he had verified». Note what that footnote does and does not establish: it answers a claim of retraction. It does not answer Ibn Bāz’s تَوَقُّف, which was never a claim that Ibn Ibrāhīm changed his mind.

مَنِ الَّذِي يُقَاتِل And the Question Everyone Arrives Here to Ask

The questioner finally asks it directly, and the answers are short. Read who the subject of every verb is.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — One who is asked to judge by the law of Allah and refuses?

Ibn Bāz — «He is fought.»

Q — He is fought?

Ibn Bāz — «Yes — so if he fights, he has disbelieved; and if he does not fight, he has not disbelieved — his ruling is the ruling of the sinners.»

Q — Who is it that fights him?

Ibn Bāz — «The Muslim state.»

Q — And if it is not able, O Shaykh?

Ibn Bāz — «He remains as he is, between him and Allah. Allah is the One whose help is sought, Allah is the One whose help is sought.»

Four answers, and three of them are restraints. The actor is a state, not a group and not a man. The trigger is armed defence of the refusal — «if he fights, he has disbelieved; and if he does not fight, he has not». And where there is no capacity, the answer is not a lesser action; it is no action, and a supplication.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He grounds the precedent in Abū Bakr’s war on those who withheld the zakāh, as ابْنُ تَيْمِيَّة framed it — cited in the sharḥ from Fatḥ al-Majīd, on the fighting of the Tatars: «fighting them is the fighting of apostates, not the fighting of sinners, when they defend their falsehood». And Ibn Bāz’s own wording on the parallel: «every group that refuses and persists and says: we will not pay zakāh, or will not fast Ramaḍān — then upon the one in authority is that he strive against them until they adhere to the law of Allah, because their fighting over this is a kind of denial.»

And elsewhere he makes the sign explicit: «they are not declared disbelievers for the withholding unless they fight in defence of it — so their fighting in defence of it is a sign of their denial.» The fighting is not the crime; it is the evidence of the belief that is.

Finally the question is put in its sharpest form — and the answer is the one that matters most on this page. The questioner asks: if we do not rule them disbelievers, may we not still treat this as the kufr referred to in «إِلَّا أَنْ تَرَوْا كُفْرًا», and does that not warrant الخُرُوج against him?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«The apparent meaning of the speech of many of the people of knowledge is that the like of this, if there is a capable force or a capable state able to compel him and to fight him, they have that, until he adheres… and Shaykh Taqī al-Dīn Ibn Taymiyyah mentioned the consensus of the people of knowledge on this: if a state is found that does not implement the Sharīʿah, or does not prevent wine, or does not prevent ribā — it is obligatory upon the Muslim state to compel it to this thing, and to fight it if it persists and refuses.»

Notice that the answer does not grant what the question asked. The question was about الخُرُوج — rising against a ruler. The answer is given entirely at the level of a capable force or a state compelling another state, with Ibn Taymiyyah’s reported consensus cited for that, and with the examples being wine and ribā as much as the Sharīʿah. Taken with the previous answer — «who fights him?» «the Muslim state»; «and if it is not able?» «he remains as he is between him and Allah» — the sharḥ gives no door at all to an individual or a faction.

And the sentence to leave this page with is his, not mine: مَسَائِلُ خَطِيرَة، هَذِهِ مَا هِيَ بِسَهْلَة — «these are dangerous matters; these are not easy.» A man who has read these twelve folios and come away more confident of his verdicts has read them backwards.

Check Your Understanding · MCQ أَسْئِلَة

What does Ibn Bāz say is the «default» in this clause?

Declaring the ruling and leaving the detail to the scholars
التَّفْصِيل — making the distinction between the states: «this is the default»
Suspending all discussion of the subject
Applying the ruling of apostasy and then examining excuses

How does he divide the ṭāghūt?

Into a ruler and a judge
Into a lesser ṭāghūt and a greater ṭāghūt
Into the ancient and the modern
Into one who legislates and one who enforces

One who judges by other than Allah’s law while acknowledging that he is disobeying is —

a disbeliever, an apostate
a LESSER ṭāghūt: an oppressor, a transgressor, a sinner — «but he does not become a disbeliever with that greater disbelief»
excused entirely
a hypocrite in creed

And one who deems it lawful and deems it permissible?

He is a sinner only
He becomes a GREATER ṭāghūt — «a disbeliever, astray» — and an apostate
His case is a place of consideration
He is judged by his intention alone

Whose tafsīr is «كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر», and of which verse?

al-Ṭabarī, of al-Nisāʾ 4:65
Ibn ʿAbbās — and Mujāhid with him — of al-Māʾidah 5:44
Ibn Kathīr, of al-Tawbah 9:31
Mujāhid alone, of al-Māʾidah 5:50

Why does the lesson point out how old that qualification is?

To show it is weaker than the main position
Because it is not a later concession — it is as old as the verse’s earliest explanation, from a Companion
Because Ibn ʿAbbās retracted it
Because it applies only to the Companions’ era

What single thing divides the greater from the lesser state?

The scale of the wrong done
What the man himself holds about what he is doing — whether he still holds that he is wrong and that Allah’s ruling is what must be followed
Whether he holds public office
Whether he was taught the Sharīʿah

What is notable about the footnote on that condition?

It cites a single wording from a single place
It collects six separate wordings of it from six separate places — and every one of them is a verdict the man passes on HIMSELF
It contradicts the main text
It attributes the condition to Ibn Taymiyyah

What exception does the compiler add to the word «ẓālim» in that condition?

That it excludes rulers
That it excludes the one who declares openly that he is JUST, and that his ṭāghūtī rulings are justice and equity
That it excludes the ignorant
That it excludes one who was coerced

Why is that exception the hinge?

Because it narrows the clause to judges
Because the excuse is for a man who concedes he is in the wrong — it is not available to one who argues that what he did is the better justice, which is the very preference the matn named
Because it transfers the ruling to the ruler
Because it makes the condition impossible to meet

What does Ibn Bāz say about codes that do NOT contravene the Sharīʿah?

That they are disliked but permitted
That there is no harm in them — in traffic, the judiciary, the government departments or anywhere; and that regulating affairs is itself from the law of Allah
That they must be renamed regulations
That they are permitted only in necessity

State the objection of those who declare the people of the codes disbelievers.

That the codes are of foreign origin
That one who sets the Sharīʿah aside and puts a binding code in its place cannot be conceived EXCEPT as deeming it lawful or holding it better — and so differs from one who judged a single case for kinship or a bribe
That codification is an innovation with no precedent
That the codes cannot be reconciled with the ḥudūd

What is the rule Ibn Bāz answers it with?

الضَّرُورَاتُ تُبِيحُ المَحْظُورَات
لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم — the necessary implication of a ruling is not itself a ruling
الأَصْلُ بَرَاءَةُ الذِّمَّة
لَا يُنْسَبُ لِسَاكِتٍ قَوْل

How does he show that rule is an answer and not merely an assertion?

By citing a consensus for it
By showing it cuts both ways — the same inference could be pressed on the petty judge who ruled for his own relative, and a principle that would convict him too is not doing the work it claims
By restricting it to matters of creed
By deriving it from al-Māʾidah 5:44

What does he say about ruling on the basis of supposition?

That supposition suffices where the matter is grave
That the supposition about them may be evil and disbelief — «but the ruling is not by supposition», and a person restrains himself from saying «disbeliever» unless he knows that he deemed it lawful
That supposition is enough for the scholars but not the laymen
That supposition must be supported by two witnesses

Asked whether the قَرَائِنُ الأَحْوَال suffice, what did he answer?

That they suffice when numerous
That they do entail that they deem it permissible — «but the ruling of apostasy is a matter BEYOND that»
That they never indicate anything
That they suffice only against a state

What does he say is NOT in doubt, and what does he leave as a «place of consideration»?

Everything is a place of consideration
The fisq and the LESSER kufr are «no doubt about it»; whether the GREATER kufr has occurred — whether they are apostates — is the place of consideration
The greater kufr is certain; the lesser is disputed
Neither can be spoken about at all

What is obligatory where no open declaration has been made?

Applying the ruling of apostasy
التَّوَقُّف — suspending judgement; «it may be desire, and regard for their own interests»
Emigration from that land
Referring the question to the ruler

What does he say of merely judging by other than what Allah sent down, or ordering it?

That it is apostasy in every case
That he does not disbelieve by that — like one who ordered judgement against so-and-so — «until he deems it lawful»
That it is forgiven if done once
That it depends on the scale of the case

Where did Shaykh Muḥammad ibn Ibrāhīm draw the line differently?

He denied the kufr dūna kufr altogether
He applied «kufr dūna kufr» to the single instance, and held that making codes «the REFERENCE» is a different act — a disbelief removing from the religion — even if they say they erred and the Sharīʿah is more just
He restricted the clause to the Tatars
He held the whole question to be one of ijtihād

What did Ibn Bāz record about that risālah?

That he endorsed it fully
That he read it and has a تَوَقُّف about it — «because the outward is not enough; it is not enough until it is known that he deems it lawful»
That he had never seen it
That its attribution is doubtful

What does the compiler’s footnote carrying al-Fawzān’s words actually answer?

Ibn Bāz’s tawaqquf
A claim that Ibn Ibrāhīm had RETRACTED his position — which is not what Ibn Bāz’s tawaqquf asserted
The authenticity of the risālah
The grading of Ibn ʿAbbās’s tafsīr

Asked «who is it that fights him?», what did Ibn Bāz answer?

Whoever has the strength among the Muslims
The Muslim STATE
The scholars of the land
The people of the city in which he rules

And asked «if it is not able?»

Then the duty passes to groups within it
«He remains as he is, between him and Allah» — and a supplication, not a lesser action
Then emigration becomes obligatory on everyone
Then the matter is referred to another state’s scholars

What is the trigger, in his wording, for the ruling of disbelief in that exchange?

The refusal itself
Armed defence of the refusal — «if he fights, he has disbelieved; and if he does not fight, he has not disbelieved; his ruling is the ruling of the sinners»
The passage of a year
The judgement of a council

In the zakāh parallel, what is the significance of fighting in defence of the withholding?

It is an additional crime only
It is a SIGN of denial — «they are not declared disbelievers for the withholding unless they fight in defence of it»
It abrogates the obligation
It transfers the ruling to the one in authority

The questioner asked whether this warrants الخُرُوج against a ruler. How was it answered?

That it does, once the kufr is seen
It was not granted — the answer is given entirely at the level of a capable force or STATE compelling another state, with Ibn Taymiyyah’s reported consensus cited for that, and wine and ribā given as parallel examples
That the question may not be asked
That it warrants it only after the scholars agree

How does Ibn Bāz characterise this whole subject?

As a settled matter requiring no research
As «dangerous matters; these are not easy; weighty matters» — and he leaves each person to exert himself in the research
As a matter of the branches, not the foundations
As a question for rulers alone to consider
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
مَنْ حَكَمَ بِغَيْرِ مَا أَنْزَلَ اللهُ لِرَشْوَةٍ وَهُوَ يَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُ عَاصٍ فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ كُفْرًا أَكْبَر. One who judges by other than what Allah sent down for a bribe, knowing he is disobeying, is a disbeliever with the greater disbelief.
«كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْرٍ» تَفْسِيرُ ابْنِ عَبَّاسٍ لِآيَةِ المَائِدَة. «A disbelief short of disbelief» is Ibn ʿAbbās’s tafsīr of the verse of al-Māʾidah.
القَوَانِينُ الَّتِي لَا تُخَالِفُ الشَّرْعَ لَا بَأْسَ بِهَا عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. Codes that do not contravene the Sharīʿah are, for Ibn Bāz, unobjectionable.
قَرَائِنُ الأَحْوَالِ تَكْفِي لِلْحُكْمِ بِالرِّدَّة. Circumstantial indications suffice for a ruling of apostasy.
أَقَرَّ ابْنُ بَازٍ رِسَالَةَ الشَّيْخِ مُحَمَّدِ بْنِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ بِلَا تَوَقُّف. Ibn Bāz endorsed Shaykh Muḥammad ibn Ibrāhīm’s risālah without reservation.
الَّذِي يُقَاتِلُ مَنِ امْتَنَعَ عَنِ الحُكْمِ بِشَرْعِ اللهِ هُوَ الدَّوْلَةُ المُسْلِمَة. The one who fights whoever refuses to judge by the law of Allah is the Muslim state.
إِذَا لَمْ تَكُنِ الدَّوْلَةُ قَادِرَةً انْتَقَلَ الأَمْرُ إِلَى الأَفْرَادِ وَالجَمَاعَات. If the state is not able, the matter passes to individuals and groups.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

قَرَائِنُ الأَحْوَالِ تَقْتَضِي أَنَّهُمْ يَسْتَجِيزُونَهُ، لَكِنَّ الحُكْمَ بِالرِّدَّةِ أَمْرٌ وَرَاءَ ذَلِكَ

Ibn Bāz, asked whether circumstantial evidence was enough. He grants the appearance and refuses the verdict in the same breath — and that is the shape of the entire تَفْصِيل. Two different questions: what does this look like? and what may be ruled? He answers the first honestly — it looks like istiḥlāl — and answers the second with «الظَّاهِرُ مَا يَكْفِي». Beside it keep his other sentence: «مَسَائِلُ خَطِيرَة، هَذِهِ مَا هِيَ بِسَهْلَة.»
