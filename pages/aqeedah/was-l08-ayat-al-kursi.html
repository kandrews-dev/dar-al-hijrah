آيَةُ الكُرْسِيّ · أَعْظَمُ آيَةٍ فِي كِتَابِه

Āyat al-Kursī — the Greatest Āyah

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why Āyat al-Kursī is the greatest āyah, and give the ḥadīth that establishes it.
  • Define الحَيّ and القَيُّوم, and say why they are called the greatest name.
  • Walk the āyah clause by clause, labelling each as نَفْي or إِثْبَات.
  • Name the three kinds of عُلُوّ affirmed in العَلِيّ.
٢ · أَعْظَمُ آيَةٍ فِي كِتَابِه Section 2 — The Greatest Āyah in His Book

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمَا وَصَفَ بِهِ نَفْسَهُ فِي أَعْظَمِ آيَةٍ فِي كِتَابِهِ، حَيْثُ يَقُولُ: ﴿اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ...﴾ — وَلِهَذَا كَانَ مَنْ قَرَأَ هَذِهِ الآيَةَ فِي لَيْلَةٍ لَمْ يَزَلْ عَلَيْهِ مِنَ اللهِ حَافِظٌ، وَلَا يَقْرَبُهُ شَيْطَانٌ حَتَّى يُصْبِحَ.

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ وَلَا يَؤُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ

"Allah — there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all. Neither drowsiness overtakes Him nor sleep. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth. Who is there that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what is behind them, and they encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills. His Kursī extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation does not weary Him. And He is the Most High, the Most Great."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:255 · سورة البقرة — آيَةُ الكُرْسِيّ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That it is the greatest is not a scholarly estimate. The Prophet ﷺ asked a Companion outright, and al-Fawzān brings the report from Muslim: he ﷺ asked Ubayy ibn Kaʿb رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, “which āyah in the Book of Allah is greatest?” Ubayy said: Allah and His Messenger know best. He repeated the question several times, until Ubayy said: Āyat al-Kursī. And the Prophet ﷺ said:

لِيَهْنِكَ العِلْمُ أَبَا المُنْذِرِ

"May the knowledge bring you joy, O Abū al-Mundhir!"

أُبَيُّ بْنُ كَعْبٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ · Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the reason it is greatest, al-Fawzān says, is what it contains of affirming Allah's names and attributes and declaring Him free of what does not befit Him. Which is to say: the greatest āyah in the Qurʾān is great because of what it teaches about Allah. That is a judgement about what matters most.

He then takes it word by word:

  • لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ — there is none rightfully worshipped but He; and the worship of anything besides Him is the most futile of all futility.
  • الحَيُّ — the Everlasting, the Abiding, who has the perfection of life, and over whom annihilation has no path.
  • القَيُّومُ — the One established by Himself, who establishes all else. So He is free of need of His creation, and His creation stands in need of Him.

And then a point worth stopping on. It is reported that الحَيُّ القَيُّومُ is the greatest name — the name by which, when Allah is called, He answers, and when He is asked by it, He gives. al-Fawzān explains why these two: because الحَيّ points to the attributes of His Essence and القَيُّوم to the attributes of His actions — so all the attributes return to these two great names.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two more definitions he gives, both finer than the usual translations:

  • السِّنَة is drowsiness — a light sleep, and it is in the eye only. While النَّوْم is stronger than it: it is the brother of death, and it is in the heart. So the āyah negates the lesser before the greater.
  • الشَّفَاعَة is derived from الشَّفْع, the even number, the opposite of الوِتْر, the odd — as though the intercessor joined his request to another’s, making it even where it had been odd. And intercession itself is سُؤَالُ الخَيْرِ لِلْغَيْرِ: asking good for someone else.

On كُرْسِيِّه: some said it is the Throne itself, others that it is something else; and it is reported that it is the place of the two Feet — a Kursī that reached such greatness and expanse that it encompasses the heavens and the earth. وَلَا يَؤُودُهُ means it does not burden Him, weigh on Him, or tire Him — the preservation of the upper and lower worlds, by the perfection of His power and strength.

And العَلِيّ gathers three kinds of highness at once:

  • عُلُوُّ الذَّات — He is above all His creation: الرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى العَرْشِ اسْتَوَى (Ṭā Hā 20:5).
  • عُلُوُّ القَدْر — His are all the attributes of perfection and descriptions of majesty.
  • عُلُوُّ القَهْر — He is able over everything, disposing of everything; nothing can withhold itself from Him.
الآيَةُ كَامِلَةً: نَفْيٌ وَإِثْبَاتٌ سَطْرًا سَطْرًا The Āyah, Clause by Clause

Here is the heart of why the author cited it. He has claimed that Allah joined negation and affirmation in describing Himself. al-Fawzān proves it by walking the greatest āyah from beginning to end and labelling every clause. Read down the middle column.

The clauseWhich?What it does
اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَbothnegates divinity from all besides Him, and affirms it for Him
الحَيُّ القَيُّومُإِثْبَاتaffirms life and self-subsisting sustainment
لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌنَفْيnegates drowsiness and sleep
لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الأَرْضِإِثْبَاتaffirms His complete ownership of the upper and lower worlds
مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِنَفْيnegates intercession without His permission — for the perfection of His greatness and His freedom from need of His creation
يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْإِثْبَاتaffirms the perfection of His knowledge of everything, past or future
وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَنَفْيshows the creation’s need of Him, and affirms His freedom from need of them
وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضَإِثْبَاتaffirms His Kursī, and the perfection of His greatness and majesty — and the smallness of the creation beside Him
وَلَا يَؤُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَانَفْيnegates inability and weariness
وَهُوَ العَلِيُّ العَظِيمُإِثْبَاتaffirms highness and greatness

Ten clauses, alternating almost all the way down. Nothing has been imposed on the text — the pattern is the text. And al-Fawzān closes the point the way the author would want: an āyah containing these meanings is fit to be the greatest āyah in the Qurʾān, and fit to guard its reciter from evils and from the devils.

إِذَا أَوَيْتَ إِلَى فِرَاشِكَ فَاقْرَأْ آيَةَ الكُرْسِيِّ ﴿اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ﴾ حَتَّى تَخْتِمَ الآيَةَ، فَإِنَّكَ لَنْ يَزَالَ عَلَيْكَ مِنَ اللهِ حَافِظٌ، وَلَا يَقْرَبُكَ شَيْطَانٌ حَتَّى تُصْبِحَ

"When you go to your bed, recite Āyat al-Kursī — Allah, there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer — until you complete the āyah. For a guardian from Allah will not cease to be over you, and no devil will come near you until morning."

أَبُو هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ · رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān adds a note on the word itself: الشَّيْطَان is applied to every rebel and transgressor, whether of the jinn or of mankind. It is from شَطَنَ — to be far — and he is named so for his distance from the mercy of Allah.

Which puts the protection in the ḥadīth in its proper light. The one who recites this āyah at night is guarded, through the night, by the very meanings he has just recited. The protection is not a charm attached to the words; it is what the words say about the One who protects.

Its length
Its place in the middle of the Qurʾān
What it contains of affirming Allah’s names and attributes and declaring Him free of what does not befit Him
The number of times it is recited

Because they appear most often in the Qurʾān

Because they are mentioned in every sūrah

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
﴿اللهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ﴾ فِيهِ نَفْيٌ وَإِثْبَاتٌ مَعًا. "Allah, there is no god but He" contains negation and affirmation together.
الشَّيْطَانُ مِنَ الجِنِّ فَقَطْ. The word shayṭān applies only to the jinn.
الكُرْسِيُّ وَسِعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضَ. The Kursī encompasses the heavens and the earth.
قِرَاءَةُ آيَةِ الكُرْسِيِّ عِنْدَ النَّوْمِ فِيهَا حِفْظٌ مِنَ اللهِ حَتَّى الصَّبَاحِ. Reciting Āyat al-Kursī at bedtime brings a guardian from Allah until morning.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ

You likely recite it every night already. Tonight, recite it knowing that all of Allah’s attributes return to those two names — the Ever-Living and the Sustainer — and that the guardian promised over you until morning is guaranteed by the One those two names describe.
