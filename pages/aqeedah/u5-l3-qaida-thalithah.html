القَاعِدَةُ الثَّالِثَة

He Fought Them All Alike

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the third foundation in the author’s own words.
  • Name the five categories of what the people worshipped — and give the proof against each one.
  • Explain why the Prophet ﷺ made no distinction between them.
  • Learn the ḥadīth of ذَاتِ أَنْوَاطٍ and what it warns against.
  • State the principle المَدَارُ عَلَى عُبُودِيَّةِ القَلْبِ and use it to answer the objection about rank.
  • Give the conclusion (النَّتِيجَة) of this foundation from al-Tawbah 9:36.
  • Answer “but we turn to righteous people” using al-Naḥl 16:21.
القَاعِدَةُ الثَّالِثَة The Third Foundation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ ظَهَرَ عَلَى أُنَاسٍ مُتَفَرِّقِينَ فِي عِبَادَاتِهِمْ: مِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ المَلَائِكَةَ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ الأَنْبِيَاءَ وَالصَّالِحِينَ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ الأَحْجَارَ وَالأَشْجَارَ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَعْبُدُ الشَّمْسَ وَالقَمَرَ؛ وَقَاتَلَهُمْ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ وَلَمْ يُفَرِّقْ بَيْنَهُمْ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The force of this foundation is in the last clause: وَلَمْ يُفَرِّقْ بَيْنَهُمْ — he did not distinguish between them. The objects of worship were wildly different in dignity. An angel is a noble creature of light; a stone is a stone. A prophet is the best of mankind; a tree is a tree.

A person reasoning on his own would surely have graded them — surely worshipping an angel or a prophet is not like worshipping an idol? The third foundation closes that door. The Prophet ﷺ fought them all alike, because the fault is not in the rank of the thing worshipped; the fault is in worshipping anything besides Allah.

And this is precisely where people fall today. Nobody now prays to a stone idol. But calling upon a righteous dead man, or a prophet, or an angel, feels different — more respectable. The author’s answer is that the Prophet ﷺ already settled it, and he settled it with no exceptions.

وَقَاتِلُوهُمْ حَتَّىٰ لَا تَكُونَ فِتْنَةٌ وَيَكُونَ الدِّينُ كُلُّهُ لِلَّهِ

"And fight them until there is no fitnah, and the religion — all of it — is for Allah."

Sūrat al-Anfāl 8:39 · سورة الأنفال — the general proof

A note on this citation · تَنْبِيهٌ

The print of the matn we follow cites this proof as البَقَرَة 2:193. The words quoted here — with كُلُّهُ, “all of it” — are the wording of al-Anfāl 8:39; al-Baqarah 2:193 carries the same command without it: وَيَكُونَ الدِّينُ لِلَّهِ. We cite the sūrah the quoted wording belongs to, and flag the difference here rather than quietly correcting the author: both āyahs carry the point he is making, and the two are commonly interchanged in prints of this treatise.

Note كُلُّهُ — "all of it." Not most of the religion, not the major part of worship. The whole of it, undivided, for Allah. That word is the reason no category could be left standing.

الدَّلِيلُ عَلَى كُلِّ صِنْفٍ The Proof Against Each Category

The author named five kinds of worship the Prophet ﷺ confronted. The Qur’ān answers every one of them by name — so none of the five can claim to be the exception.

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ اللَّيْلُ وَالنَّهَارُ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ الَّذِي خَلَقَهُنَّ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

"And among His signs are the night and the day, and the sun and the moon. Do not prostrate to the sun or to the moon, but prostrate to Allah who created them — if it is Him you worship."

Sūrat Fuṣṣilat 41:37 · سورة فصلت — against worship of the sun and moon

وَلَا يَأْمُرَكُمْ أَنْ تَتَّخِذُوا الْمَلَائِكَةَ وَالنَّبِيِّينَ أَرْبَابًا أَيَأْمُرُكُمْ بِالْكُفْرِ بَعْدَ إِذْ أَنْتُمْ مُسْلِمُونَ

"Nor would he command you to take the angels and the prophets as lords. Would he command you to disbelief after you had submitted?"

Sūrat Āl ‘Imrān 3:80 · سورة آل عمران — against worship of the angels

وَإِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ أَأَنْتَ قُلْتَ لِلنَّاسِ اتَّخِذُونِي وَأُمِّيَ إِلَٰهَيْنِ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ قَالَ سُبْحَانَكَ مَا يَكُونُ لِي أَنْ أَقُولَ مَا لَيْسَ لِي بِحَقٍّ إِنْ كُنْتُ قُلْتُهُ فَقَدْ عَلِمْتَهُ تَعْلَمُ مَا فِي نَفْسِي وَلَا أَعْلَمُ مَا فِي نَفْسِكَ إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ عَلَّامُ الْغُيُوبِ

"And when Allah will say: O ‘Īsā, son of Maryam, did you say to the people: take me and my mother as two gods besides Allah? He will say: Glory be to You! It was not for me to say what I had no right to say. Had I said it, You would have known it. You know what is within me, and I do not know what is within You. Indeed it is You who is the Knower of all unseen."

Sūrat al-Mā’idah 5:116 · سورة المائدة — against worship of the prophets

أُولَٰئِكَ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ يَبْتَغُونَ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهِمُ الْوَسِيلَةَ أَيُّهُمْ أَقْرَبُ وَيَرْجُونَ رَحْمَتَهُ وَيَخَافُونَ عَذَابَهُ

"Those whom they call upon are themselves seeking a means of nearness to their Lord — which of them is nearest — and they hope for His mercy and fear His punishment."

Sūrat al-Isrā’ 17:57 · سورة الإسراء — against worship of the righteous

That one repays a careful reading. The very ones being called upon are themselves petitioners — hoping, fearing, competing for nearness to Allah. A beggar cannot be petitioned as though he were the king.

أَفَرَأَيْتُمُ اللَّاتَ وَالْعُزَّىٰ وَمَنَاتَ الثَّالِثَةَ الْأُخْرَىٰ

"So have you considered al-Lāt and al-‘Uzzā, and Manāt, the third one?"

Sūrat al-Najm 53:19–20 · سورة النجم — against worship of stones and trees

الشَّرْح · the explanation

These three were the great idols of the Arabs, and the scholars of tafsīr record what they were: al-Lāt was associated with a stone at Ṭā’if, and al-‘Uzzā with a tree venerated in a valley near Makkah. So the two categories the author mentioned — الأَحْجَارَ وَالأَشْجَارَ — are answered here by name.

And with that, all five categories of the third foundation have a proof against them from the Book of Allah. None is left as a special case.

حَدِيثُ ذَاتِ أَنْوَاطٍ The Ḥadīth of Dhāt Anwāṭ

The sharḥ brings a ḥadīth here that makes the danger immediate, because it concerns not the mushrikīn but the Companions themselves — new Muslims, on their way to battle alongside the Prophet ﷺ. The mushrikīn had a tree on which they hung their weapons for blessing, called ذَاتَ أَنْوَاطٍ. Some of the Companions asked the Prophet ﷺ to appoint one like it for them.

سُبْحَانَ اللهِ! هَذَا كَمَا قَالَ قَوْمُ مُوسَى: اجْعَلْ لَنَا إِلَٰهًا كَمَا لَهُمْ آلِهَةٌ. وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، لَتَرْكَبُنَّ سُنَّةَ مَنْ كَانَ قَبْلَكُمْ

"Glory be to Allah! This is just as the people of Mūsā said: appoint for us a god as they have gods. By the One in whose Hand is my soul, you will certainly follow the way of those who came before you."

حَدِيثُ أَبِي وَاقِدٍ اللَّيْثِيِّ · narrated by al-Tirmidhī — graded ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albānī

اجْعَلْ لَنَا إِلَٰهًا كَمَا لَهُمْ آلِهَةٌ

"Appoint for us a god as they have gods."

Sūrat al-A‘rāf 7:138 · سورة الأعراف — the words the Prophet ﷺ compared them to

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three lessons the scholars draw from it. First, they only asked — they had not yet done it — and the Prophet ﷺ still met the request with سُبْحَانَ اللهِ, the word of astonishment at something grave.

Second, they intended good. They wanted blessing, not a rival to Allah. Good intention did not make the act safe — which is the second foundation showing up again from a different direction.

Third, seeking blessing (التَّبَرُّك) from trees, stones, graves or shrines is the doorway. The people of Mūsā did not begin by worshipping a calf; they began by asking for something to attach their hearts to.

لَا فَرْقَ: المَدَارُ عَلَى عُبُودِيَّةِ القَلْبِ No Distinction — the Pivot Is the Heart’s Servitude

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ points out that this foundation is built as مُقَدِّمَة and نَتِيجَة — a premise and a conclusion. The premise is everything above: the Arabs differed in what they worshipped. The conclusion is a single command.

And the categories ran wider still than the five. Some worshipped the jinn: بَلْ كَانُوا يَعْبُدُونَ الْجِنَّ — “rather, they used to worship the jinn” (Sabaʾ 34:41); and كَانَ رِجَالٌ مِنَ الْإِنْسِ يَعُوذُونَ بِرِجَالٍ مِنَ الْجِنِّ — “men among mankind used to seek refuge with men among the jinn” (al-Jinn 72:6).

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ سَبَقَتْ لَهُمْ مِنَّا الْحُسْنَىٰ أُولَٰئِكَ عَنْهَا مُبْعَدُونَ

"Indeed those for whom the good from Us has preceded — they will be kept far from it [the Fire]."

Sūrat al-Anbiyā’ 21:101 · سورة الأنبياء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

There is an instructive occasion behind that āyah. When Allah revealed إِنَّكُمْ وَمَا تَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ حَصَبُ جَهَنَّمَ — “indeed you and what you worship besides Allah are the fuel of Hell” (al-Anbiyāʾ 21:98) — the mushrikīn of the Arabs were pleased. They said: then we shall be with ʿĪsā, and with ʿUzayr, and with so-and-so — since those too had been worshipped. And then 21:101 came down, excluding the righteous from the Fire, and their argument collapsed. (Reported by al-Ḥākim in al-Mustadrak from Ibn ʿAbbās; al-Ḥākim graded its isnād ṣaḥīḥ.)

The worshipped righteous are not punished for being worshipped. The one who worshipped them is.

وَقَاتِلُوا الْمُشْرِكِينَ كَافَّةً كَمَا يُقَاتِلُونَكُمْ كَافَّةً

"And fight the mushrikīn collectively, as they fight you collectively."

Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:36 · سورة التوبة — the conclusion of the third foundation

الشَّرْح · the explanation

كَافَّةً — all of them together, general in every one of them. The sharḥ calls this the natījah: no command ever came distinguishing those who worshipped stones and idols and the sun from those who made the prophets and the righteous intercessors and means of nearness. One command, one ruling, one war.

So why does the rank of the object make no difference? Here the sharḥ gives the principle that carries this whole foundation: المَدَارُ عَلَى عُبُودِيَّةِ القَلْبِ — the pivot is the servitude of the heart. If rivalry and association with Allah stand in the heart, then whether the one associated was righteous or corrupt, a prophet or not, a tree or an angel — the case is one, because the heart's servitude must be for Allah alone.

And the decisive move: this servitude is judged from the side of the worshipper, not from the side of the one he turned to. If he turned to Allah alone he is sincere, a muwaḥḥid. If he turned to anything else he is a mushrik — whatever that other thing was.

  • أَلَا لِلَّهِ الدِّينُ الْخَالِصُ (al-Zumar 39:3) — the religion, purely, belongs to Allah.
  • فَلَا تَدْعُوا مَعَ اللَّهِ أَحَدًا (al-Jinn 72:18) — and أَحَدًا, “anyone”, takes in every single one.
  • وَمَنْ يَدْعُ مَعَ اللَّهِ إِلَٰهًا آخَرَ لَا بُرْهَانَ لَهُ بِهِ (al-Muʾminūn 23:117) — “no proof for it”: no one who worships another has any evidence whatsoever for that other's entitlement to worship.
«لَكِنَّنَا نَتَوَجَّهُ إِلَى صَالِحِينَ» "But We Turn to Righteous People"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ states the objection in the words people actually use. Those who turn to the awliyāʾ, to graves and shrines and tombs of the Companions, say: this is not the worship of the earlier mushrikīn — they worshipped idols, stones; we turn to the righteous.

The first half of the answer has already been given: the earlier ones also turned to the righteous — al-Lāt was a grave, and Wadd and Suwāʿ were righteous men of Nūḥ's people. But the sharḥ adds a second answer from the Qur’ān's own wording, and it is subtle:

أَمْوَاتٌ غَيْرُ أَحْيَاءٍ وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ أَيَّانَ يُبْعَثُونَ

"…dead, not alive; and they do not perceive when they will be resurrected."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:21 · سورة النحل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Abū Ḥayyān, in al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ, draws out what the description requires. أَمْوَاتٌ غَيْرُ أَحْيَاءٍ — “dead, not alive” — can only be said of something that was once alive and then died. Idols of stone and wood are not described as dead; they were never alive. And the next clause settles it: وَمَا يَشْعُرُونَ أَيَّانَ يُبْعَثُونَ — “they do not perceive when they will be resurrected” — which is said only of those who will be resurrected.

So the Qur’ān was already speaking about dead human beings who are turned to, not only about carved stone. The objection assumes the Qur’ān addressed a cruder case than ours. It addressed ours.

The sharḥ closes the foundation on this: they asked for mediation, nearness and intercession, and did not claim independence for their gods — and so do these. “The case is the case, even if the names have changed and the claims have changed — and how like tonight is to last night.”

الصَّالِحُونَ الَّذِينَ يُدْعَوْنَ هُمْ أَنْفُسُهُمْ يَبْتَغُونَ إِلَى رَبِّهِمُ الوَسِيلَةَ. The righteous who are called upon are themselves seeking nearness to their Lord.
أَقَرَّ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ طَلَبَ الصَّحَابَةِ ذَاتَ أَنْوَاطٍ. The Prophet ﷺ approved the Companions’ request for a dhāt anwāṭ.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَيَكُونَ الدِّينُ كُلُّهُ لِلَّهِ

"And the religion — all of it — for Allah." The third foundation is one question asked of every attachment of the heart: is this act of mine worship? If it is, then it has only one rightful direction, no matter how noble the thing standing in the way.
