الفَرَحُ وَالضَّحِكُ وَالعَجَبُ وَالنَّظَر

That Allah Rejoices, Laughs, Wonders and Looks

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why الفَرَح for Allah is an attribute of perfection and not of need.
  • Give the reason, from the ḥadīth itself, that Allah laughs at the two men.
  • Distinguish the two kinds of العَجَب and say which is meant of Allah.
  • Name the three attributes established in the ḥadīth of §3.
٢ · إِثْبَاتُ أَنَّ اللهَ يَفْرَحُ وَيَضْحَك Section 2 — That Allah Rejoices and Laughs

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «لِلَّهِ أَشَدُّ فَرَحًا بِتَوْبَةِ عَبْدِهِ مِنْ أَحَدِكُمْ بِرَاحِلَتِهِ…» الحَدِيثَ، مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ. وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «يَضْحَكُ اللهُ إِلَى رَجُلَيْنِ يَقْتُلُ أَحَدُهُمَا الآخَرَ، كِلَاهُمَا يَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

لِلَّهِ أَشَدُّ فَرَحًا بِتَوْبَةِ عَبْدِهِ مِنْ أَحَدِكُمْ بِرَاحِلَتِهِ

"Allah is more intensely joyful at the repentance of His servant than any one of you is at his riding-camel…"

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The grammar first, as al-Fawzān always gives it. لِلَّهِ — the lām is the lām of beginning; and فَرَحًا is manṣūb as التَّمْيِيز, specifying in what respect the intensity holds.

الفَرَح — in the language it is gladness and the delight of the heart. And بِتَوْبَةِ عَبْدِهِ — and al-tawbah is the desisting from the sin and the returning to Allah.

Note the author's citation: he writes الحَدِيثَ, and al-Fawzān explains that this is an implied word — that is: complete the ḥadīth, because the author restricted himself to the point of evidence from it. The full ḥadīth is the famous parable of the man who loses his mount in the desert with his food and drink on it, despairs of life, and then finds it standing there — and the author quotes only as much as makes his point.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the shāhid: the affirming of joy for Allah سبحانه, in the manner that befits His majesty; and it is an attribute of perfection — the joy of no one of His creation resembles it.

And then al-Fawzān heads off the obvious misunderstanding before it forms. The servant's repentance is not something Allah gains by: لَا تَنْفَعُهُ طَاعَةُ المُطِيعِ وَلَا تَضُرُّهُ مَعْصِيَةُ العَاصِي — for He سبحانه is not benefited by the obedience of the obedient, nor harmed by the disobedience of the disobedient.

Which makes the ḥadīth stranger and better, not weaker. A joy that gains nothing is a joy that is entirely for the sake of the one repenting. The man in the parable rejoices because he was going to die and now will live; Allah rejoices at the return of someone whose return adds nothing to Him. That is the whole doctrine of mercy in one comparison.

يَضْحَكُ اللهُ إِلَى رَجُلَيْنِ يَقْتُلُ أَحَدُهُمَا الآخَرَ، كِلَاهُمَا يَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ

"Allah laughs at two men, one of whom kills the other — and both of them enter the Garden."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read on its own the ḥadīth is a riddle: a killer and his victim, both in Paradise. And the ḥadīth states the reason for that in its own words:

يُقَاتِلُ هَذَا فِي سَبِيلِ اللهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ فَيُسْتَشْهَدُ، ثُمَّ يَتُوبُ اللهُ عَلَى القَاتِلِ فَيُسْلِمُ، فَيُقَاتِلُ فِي سَبِيلِ اللهِ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ فَيُسْتَشْهَدُ.

— this one fights in the path of Allah and is martyred; then Allah turns to the killer in acceptance and he becomes Muslim, and he fights in the path of Allah and is martyred.

And this is from the perfection of Allah's beneficence and the breadth of His mercy: for the Muslim fights in the path of Allah and the disbeliever kills him, so Allah honours the Muslim with martyrdom; then Allah bestows a favour upon that very disbelieving killer and guides him to Islam — so the two of them enter the Garden together.

So this is a wondrous affair — and laughter comes about from the things that cause wonder. That last clause is the key to reading the attribute correctly. The laughter is not amusement at the killing; it is the response to something astonishing. The man who took a believer's life ends beside him in Paradise, by the mercy of the One both of them now worship.

And the shāhid: the affirming of laughter for Allah سبحانه — an attribute of His attributes of the Actions, which we affirm for Him in the manner befitting His majesty and His greatness; it is not like the laughter of the created thing.

٣ · إِثْبَاتُ أَنَّ اللهَ يَعْجَبُ وَيَضْحَكُ وَيَنْظُر Section 3 — That Allah Wonders, Laughs and Looks

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «عَجِبَ رَبُّنَا مِنْ قُنُوطِ عِبَادِهِ وَقُرْبِ غِيَرِهِ، يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْكُمْ أَزِلِينَ… فَيَظَلُّ يَضْحَكُ».

عَجِبَ رَبُّنَا مِنْ قُنُوطِ عِبَادِهِ وَقُرْبِ غِيَرِهِ، يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْكُمْ أَزِلِينَ… فَيَظَلُّ يَضْحَكُ

"Our Lord wonders at the despair of His servants, and how near His change is. He looks at you straitened… and He keeps on laughing."

al-Fawzān cites it as رَوَاهُ أَحْمَدُ وَغَيْرُهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

تَنْبِيهٌ — al-Fawzān gives this ḥadīth’s attribution, reported by Aḥmad and others, without stating a grading for it. This track’s rule is that every ḥadīth carries a grading attributed to a named scholar — so the page reports what the sharḥ reports and does not supply a grading of its own. The two aḥādīth of §2 above are مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِمَا, and the attributes taught here — the wonder, the laughter and the looking — rest on more than this one text.

Now the attribute. العَجَب is of two kinds, and the distinction decides how the ḥadīth is read:

١ what the doer praisesits meaning is finding it good, and informing of his being pleased with it (الاسْتِحْسَانُ وَالإِخْبَارُ عَنْ رِضَاهُ بِهِ)
٢ what he dislikesits meaning is rejection and censure of it (الإِنْكَارُ وَالذَّمُّ لَهُ)

And the ḥadīth is plainly the second kind: the wonder is at despair. مِنْ قُنُوطِ عِبَادِهِ — al-qunūṭ is the severity of despair; and وَقُرْبِ غِيَرِهِ — vowelled with a kasrah on the غ and a fatḥah on the ي — is His changing of the condition from hardship to ease.

Put the two halves of the clause together and you have the thing being wondered at: they are giving up precisely when the relief is nearly here. The wonder is not at suffering; it is at despairing a moment before the change — and the censure in it is a mercy, because the One wondering is the One who was about to change it.

يَنْظُرُ إِلَيْكُمْ أَزِلِينَ — and الأَزْل, with a sukūn on the ز, is straitness; one says أَزِلَ الرَّجُلُ يَأْزِلُ أَزْلًا — the man became in straits and in drought.

فَيَظَلُّ يَضْحَكُ — this is of His attributes of the Actions, in which nothing of His creation resembles Him.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān counts the attributes in the ḥadīth: in it is the affirming of two of Allah's attributes of the Actions — العَجَب and الضَّحِك — and they are two attributes befitting His majesty, not like the wonder of the created thing nor the laughter of the created thing. And in the ḥadīth is also the affirming of النَّظَر for Allah سبحانه, and it too is of His attributes of the Actions, for He looks at His servants, and nothing in the earth or in the heaven is hidden from Him.

Three attributes, then — and notice what the sequence of the ḥadīth does with them. He looks at them in their straits, He wonders at their despair, and He keeps on laughing. Each one presupposes that He is attending to a particular person in a particular difficulty, which is the whole reason these texts were ever worth preserving. A God described only in negations does not look at anyone.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

عَجِبَ رَبُّنَا مِنْ قُنُوطِ عِبَادِهِ وَقُرْبِ غِيَرِهِ

The two halves belong together or the sentence loses its point: they despair, and the change is already near. Whatever you are in the middle of, this is the clause that is about you.
