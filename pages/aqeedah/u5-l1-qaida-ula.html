Unit 5 · al-Qawā‘id al-Arba‘ · Lesson 1Year 2

القَاعِدَةُ الأُولَى

Affirming the Lord Is Not Islam

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the first foundation in the author’s own words.
  • Give the proof from Sūrat Yūnus, and say exactly what it establishes.
  • Distinguish تَوْحِيدُ الرُّبُوبِيَّةِ from تَوْحِيدُ الأُلُوهِيَّةِ, and say which one the mushrikīn already affirmed.
  • Explain why affirming the Creator did not make them Muslims.
  • Name the Qur’ān’s method: arguing from what they conceded to what they denied.
  • Explain why defining إِلَٰه as “the One able to create” is, as the sharḥ says, the gravest error made about Islam.
  • Give the correct meaning of the kalimah as al-Ṭabarī and al-Shawkānī state it.
القَاعِدَةُ الأُولَى The First Foundation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَنْ تَعْلَمَ أَنَّ الكُفَّارَ الَّذِينَ قَاتَلَهُمْ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ مُقِرُّونَ بِأَنَّ اللهَ تَعَالَى هُوَ الخَالِقُ الرَّازِقُ المُدَبِّرُ، وَأَنَّ ذَلِكَ لَمْ يُدْخِلْهُمْ فِي الإِسْلَامِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read that twice, because it overturns how most people imagine the disbelievers of Makkah. They were not atheists. They did not deny that Allah exists, nor that He alone created, provided and governed. They affirmed all of it — and the Qur’ān itself reports their affirmation.

So the fighting between the Prophet ﷺ and his people was never a dispute about who the Creator is. They agreed on that. The dispute was about who deserves to be worshipped.

قُلْ مَنْ يَرْزُقُكُمْ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ أَمَّنْ يَمْلِكُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَمَنْ يُخْرِجُ الْحَيَّ مِنَ الْمَيِّتِ وَيُخْرِجُ الْمَيِّتَ مِنَ الْحَيِّ وَمَنْ يُدَبِّرُ الْأَمْرَ فَسَيَقُولُونَ اللَّهُ فَقُلْ أَفَلَا تَتَّقُونَ

"Say: Who provides for you from the sky and the earth? Or who controls hearing and sight? And who brings the living out of the dead and brings the dead out of the living? And who arranges every matter? They will say: Allah. So say: Then will you not fear Him?"

Sūrat Yūnus 10:31 · سورة يونس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Notice the structure of the āyah, because the author chose it deliberately. Allah lists the acts of Lordship one after another — provision, control of hearing and sight, giving life and death, governing all affairs — and then reports their answer in a single word: اللَّهُ. They did not hesitate.

And then comes the rebuke: أَفَلَا تَتَّقُونَ — "then will you not fear Him?" The argument is that their own admission condemns them. If you already know that He alone does all this, then worshipping anything beside Him is not merely wrong, it is incoherent.

This is the division the scholars express with two terms:

TermMeaningDid they affirm it?
تَوْحِيدُ الرُّبُوبِيَّةِAllah alone creates, provides, governsYes — and it did not save them
تَوْحِيدُ الأُلُوهِيَّةِAllah alone is worshipped — du‘ā’, sacrifice, hope, fearNo — and this is what they were fought over

The practical warning is for us, not only for them. A man may hold a flawless belief that Allah is the only Creator, and still direct an act of worship to a grave, a saint or a jinn — and the first foundation tells him plainly that his correct belief about creation will not rescue the act.

وَلَئِنْ سَأَلْتَهُمْ مَنْ خَلَقَهُمْ لَيَقُولُنَّ اللَّهُ فَأَنَّىٰ يُصْرَفُونَ

"And if you asked them who created them, they would surely say: Allah. So how are they turned away?"

Sūrat al-Zukhruf 43:87 · سورة الزخرف

A second witness to the same point, and the closing question فَأَنَّىٰ يُصْرَفُونَ — "how then are they turned away?" — is the whole problem of the first foundation in three words.

طَرِيقَةُ القُرْآنِ فِي إِقَامَةِ الحُجَّةِ How the Qur’ān Argues Against Them

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ names the method exactly: Allah established the proof against them by what they conceded, concerning what they denied. They granted that He alone creates, provides and governs; so He argues from that grant to the thing they refused — that He alone be worshipped. The sharḥ calls this طَرِيقَةُ القُرْآنِ, the Qur’ān's way of establishing the proof against the mushrikīn.

The logic is plain once stated: the One who alone does all of it is the One who alone deserves to be worshipped. So tawḥīd of Lordship is not a rival to tawḥīd of worship — it is the evidence for it.

وَمَا يُؤْمِنُ أَكْثَرُهُمْ بِاللَّهِ إِلَّا وَهُمْ مُشْرِكُونَ

"And most of them do not believe in Allah except that they associate others with Him."

Sūrat Yūsuf 12:106 · سورة يوسف

Read it slowly: they do believe in Allah — and they are mushrikūn at the same time, in the same breath. Belief in His Lordship, shirk in His worship. The first foundation in one āyah.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Qur’ān presses the absurdity of the alternative. Allah asks:

  • أَيُشْرِكُونَ مَا لَا يَخْلُقُ شَيْئًا وَهُمْ يُخْلَقُونَ — “do they associate those who create nothing, and are themselves created?” (al-Aʿrāf 7:191)
  • أَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ أَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ — “is Allah better, or what they associate?” (al-Naml 27:59)
  • أَجَعَلَ الْآلِهَةَ إِلَٰهًا وَاحِدًا — “has he made the gods one God?” (Ṣād 38:5) — their own complaint, which shows they were never arguing about the Creator, only about the number of objects of worship.

إِنْ يَسْلُبْهُمُ الذُّبَابُ شَيْئًا لَا يَسْتَنْقِذُوهُ مِنْهُ ضَعُفَ الطَّالِبُ وَالْمَطْلُوبُ

"If a fly should take something from them, they could not retrieve it from it. Weak is the seeker, and weak the sought."

Sūrat al-Ḥajj 22:73 · سورة الحج

Not an eagle — a fly. And the closing verdict falls on both at once: ضَعُفَ الطَّالِبُ وَالْمَطْلُوبُ — weak is the one who asks, and weak is the one asked of. That is the description of everything turned to besides Allah.

أَعْظَمُ غَلَطٍ عَلَى دِينِ الإِسْلَامِ The Gravest Error Made About Islam

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here the sharḥ stops to correct a mistake that matters more than it first appears. Some of the leading mutakallimūn held that إِلَٰه means “the one able to originate and create” — so that whoever affirms Allah alone can create has thereby testified لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ.

Ibn Taymiyyah answers it directly: that is not what ilāh means. And the refutation is the first foundation itself — the mushrikīn affirmed exactly that, and they were mushrikūn. If the kalimah meant what those theologians said, the people the Prophet ﷺ fought would have been Muslims by their own admission.

The sharḥ calls this view أَعْظَمُ غَلَطٍ عَلَى دِينِ الإِسْلَامِ — the gravest error made about the religion of Islam — because it relocates the test. It puts the trial in Lordship: settle that Allah is the Maker of things, and you are a believer. But that was never where the trial lay.

The correct meaning, with the mufassirūn naming it: لَا مَعْبُودَ بِحَقٍّ إِلَّا اللهُ — there is no one truly worshipped except Allah. al-Ṭabarī: “there is none rightfully worshipped, whose worship is permitted and to whom divinity belongs, except Allah — so call upon Him, O people, making the religion sincerely His.” al-Shawkānī in Fatḥ al-Qadīr says the same in a line: “there is none rightfully worshipped except Him.” The meaning of the kalimah returns to العُبُودِيَّة, not to الرُّبُوبِيَّة.

فِي زَمَانِنَا And in Our Own Time

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ brings the rule home, and the application is uncomfortably ordinary. Weakness comes over some people: they hear a man say in shā’ Allāh, or mention Allah, or say that Allah is his Lord and his Protector — and they take him for a Muslim on that basis and are satisfied.

But “this is not where the trial lies at all.” What settles it is that he be a muwaḥḥid in his worship — worshipping Allah by what the Chosen One ﷺ brought, free of shirk. Affirming the Creator is where the mushrikīn of Makkah started, not where Islam begins.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَسَيَقُولُونَ اللَّهُ

"They will say: Allah." Three words that close off every escape. They knew the answer — and knowing it was not enough. Ask of your own life: is there anything I turn to in need, in hope or in fear, besides the One I know to be my only Provider?
