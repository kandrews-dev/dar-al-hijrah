الوَاجِبُ نَحْوَ أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ

The Obligation toward the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٦٧–١٦٩
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 167–169.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the two soundnesses the matn requires, and find each of them inside al-Ḥashr 59:10.
  • Give al-Fawzān’s four reasons for the Companions’ precedence.
  • Explain why the little a Companion spent outweighs a mountain of gold from anyone after them.
  • Give the principle about deeds that the ḥadīth establishes in passing.
الوَاجِبُ نَحْوَ أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ The Obligation toward the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ: سَلَامَةُ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَأَلْسِنَتِهِمْ لِأَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ، كَمَا وَصَفَهُمُ اللهُ بِهِ فِي قَوْلِهِ تَعَالَى: ﴿وَالَّذِينَ جَاءُوا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ﴾، وَطَاعَةُ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ فِي قَوْلِهِ: «لَا تَسُبُّوا أَصْحَابِي، فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ لَوْ أَنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ أَنْفَقَ مِثْلَ أُحُدٍ ذَهَبًا مَا بَلَغَ مُدَّ أَحَدِهِمْ وَلَا نَصِيفَهُ».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān unpacks the two soundnesses the matn names: سَلَامَةُ قُلُوبِهِمْ — from rancour, spite and hatred; and وَأَلْسِنَتِهِمْ — from disparagement, cursing and reviling.

Note that the obligation is stated in two places — the heart and the tongue — and in that order. It is not enough to hold the tongue while the heart carries the grudge, which is why the matn names the heart first.

And why them? al-Fawzān gives four grounds: for their excellence, and their precedence, and their being singled out by the companionship of the Prophet ﷺ, and for what they have of excellence over the whole Ummah —

— because they are the ones who carried the Sharīʿah from him ﷺ and conveyed it to those after them; and for their jihād alongside the Messenger ﷺ and their supporting of him.

The first of those is the one with teeth. Every ruling the student has learned on this track reached him through these men. To hold that they were unreliable is to hold that the religion did not arrive — which is why this is a chapter of creed and not of manners.

And the Shaykh's purpose in composing this section is the refutation of the Rāfiḍah and the Khawārij, who revile the Companions and hate them and deny their excellences; and the making clear of Ahl al-Sunnah's innocence of this foul madhhab, and that they are with the Companions of their Prophet.

وَالَّذِينَ جَاءُوا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ يَقُولُونَ رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا رَبَّنَا إِنَّكَ رَءُوفٌ رَحِيمٌ

"And those who came after them say: our Lord, forgive us and our brothers who preceded us in faith, and put not in our hearts rancour toward those who believed. Our Lord, You are Kind, Merciful."

Sūrat al-Ḥashr 59:10 · سُورَةُ الحَشْر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَالَّذِينَ جَاءُوا مِنْ بَعْدِهِمْ — that is: after the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār; and they are those who follow them in excellence, until the Day of Rising, from the generality of the Muslims.

وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ — what is meant by the brotherhood here is the brotherhood of religion; so they seek forgiveness for themselves and for those who went before them of the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār.

وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا — that is: deceit, hatred and envy; لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا — that is, the people of faith; and the Companions enter into that by the foremost entry, because they are the noblest of the believers, and because the passage is about them.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And now the observation that makes the author's choice of this āyah exact, rather than merely apt. The matn asks for soundness of hearts and tongues — and the āyah contains both halves, in order:

رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا→ سَلَامَةُ الأَلْسِنَة — the soundness of the tongues
وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا→ سَلَامَةُ القُلُوب — the soundness of the hearts

So the shāhid of the āyah is: the excellence of the Companions, for their precedence in faith; and the seeking of forgiveness for them and asking Allah's pleasure for them; and in it is the soundness of the hearts and the tongues of Ahl al-Sunnah toward the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

And a point from the āyah's context that sharpens it. These verses are about the distribution of the الفَيْء — the three groups entitled to a share: the Muhājirūn (59:8), the Anṣār (59:9), and those who came after them (59:10), described by this very duʿāʾ. al-Fawzān notes that whoever is not of this description does not deserve anything of the fayʾ.

Love of the Companions is written into the qualification for a share of the Muslims' wealth. It is not a sentiment the later generations are encouraged toward; it is the description by which the Qurʾān identifies them at all.

كَلَامُ الشَّوْكَانِيِّ عَلَى الآيَة al-Shawkānī on This Āyah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

— So whoever does not seek forgiveness for the Companions in general, and ask Allah’s pleasure for them, has opposed what Allah commanded in this āyah.

— And if he finds in his heart rancour toward them, then a goading of Shayṭān has struck him, and there has befallen him an ample share of disobedience to Allah, by enmity toward His allies and toward the best nation of His Prophet ﷺ — and a door of abandonment has opened for him, by which he arrives at the Fire of Hell, if he does not rescue himself by taking refuge with Allah سُبْحَانَهُ and imploring Him to pull out of his heart what has entered it of rancour toward the best of generations and the noblest of this Ummah.

— And such a man is only afflicted with it by being tried with a teacher from the Rāfiḍah, or a companion from the enemies of the best of the Ummah — those whom Shayṭān has toyed with, and for whom he has made attractive the assorted lies, the fabricated tales and the invented fictions; and has turned them away from the Book of Allah, which falsehood does not approach from before it or from behind it.

Two things in that are worth keeping. First, the diagnosis is not of an argument but of a route: al-Shawkānī says nobody arrives at this on his own — it comes through a teacher or a companion. Second, the cure he prescribes is not a counter-argument either. It is duʿāʾ — asking Allah to pull the rancour out — which is exactly what the āyah itself was already teaching the reader to say.

حَدِيثُ «لَا تَسُبُّوا أَصْحَابِي» The Ḥadīth: "Do Not Revile My Companions"

لَا تَسُبُّوا أَصْحَابِي، فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ لَوْ أَنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ أَنْفَقَ مِثْلَ أُحُدٍ ذَهَبًا مَا بَلَغَ مُدَّ أَحَدِهِمْ وَلَا نَصِيفَهُ

"Do not revile my Companions. For by the One in whose Hand is my soul, were one of you to spend the like of Uḥud in gold, he would not reach the mudd of one of them, nor half of it."

الحَدِيثُ مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

لَا تَسُبُّوا — that is: do not disparage and do not insult. أَصْحَابِي is the plural of صَاحِب; and one who kept company with the Prophet ﷺ is called a صَحَابِيّ — and he is whoever met the Prophet ﷺ as a believer in him and died upon that.

فَوَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ — this is an oath from the Prophet ﷺ, by which he intends to emphasise what follows it. لَوْ أَنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ أَنْفَقَ — the answer to the conditional; and أُحُد is a well-known mountain in Madīnah, named so because it stands alone, apart from the mountains; and ذَهَبًا is manṣūb as tamyīz.

مَا بَلَغَ مُدَّ أَحَدِهِمْ — al-mudd is a measure, and it is a quarter of the Prophetic ṣāʿ; وَلَا نَصِيفَهُ — a variant form of النِّصْف.

So the comparison is deliberately extreme at both ends: a mountain of gold against a double handful of grain — and not even half of it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the meaning is: that abundant spending in the path of Allah from other than the Companions does not equal the little spending of the Companions. And al-Fawzān gives the reason, which is the finest reasoning in the lesson because it explains rather than merely asserts the precedence:

— And that is because the īmān which was in their hearts at the time of the spending — at the beginning of Islam, and the fewness of its people, and the abundance of what turned people away from it, and the weakness of the motives toward it — it is not possible that the like of it should be attained by anyone who came after them.

Four circumstances, and every one of them a disincentive. The religion was new, its people were few, the pressures against it were many, and there was nothing in the world to be gained by joining it. A dirham given under those conditions is not the same act as a dirham given now, and the ḥadīth is not exaggerating — it is weighing the faith that produced the gift, not the gift.

And the shāhid from the ḥadīth: the forbiddance of reviling the Companions, and the making clear of their excellence over others — and that the deed admits of degrees according to the intention of its doer, and according to the TIME in which it was discharged.

That last clause is a principle worth carrying well beyond this chapter. The same act is not the same deed in every age. Which cuts both ways, and the second way is the encouraging one: a small obedience held onto in a time when almost nobody is holding onto it is weighed by the conditions it was done in.

And in the ḥadīth: that whoever loves the Companions and praises them has obeyed the Messenger ﷺ; and whoever reviles them and hates them has disobeyed the Messenger ﷺ.

مُوَافَقَةُ الإِمَامِ أَحْمَدَ فِي أُصُولِ السُّنَّة Imām Aḥmad, in Uṣūl al-Sunnah, on the Same Point

It is worth setting the author's formulation beside one written some four centuries earlier. الإِمَامُ أَحْمَدُ بْنُ حَنْبَل ruled on this in أُصُولُ السُّنَّة, and he reaches for the same two words the matn uses — the heart, and its soundness:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَمَنِ انْتَقَصَ أَحَدًا مِنْ أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ، أَوْ أَبْغَضَهُ بِحَدَثٍ كَانَ مِنْهُ، أَوْ ذَكَرَ مَسَاوِئَهُ — كَانَ مُبْتَدِعًا، حَتَّى يَتَرَحَّمَ عَلَيْهِمْ جَمِيعًا، وَيَكُونَ قَلْبُهُ لَهُمْ سَلِيمًا

— and whoever disparages one of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, or hates him for something that occurred from him, or mentions his faults — he is an innovator, until he asks mercy for all of them and his heart is sound toward them.

أُصُولُ السُّنَّة §٤٢

Three details reward a second look. First: وَيَكُونَ قَلْبُهُ لَهُمْ سَلِيمًا — «his heart is sound toward them». That is the matn's own سَلَامَةُ القُلُوب, in the words of an imām of the third century. The author did not coin the expression; he inherited it.

Second: أَوْ أَبْغَضَهُ بِحَدَثٍ كَانَ مِنْهُ — «for something that occurred from him». Imām Aḥmad is not ruling on someone who hates a Companion out of pure malice; he is ruling on the man who has a reason — some incident he has heard of. Having a grievance is precisely the case being addressed, and it is not accepted as an excuse.

Third, the remedy is stated as a condition, not a punishment: حَتَّى يَتَرَحَّمَ عَلَيْهِمْ جَمِيعًا — until he asks mercy for them all and his heart is sound. The door is left open, and the way back through it is the same duʿāʾ of al-Ḥashr 59:10 that this lesson began with — which is the whole argument closing on itself.

سَلَامَةُ اللِّسَانِ وَحْدَهَا تَكْفِي فِي حَقِّ الصَّحَابَةِ. Soundness of the tongue alone suffices regarding the Companions.
الصَّحَابَةُ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي ﴿لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا﴾ دُخُولًا أَوَّلِيًّا. The Companions enter into "those who believed" by the foremost entry.
العَمَلُ يَتَفَاضَلُ بِحَسَبِ الوَقْتِ الَّذِي أُدِّيَ فِيهِ. A deed admits of degrees according to the time in which it was discharged.
مَنْ أَبْغَضَ صَحَابِيًّا بِحَدَثٍ كَانَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ مَعْذُورٌ. Whoever hates a Companion for something that occurred from him is excused.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا

The Qurʾān does not merely forbid the rancour — it teaches you the duʿāʾ against it, which is an admission that it can enter a heart uninvited. Imām Aḥmad makes the same duʿāʾ the way back: until he asks mercy for all of them and his heart is sound toward them.
