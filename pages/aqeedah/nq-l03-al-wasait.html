النَّاقِضُ الثَّانِي · اتِّخَاذُ الوَسَائِط

The Second Nullifier — Taking Intermediaries

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the three acts the matn names, and who reported the إِجْمَاع on this clause.
  • Give the reasoning the people of the Jāhiliyyah actually used — and name the two errors they combined.
  • Answer the king-and-minister argument for intermediaries.
  • State what the mushrikūn knew about their objects of worship, and why it did not excuse them.
  • Answer the objection «we only intend their intercession with Allah» the way Ibn Bāz answers it.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ٦٨–٩٨
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 68–98, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الثَّانِي: مَنْ جَعَلَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ اللهِ وَسَائِطَ يَدْعُوهُمْ وَيَسْأَلُهُمُ الشَّفَاعَةَ وَيَتَوَكَّلُ عَلَيْهِمْ — كَفَرَ إِجْمَاعًا.

Three acts, not one: calling upon them, asking them for intercession, and relying upon them.

The author claims an إِجْمَاع here, and the sharḥ names who reported it. Ibn Taymiyyah, in Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā: «whoever affirms intermediaries between Allah and His creation, like the intermediaries that exist between kings and the subjects, is a mushrik — rather this is the religion of the mushrikūn, the worshippers of idols.» A claim of consensus with a named reporter is worth more than one without, and this track states it either way.

حُجَّةُ الجَاهِلِيَّة The Reasoning They Actually Used

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The nullifier is unintelligible until you know what the mushrikūn thought they were doing. Ibn Bāz states it without caricature:

— «Their belief was that their faith that Allah is their Lord suffices, and that He is not worshipped except through an intermediary.»

— «And they say: they have, of bad deeds and blameworthy qualities, what makes them not fit to undertake the worship of Allah directly» — as one does not reach kings and chiefs except through a courtier.

Read that twice, because it is not arrogance. It is a kind of humility. The man reasons: I am too soiled to approach Him myself; let someone cleaner carry it. That is why the error has survived every century — it feels like modesty, and it recruits a true premise (he is unworthy) to reach a false conclusion.

And Ibn Bāz names the two errors it joins: «so they liken Allah to His creation, then they associate partners with Him… so they combined تَشْبِيه and شِرْك; and all of that is from little knowledge, dense ignorance, and imitating the fathers and the grandfathers.»

التَّشْبِيهlikening Allah to a king who cannot be reached without a courtier — an error about Him
الشِّرْكthen directing worship to the courtier — an error about what is owed to Him

The order matters: the tashbīh comes first and produces the shirk. A man does not usually set out to give worship away. He first pictures Allah wrongly — as remote, as standing on ceremony — and the shirk follows as the reasonable response to a God who is not like that. Which is why the first half of every creed in this school is spent on who Allah is.

حُجَّةُ المَلِكِ وَالوَزِير The King-and-Minister Argument

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ does something unusual in a footnote here: it quotes the argument for intermediaries at its most persuasive, from a man who wrote a book defending it, and then answers it. The argument runs:

«Suppose the sultan said to you: I have ordered my minister so-and-so to raise your needs to me, and I will grant what I wish of them and refuse what I wish — so submit your needs and he will raise them to me. Would you consider it good manners and obedience to comply — or to refuse, and say: I will not have an intermediary between me and you, because I hold that it is association with your sovereignty? I think you see the ugliness in such a refusal — for you would have opposed the command, rebelled against obedience, belittled the minister's station, and claimed to know better than the sultan what counts as association in his sovereignty.»

It is a good argument, and it is worth seeing why it is fatal. The answer is one sentence: «look how he likened Allah to His creation and equated Allah with His creation — and this is the belief of the mushrikūn», as Allah reports of them:

تَاللَّهِ إِنْ كُنَّا لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُبِينٍ إِذْ نُسَوِّيكُمْ بِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"By Allah, we were surely in clear error when we equated you with the Lord of the worlds."

Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:97–98 · سُورَةُ الشُّعَرَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The whole argument is an analogy — and the analogy is the shirk. It does not go wrong at the end; it goes wrong at the word «suppose». Everything after that is sound reasoning about a sultan. The question was never whether the reasoning holds, but whether Allah is the sort of thing it applies to.

And then the sharp edge, which is the best line in the section: «And this is from the completeness of their ignorance, when they likened Allah to the TYRANTS among His servants — those who shut their doors against the needs of the subjects and did not care for them.»

Follow what that observation does to the analogy. The king who needs a minister to reach him is a king with a defect — he is busy, or distant, or indifferent, or does not know his people's needs. A ruler who could hear every subject directly and chose to would be the better ruler, not the worse one.

So the argument does not merely liken Allah to a creature. It likens Him to a bad creature — and offers that as reverence. Which is why the Qurʾān answers this whole family of reasoning not with a rule but with a description: He is nearer to him than his jugular vein, and when My servants ask you about Me, I am near.

مَاذَا كَانُوا يَعْلَمُون؟ What They Knew Perfectly Well

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«So Abū Jahl and his like believed that Allah was their Creator and their Provider, and the Creator of the heavens and the earth — but this belief did not benefit them, because they associated partners by worshipping idols.»

«And the mushrikūn knew that the things they worshipped do not create and do not provide, and that they are poor and owned — yet Allah did not excuse them by that, and declared them disbelievers for seeking intercession from other than Allah.»

That removes the excuse a student instinctively reaches for. One imagines the mushrik as a man who thinks his idol is a god — confused about metaphysics, and perhaps to be pitied for it. He was not confused. He knew the stone did not make the rain. He knew it owned nothing and was owned. He was condemned for what he did with that knowledge, not for lacking it — which is also why «but he knows Allah is the only Creator» establishes nothing about a man today.

شُبْهَةُ المُتَأَخِّرِين The Objection of the Later Ones

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz states the objection in the words its holders use, which is the only honest way to answer one:

«If one of these later mushrikūn says: We do not intend that those ones benefit by themselves, or cure our sick, or help us by themselves, or harm an enemy by themselves. We only intend their intercession with Allah in that.»

«The answer is to say to them: this is exactly the aim and the intent of the FIRST disbelievers. It was not their intent that their gods create, or provide, or benefit, or harm by themselves — for what Allah mentioned about them in the Qurʾān refutes that — they only wanted their intercession, their standing, and their bringing them near to Allah in rank.»

وَيَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنْفَعُهُمْ وَيَقُولُونَ هَؤُلَاءِ شُفَعَاؤُنَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ قُلْ أَتُنَبِّئُونَ اللَّهَ بِمَا لَا يَعْلَمُ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَلَا فِي الْأَرْضِ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

"And they worship besides Allah that which neither harms them nor benefits them, and they say: these are our intercessors with Allah. Say: do you inform Allah of something He does not know in the heavens or in the earth? Exalted is He, and high above what they associate."

Sūrat Yūnus 10:18 · سُورَةُ يُونُس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The āyah states the objection and answers it in one verse, which is why it is the proof-text of this nullifier. The mushrikūn's own words are quoted — «these are our intercessors with Allah» — and they are not accused of thinking the idols independent. They are accused of informing Allah of an intercessor He does not know of.

And notice the form of the refutation: أَتُنَبِّئُونَ اللَّهَ بِمَا لَا يَعْلَمُ — a question. An intercession that Allah has not appointed does not exist; to claim one is to tell Him of something in His own heavens and earth that He is unaware of. Put that way, the claim collapses without any need to discuss whether the intercessor is righteous.

أَلَا لِلَّهِ الدِّينُ الْخَالِصُ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّخَذُوا مِنْ دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا إِلَى اللَّهِ زُلْفَى

"Unquestionably, to Allah belongs the pure religion. And those who take allies besides Him: we do not worship them except that they may bring us nearer to Allah in rank."

Sūrat al-Zumar 39:3 · سُورَةُ الزُّمَر

«We do not worship them except…» — and the Qurʾān calls it worship anyway. That is the sentence to carry out of this lesson. The mushrikūn supplied their own disclaimer, in the same words used today, and Allah recorded the disclaimer and the ruling together in one verse. Intending something as a means does not stop it being worship.

وَفِي زَمَانِنَا And In Our Own Time

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz does not leave this in the seventh century. He names the graves people actually go to: «the worshippers of al-Badawī, and the worshippers of Shaykh ʿAbd al-Qādir, and the worshippers of al-Ḥusayn, and the worshippers of others — the affliction reached them from this road. They were ignorant of the reality of tawḥīd, and ignorant of the call of the Messengers, and matters became confused for them.»

And then the clause that explains why the practice is so hard to shift: «so they fell into shirk AND DEEMED IT GOOD, and made it a religion and a means of nearness — and they rejected whoever rejected it upon them.»

«They fell into it and deemed it good» is the whole difficulty in six words. A man doing something he knows is wrong can be called back by a reminder. A man who has made the thing into an act of devotion will experience the reminder as an attack on his religion — and will answer it with the zeal of someone defending piety, which is exactly what the last clause describes.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And a warning Ibn Bāz aims at the scholars rather than the common people: «and rarely will you find, in most of the lands, the scholar who has insight into this great foundation; rather you find one who is pointed to with the fingers, and it is said: he is the scholar — and he is, along with that, of those who venerate the graves with a veneration Allah did not legislate, and calls upon their occupants, and seeks rescue of them, and vows to them.»

That is a hard thing to put in a book, and it is why this nullifier is placed second. If learning alone settled the matter, the first nullifier would be enough. The point of the second is that a man can hold the title of scholar and still be standing on the wrong side of it.

قِيَاسُ اللهِ عَلَى المَلِكِ الَّذِي لَا يُوصَلُ إِلَيْهِ إِلَّا بِوَاسِطَةٍ تَشْبِيهٌ لَهُ بِخَلْقِهِ. Likening Allah to a king unreachable except through an intermediary is likening Him to His creation.

الاسْتِشْفَاعُ بِمَنْ لَمْ يَأْذَنِ اللهُ لَهُ شَفَاعَةٌ ثَابِتَةٌ. Seeking intercession through one whom Allah has not permitted is an established intercession.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا إِلَى اللَّهِ زُلْفَى

Their own words, quoted by Allah — and He calls it worship in the same verse that records their denial of it. The disclaimer people offer today is not a new answer to the objection; it is the objection the Qurʾān was answering. Intending a thing as a means does not stop it being worship.

نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام of Imām Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb · explained from the words of Shaykh Ibn Bāz · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
