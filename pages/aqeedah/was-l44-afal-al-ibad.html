أَفْعَالُ العِبَادِ بَيْنَ الجَبْرِيَّةِ وَالقَدَرِيَّة

The Acts of the Servants, between the Jabriyyah and the Qadariyyah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٥٨–١٦٠
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 158–160.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the two parties and say which way each one went to excess.
  • Give the argument from the servant being described as a believer or a prayer-keeper.
  • Say which of al-Takwīr 81:28–29 refutes which party, and why.
  • State al-Fawzān’s correction of the matn regarding مَجُوسُ هَذِهِ الأُمَّة.
لَا تَنَافِي بَيْنَ القَدَرِ وَإِسْنَادِ أَفْعَالِ العِبَادِ إِلَيْهِمْ حَقِيقَة No Contradiction between the Decree and Ascribing the Servants’ Acts to Them in Reality

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَالعِبَادُ فَاعِلُونَ حَقِيقَةً، وَاللهُ خَالِقُ أَفْعَالِهِمْ. وَالعَبْدُ هُوَ المُؤْمِنُ وَالكَافِرُ وَالبَرُّ وَالفَاجِرُ وَالمُصَلِّي وَالصَّائِمُ. وَلِلْعِبَادِ قُدْرَةٌ عَلَى أَعْمَالِهِمْ، وَلَهُمْ إِرَادَةٌ، وَاللهُ خَالِقُهُمْ وَخَالِقُ قُدْرَتِهِمْ وَإِرَادَتِهِمْ، كَمَا قَالَ تَعَالَى: ﴿لِمَنْ شَاءَ مِنْكُمْ أَنْ يَسْتَقِيمَ ۝ وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ﴾. وَهَذِهِ الدَّرَجَةُ مِنَ القَدَرِ يُكَذِّبُ بِهَا عَامَّةُ القَدَرِيَّةِ، الَّذِينَ سَمَّاهُمُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ مَجُوسَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ. وَيَغْلُو فِيهَا قَوْمٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الإِثْبَاتِ حَتَّى سَلَبُوا العَبْدَ قُدْرَتَهُ وَاخْتِيَارَهُ، وَيُخْرِجُونَ عَنْ أَفْعَالِ اللهِ وَأَحْكَامِهِ حِكَمَهَا وَمَصَالِحَهَا.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān states the author's purpose: his intent by this is the refutation of whoever claimed that affirming that — the decree with its levels, together with the servants acting by their own choice — entails a contradiction.

And from there one party went to excess in affirming the decree, until they stripped the servant of his power and his choice; and the second party went to excess in affirming the actions of the servants and their choice, until they made them the creators of them — having no connection to Allah's volition and not falling under His power.

الجَبْرِيَّةbecause they say the servant is compelled in what issues from him, having no choice in it
القَدَرِيَّةُ النُّفَاةbecause they negate the decree

And then al-Fawzān does something worth watching: he takes the matn clause by clause and assigns each clause to the party it answers. The author did not write a paragraph against one error and a paragraph against the other; he wrote sentences that alternate.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَالعِبَادُ فَاعِلُونَ حَقِيقَةً→ against the Jabriyyah, because they say the servants are not doers in reality, and ascribing the actions to them is of the category of the figurative
وَاللهُ خَالِقُ أَفْعَالِهِمْ→ against the Qadariyyah nufāh, because they say Allah did not create the actions of the servants, but they created them independently, without Allah’s volition and His decreeing of them
وَالعَبْدُ هُوَ المُؤْمِنُ وَالكَافِرُ… وَلِلْعِبَادِ قُدْرَةٌ… وَلَهُمْ إِرَادَةٌ→ against the Jabriyyah again
وَاللهُ خَالِقُهُمْ وَخَالِقُ قُدْرَتِهِمْ→ against the Qadariyyah nufāh again

And the argument behind the third row is the best thing in the lesson, because it needs no theology at all — only attention to how we speak. That is: the servants are not compelled in those deeds; because had it been so, it would not be valid to describe them with them — for the act of one compelled is not ascribed to him, and he is not described by it, and he does not deserve reward or punishment for it.

Look at the author’s list: the believer · the disbeliever · the righteous · the corrupt · the one who prays · the one who fasts. Every one of those is a description of a person taken from what he does. And nobody is called a thief for being pushed into a shop. So if the Jabriyyah were right, the Qurʾān’s whole vocabulary for people would be a mistake.

Note also what else that argument reaches: and he does not deserve reward or punishment for it. Lesson 37 established the recompense by reason — that it does not befit His wisdom to make the good-doer like the evil-doer. The Jabriyyah position makes the recompense itself unjust, which is the charge al-Fawzān closes the lesson with.

آيَتَانِ مُتَتَابِعَتَانِ · لِكُلِّ طَائِفَةٍ وَاحِدَة Two Consecutive Āyāt — One for Each Party

لِمَنْ شَاءَ مِنْكُمْ أَنْ يَسْتَقِيمَ وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"For whoever among you wills to go straight. And you do not will, except that Allah — Lord of the worlds — wills."

Sūrat al-Takwīr 81:28–29 · سُورَةُ التَّكْوِير

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the Shaykh adduced, in refutation of the two parties, His saying تَعَالَى… — and al-Fawzān divides the two āyāt between them:

لِمَنْ شَاءَ مِنْكُمْ أَنْ يَسْتَقِيمَ→ in it is the refutation of the Jabriyyah, because He affirmed a volition FOR THE SERVANTS — and they say they have no volition.
وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ→ in it is the refutation of the Qadariyyah, who hold that the servant’s volition is independent in bringing the act into being, without depending on Allah’s volition. And this is false, because Allah attached the volition of the servants to His own volition, and tied it to it.

Two āyāt, side by side, each one dismantling one of the two errors. And the order is not incidental: the first grants the servant a real will, and the second — immediately, in the next breath — makes it dependent. Anyone who stops at either āyah has one of the two heresies.

This is the same passage the student met in Lesson 30, where it established that the servant's will is real, and downstream. Here it is doing the work twice over — and it is worth noticing that the author needed no third text to balance the two: one āyah and its neighbour were enough.

«مَجُوسُ هَذِهِ الأُمَّة» · تَصْحِيحٌ مِنَ الشَّارِح "The Magians of This Ummah" — a Correction by the Commentator

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author writes that the Qadariyyah are الَّذِينَ سَمَّاهُمُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ مَجُوسَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ — those whom the Prophet ﷺ named the Magians of this Ummah. al-Fawzān first explains the comparison, and then corrects the attribution.

The likeness is for their resembling the Magians, who affirm two creators: the light and the darkness — so they say the good is from the act of the light and the evil from the act of the darkness, and so they became dualists. And likewise these Qadariyyah made a creator alongside Allah, in that they claimed the servants create their actions without Allah's will and volition; rather they are independent in creating them.

That is the point of the name, and it is exact: the charge is not merely error but a second creator. The Magian put the evil down to a rival principle; the Qadarī puts it down to man — and in both cases something besides Allah creates.

تَنْبِيهٌ — and then al-Fawzān corrects the matn itself, in his own words:

وَلَمْ يَثْبُتْ أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ سَمَّاهُمْ مَجُوسَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ، لِتَأَخُّرِ ظُهُورِهِمْ عَنْ وَقْتِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ؛ فَأَكْثَرُ مَا يَجِيءُ مِنْ ذَمِّهِمْ إِنَّمَا هُوَ مَوْقُوفٌ عَلَى الصَّحَابَةِ.

— it is not established that the Prophet ﷺ named them the Magians of this Ummah, because of the lateness of their appearance after the Prophet’s ﷺ time; so most of what comes censuring them is only مَوْقُوفٌ upon the Ṣaḥābah.

This is worth stopping on, because it is the standard of this whole subject applied to the author himself. al-Fawzān does not pass over a phrase in the matn he is commenting on. He explains what it means, and then says plainly that the attribution to the Prophet ﷺ is not established — and gives the reason, which is a reason of history rather than of chains: the sect arose after his lifetime.

And he does not leave the censure unsupported either. The word مَوْقُوف is the muṣṭalaḥ term from the Hadeeth track: a report that stops at a Companion rather than reaching the Prophet ﷺ. So the censure of the Qadariyyah stands as the words of the Companions, and is reported as theirs.

That is the discipline this track works to, demonstrated by the scholar we are following. A matn is not scripture; a named grading belongs to every report; and where the grading will not support the attribution, the honest course is to say so and keep the content at the level the evidence carries it.

غُلُوُّ الجَبْرِيَّةِ وَمَا يَلْزَمُ مِنْه The Excess of the Jabriyyah, and What Follows from It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَيَغْلُو فِيهَا — that is, in this degree of the decree; and الغُلُوّ is exceeding the required limit in a thing. قَوْمٌ مِنْ أَهْلِ الإِثْبَاتِ — and what is meant by them is the Jabriyyah, who said the servant is compelled in his act — حَتَّى سَلَبُوا العَبْدَ قُدْرَتَهُ وَاخْتِيَارَهُ.

And al-Fawzān sets the two excesses opposite each other in one sentence, which is the cleanest statement of the whole part: so the former went to excess in affirming the actions of the servants until they took them outside Allah's volition; and these went to excess in negating the actions of the servants until they stripped them of power and choice.

وَيُخْرِجُونَ عَنْ أَفْعَالِ اللهِ وَأَحْكَامِهِ حِكَمَهَا وَمَصَالِحَهَا — the plurals of حِكْمَة and مَصْلَحَة; that is: that the Jabriyyah, in this madhhab of theirs, when they negated the actions of the servants and stripped them of power and choice, negated Allah's wisdom in His command and His prohibition and His reward and His punishment.

And then the consequence, which al-Fawzān states without softening: so they said: that He rewards or punishes the servants for what is not of their own doing, and commands them with what they are not able to do — so they accused Allah of injustice and of purposelessness; تَعَالَى اللهُ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ عُلُوًّا كَبِيرًا.

Which is where Part 5 ends, and it ends on an irony worth keeping. The Jabriyyah went to excess in affirming the decree in order to magnify Allah’s power — and arrived at a God who commands the incapable and punishes the innocent. So the party that tried hardest to protect His power ended by impugning His justice and His wisdom, which is the same shape as the Jahmiyyah of Lesson 30, who went to excess in tanzīh and ended by stripping Him of His names.

Excess in defending one attribute costs another. That is the lesson of the five pairs, and al-qadar is where it is most expensive — because here the attributes at stake are His power on one side and His justice and wisdom on the other, and Ahl al-Sunnah are the only party that does not have to trade one for the other.

With this Part 5 is complete: the four levels of the decree, grouped into two degrees; that there is no conflict between the decree and the Law; that His decreeing a sin is not His loving it; and that the servants are doers in reality while Allah is the Creator of their acts. And the whole of it rests on two words of Ibn Taymiyyah's from the previous lesson — يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهَذَا وَهَذَا, they believe in this and in that.

فِعْلُ المُجْبَرِ يُنْسَبُ إِلَيْهِ وَيَسْتَحِقُّ عَلَيْهِ الثَّوَابَ. The act of one compelled is ascribed to him and earns him reward.
ثَبَتَ أَنَّ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ سَمَّى القَدَرِيَّةَ مَجُوسَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ. It is established that the Prophet ﷺ named the Qadariyyah the Magians of this Ummah.
الجَبْرِيَّةُ غَلَوْا فِي نَفْيِ أَفْعَالِ العِبَادِ. The Jabriyyah went to excess in negating the actions of the servants.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لِمَنْ شَاءَ مِنْكُمْ أَنْ يَسْتَقِيمَ · وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ

Memorize the two together and in order, because each one alone is a heresy. The first gives you a real will; the second, in the very next breath, gives it an owner. That is the whole of al-qadar in two āyāt that happen to be neighbours.
