النَّاقِضُ السَّادِس · الاسْتِهْزَاء

The Sixth Nullifier, Part 1 — Mockery

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Quote the sixth nullifier and its daleel, and say what the mockers’ own excuse in that verse was.
  • Give the argument for why mockery is disbelief — not that it is rude, but what the act is.
  • Name four scholars from whom the ijmāʿ is transmitted here, and explain Ibn Bāz’s gloss on the word عَوَامّ.
  • State the ruling where a man affirms tawḥīd and the messengers but mocks one thing — and whether mocking all of them is required.
  • Explain the footnote’s rule about «اسْتِهَانَةً بِه»: when a qualifier in a text is for clarification rather than restriction.
  • State what Ibn Bāz answered about the Ashʿarī position on a major sin committed مُسْتَخِفًّا بِهَا — and what Ahl al-Sunnah hold.
  • Distinguish mocking the religion from taking a sin lightly, and give his wording for each.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٤٦–١٥١
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 146–151, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

السَّادِس: مَنِ اسْتَهْزَأَ بِشَيْءٍ مِنْ دِينِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ أَوْ ثَوَابِهِ أَوْ عِقَابِه — كَفَر؛ وَالدَّلِيلُ قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿وَلَئِنْ سَأَلْتَهُمْ لَيَقُولُنَّ إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلْعَبُ قُلْ أَبِاللهِ وَآيَاتِهِ وَرَسُولِهِ كُنْتُمْ تَسْتَهْزِئُونَ﴾.

Some commentaries read «مِنْ دِينِ الله» in place of «مِنْ دِينِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ» — the compiler notes the variant.

وَلَئِن سَأَلْتَهُمْ لَيَقُولُنَّ إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلْعَبُ قُلْ أَبِاللَّهِ وَآيَاتِهِ وَرَسُولِهِ كُنتُمْ تَسْتَهْزِئُونَ

And if you asked them, they would surely say, “We were only jesting and playing.” Say: was it Allah, and His verses, and His Messenger that you were mocking?

سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة · 9:65

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read the verse as an exchange, because that is what it is. Their defence is already in it — «we were only jesting and playing» — and it is not accepted. Then the next verse closes the door: «Do not make excuses; you have disbelieved after your belief.»

So the first thing the daleel settles is not whether mockery is disbelief. It is that “I was only joking” is the mockers’ own plea, answered in the Qurʾān before anyone thought to offer it.

Ibn Bāz begins from that: «the Book of Allah has pronounced the disbelief of whoever mocked the Great Messenger, or anything of the clear Book of Allah and His wise law» — and then calls the verse «a manifest text and a decisive proof» of the disbelief of one who mocks Allah, His verses, or His Messenger ﷺ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He then relays the consensus, and the wording is careful:

«And the scholars of Islām in all ages and all lands are agreed upon the disbelief of whoever mocked Allah, or His Messenger, or His Book, or anything of the religion; and they are agreed that whoever mocked anything of that while being a Muslim, he becomes by that a disbeliever, an apostate from Islām.»

The sharḥ adds in another place that the text is explicit that one who mocks has disbelieved مُطْلَقًا — unrestrictedly — «because it indicates his denial, his rancour and his contempt… so this shows us that mockery is disbelief and apostasy, and that whenever it occurs from one who outwardly professes Islām it indicates his hypocrisy and his disbelief, and he has the ruling of the apostates.»

The killing is grounded on «مَنْ بَدَّلَ دِينَهُ فَاقْتُلُوه». The sharḥ gives that ḥadīth here with neither an attribution nor a grading, so this page supplies none.

لِمَاذَا كَانَ الاسْتِهْزَاءُ كُفْرًا The Argument — What the Act Actually Is

This is the part to understand, because without it the clause looks like a rule about manners. Ibn Bāz does not argue that mockery is punished as contempt of Allah. He argues that it is contempt of Allah — and he builds it in two steps.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And of the decisive proofs of the disbelief of whoever mocked Allah, or His Messenger, or His Book, is that mockery is a belittling and a contempt of the one mocked — and Allah, glorified is He, has the attribute of perfection; and His Book is of His speech, and His speech is of His attributes of perfection; and His Messenger Muḥammad is the most perfect of creation and their master, and the seal of the messengers, and the intimate friend of the Lord of the worlds. So whoever mocked Allah, or His Messenger, or His Book, or anything of His religion — he has belittled it and held it in contempt; and contempt of anything of that, and belittling it, is manifest disbelief, and bare hypocrisy, and enmity toward the Lord of the worlds, and disbelief in His trustworthy Messenger.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

stepthe claimwhat it rests on
١Mockery is belittling and contempt — that is what the act consists ofthe meaning of the word itself; no premise about the mocker’s intention is needed
٢The objects here are perfection itselfAllah has the attribute of perfection; His Book is His speech, and His speech is of His attributes; the Messenger ﷺ is the most perfect of creation
∴Therefore mockery of any of them is contempt of what is perfect — and that is not compatible with faith in itwhich is why he later says the mockery is «a proof that his claim to faith is false»

Note what step 1 does. It means the clause does not require proving that the man intended to insult Allah. The intention to mock is itself the contempt — which is exactly why the «we were only jesting» plea in the daleel fails. Keep that; it is the pivot of the whole clause, and part 2 returns to it in his words «even if he was joking».

نَقْلُ الإِجْمَاع Four Named Transmitters, Not an Assertion

The sharḥ does not leave the ijmāʿ as a claim. It names who transmitted it. That is worth imitating: an ijmāʿ with no transmitter named is not evidence of anything.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَبُو بَكْرٍ ابْنُ المُنْذِر«The generality of the people of knowledge are agreed that the ḥadd of one who reviles the Prophet ﷺ is death; and among those who said it are Mālik, al-Layth, Aḥmad and Isḥāq — and it is the madhhab of al-Shāfiʿī.»
the glossIbn Bāz stops to explain the word: «his saying عَوَامّ is the plural of عَامَّة, and العَامَّة here is in the sense of the group — so his intent is that the groups of scholars were agreed.» Not “the laymen”, which is how the word would be misread today.
القَاضِي عِيَاض«The Ummah is agreed upon the killing of the one who belittles him from among the Muslims, and the one who reviles him.»
مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ سَحْنُونof the imāms of the Mālikīs: «The scholars are agreed that the one who reviles the Prophet and belittles him is a disbeliever, and the threat has come upon him of Allah’s punishment; and his ruling with the Ummah is death — and whoever doubts about his disbelief and his punishment has disbelieved.»
ابْنُ تَيْمِيَّةin al-Ṣārim al-Maslūl ʿalā Shātim al-Rasūl, after relaying the scholars’ statements: «and the precise statement on it is that the reviler, if he is a Muslim, disbelieves and is killed without disagreement — and it is the madhhab of the four imāms and others.»
أَحْمَد, via Ḥanbal«I heard Abū ʿAbdillāh say: whoever reviles the Messenger ﷺ or belittles him — Muslim or disbeliever — upon him is death; and I hold that he be killed and not asked to repent.»

And Ibn Bāz closes the transmission with a sentence that is itself a method: «the speech of the scholars in this chapter is much, and in what we have relayed from them there is sufficiency for the seeker of truth.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also addresses the worst form directly, because it is the form the question usually comes in: «and there is no doubt that reviling takes many kinds, and no doubt that mockery of him — upon him be peace and blessings — and belittling him, and likening him to a despicable animal, is of the ugliest reviling and the greatest belittlement; so the doer of that is a disbeliever, his blood and property forfeit.»

Keep in mind through all of this what part 2 makes explicit in his own words: the ruling of disbelief and the carrying out of a killing are two different things, and the second belongs to the authority — «as for the killing, that is another matter; the killing requires a demand for repentance.»

سِمَةُ الكُفَّارِ وَالمُنَافِقِين Whose Trait This Is

Then four passages, and the sharḥ’s purpose in stacking them is stated at the end: mockery of the messengers and the believers is a trait of the disbelievers, the hypocrites and the mushrikīn. Not an incidental sin that anyone might fall into — a marker of a party.

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ أَجْرَمُوا كَانُوا مِنَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا يَضْحَكُونَ وَإِذَا مَرُّوا بِهِمْ يَتَغَامَزُونَ

Indeed, those who committed crimes used to laugh at those who believed; and when they passed by them, they would exchange derisive glances.

سُورَةُ المُطَفِّفِين · 83:29–30

فَاتَّخَذْتُمُوهُمْ سِخْرِيًّا حَتَّىٰ أَنسَوْكُمْ ذِكْرِي وَكُنتُم مِّنْهُم تَضْحَكُونَ

But you took them for mockery, until they made you forget My remembrance, and you used to laugh at them.

سُورَةُ المُؤْمِنُون · 23:110

The sharḥ gives that one with the verse before it and the verse after it, and the three together are the whole scene: «a party of My slaves used to say, our Lord, we have believed, so forgive us and have mercy on us, and You are the best of the merciful» — then the mockery — then «indeed I have rewarded them this Day for their patience: it is they who are the triumphant.» The mocked are named the winners in the same breath.

قَالَ إِن تَسْخَرُوا مِنَّا فَإِنَّا نَسْخَرُ مِنكُمْ كَمَا تَسْخَرُونَ

“If you mock us, then we will mock you just as you mock.”

سُورَةُ هُود · 11:38 — the words of Nūḥ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام

الَّذِينَ يَلْمِزُونَ الْمُطَّوِّعِينَ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ فِي الصَّدَقَاتِ وَالَّذِينَ لَا يَجِدُونَ إِلَّا جُهْدَهُمْ فَيَسْخَرُونَ مِنْهُمْ سَخِرَ اللَّهُ مِنْهُمْ وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ أَلِيمٌ

Those who criticise the believers who give charity voluntarily, and those who find nothing but their own effort, and ridicule them — Allah will ridicule them, and for them is a painful punishment.

سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة · 9:79

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That last verse is the one to sit with, because it is the smallest case in the set. No one in it mocked Allah or the Messenger ﷺ. They mocked a poor man’s small ṣadaqah — a man «who finds nothing but his own effort». And Allah answered it Himself.

The sharḥ does not draw a ruling of disbelief from this verse — it uses the four passages together to show whose trait mockery is. But the smallness of the occasion is the lesson: the religion of a man is visible in what he finds funny.

الاسْتِهَانَةُ بِالمُصْحَف Degrading the Muṣḥaf, and a Rule About Qualifiers

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He extends the clause to the physical book, by the same argument:

«And likewise if he degraded something of what Allah has magnified, out of contempt for it and scorn — as if he were to degrade the muṣḥaf, or urinate on it, or tread on it, or sit on it, or the like of that, degrading it — he disbelieves by consensus; because by that he becomes one who belittles Allah and holds Him in contempt, since the Qurʾān is His speech — so whoever degraded it has degraded Allah.»

And elsewhere, flatly: «if he degraded the muṣḥaf, or smeared it with filth, or trod on it, or sat on it — he is a disbeliever, even if he claims to be a Muslim; a disbeliever by consensus.» And: «likewise the one who degrades the muṣḥaf… while he knows it is the Book of Allah — this is a proof that this man has no faith; he merely claims faith, for had he sound faith it would have barred him from this deed.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the footnote, which teaches a rule of reading worth more than the case it settles. His words «اسْتِهَانَةً بِه» — degrading it — might be read as a condition: that only a degradation done in order to degrade counts.

The compiler says: «this description is for statement and clarification, and it is not a restriction in the ruling — unless what is intended by it is to exclude the unintending and the coerced and their like.»

And he grounds it in Ibn Taymiyyah, from Kitāb al-Īmān: «and the counterpart of this is His saying — exalted is He — «اسْتَجِيبُوا لِلَّهِ وَلِلرَّسُولِ إِذَا دَعَاكُمْ لِمَا يُحْيِيكُم» — and He does not call except to that; so the qualification here has no contrary implication.» Ibn Taymiyyah gives further examples and concludes: «so the qualification in all of this is for statement and clarification, not to exclude another description; and for this reason some of the grammarians say: adjectives on definite nouns are for clarification, not for restriction — and on indefinite nouns, for restriction.»

So: a man does not escape by saying “I trod on it but I did not mean it as degradation.” The qualifier describes the act, it does not add a test. But the footnote keeps the one exclusion that is real — the man who did not intend the act at all, and the man under coercion.

وَلَوْ فِي شَيْءٍ وَاحِد One Thing Is Enough — and Why

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«Even if he affirmed the oneness of Allah and believed the messengers — but mocked the Messenger in something, or belittled him in something, or [mocked] some of the messengers — he becomes a disbeliever by that.»

And the footnote states the generalisation outright: «so it is not a condition in the disbelief of the mocker that he mock all the messengers, or all the religion — if he mocked one messenger, or one thing of what the Messenger brought, he has disbelieved.»

That is the same argument you met in the fifth nullifier against «only if he hates all of it» — and here it is made for mockery. And it follows from the argument, not merely from a text: if the act is contempt of what is perfect, then contempt of one thing is contempt of what is perfect. There is no quantity at which contempt becomes compatible with faith.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He then puts it as an analogy that the matn’s own title invites:

«So the one who said “there is no god but Allah”, and brought the two testimonies, or the pillars of Islām, and then reviled the religion, or reviled Allah, or reviled His Messenger, or denied what Allah has obliged, or denied some of what Allah has forbidden — that testimony is nullified, and he becomes like one who has broken his purity by one of the nullifiers, by the consensus of the people of knowledge. And for this they mentioned, in the chapter on the ruling of the apostate, the many nullifiers: if he brings one of them, his Islām is nullified and he comes under the ruling of the apostates.»

That is where the book’s own title comes from — نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام, the things that break it, the way an act of impurity breaks a wuḍūʾ. And the comparison is exact in one respect: one is enough, and the state before it does not survive it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then, in the same breath, he warns about a habit of argument.

«But it is the custom of the weak of insight, or of one whose aim is not sound, to cling to the ambiguous, the unrestricted and the general — and this is not the way of the people of faith; for Allah has described the people of faith as believing in the ambiguous and returning it to the clear, while the people of deviation follow what is ambiguous of it, seeking discord.»

The compiler’s footnote says the Shaykh means here to answer those who argue from the ḥadīth of the man with the slip of paper that one who leaves the prayer is saved from remaining forever in the Fire.

الاسْتِخْفَافُ بِالمَعْصِيَةِ لَيْسَ اسْتِهْزَاءً بِالدِّين The Line the Sharḥ Draws Itself

Here is why this is a lesson and not an excerpt. Everything above would let a reader conclude that a careless Muslim who laughs while sinning has left Islām. The sharḥ is asked exactly that, twice, and answers it the other way both times.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Some of the Ashʿarīs mentioned in their books that one who commits a major sin مُسْتَخِفًّا بِهَا — taking it lightly — disbelieves. Has anyone of Ahl al-Sunnah agreed with them in that?

Ibn Bāz — «I do not know of that.… He only commits it taking it lightly — were it not for his making light of it he would not have committed it; were it not for his making light of fornication and disobedience to parents he would not have done it. So what Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah hold is that he is a sinner, deficient in faith — even if he was lax; the reckless make light.»

Read his reasoning, because it is the decisive point. Every deliberate sin involves some making-light of the matter — that is why a man commits it. So if making light were itself disbelief, no sinner would remain a Muslim, and the whole category of عَاصٍ would be empty. The Ashʿarī position is answered not by a counter-text but by what it would entail.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Is the one who makes light of disobedience — of the major sins specifically — a disbeliever, an apostate? And what is the standard of this making-light?

Ibn Bāz — «Making the sins open is not disbelief — unless he deems that lawful; but he becomes a fāsiq, and severely so, we seek refuge with Allah, and reckless… And his making light of the sins indicates the weakness of his faith, and that he is in danger of leaving Islām. But if he permits them — he says: fornication is permitted, or wine is permitted — then this is disbelief and apostasy from Islām, we seek refuge with Allah.»

Three rows, and the middle one is the one that gets lost. A man laughing while he sins is not, on these folios, a man mocking the religion — he is a fāsiq in danger. The difference is not a matter of degree: it is the difference between making light of a command one still acknowledges and holding the Legislator in contempt.

خَطَرُ المَعَاصِي But Not Comfortable Either

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«The danger of the sins is great, because they are a making-light of the command of Allah, and a laxity with His right, and a boldness against His sanctities… Then they drag one to disbelief in Allah. How many a sinner whose disobedience dragged him to disbelief in Allah — while he is among the sinners, suddenly he is among the disbelievers! The sin drags him to mockery of Allah, and to mockery of His religion, and to deriding what His prophets brought — so he joins the disbelievers, we seek refuge with Allah from that… his laxity with the prayer at home drags him until he abandons it; and when he abandons it, he falls into disbelief in Allah.»

This is the same structure as «المَعَاصِي بَرِيدُ الكُفْر» in the fifth nullifier, and it resolves the same way. Making light of a sin is not mockery of the religion — and it is the road by which mockery of the religion arrives. The first is a rule for judging a man; the second is a warning about yourself. Holding both is the whole skill.

Part 2 takes printed folios ١٥٢–١٥٨, where the clause turns from establishing the ruling to the cases: the word said «with the tongue and not the heart»; mockery «even if he was joking, or means to annoy someone»; reviling the Ṣaḥābah, where all and one or two are given different rulings; mocking knowledge and its people, where Ibn Bāz says «the correct position is to make the distinction»; and the ruling on sitting with those who mock.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
قَوْلُهُمْ «إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلْعَبُ» عُذْرٌ مَقْبُولٌ فِي الآيَة. «We were only jesting and playing» is an accepted excuse in the verse.
الاسْتِهْزَاءُ فِي نَفْسِهِ تَنَقُّصٌ وَاحْتِقَارٌ لِلْمُسْتَهْزَأِ بِه. Mockery is in itself a belittling and a contempt of the one mocked.
«عَوَامُّ أَهْلِ العِلْم» عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَازٍ مَعْنَاهَا عَامَّةُ النَّاس. For Ibn Bāz, «ʿawāmm ahl al-ʿilm» means the common people.
يُشْتَرَطُ فِي كُفْرِ المُسْتَهْزِئِ أَنْ يَسْتَهْزِئَ بِالدِّينِ كُلِّه. It is a condition in the mocker’s disbelief that he mock the whole religion.
إِظْهَارُ المَعَاصِي كُفْرٌ عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. Making sins open is disbelief for Ibn Bāz.
مَنْ أَبَاحَ الزِّنَا أَوِ الخَمْرَ فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ مُرْتَدّ. Whoever declares fornication or wine permitted is a disbelieving apostate.
وَافَقَ ابْنُ بَازٍ الأَشَاعِرَةَ فِي تَكْفِيرِ المُسْتَخِفِّ بِالكَبِيرَة. Ibn Bāz agreed with the Ashʿarīs in declaring a disbeliever the one who makes light of a major sin.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَمَنِ اسْتَهْزَأَ بِاللهِ أَوْ رَسُولِهِ أَوْ كِتَابِهِ أَوْ شَيْءٍ مِنْ دِينِهِ فَقَدْ تَنَقَّصَهُ وَاحْتَقَرَه

Ibn Bāz. This is the clause’s whole argument in one line, and the word to hold is فَقَدْ — he has already belittled it. The mockery is not evidence of contempt somewhere behind it; the mockery is the contempt. And keep beside it the sentence that stops this clause from swallowing every careless Muslim: «إِظْهَارُ المَعَاصِي لَيْسَ بِكُفْر، إِلَّا إِذَا اسْتَحَلَّ ذَلِك؛ لَكِنَّهُ يَكُونُ فِسْقًا.» الاسْتِهْزَاء is contempt of the Legislator; الاسْتِخْفَاف is making light of a command still acknowledged.
