مَعْنَى كَوْنِهِ «فِي السَّمَاءِ» · وَالظُّنُونُ الكَاذِبَة

The Meaning of His Being "in the Heaven", and the False Suppositions

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Distinguish التَّحْرِيف from الصِّيَانَةُ عَنِ الظُّنُونِ الكَاذِبَة, and say which the author permits.
  • Define تُقِلّه and تُظِلّه, and state the false supposition they name.
  • Reproduce the consensus in its two-way form.
  • Lay out the argument from the Kursī and the Throne, and the argument from Allah’s holding up the heavens.
مَا يَجِبُ اعْتِقَادُهُ فِي عُلُوِّهِ وَمَعِيَّتِهِ · وَمَعْنَى كَوْنِهِ فِي السَّمَاء What Must Be Held regarding His Highness and Withness — and the Meaning of His Being "in the Heaven"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَكُلُّ هَذَا الكَلَامِ الَّذِي ذَكَرَهُ اللهُ مِنْ أَنَّهُ فَوْقَ العَرْشِ، وَأَنَّهُ مَعَنَا — حَقٌّ عَلَى حَقِيقَتِهِ، لَا يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى تَحْرِيفٍ، وَلَكِنْ يُصَانُ عَنِ الظُّنُونِ الكَاذِبَةِ، مِثْلُ أَنْ يُظَنَّ أَنَّ ظَاهِرَ قَوْلِهِ ﴿فِي السَّمَاءِ﴾ أَنَّ السَّمَاءَ تُقِلُّهُ أَوْ تُظِلُّهُ. وَهَذَا بَاطِلٌ بِإِجْمَاعِ أَهْلِ العِلْمِ وَالإِيمَانِ، فَإِنَّ اللهَ قَدْ ﴿وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ﴾، وَهُوَ الَّذِي ﴿يُمْسِكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ أَنْ تَزُولَا﴾، ﴿وَيُمْسِكُ السَّمَاءَ أَنْ تَقَعَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ﴾، ﴿وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ تَقُومَ السَّمَاءُ وَالْأَرْضُ بِأَمْرِهِ﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author draws a distinction here that is easy to miss and that the whole section turns on. The texts حَقٌّ عَلَى حَقِيقَتِهِ — are true upon their reality; they لَا يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى تَحْرِيفٍ — do not stand in need of taḥrīf. But — وَلَكِنْ يُصَانُ عَنِ الظُّنُونِ الكَاذِبَةِ — they are to be guarded from false suppositions.

التَّحْرِيفchanging what the text says — forbidden, and the text needs none of it
الصِّيَانَةruling out what the text does not say — obligatory

These are opposite operations, and the muʿaṭṭilah collapse them. They say: the apparent meaning would be absurd, therefore the wording must be reinterpreted. Ahl al-Sunnah answer: the apparent meaning is true, and the absurd thing you imagined was never the apparent meaning. You do not have to move the text to be rid of a supposition you brought to it.

al-Fawzān names what is being refused: it is obligatory to believe in it as Allah informed, and it is not permitted to reinterpret it and turn it from its apparent sense, as the muʿaṭṭilah of the Jahmiyyah and the Muʿtazilah and their like do, claiming that it is not real but figurative. So they reinterpret the istiwāʾ upon the Throne as the seizing of dominion, and Allah's highness over His creation as the loftiness of His rank and His overpowering, and the like of these false reinterpretations, which are a distorting of the speech of Allah from its places.

And the other extreme, which is the mirror error: and among them are those who say that the meaning of His being with us is that He is indwelling in every place, as حُلُولِيَّةُ الجَهْمِيَّة and others say — تَعَالَى اللهُ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ عُلُوًّا كَبِيرًا. One party emptied the texts of height; the other filled all of space with Him. Both read مَعَكُمْ as mixed in among, and then differed only over which half to sacrifice.

تُقِلُّهُ أَوْ تُظِلُّه "Carries Him, or Shades Him"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the two words of the false supposition, defined: تُقِلُّه — that is: carries Him; تُظِلُّه — that is: covers Him over; and الظُّلَّة is the thing that shades you from above.

So the supposition is a picture: the sky as a floor He stands on, or a canopy over His head. And al-Fawzān: and these two meanings are not what is intended in His being سُبْحَانَهُ in the heaven. And whoever supposes that has erred the utmost error — and that is for two reasons.

الأَمْرُ الأَوَّل · خِلَافُ الإِجْمَاع First — It Contradicts the Consensus

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That this contradicts what the people of knowledge and faith were agreed upon: for they were agreed that He سُبْحَانَهُ is above His Throne, separate from His creation — and then the clause to memorise, because it closes the door in both directions at once:

لَيْسَ فِي ذَاتِهِ شَيْءٌ مِنْ مَخْلُوقَاتِهِ، وَلَا فِي مَخْلُوقَاتِهِ شَيْءٌ مِنْ ذَاتِهِ

— there is nothing of His creation in His Self, and nothing of His Self in His creation.

Two denials, and each one answers a different error. «Nothing of His creation in His Self» rules out the sky carrying Him or shading Him — the very supposition this section is guarding against, in which something created becomes part of His situation. «Nothing of His Self in His creation» rules out the ḥulūliyyah — the indwelling in every place. One sentence, both extremes.

And the discussion of the explanation of His saying مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ has already come — Lesson 16 and Lesson 26: if what is intended by «the heaven» is the built heaven, then فِي is in the sense of عَلَى — that is, above the heaven, as in His saying وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ فِي جُذُوعِ النَّخْلِ, meaning upon the trunks of the palm trees; and if what is intended by «the heaven» is the height, then the meaning of «in the heaven» is in the loftiness. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

الأَمْرُ الثَّانِي · مُصَادَمَةُ أَدِلَّةِ القُرْآن Second — It Collides with the Proofs of the Qurʾān

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That this supposition opposes and collides with the proofs of the Qurʾān which indicate the greatness of Allah, and His freedom from need of His creation, and His creation's need of Him. And the author brought four āyāt for it. Take the first:

وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ

"His Kursī extends over the heavens and the earth, and their preservation does not weary Him — and He is the Most High, the Most Great."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:255 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān sets out the scale, and the argument is simply a matter of following it: and the Kursī is a mighty created thing before the Throne, and it is greater than the heavens and the earth; and the Throne is greater than it.

the heavens and the earth  <  the Kursī  <  the Throne  <  Allah, greater than everything

— so how should the heaven contain Him, or carry Him, or shade Him?!

That is the whole refutation, and notice that it never leaves the Qurʾān's own vocabulary. The supposition needs the sky to be big enough to hold Him. The āyah says the sky is not even big enough to hold a single one of His created things.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُمْسِكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ أَنْ تَزُولَا

"Indeed Allah holds the heavens and the earth lest they cease."

Sūrat Fāṭir 35:41 · سُورَةُ فَاطِر

وَيُمْسِكُ السَّمَاءَ أَنْ تَقَعَ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ

"And He holds the heaven from falling upon the earth, except by His permission."

Sūrat al-Ḥajj 22:65 · سُورَةُ الحَجّ

وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ أَنْ تَقُومَ السَّمَاءُ وَالْأَرْضُ بِأَمْرِهِ

"And among His signs is that the heaven and the earth stand by His command."

Sūrat al-Rūm 30:25 · سُورَةُ الرُّوم

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So these āyāt indicate that the heavens and the earth are in need of Him: for He is the One who holds them lest they cease or fall, and their standing is by His command alone.

And then the conclusion, which is the finest turn in the section: so it is not conceivable, along with this, that He سُبْحَانَهُ should be in need of them, so that they might carry Him or shade Him. تَعَالَى اللهُ عَنْ هَذَا الظَّنِّ البَاطِلِ عُلُوًّا كَبِيرًا.

Follow the inversion, because it is exact. The false supposition has the heaven holding Him up. The āyāt have Him holding the heaven up — and say that without Him it would fall. So the supposition does not merely exaggerate; it reverses the relation the Qurʾān states.

And note which attribute the supposition would have cost Him: not the height, but the الغِنَى — the freedom from all need. The one who imagines the sky as His floor has made the Creator dependent on a thing He made. That is why the author calls it a false supposition rather than a weak opinion — it is not a small mistake about where Allah is; it is a mistake about what He is.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لَيْسَ فِي ذَاتِهِ شَيْءٌ مِنْ مَخْلُوقَاتِهِ، وَلَا فِي مَخْلُوقَاتِهِ شَيْءٌ مِنْ ذَاتِهِ

The consensus of the people of knowledge and faith, and the most economical sentence in this part of the book: one clause closes the door on the sky carrying Him, the other on His indwelling the world. Learn it and you will not need to argue either case twice.
