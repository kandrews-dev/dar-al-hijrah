ما هي الأسماء الحسنى؟ What Are the Beautiful Names?

In Unit 1 we learned that the third category of tawhid is tawḥīd al-asmāʾ wa-l-ṣifāt. This unit is where we study that category in detail — Allah's الأسماء الحسنى (al-Asmāʾ al-Ḥusnā) — His most beautiful names.

These are not names we invent. We learn them only from two sources: the Quran and the authentic Sunnah. Every name Allah calls Himself by, and every name the Prophet ﷺ used for Him, is part of this beautiful collection. Each name carries a meaning that tells us something true and perfect about our Lord.

الأسماء الحسنى al-Asmāʾ al-Ḥusnā — The Beautiful Names

أَسْمَاءُ اللهِ تَعَالَى الدَّالَّةُ عَلَى صِفَاتِ الكَمَالِ، الَّتِي بَلَغَتْ غَايَةَ الحُسْنِ

The names of Allah which indicate attributes of absolute perfection — names that have reached the utmost beauty.

They are called al-Ḥusnā (the most beautiful) because each one is perfect in its meaning — there is no flaw, limit, or shortcoming in any of them.

وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا

"And to Allah belong the most beautiful names, so call upon Him by them."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:180 · سورة الأعراف

حديث التسعة والتسعين اسماً The "Ninety-Nine Names" Hadith

«إِنَّ لِلَّهِ تِسْعَةً وَتِسْعِينَ اسْمًا، مِائَةً إِلَّا وَاحِدًا، مَنْ أَحْصَاهَا دَخَلَ الْجَنَّةَ»

"Indeed Allah has ninety-nine names — one hundred minus one — whoever encompasses them (aḥṣāhā) will enter Paradise."

Narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim · رواه البخاري ومسلم

This is one of the most famous hadiths about Allah's names. But there is an important point that scholars stress — and it is easy to misunderstand:

The number 99 is not a limit on Allah's names. The hadith does not mean Allah has only 99 names. It means: among His names there are ninety-nine which carry this special promise — whoever encompasses them enters Paradise. Allah has names He kept hidden in His knowledge.

The proof is another authentic supplication of the Prophet ﷺ:

«...أَسْأَلُكَ بِكُلِّ اسْمٍ هُوَ لَكَ، سَمَّيْتَ بِهِ نَفْسَكَ، أَوْ أَنْزَلْتَهُ فِي كِتَابِكَ، أَوْ عَلَّمْتَهُ أَحَدًا مِنْ خَلْقِكَ، أَوِ اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ عِنْدَكَ»

"...I ask You by every name that is Yours — which You named Yourself with, or revealed in Your Book, or taught to any of Your creation, or kept hidden with Yourself in the knowledge of the unseen."

Narrated by Aḥmad, ṣaḥīḥ · حديث الكرب

The last phrase — اسْتَأْثَرْتَ بِهِ فِي عِلْمِ الْغَيْبِ ("kept hidden with Yourself") — proves there are names of Allah known only to Him. So His names are more than 99; the 99 are simply those tied to this great promise.

معنى الإحصاء What Does "Iḥṣāʾ" (Encompassing) Mean?

The hadith promises Paradise to whoever does إحصاء (iḥṣāʾ) of the names. This word means far more than just "counting." Scholars explain it has three levels — and true iḥṣāʾ requires all three:

1

الحفظ والعدّ

Memorise & count

To know the names and preserve them — to be able to list and recall them.

2

فهم المعنى

Understand the meaning

To understand what each name means about Allah — not just the word, but the reality it points to.

3

العمل بمقتضاها

Act upon them

To let the names change you — to call on Allah by them and live by what they require.

Example: Knowing the name الرَّقِيب (al-Raqīb — the Watchful) is level 1. Understanding that it means Allah watches every action and thought is level 2. Letting that knowledge stop you from sin when no human is watching is level 3 — that is true iḥṣāʾ.

قواعد الإيمان بالأسماء والصفات The Four Rules for Believing in the Names

We met these four rules briefly in Unit 1. Because this entire unit is about the names, we now learn them properly. To believe in Allah's names correctly, we affirm them as they came — guarding against four mistakes:

القاعدة — Rule We avoid... Meaning
بلا تحريف taḥrīf Distorting the meaning — e.g. twisting "the Most Merciful" into something else
بلا تعطيل taʿṭīl Denying or emptying a name of its meaning — refusing to affirm what Allah affirmed
بلا تكييف takyīf Asking "how?" — trying to describe the exact manner of Allah's attributes
بلا تمثيل tamthīl Resembling Allah to creation — saying His attributes are like ours

All four are summed up in one verse — the golden rule of this entire science:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."

Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:11 · سورة الشورى

Notice how the verse itself teaches the balance: لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ("nothing is like Him") denies tamthīl, while السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ ("the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing") affirms His names — denying taʿṭīl. We affirm without resembling, and we declare Him beyond likeness without denying.

تدريب القواعد Grammar Drill — The Superlative الحُسْنَى (فُعْلَى)

The word الحُسْنَى (al-Ḥusnā — "the most beautiful") is the feminine superlative. In Arabic, the masculine superlative is أَفْعَل (e.g. أَحْسَن — "more beautiful"), and its feminine partner is فُعْلَى (e.g. حُسْنَى). Because أسماء is grammatically feminine plural, we use the feminine form: الأسماء الحُسْنَى.

نتعلم أسماء الله من القرآن والسنة فقط. We learn Allah's names only from the Quran and the Sunnah.
