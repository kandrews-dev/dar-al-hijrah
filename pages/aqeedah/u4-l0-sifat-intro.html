أشرف العلومThe Noblest Knowledge

In Unit 2 you studied some of Allah's beautiful names one by one. Now we step back and ask the deeper question: how do we believe in Allah's names and attributes correctly? Knowing Allah by His names and attributes is the noblest of all knowledge, because its subject is the noblest — Allah Himself.

This unit draws on al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, written by Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah (رحمه الله) as a concise statement of the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah — the people of the Sunnah and the united body of Muslims who follow the way of the Prophet ﷺ and his Companions.

القاعدة الذهبيةThe Golden Rule

All of this unit rests on one balanced rule. Ahl al-Sunnah believe in Allah's names and attributes:

يُثبِتون لله ما أثبته لنفسه، وما أثبته له رسوله ﷺ، وينفون عنه ما نفاه عن نفسه — من غير تحريفٍ ولا تعطيل، ومن غير تكييفٍ ولا تمثيل

They affirm for Allah what He affirmed for Himself and what His Messenger ﷺ affirmed for Him, and they negate what He negated of Himself — without taḥrīf (distorting the texts), without taʿṭīl (denying the meanings), without takyīf (asking "how?"), and without tamthīl (likening Him to creation).

In short: our knowledge of Allah comes only from revelation (this is called tawqīf) — we do not invent names or attributes by our intellect, nor do we strip away what Allah affirmed. We say what He said, and we stay silent where He was silent.

الآية الجامعةThe Master Verse — Two Halves, Whole Creed

Ahl al-Sunnah call one verse "the verse that gathers the whole creed of the attributes," because it holds both sides of the balance at once:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."

Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:11 · سورة الشورى

«لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ»

Tanzīh / no likeness. Allah resembles nothing of His creation. This half refutes those who liken Him to creatures (tamthīl).

«وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ»

Ithbāt / affirmation. He really hears and really sees. This half refutes those who deny His attributes (taʿṭīl).

Notice the order: Allah declares there is nothing like Him and then affirms that He hears and sees. So affirming an attribute does not mean He resembles us — our hearing is created and limited; His is befitting His majesty. Affirmation without resemblance: that is the whole creed in a single āyah.

طرفان ووسطThe Two Extremes & the Straight Middle

Throughout history people deviated to one of two extremes. Ahl al-Sunnah walk the straight path between them:

Extreme 1 — Denial

أهل التعطيل

The Muʿaṭṭilah (e.g. Jahmiyyah, Muʿtazilah)

Deny Allah's attributes — or most of them — claiming that to affirm them would liken Him to creation. In fleeing tashbīh they fell into taʿṭīl, worshipping a god stripped of His perfect qualities.

★ The Straight Path

أهل السنة والجماعة

The People of the Sunnah

Affirm without likening (ithbāt bilā tamthīl) and declare Him free of defect without denial (tanzīh bilā taʿṭīl). They take the texts as they came, in a manner befitting His majesty.

Extreme 2 — Resemblance

أهل التمثيل

The Mushabbihah (anthropomorphists)

Liken Allah's attributes to those of creation ("His hand is like our hand"). In affirming, they fell into tamthīl — comparing the Creator to the created, which 42:11 forbids.

The principle that protects us from both: His attributes are real, but their "how" is unknown to us. We affirm the meaning and leave the manner (the kayfiyyah) to Allah. We will study these four words — taḥrīf, taʿṭīl, takyīf, tamthīl — in detail next lesson.
