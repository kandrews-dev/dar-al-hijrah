إِثْبَاتُ الكَلَامِ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى

The Affirmation of Speech for Allah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٨٠–٨٢
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 80–82.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Show how حَدِيثًا and قِيلًا each affirm speech for Allah.
  • Explain why تَكْلِيمًا in al-Nisāʾ 4:164 is a مَصْدَرٌ مُؤَكِّد, and what it rules out.
  • Distinguish المُنَادَاة from المُنَاجَاة as two kinds of speech.
  • Name the three messengers Allah addressed without an intermediary, and the source for the third.
٢١ · إِثْبَاتُ الكَلَامِ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى Section 21 — The Affirmation of Speech for Allah

This is one of the longest sections of the book, and deliberately so. The denial of Allah's speech was the doctrine the Jahmiyyah and the Muʿtazilah fought for hardest, and it is the issue over which Imām Aḥmad was imprisoned and beaten. So the author does not argue; he piles up the texts — sixteen āyāt, each affirming speech from a different angle: His truthfulness in speech, His saying, His word, His addressing Mūsā, His calling out, His conferring privately, and the Qurʾān itself being His speech. This lesson takes the first ten; the next takes the rest and al-Fawzān's summary of the madhhab of Ahl al-Sunnah.

١ · أَصْدَقُ الحَدِيثِ وَأَصْدَقُ القِيل First — Truest in Ḥadīth, Truest in Qīl

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حَدِيثًا﴾، ﴿وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ قِيلًا﴾.

وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حَدِيثًا

"And who is truer than Allah in speech?"

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:87 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ — no one is truer than Him سُبْحَانَهُ; so it is اسْتِفْهَامٌ إِنْكَارِيّ, a rhetorical question meant as a denial, not an enquiry. And حَدِيثًا — in His ḥadīth and His report, His command, His promise and His threat.

وَعْدَ اللَّهِ حَقًّا وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ قِيلًا

"The promise of Allah, in truth — and who is truer than Allah in word?"

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:122 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

القِيل — a verbal noun of قَالَ, like القَوْل; that is: no one is truer in speech than Allah عَزَّ وَجَلَّ.

And the shāhid from the two noble āyāt: that in them is the affirming of ḥadīth and qīl for Allah سُبْحَانَهُ — so in them is the affirming of speech for Him.

Follow the shape of that argument, because it is the one the whole section repeats. Neither āyah sets out to prove that Allah speaks; both are making a point about truthfulness. But truthfulness is a property of speech. You cannot be the truest speaker and not be a speaker — so the attribute arrives inside a statement about something else, which is why it cannot be argued away without dismantling the āyah.

٢ · إِثْبَاتُ القَوْلِ وَالكَلِمَة Second — His Saying and His Word

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَإِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا﴾.

وَإِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ أَأَنْتَ قُلْتَ لِلنَّاسِ اتَّخِذُونِي وَأُمِّيَ إِلَهَيْنِ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ

"And when Allah will say: O ʿĪsā son of Maryam, did you say to the people: take me and my mother as two gods besides Allah?"

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:116 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَإِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ — the implied verb is recall. And on the timing, the majority of the mufassirūn held that this saying from Him سُبْحَانَهُ will be on the Day of Rising, and it is a rebuke to those of the Christians who worshipped the Messiah and his mother.

And it is like the two preceding āyāt: in it is the affirming of saying for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He says when He wills.

That last clause is doing real work, and it will be the hinge of the next lesson. The āyah is future. A saying that has not happened yet, and will happen on a named day, is a saying tied to His will — which is precisely why Ahl al-Sunnah call speech an attribute of His actions as well as of His essence.

وَتَمَّتْ كَلِمَتُ رَبِّكَ صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ

"And the word of your Lord has been completed in truth and justice. There is no changer of His words."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:115 · سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

What is intended by الكَلِمَة is His speech سُبْحَانَهُ. And then the two accusatives are divided precisely: صِدْقًا — in His reports — and وَعَدْلًا — in His rulings. Grammatically, صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا are manṣūb as التَّمْيِيز, specifying in what respect the completion holds.

That division is worth holding on to as a rule for reading the Qurʾān: where it reports, it is true; where it rules, it is just. Those are the only two things a revelation does, and the āyah claims perfection in both. And in the āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى.

٣ · تَكْلِيمُ مُوسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام Third — His Addressing Mūsā

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًا﴾، ﴿مِنْهُمْ مَنْ كَلَّمَ اللَّهُ﴾، ﴿وَلَمَّا جَاءَ مُوسَى لِمِيقَاتِنَا وَكَلَّمَهُ رَبُّهُ﴾.

وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًا

"And Allah spoke to Mūsā with a direct speaking."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:164 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is an ennobling of Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام, in that Allah spoke to him — that is: He made him hear His speech; and this is why he is called الكَلِيم.

And now the single most quoted grammatical point in this whole subject. تَكْلِيمًا is مَصْدَرٌ مُؤَكِّد — a verbal noun brought for emphasis, to repel the speaking being figurative.

This is worth being slow about. In Arabic, a verb reinforced by its own maṣdar (كَلَّمَ … تَكْلِيمًا) is the construction that forecloses metaphor. One may say «the wall spoke to me» loosely; one does not say «the wall spoke to me with a speaking». So the āyah does not merely say that Allah spoke to Mūsā — it says so in the one grammatical form Arabic has for ruling out that the words were meant loosely. The taʾwīl was blocked before it was ever proposed. So in the āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah, and that He spoke to Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام.

تِلْكَ الرُّسُلُ فَضَّلْنَا بَعْضَهُمْ عَلَى بَعْضٍ مِنْهُمْ مَنْ كَلَّمَ اللَّهُ وَرَفَعَ بَعْضَهُمْ دَرَجَاتٍ

"Those messengers — We favoured some of them over others. Among them is he to whom Allah spoke, and some of them He raised in degrees."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:253 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

مِنْهُمْ — of the messengers عَلَيْهِمُ الصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَام — مَنْ كَلَّمَ اللَّهُ — He made him hear His speech without an intermediary.

And al-Fawzān names them: meaning Mūsā and Muḥammad عَلَيْهِمَا الصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَام; and likewise Ādam, as has come in the ḥadīth in صَحِيحُ ابْنِ حِبَّان. So in the āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He spoke to some of the messengers.

Note the care in the attribution. al-Fawzān does not leave the third name resting on nothing; he tells you which collection carries it. That is the standard this track holds itself to, and it is why the Further reading below names the sharḥ rather than asking you to take the lesson's word for it.

وَلَمَّا جَاءَ مُوسَى لِمِيقَاتِنَا وَكَلَّمَهُ رَبُّهُ

"And when Mūsā came to Our appointed meeting and his Lord spoke to him."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:143 · سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَلَمَّا جَاءَ مُوسَى لِمِيقَاتِنَا — his coming took place at the time Allah had appointed with him for it. وَكَلَّمَهُ رَبُّهُ — He made him hear His speech without an intermediary.

al-Fawzān gathers the three: so the āyāt contain the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He speaks whenever He wills سُبْحَانَهُ, and that He spoke to Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام without an intermediary.

٤ · النِّدَاءُ وَالمُنَاجَاة Fourth — Calling Out and Conferring Privately

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَنَادَيْنَاهُ مِنْ جَانِبِ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنِ وَقَرَّبْنَاهُ نَجِيًّا﴾، ﴿وَإِذْ نَادَى رَبُّكَ مُوسَى أَنِ ائْتِ الْقَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ﴾، ﴿وَنَادَاهُمَا رَبُّهُمَا أَلَمْ أَنْهَكُمَا عَنْ تِلْكُمَا الشَّجَرَةِ﴾، ﴿وَيَوْمَ يُنَادِيهِمْ فَيَقُولُ مَاذَا أَجَبْتُمُ الْمُرْسَلِينَ﴾.

وَنَادَيْنَاهُ مِنْ جَانِبِ الطُّورِ الْأَيْمَنِ وَقَرَّبْنَاهُ نَجِيًّا

"And We called him from the right side of the Mount, and We brought him near, conferring privately."

Sūrat Maryam 19:52 · سُورَةُ مَرْيَم

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَنَادَيْنَاهُ — Allah تَعَالَى called out to Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام; and النِّدَاء is the raised voice. الطُّور — a mountain between Egypt and Madyan.

And then a precision that shows how carefully the mufassirūn read: الأَيْمَن means the right side of Mūsā when he went seeking a burning brand from the fire he had seen — and what is intended is not the right of the mountain itself, for mountains have no right and no left.

وَقَرَّبْنَاهُ — We drew him near until We spoke to him; نَجِيًّا — conferring in secret. And al-munājāh is the opposite of al-munādāh.

المُنَادَاةwith a raised voice
المُنَاجَاةwith a voice that is not raised

And in the noble āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He calls out and confers privately — and these are two kinds of speech. The single āyah contains both, which is why it is the one the author picked.

وَإِذْ نَادَى رَبُّكَ مُوسَى أَنِ ائْتِ الْقَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ

"And when your Lord called Mūsā: go to the wrongdoing people."

Sūrat al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:10 · سُورَةُ الشُّعَرَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَإِذْ — the implied verb is recite, or recall. And النِّدَاء is the calling. The أَنْ of أَنِ ائْتِ may be explanatory or maṣdariyyah — either way the sense is: go to…

And why were they described as الظَّالِمِينَ? Because they joined together the disbelief by which they wronged themselves and the acts of disobedience by which they wronged others — such as their enslaving of the Children of Israel and the slaughtering of their sons.

A definition of ẓulm in two directions, dropped in passing: it runs inward before it runs outward. And in the noble āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He calls whom He wills of His servants and makes him hear His speech.

وَنَادَاهُمَا رَبُّهُمَا أَلَمْ أَنْهَكُمَا عَنْ تِلْكُمَا الشَّجَرَةِ

"And their Lord called out to them: did I not forbid you both that tree?"

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:22 · سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف

Allah تَعَالَى called out to Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ عَلَيْهِمَا السَّلَام, saying to them: «did I not forbid you both that tree» — that is, from eating of it. And this is a reproach from Allah to them both, and a rebuke, since they did not take care against what He had warned them against. And in the noble āyah is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and of the calling out from Him to Ādam and his wife.

وَيَوْمَ يُنَادِيهِمْ فَيَقُولُ مَاذَا أَجَبْتُمُ الْمُرْسَلِينَ

"And on the Day He will call out to them and say: what answer did you give the messengers?"

Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ 28:65 · سُورَةُ القَصَص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Allah سُبْحَانَهُ will call out to these mushrikūn on the Day of Rising, and will say to them: مَاذَا أَجَبْتُمُ الْمُرْسَلِينَ — that is: what was your answer to those of the prophets who were sent to you, when they conveyed My messages to you?

And the shāhid from the āyah: the affirming of speech for Allah, and that He speaks when He wills.

Four āyāt of calling out, and look at the range of who is called: Mūsā at the Mount, Ādam and Ḥawwāʾ in the Garden, and the mushrikūn on the Day of Rising. Past, primordial and future. The attribute is not confined to one event that might be explained away as a special case — and each one is a speaking, with a question in it that expects an answer.

What is تَكْلِيمًا in al-Nisāʾ 4:164, and what does it do?

A ḥāl, describing Mūsā’s state
An emphatic verbal noun, brought to repel the speaking being figurative
A tamyīz, specifying the manner
A badal for the name of Mūsā

How are صِدْقًا وَعَدْلًا divided in al-Anʿām 6:115?

Truth in the past, justice in the future
True in His reports, just in His rulings
Truth for the believers, justice for the disbelievers
Both refer only to the Torah

Which messengers does al-Fawzān name as spoken to without an intermediary?

Mūsā alone
Mūsā and Muḥammad ﷺ, and likewise Ādam — the last per a hadith in Ṣaḥīḥ Ibn Ḥibbān
Mūsā and ʿĪsā
All of the messengers without exception

What is the difference between المُنَادَاة and المُنَاجَاة?

One is to prophets, the other to angels
Munādāh is with a raised voice; munājāh with a voice not raised
One is in this world, the other in the Hereafter
One is speech, the other is inspiration

What does الأَيْمَن refer to in Sūrat Maryam 19:52?

The right side of the mountain
The right side of Mūsā as he approached the fire — mountains have no right or left
The more blessed of two mountains
The right hand of the angel

Why were Pharaoh’s people called الظَّالِمِينَ?

Because they rejected Mūsā only
Because they joined the disbelief by which they wronged themselves to the disobedience by which they wronged others
Because they were tyrants in trade
Because they worshipped the Nile
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
«وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ» اسْتِفْهَامٌ إِنْكَارِيٌّ. "Who is truer than Allah" is a rhetorical question meant as a denial.
«تَكْلِيمًا» تُفِيدُ أَنَّ الكَلَامَ مَجَازٌ. The word taklīman indicates that the speaking is figurative.
المُنَاجَاةُ ضِدُّ المُنَادَاةِ. Munājāh is the opposite of munādāh.
قَوْلُ اللهِ لِعِيسَى فِي المَائِدَةِ يَكُونُ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ عِنْدَ جُمْهُورِ المُفَسِّرِينَ. The majority of mufassirūn held that Allah’s address to ʿĪsā in al-Māʾidah will be on the Day of Rising.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًا

Four words that took an imām to prison. Memorize the grammar with the āyah: the verb is reinforced by its own maṣdar, and that is the one construction in Arabic which says — plainly, in advance — that the words are not to be read loosely.
