نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام · الدَّرْسُ الخَامِسYear 5

النَّاقِضُ الرَّابِع · تَفْضِيلُ غَيْرِ هُدَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ

The Fourth Nullifier, Part 1 — Preferring Other Than His Guidance

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the fourth nullifier’s two halves, and the إِجْمَاع Ibn Bāz reports on it.
  • Say why man-made codes are not ranked against each other, from النِّسَاء ٤:٦٠–٦١.
  • Show that تَوْحِيدُ الحُكْم is part of what لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ requires — from النَّحْل ١٦:٣٦ and الأَعْرَاف ٧:٥٤.
  • Give the three things النِّسَاء ٤:٦٥ demands, and what the footnote says about the إِيمَان it negates.
  • State the precision Ibn Bāz adds about one who believes Allah’s rulings are better and still permits man-made systems to replace them.
  • Grade the ḥadīth of ʿAdī ibn Ḥātim as the sharḥ grades it — and say what the sharḥ supplies for the ḥadīth of الغُرْبَة.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٠٨–١١٩
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 108–119, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الرَّابِع: مَنِ اعْتَقَدَ أَنَّ غَيْرَ هُدَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ أَكْمَلُ مِنْ هُدَاهُ، أَوْ أَنَّ حُكْمَ غَيْرِهِ أَحْسَنُ مِنْ حُكْمِهِ — كَالَّذِينَ يُفَضِّلُونَ حُكْمَ الطَّوَاغِيتِ عَلَى حُكْمِهِ — فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ.

Two halves: a belief about guidance, and a belief about judgement. Note the verb in both — اعْتَقَدَ, يُفَضِّلُونَ. The clause is written about a preference held, not only about an act done.

This is the longest of the ten — twenty-eight folios — and it is also the one most often made to serve an argument its author did not make. So read the clause’s own verbs first: اعْتَقَدَ, يُفَضِّلُونَ. It is written about a preference held in the heart.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This clause has a تَفْصِيل, and it is not on this page.

The sharḥ gives twelve folios to establishing the ruling (108–119, which this lesson covers) and then sixteen folios to the detail (120–135): كُفْرٌ أَكْبَر against كُفْرٌ أَصْغَر, Ibn ʿAbbās’s «كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر», the judge who decides one case for kinship or a bribe, the rule «لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم», and the ruling on الخُرُوج.

Nothing on this page licenses a ruling about a particular man or a particular state. Ibn Bāz’s sharpest words here are about codes and systems. What follows for an individual is decided by the detail — and the sharḥ itself decides it twelve folios later, which is part 2.

الإِجْمَاع What Exactly the Consensus Is On

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz states it in one sentence, and the wording is narrow on purpose:

«The scholars have agreed that whoever claims that the judgement of other than Allah is better than the judgement of Allah, or that the guidance of other than the Messenger ﷺ is better than the guidance of the Messenger ﷺ — he is a disbeliever.»

Read what the ijmāʿ is reported on. It is on the man who claims a superiority — زَعَمَ. It is not reported, in this sentence, on every ruling given by other than Allah’s law. That distinction is the hinge the whole twenty-eight folios turn on, and the sharḥ is careful with it from its first line.

القَوَانِينُ الوَضْعِيَّة Why the Codes Are Not Ranked

الشَّرْح · the explanation

On laws laid down by men with no reliance on the Book and the Sunnah:

«They are all of them the laying-down of human beings, and it is not permitted for the people of Islam to seek judgement from them — and none of them is more worthy of being judged by than another, because they are all of them from the judgement of the Jāhiliyyah, and from the judgement of the ṭāghūt which Allah warned against, and attributed to the hypocrites the desire to seek judgement from it.»

«None of them is more worthy than another» is a refusal to enter the comparison at all. The question «which code is best?» is not answered on the merits and then decided for the sharīʿah; it is declined, because the sharīʿah is not a competitor in that field. And note where he locates the desire to arbitrate elsewhere — the Qurʾān attributes it to the munāfiqūn, and he is quoting the verse that does so.

أَلَمْ تَرَ إِلَى الَّذِينَ يَزْعُمُونَ أَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْكَ وَمَا أُنزِلَ مِن قَبْلِكَ يُرِيدُونَ أَن يَتَحَاكَمُوا إِلَى الطَّاغُوتِ وَقَدْ أُمِرُوا أَن يَكْفُرُوا بِهِ وَيُرِيدُ الشَّيْطَانُ أَن يُضِلَّهُمْ ضَلَالًا بَعِيدًا

Have you not seen those who claim to have believed in what was sent down to you and what was sent down before you — they wish to refer judgement to the ṭāghūt, while they were commanded to disbelieve in it; and Satan wishes to lead them far astray.

سُورَةُ النِّسَاء · 4:60

وَقَدْ أُمِرُوا أَن يَكْفُرُوا بِهِ — while they were commanded to disbelieve in it. The verse does not merely forbid arbitrating to the ṭāghūt; it says disbelieving in it was already an order. Which is why this clause sits in a treatise on tawḥīd and not in a chapter on politics. And the next verse names what they do when called to Allah’s revelation: النِّسَاء ٤:٦١ — you see the hypocrites turning away in aversion.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He then puts the Qurʾān’s own question beside it — «and every ruling that contradicts the legislation of Allah is from the judgement of the Jāhiliyyah»:

أَفَحُكْمَ الجَاهِلِيَّةِ يَبْغُونَ وَمَنْ أَحْسَنُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حُكْمًا لِّقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ

Is it the judgement of the Jāhiliyyah that they seek? And who is better than Allah in judgement, for a people who are certain?

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:50

Note the last three words, which are easy to read past: لِّقَوْمٍ يُوقِنُونَ — for a people who are certain. The verse ties the recognition to yaqīn. It is not arguing that Allah’s judgement will look best to anyone; it is saying who it looks best to. And Ibn Bāz adds that Allah «informed us that judging by other than what Allah sent down is كُفْرٌ, ظُلْمٌ and فِسْقٌ» — the three words of المَائِدَة ٥:٤٤، ٤٥، ٤٧ — and that these verses «oblige the Muslims to beware of it, to disown it, and to hasten to the judgement of Allah and His Messenger, with an expansion of the chest to it and submission to it.»

تَوْحِيدُ الحُكْم Judgement Belongs to the One Who Creates

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The argument that makes this a creedal matter rather than a legal one is built from two verses. First, the mission of every messenger:

وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَّسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ

And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying]: worship Allah and shun the ṭāghūt.

سُورَةُ النَّحْل · 16:36

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«So whoever submitted to Allah, obeyed Him and referred judgement to His revelation — he is the one who worships Him; and whoever submitted to other than Him and referred judgement to other than His law — he has worshipped the ṭāghūt and yielded to it… And servitude to Allah alone, and barāʾah from the worship of the ṭāghūt and from referring judgement to it, is from what the testimony that there is no deity but Allah requires.»

Then the second verse, and it is the one that makes the whole argument short:

أَلَا لَهُ الخَلْقُ وَالأَمْرُ

Unquestionably, His is the creation and the command.

سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف · 7:54

«So just as He alone is the Creator, He alone is the One who commands — and what is obligatory is obedience to His command.» الخَلْق and الأَمْر are placed in one sentence by the Qurʾān. A man who grants the first and withholds the second has divided a verse. That is the whole of تَوْحِيدُ الحُكْم, and it is why the fourth nullifier is a nullifier at all and not merely a sin.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ibn Bāz shows that this was the actual mechanism of the ahl al-kitāb’s shirk — not statues, but obedience in making lawful and unlawful:

«And Allah has related of the Jews and the Christians that they took their rabbis and their monks as lords besides Allah — when they obeyed them in making the forbidden lawful and the lawful forbidden.»

اتَّخَذُوا أَحْبَارَهُمْ وَرُهْبَانَهُمْ أَرْبَابًا مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ وَالمَسِيحَ ابْنَ مَرْيَمَ وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا إِلَهًا وَاحِدًا لَّا إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ سُبْحَانَهُ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ

They took their rabbis and their monks as lords besides Allah, and the Messiah son of Maryam — while they were not commanded except to worship one God. There is no deity except Him; exalted is He above what they associate.

سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة · 9:31

الشَّرْح · the explanation

ابْنُ كَثِير, whom the sharḥ quotes from his tafsīr on this verse, draws out the definition: the One God is «the One who, when He forbids a thing, it is the forbidden; and what He makes lawful is the lawful; and what He legislates is followed; and what He rules by is carried out.»

And then the ḥadīth that stopped a Companion’s own objection. عَدِيُّ بْنُ حَاتِم had been a Christian before his Islam. He heard the Prophet ﷺ recite this verse and said: O Messenger of Allah, we did not worship them — understanding worship to mean slaughtering, vowing, prostration and bowing. The answer reframed the word for him:

أَلَيْسَ يُحَرِّمُونَ مَا أَحَلَّ اللهُ فَتُحَرِّمُونَهُ، وَيُحِلُّونَ مَا حَرَّمَ فَتُحِلُّونَهُ؟ قَالَ: بَلَى. قَالَ: فَتِلْكَ عِبَادَتُهُمْ

“Did they not forbid what Allah had made lawful, and you forbade it; and make lawful what He had forbidden, and you made it lawful?” He said: yes. He ﷺ said: “then that is your worship of them.”

رَوَاهُ أَحْمَدُ وَالتِّرْمِذِيُّ — gradings below

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This ḥadīth is the reason the clause is about worship, so its grading matters, and the sharḥ gives it in full rather than in a word. Here is everything it says:

ابْنُ بَازin Sharḥ Fatḥ al-Majīd: «it is of the category of الحَسَن»
ابْنُ بَاز (elsewhere)«there is weakness in it, but it is well known»
ابْنُ بَاز (a third time)«this ḥadīth, even though in its route is غُطَيْفُ بْنُ أَعْيَن, and he is weak, is supported by the athar of حُذَيْفَة and by what has come in that meaning from others than Ḥudhayfah among the righteous Salaf»
ابْنُ تَيْمِيَّةgraded it حَسَن (al-Fatāwā, and Minhāj al-Sunnah)
عَبْدُ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنُ حَسَنgraded it صَحِيح, in Fatḥ al-Majīd
الشِّنْقِيطِيّgraded it صَحِيح, in al-ʿAdhb al-Namīr
the عِلَّة namedal-Tirmidhī and a group narrate it through Ghuṭayf ibn Aʿyan ← Muṣʿab ibn Saʿd ← ʿAdī, and Ghuṭayf is weak; its shāhid from Ḥudhayfah is mawqūf, through Ḥabīb ibn Abī Thābit ← Abū al-Bukhtarī ← Ḥudhayfah — and Abū al-Bukhtarī did not hear from Ḥudhayfah

That is what an honest grading looks like. A weak narrator, named; a supporting athar, named, with its own defect named; four scholars raising it to ḥasan or ṣaḥīḥ on the strength of the support; and Ibn Bāz himself phrasing it three different ways in three places. Nothing here is tidied.

فَلَا وَرَبِّكَ An Oath, and Three Conditions

This is the verse Ibn Bāz returns to more than any other in the clause — he quotes it at least five separate times across these folios. «Allah swore an oath in this noble verse that the slaves do not believe until they make the Messenger ﷺ the judge in what arises between them.»

فَلَا وَرَبِّكَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ حَتَّى يُحَكِّمُوكَ فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيْنَهُمْ ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُوا فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ حَرَجًا مِّمَّا قَضَيْتَ وَيُسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

But no, by your Lord, they will not believe until they make you the judge in what arises between them, and then find within themselves no discomfort over what you have decided, and submit in full submission.

سُورَةُ النِّسَاء · 4:65

حَتَّى يُحَكِّمُوكَthat they bring it to him — in his lifetime to him himself, and after his death to his Sunnah
لَا يَجِدُوا فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ حَرَجًاthat no constriction remains in the chest at what he decided — an inward condition, not an outward one
وَيُسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًاthat they submit a complete submission — «contentment with the judgement of Allah and tranquillity in it»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three things, and the second two are of the heart. «So referring judgement to his Sunnah is referring judgement to what was sent down of the Qurʾān and the Sunnah» — and Ibn Bāz insists the verse is عَامَّةٌ عَلَى ظَاهِرِهَا, general upon its apparent meaning: «it is not permitted for the Muslims to depart from the legislation of Allah; rather it is obligatory upon them to judge by the law of Allah in everything — in what pertains to the acts of worship, and in what pertains to dealings, and in all affairs of religion and of the world.»

And he reads «لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ» widely too: «meaning the people, from the Muslims and others»; and «فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيْنَهُمْ» as «in what they disputed over» — whether between a state and another, a group and another, or one Muslim and his brother: «the ruling in all of that is the same.»

And the sharḥ’s footnote closes the escape that is usually taken here: «the إِيمَان negated in this place is the root of īmān, not merely its completeness» — and it gives the reason from the verse itself: by the evidence of what comes after it.

That footnote is doing real work. «They will not believe» is frequently softened into «their faith will not be complete». The sharḥ declines the softening and shows its working — the verse goes on to make submission itself the condition, which a man of deficient but real faith would already have.

الشَّهَادَةُ الثَّانِيَة What “Muḥammad Is the Messenger of Allah” Commits You To

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He turns the clause onto the second half of the kalimah — and this is the same move the third nullifier made with the first half:

«Likewise, “I bear witness that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah” does not benefit the one who says it if he does not make the Messenger ﷺ the judge, and does not yield to his judgement and his law, and does not magnify his command and his prohibition… the testimony benefits only the one who acts by it.»

And he gives the shape of the self-contradiction plainly: a man who says «I bear witness that there is no deity but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, and judging by the law of Allah is obligatory, and obligatory, and obligatory» — and then breaks this and does not do it — this testimony will not avail him; just as it will not avail the one who says it and then reviles Allah.

And here is the precision that most changes how the clause is read. It is easy to assume the nullifier requires a man to think man-made law superior. Ibn Bāz closes that door:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And there is no إِيمَان for one who believes that the rulings of people and their opinions are better than the ruling of Allah and His Messenger, or equal and similar to it, or who permits that man-made rulings and human systems take its place — even if he believes that the rulings of Allah are better, more complete and more just.»

Three separate things, then, and only the first is about superiority: holding the man-made better; holding it equal; or permitting it to take the sharīʿah’s place while privately granting that Allah’s law is better. The third is the one a man is most likely to think safe, and it is named explicitly.

And in another place he adds a fourth wording and a further point: «or that it is more suitable for society and more accommodating to society than the ruling of Allah, or that it is better than the ruling of Allah — all of this is apostasy from Islam, even if he did not act upon it»; and in a third place, of one who preferred the codes over the law: «this one’s disbelief is apparent.» The belief alone, unacted upon, is what the clause names — which is consistent with its verb, اعْتَقَدَ.

شُمُولُ الدِّين A Religion That Does Not Divide

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Why there is no portion of life left over, in his own words:

«The religion of Allah is a comprehensive religion — it takes in the interests of the slaves in this life and the next… so it is worship and leadership: the believer is a worshipper and is a commander of the army; worship and judgement — he is a worshipper praying and fasting, and a ruler judging by the law of Allah carrying out its rulings; worship and jihād… a muṣḥaf and a sword.»

And the consequence he draws from it is the sentence to keep: «He is not a worshipper of Allah who submitted to his Lord in some sides of his life and submitted to created beings in other sides.» Which is simply al-Nisāʾ 4:65 again, read as a statement about a man rather than about a court.

He also gives the comparison that fixes the priority: «the people’s need for the ruling of the Sharīʿah is greater and more severe than their need for food and drink — because the utmost of their affair, if food and drink fail them, is that they die, and death is inevitable; but whoever loses the Sharīʿah and turns away from it and follows other than it, his end is everlasting punishment.»

لِمَاذَا أُرْسِلَ What the Messenger ﷺ Was Sent to Replace

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The positive statement of the clause, which is usually left out of it altogether — what the law is for:

«Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ to put an end to the corrupt systems in human society, and the deviant morals, and the oppression and injustice, and to put in their place sound systems and just rulings… to remove what is in the earth of oppression and tyranny… and to displace the corrupt systems and the despotic ṭawāghīt who rule over people by falsehood, and wrong them, and transgress against their rights, and enslave them.»

And what replaces them: «leaders who are reformers, and just, upright systems, and wise and just legislations, which stop people at their limit, and do not distinguish between white and black, nor between red and other, nor between rich and poor, nor between one noble in people’s eyes and one lowly in their eyes; rather He made His law not distinguish between people — rather it directs them all, and commands them all, and forbids them all.»

That is the argument of the clause stated as a claim about justice rather than as a threat, and it belongs on the page as much as the ruling does. The objection to man-made codes is not that they are foreign; it is that a law written by men who are subject to it will bend for the rich and the well-born, and this one does not.

الغُرْبَة وَالشَّبَاب Strangeness, and a Word About the Young

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz quotes here a ḥadīth about the strangeness of Islam returning, and the «strangers» being «those who set right when the people have become corrupt», and in the other wording «who set right what the people have corrupted of my Sunnah». The sharḥ gives this one neither an attribution nor a grading at this place, so this page supplies none — it is recorded here as what he said, and no more than that.

What he does with it is the point: «the claim of Islam is not strange — the claims are many; but a strangeness in reality: in implementing the Sharīʿah, and carrying out the command of Allah, and truthfulness in that. This is the strange thing.»

And then, immediately, the restraint on the strangers themselves — those who rise to set things right must do so «beginning with setting themselves right, by striving against their own souls, and that they be upon insight and knowledge and guidance — not upon ignorance and misguidance; and that they seek the help of their scholars and their elders and consult with them.»

Insight before action, and the scholars consulted first. That is inside the same paragraph as the strongest language on these folios, and it is the sharḥ putting its own fence up before part 2 arrives.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He adds an encouragement from Ibrāhīm عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام, which is worth keeping for its own sake: «one man, there was none other than him on the earth [upon tawḥīd]… and the people were all upon disbelief and misguidance — and even so he did not stop from jihād and calling to Allah, until he went out against their idols and broke them and smashed them to pieces… this shows that strength and vigour and high resolve make a man undertake great works which a great multitude of weak resolve do not undertake.»

And on the young who ask for the Sharīʿah, a judgement that is his own and is often not quoted: «many of the rulers fear the young, because the young’s offence is that they demand the law of Allah and call to the law of Allah… and this is a good demand from the righteous youth, for which they are to be thanked; so the leaders ought to help them upon good, and guide them to what is correct if they err, and cultivate in them Islamic zeal and the strength of faith… and if a foot slips, or an error is found from them, they are directed to good.»

The demand is legitimate; the correction of those who overreach in it is by teaching them. Hold that sentence, because it is the bridge into part 2.

The hardest words on these folios are in the same passage — that where leaders «deviate and leave the truth and hold to falsehood» there is then «no recourse but to strive against them», and that states called Islamic are «themselves in need of being taken by the hand until they judge by the law of Allah». What that does and does not license is decided by the sharḥ’s own تَفْصِيل, at folios 120–131 — the akbar/aṣghar distinction, Ibn ʿAbbās’s «kufr short of kufr», and the ruling on الخُرُوج. They are part 2, and this clause is not finished without them.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَلَا وَرَبِّكَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ حَتَّى يُحَكِّمُوكَ فِيمَا شَجَرَ بَيْنَهُمْ ثُمَّ لَا يَجِدُوا فِي أَنفُسِهِمْ حَرَجًا مِّمَّا قَضَيْتَ وَيُسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

The verse Ibn Bāz returns to five times in these folios. Three conditions, and two of them are of the heart — bring it to him; find no constriction at what he decided; submit completely. And the sharḥ’s footnote refuses the usual softening: the إِيمَان negated here is the root, not merely the completeness — by the evidence of what comes after it in the verse itself.
