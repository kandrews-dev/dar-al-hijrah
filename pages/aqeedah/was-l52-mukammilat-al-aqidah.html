مُكَمِّلَاتُ العَقِيدَةِ مِنْ مَكَارِمِ الأَخْلَاق

The Completers of the Creed, from Noble Character

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٩٦–١٩٨
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 196–198.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Explain why these manners are called مُكَمِّلَاتُ العَقِيدَة, and what that says about the relation between creed and conduct.
  • State the order the Sharīʿah obliges in forbidding the wrong, and what the Muʿtazilah hold against it.
  • Give the two reasons Ahl al-Sunnah hold the Ḥajj, the Jumuʿah and the ʿĪds with the rulers whether they are righteous or corrupt — and Ibn Taymiyyah’s observation about those who rebel.
  • Define النَّصِيحَة linguistically and in the Sharīʿah.
  • Explain why al-Fawzān says the ḥadīth of the body is خَبَرٌ مَعْنَاهُ الأَمْر.
  • Give the three holdings-back that make up الصَّبْر, and define مُرُّ القَضَاء.
فَصْلٌ · فِي بَيَانِ مُكَمِّلَاتِ العَقِيدَةِ مِنْ مَكَارِمِ الأَخْلَاق A Section on the Completers of the Creed, from Noble Character

This section is like a completion of the section before it; in it is a clarification of the qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah which are of the completers of the creed. And the opening words carry the whole idea: «ثُمَّ هُمْ مَعَ هَذِهِ الأُصُول» — that is: along with their standing by them in knowledge and in deed, they adorn themselves with qualities which are of their completers and their fruits.

Read that word ثَمَرَاتُهَا carefully, because it decides how the whole section is to be taken. These manners are not a second subject appended to the creed once the creed is finished. They are what the creed produces — so a man’s conduct is evidence about his ʿaqīdah, and a creed that bears no such fruit is being held in some way other than the way Ahl al-Sunnah hold it.

Which is why this, and not the attributes of Allah, is where a book of ʿaqīdah ends. The last word of the Wāsiṭiyyah is about character.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

ثُمَّ هُمْ مَعَ هَذِهِ الأُصُولِ يَأْمُرُونَ بِالمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ المُنْكَرِ عَلَى مَا تُوجِبُهُ الشَّرِيعَةُ، وَيَرَوْنَ إِقَامَةَ الحَجِّ وَالجُمَعِ وَالأَعْيَادِ مَعَ الأُمَرَاءِ أَبْرَارًا كَانُوا أَوْ فُجَّارًا، وَيُحَافِظُونَ عَلَى الجَمَاعَاتِ، وَيَدِينُونَ بِالنَّصِيحَةِ لِلْأُمَّةِ. وَيَعْتَقِدُونَ مَعْنَى قَوْلِهِ ﷺ: «المُؤْمِنُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِ كَالْبِنْيَانِ يَشُدُّ بَعْضُهُ بَعْضًا» وَشَبَّكَ بَيْنَ أَصَابِعِهِ، وَقَوْلِهِ ﷺ: «مَثَلُ المُؤْمِنِينَ فِي تَوَادِّهِمْ وَتَرَاحُمِهِمْ وَتَعَاطُفِهِمْ كَمَثَلِ الجَسَدِ، إِذَا اشْتَكَى مِنْهُ عُضْوٌ تَدَاعَى لَهُ سَائِرُ الجَسَدِ بِالحُمَّى وَالسَّهَرِ». وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِالصَّبْرِ عِنْدَ البَلَاءِ، وَالشُّكْرِ عِنْدَ الرَّخَاءِ، وَالرِّضَا بِمُرِّ القَضَاءِ.

١ · الأَمْرُ بِالمَعْرُوفِ وَالنَّهْيُ عَنِ المُنْكَر Enjoining the Right and Forbidding the Wrong

الشَّرْح · the explanation

As Allah described them with that in His saying:

كُنْتُمْ خَيْرَ أُمَّةٍ أُخْرِجَتْ لِلنَّاسِ تَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَتَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنْكَرِ وَتُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ

"You are the best nation brought forth for mankind: you enjoin the right, forbid the wrong, and believe in Allah."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:110 · سُورَةُ آلِ عِمْرَان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And المَعْرُوف is a comprehensive name for everything Allah loves of sayings and of deeds. So the duty is not a list of causes; it is as wide as what Allah loves.

Then the restriction the matn itself attaches — عَلَى مَا تُوجِبُهُ الشَّرِيعَة: that is, by the hand, then by the tongue, then by the heart, according to ability and to maṣlaḥah — in opposition to the Muʿtazilah, who contravene what the Sharīʿah obliges in this.

Two things in that order are easy to miss. The first is that it is an order descending by capacity: a man does what he is able to do and is not asked for what he cannot. The second is المَصْلَحَة — the benefit. An act of forbidding that produces a greater evil than the one it removes is not what the Sharīʿah obliges, and that single word is what separates this clause from the next one.

٢ · إِقَامَةُ الشَّعَائِرِ مَعَ الأُمَرَاء Holding the Rites With the Rulers, Whoever They Are

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: Ahl al-Sunnah hold the obligation of establishing these rites together with the guardians of the Muslims’ affairs — أَبْرَارًا كَانُوا أَوْ فُجَّارًا, that is: whether they are upright and righteous or corrupt — a corruption that does not take them out of the religion.

That qualifying clause is load-bearing and it is the author’s own, not an apology added later. The ruling is about a sinful Muslim ruler. It says nothing about one who has left Islam, and so it cannot be quoted either way on a question it does not address.

Then the two reasons:

١because the Muslims’ purpose in this is the gathering of the word, and distance from division and disagreement
٢and because the corrupt ruler is not deposed by his corruption, and rebelling against him is not permitted — because of what follows from that of the loss of rights and the spilling of blood

And then al-Fawzān quotes the author of the matn himself, from elsewhere in his writings — an observation that is historical rather than theoretical:

Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah رَحِمَهُ اللهُ said: «And perhaps hardly a party is known to have rebelled against a holder of authority but that in its rebellion there was more corruption than there was in what it removed.»

Notice the form of the argument. It is not a claim that rulers are good; it is a claim about what rebellion reliably costs. Ibn Taymiyyah does not deny the injustice being complained of — he grants it and weighs it against the price of the remedy. And he states it with the caution of a man reporting a pattern rather than asserting a law: «perhaps hardly a party is known…»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ahl al-Sunnah oppose in this the people of innovation, from the Khawārij, the Muʿtazilah and the Shīʿah, who hold the fighting of the guardians and rebelling against them if they do what is injustice — or supposed it to be injustice — and they regard that as being of the category of enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong.

That parenthesis — «or supposed it to be injustice» — is the whole difficulty in four words. A rebellion is not launched upon a proven wrong; it is launched upon a wrong as the rebel judges it. And the groups named do not think of themselves as abandoning the Sharīʿah: they file their rebellion under the very duty of the previous clause. Which is why the matn put عَلَى مَا تُوجِبُهُ الشَّرِيعَة into that clause before arriving at this one.

٣ · المُحَافَظَةُ عَلَى الجَمَاعَاتِ · ٤ · النَّصِيحَةُ لِلْأُمَّة Guarding the Congregations · and Sincere Counsel to the Ummah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: they guard attending the obligatory prayer in congregation, the Jumuʿah and other than it, because that is of the greatest of the emblems of Islam, and an obedience to Allah and His Messenger in it.

خِلَافًا لِلشِّيعَةwho do not hold the prayer except with the infallible imām
وَخِلَافًا لِلْمُنَافِقِينwho stay away from the congregational prayer

Those two are placed side by side on purpose. One abandons the congregation out of a doctrine and one out of hypocrisy — and the matn is indifferent to the reason, because the Sunnah is the attendance either way. And ḥadīths have come on the excellence of the congregational prayer, the command to it and the prohibition of abandoning it; this is not the place to mention them.

And يَدِينُونَ بِالنَّصِيحَةِ لِلْأُمَّة — that is: they regard it as part of the religion.

لُغَةًand the root of النُّصْح in the language is الخُلُوص — purity, being unmixed
شَرْعًاwanting good for the one counselled, and directing him to what is in his interests

The etymology is the test. If naṣīḥah is from purity, then advice mixed with the wish to humiliate, to win, or to be seen advising is not naṣīḥah at all — whatever is true in its content. So Ahl al-Sunnah want good for the Ummah, and direct it to what its welfare lies in.

٥ · التَّعَاوُنُ وَالتَّأَلُّمُ لِأَلَمِ المُصَابِين Cooperation, and Feeling the Pain of the Afflicted

المُؤْمِنُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِ كَالْبِنْيَانِ يَشُدُّ بَعْضُهُ بَعْضًا

"The believer to the believer is like a building, each part of it bracing the other" — and he ﷺ interlaced his fingers.

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ

مَثَلُ المُؤْمِنِينَ فِي تَوَادِّهِمْ وَتَرَاحُمِهِمْ وَتَعَاطُفِهِمْ كَمَثَلِ الجَسَدِ، إِذَا اشْتَكَى مِنْهُ عُضْوٌ تَدَاعَى لَهُ سَائِرُ الجَسَدِ بِالحُمَّى وَالسَّهَرِ

"The likeness of the believers in their mutual love, mercy and tenderness is the likeness of the body: when one limb of it is in pain, the rest of the body is summoned to it with fever and sleeplessness."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ وَغَيْرُهُمَا

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So the two ḥadīths depict what the Muslims ought to be upon of cooperation and mutual mercy; and Ahl al-Sunnah act by what the two of them require. Then al-Fawzān goes through the wording:

المُؤْمِنُ لِلْمُؤْمِنand مَثَلُ المُؤْمِنِين — what is intended by īmān here is the complete īmān
كَالْبِنْيَانthis likening is intended for bringing it near to the understanding
يَشُدُّ بَعْضُهُ بَعْضًاa clarification of the point of resemblance
وَشَبَّكَ بَيْنَ أَصَابِعِهanother likening, also intended to bring it near to the understanding
كَمَثَلِ الجَسَدthat is: with respect to all of its limbs, in regard to the feeling of ease or of weariness
تَوَادِّهِمthe love of one another
تَعَاطُفِهِمthe tenderness of one toward another
اشْتَكَىwas in pain
تَدَاعَىone part of it shared with the other part in the pain
سَائِرُ الجَسَدthe rest of it
بِالحُمَّىwhat arises from the pain of heat in the body
السَّهَرsleeplessness

And then the observation that makes the ḥadīth a duty rather than a description:

— and this ḥadīth is a statement whose meaning is a command. That is: just as, when part of his body is in pain, that pain spreads to the whole of his body — so too the believers, that they may be as a single soul: when a calamity strikes one of them, all of them grieve, and they work at removing it.

خَبَرٌ مَعْنَاهُ الأَمْر — a grammatical observation doing moral work. The Prophet ﷺ did not say «feel for one another»; he said this is what you are like. So a man who does not feel it is not merely failing a duty — he is contradicting a description of himself, which is a sharper thing to be told. And in this comparison is a bringing-near to the understanding, and a showing-forth of meanings in visible forms.

٦ · الثَّبَاتُ فِي مَوَاقِفِ الامْتِحَان Standing Firm in the Places of Testing

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And of the qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah: their firmness in the situations of testing — and the matn gives three, one for each kind of circumstance a man meets: hardship, ease, and what he simply cannot alter.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الصَّبْرُ عِنْدَ البَلَاء
البَلَاء: the testing by calamities and hardships		الصَّبْر linguistically: الحَبْس — holding back; and its meaning here is three holdings-back at once:
— holding the soul back from anguish
— holding the tongue back from complaining and from discontent
— holding the limbs back from striking the cheeks and tearing the garments
الشُّكْرُ عِنْدَ الرَّخَاء
الرَّخَاء: the widening of blessing		الشُّكْر: an act that announces the magnifying of the Bestower, because He is the Bestower — and it is the servant’s expending of what Allah has favoured him with in obedience to Him
الرِّضَا بِمُرِّ القَضَاء
الرِّضَا: the opposite of السَّخَط		القَضَاء linguistically: the ruling; and in usage: the will of Allah connected to things as they are. And مُرُّ القَضَاء is what passes over the servant of what he dislikes — such as sickness, poverty, the harm of people, heat, cold, and pains.

Set the definition of شُكْر beside the definition of صَبْر and the symmetry appears. Ṣabr is defined entirely by restraint, and shukr entirely by expenditure — the one is three things a man holds in, the other is everything he has, spent outward in obedience. Which is the answer to a common confusion: gratitude in this definition is not a warm feeling about a blessing. It is the use the blessing is put to. A man who says «al-ḥamdu lillāh» over wealth he spends in disobedience has not performed shukr by the definition at all.

And notice that the third is the hardest, which is why it is placed last. Ṣabr is asked of a man in a calamity and shukr in ease — but riḍā is asked of him about the bitterness itself. Not that he should like sickness or poverty, which the definition of مُرّ concedes he dislikes, but that he should not be displeased with the Decree that brought it. That is a station above patience, and it belongs here because Part 5 established what the Decree is.

That it must be done in private first

What qualification does al-Fawzān attach to «righteous or corrupt»?

That the ruler must be a descendant of Quraysh
A corruption that does not take them out of the religion — so the ruling concerns a sinful Muslim ruler and addresses nothing else
That it applies only to the Jumuʿah, not the Ḥajj
That the ruler must have been appointed by consultation

What are the two reasons given for holding the rites with them?

That the rites are invalid otherwise, and that the ruler owns the mosques
That the Muslims’ purpose is the gathering of the word and distance from division; and that a corrupt ruler is not deposed by his corruption, rebellion bringing the loss of rights and the spilling of blood
That obedience to rulers is unconditional, and that patience is rewarded
That the Companions did so, and that the scholars agreed

What exactly does Ibn Taymiyyah’s observation claim?

That all rulers are righteous
That hardly a party is known to have rebelled but that its rebellion held more corruption than what it removed — a claim about what rebellion costs, not about the ruler’s goodness
That rebellion is disbelief
That injustice must be denied rather than weighed

What four words identify the real difficulty with the Khawārij position?

«if they do what is injustice»
«or supposed it to be injustice» — a rebellion is launched on a wrong as the rebel judges it
«they regard that as enjoining the right»
«the fighting of the guardians»

Under what duty do the Khawārij, Muʿtazilah and Shīʿah file their rebellion?

Jihād in the path of Allah
Enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong — which is why the matn restricted that duty to what the Sharīʿah obliges BEFORE arriving at this clause
The obligation of hijrah
Commanding obedience to the infallible imām

What is النُّصْح linguistically, and why does it matter?

From النَّصْب, effort — so advice must be laborious
From الخُلُوص, purity — so advice mixed with the wish to humiliate or to be seen advising is not naṣīḥah at all, whatever is true in its content
From النَّصِيب, a share — so each Muslim owes a portion
From النَّصّ, a text — so advice must cite evidence

What does al-Fawzān mean by calling the ḥadīth of the body خَبَرٌ مَعْنَاهُ الأَمْر?

That its chain is weaker than its meaning suggests
That it is phrased as a statement of what believers ARE like, so a man who does not feel it contradicts a description of himself rather than merely failing a duty
That its command was abrogated
That it applies only to those of complete īmān

What are the three holdings-back that make up ṣabr?

Of the eyes, the ears and the tongue
Of the soul from anguish, the tongue from complaining and discontent, and the limbs from striking the cheeks and tearing the garments
Of the hand, the tongue and the heart
Of food, drink and speech

How is shukr defined here, and what follows from it?

As saying al-ḥamdu lillāh upon receiving a blessing
As an act announcing the magnifying of the Bestower — the servant’s expending of what he was favoured with IN OBEDIENCE; so it is the use a blessing is put to, not a feeling about it
As refraining from complaint in times of ease
As distributing a fifth of one’s wealth

What is asked of a man by الرِّضَا بِمُرِّ القَضَاء?

That he should like sickness and poverty
Not that he should like them — the word مُرّ concedes he dislikes them — but that he should not be displeased with the Decree that brought them
That he should not seek a remedy
That he should conceal his affliction from people
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
مَكَارِمُ الأَخْلَاقِ ثَمَرَةٌ لِلْعَقِيدَةِ لَا شَيْءٌ زَائِدٌ عَلَيْهَا. Noble character is a fruit of the creed, not something added on top of it.
النَّهْيُ عَنِ المُنْكَرِ وَاجِبٌ بِالْيَدِ وَلَوْ أَفْضَى إِلَى مُنْكَرٍ أَعْظَمَ. Forbidding the wrong by the hand is obligatory even if it leads to a greater wrong.
الوَالِي الفَاسِقُ يَنْعَزِلُ بِفِسْقِهِ. A corrupt ruler is deposed by his corruption.
أَصْلُ النُّصْحِ فِي اللُّغَةِ الخُلُوصُ. The root of naṣīḥah in the language is purity.
الشُّكْرُ صَرْفُ العَبْدِ مَا أَنْعَمَ اللهُ بِهِ عَلَيْهِ فِي طَاعَتِهِ. Shukr is the servant’s expending of what Allah favoured him with in obedience to Him.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

الصَّبْرُ عِنْدَ البَلَاءِ، وَالشُّكْرُ عِنْدَ الرَّخَاءِ، وَالرِّضَا بِمُرِّ القَضَاء

Three circumstances, and between them they exhaust a life: what hurts you, what pleases you, and what you cannot change. Ṣabr is three things held in; shukr is everything you have spent outward in obedience; and riḍā is asked about the bitterness itself — not that you should like it, but that you should not be displeased with the One who decreed it. A creed that does not produce these three has not been understood, however accurately it is recited.
