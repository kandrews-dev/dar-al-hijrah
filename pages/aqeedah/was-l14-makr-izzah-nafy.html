المَكْرُ وَالعِزَّةُ وَنَفْيُ المِثْلِ وَالشَّرِيك

Stratagem, Might, and the Negating of Like and Partner

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the argument from Arabic that سَمِيعٌ بَصِيرٌ cannot be an empty name.
  • Define المَكْر as it is ascribed to Allah, and distinguish the two kinds of الكَيْد.
  • State the rule that الفِعْلُ أَوْسَعُ مِنَ الاسْمِ, and give al-Fawzān’s examples.
  • State the general rule for what is negated of Allah in the Book and the Sunnah.
١٣ · إِثْبَاتُ السَّمْعِ وَالبَصَرِ — تَمَامُ الحُجَّة Section 13 — Hearing and Sight: the Argument Completed

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author brings the āyāt of hearing and sight again, and here al-Fawzān completes the argument he began in Lesson 10. He notes first how the Qurʾān affirms it — hearing is ascribed to Allah in three grammatical forms: the past verb سَمِعَ, the present verb يَسْمَعُ, and the active participle سَمِيعٌ.

And then the argument itself, which needs no technical apparatus at all:

— It is not valid in the speech of the Arabs to say of anything that it is “hearing, seeing” unless that thing actually hears and sees. This is the default. So one does not say: a hearing, seeing mountain — because that is impossible except for something that hears and sees.

That single sentence disposes of the Jahmī position met in Lesson 5 — that the names are bare words carrying no attribute, so that السَّمِيع indicates no hearing. If that were how language worked, you could call a mountain samīʿ baṣīr and mean nothing by it. Nobody does, because nobody can.

١٤ · إِثْبَاتُ المَكْرِ وَالكَيْدِ عَلَى مَا يَلِيقُ بِه Section 14 — Stratagem and Plan, as Befits Him

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَهُوَ شَدِيدُ الْمِحَالِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَمَكَرُوا وَمَكَرَ اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الْمَاكِرِينَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَمَكَرُوا مَكْرًا وَمَكَرْنَا مَكْرًا وَهُمْ لَا يَشْعُرُونَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿إِنَّهُمْ يَكِيدُونَ كَيْدًا ۝ وَأَكِيدُ كَيْدًا﴾.

وَمَكَرُوا وَمَكَرَ اللَّهُ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرُ الْمَاكِرِينَ

"And they schemed, and Allah countered their scheme — and Allah is the best of those who counter schemes."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:54 · سورة آل عمران

الشَّرْح · the explanation

المِحَال in the language is severity — so: severe in His countering. al-Zajjāj: one says “I contended with him in miḥāl” when you pit yourself against him until it becomes clear which of you is stronger. And Ibn al-Aʿrābī says plainly: المِحَال is المَكْر.

And here is the definition that keeps the attribute clean, which al-Fawzān states outright: المَكْرُ مِنَ اللهِ: إِيصَالُ المَكْرُوهِ إِلَى مَنْ يَسْتَحِقُّهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَا يَشْعُرُ — bringing what is disliked to the one who deserves it, from where he does not perceive it.

The context of the Āl ʿImrān āyah is the Children of Israel who plotted to kill ʿĪsā and crucify him. And وَمَكَرَ اللَّهُ means He drew them on and repaid them for their scheming.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān then makes the division that answers the obvious objection — how can scheming be an attribute of perfection? الكَيْد is of two kinds:

قَبِيح — uglybringing it upon one who does not deserve it — and this is blameworthy
حَسَن — goodbringing it upon one who does deserve it, as a punishment — and this is praiseworthy

And the Lord does of this only what He is praised for — out of justice from Him and wisdom. He seizes the wrongdoer and the transgressor from where he does not reckon — not as the oppressors do with the servants of Allah.

al-Fawzān adds the controlling restriction: Allah did not describe Himself with plan, stratagem or outwitting except by way of recompense to one who did that wrongfully. And then the clinching comparison: it is known that requiting is good even from a creature — so what of the Creator?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

تَنْبِيه — and this one is a rule you will use far beyond this section. Ascribing plan and stratagem to Allah is from إِطْلَاقِ الفِعْلِ, the unrestricted use of the verb — and the verb is broader than the name.

His proof is elegant: Allah ascribed to Himself verbs from which no name of the doer was taken. He said أَرَادَ and شَاءَ — yet He is not named المُرِيد or الشَّائِي. Likewise مَكَرَ and يَمْكُرُ — and one does not name Him by the participle from it.

So the discipline is exact. What Allah said of Himself as a verb, we say as a verb. We do not manufacture a name out of it. This is why the names of Allah are not open to invention — and it is a direct application of the تَوْقِيفِيَّة principle from Lesson 4: this subject is received, not reasoned out.

١٥ · العَفْوُ وَالمَغْفِرَةُ وَالرَّحْمَةُ وَالعِزَّةُ وَالقُدْرَة Section 15 — Pardon, Forgiveness, Mercy, Might and Power

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿إِنْ تُبْدُوا خَيْرًا أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ أَوْ تَعْفُوا عَنْ سُوءٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا قَدِيرًا﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَلِلَّهِ الْعِزَّةُ وَلِرَسُولِهِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ عَنْ إِبْلِيسَ: ﴿قَالَ فَبِعِزَّتِكَ لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ﴾.

إِنْ تُبْدُوا خَيْرًا أَوْ تُخْفُوهُ أَوْ تَعْفُوا عَنْ سُوءٍ فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا قَدِيرًا

"If you disclose a good or conceal it, or pardon an offence — then indeed Allah is ever Pardoning, Able."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:149 · سورة النساء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَوْ تَعْفُوا عَنْ سُوءٍ — pass over the one who wronged you. And then the two names arrive together, which is the whole lesson of the āyah: Allah is عَفُوًّا قَدِيرًا toward His servants, passing over them while able to take retribution from them for what their hands earned.

al-Fawzān draws the instruction out: so take Him as your example — for He pardons while having the power. Pardon from the powerless is not pardon; it is inability wearing pardon's clothes. The two names are placed side by side so that the servant knows which kind he is being asked for.

قَالَ فَبِعِزَّتِكَ لَأُغْوِيَنَّهُمْ أَجْمَعِينَ إِلَّا عِبَادَكَ مِنْهُمُ الْمُخْلَصِينَ

"He said: then by Your might, I will surely mislead them all — except Your servants among them who are sincere."

Sūrat Ṣād 38:82–83 · سورة ص

Note who is speaking, and note where he stops. Iblīs swears by Allah's own might that he will misguide every one of them — and then makes an exception he was not asked to make: the sincere. Even the enemy knows where the wall is. Ikhlāṣ is named by Iblīs himself as the thing he cannot get past.

١٦ · إِثْبَاتُ الاسْمِ لِلَّهِ وَنَفْيُ المِثْلِ عَنْه Section 16 — Affirming the Name, Negating the Like

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿تَبَارَكَ اسْمُ رَبِّكَ ذِي الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿فَاعْبُدْهُ وَاصْطَبِرْ لِعِبَادَتِهِ هَلْ تَعْلَمُ لَهُ سَمِيًّا﴾، ﴿وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ﴾، ﴿فَلَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَنْدَادًا وَأَنْتُمْ تَعْلَمُونَ﴾.

تَبَارَكَ اسْمُ رَبِّكَ ذِي الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ

"Blessed is the name of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honour."

Sūrat al-Raḥmān 55:78 · سورة الرحمن

الشَّرْح · the explanation

البَرَكَة in the language is growth and increase; التَّبْرِيك is supplicating for blessing. And the meaning of تَبَارَكَ is He has become immense, or His affair has risen and become exalted — and al-Fawzān notes that this word is not applied to anyone but Allah.

And فَاعْبُدْهُ: single Him out in worship, and worship no other alongside Him. العِبَادَة in the language is lowliness and submission; in the Sharīʿah it is a comprehensive name for what Allah loves and is pleased with, of deeds and of statements — the definition you met on the Islam 101 track from Ibn Taymiyyah, arriving here from the other direction.

وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَنْ يَتَّخِذُ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ أَنْدَادًا يُحِبُّونَهُمْ كَحُبِّ اللَّهِ

"And among mankind are those who take, besides Allah, rivals — loving them as they love Allah."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:165 · سورة البقرة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān reads it against the āyah before it, which had established Allah's oneness by a plain proof of His immense dominion and His being alone in creating. And even with that proof standing, He informs us that there are still found among people those who take a rival beside Him and worship it — powerless idols.

And then the precise point: يُحِبُّونَهُمْ كَحُبِّ اللَّهِ means these disbelievers did not stop at merely worshipping those rivals; they loved them with an immense love and went to excess in loving them, as they love Allah. So — and this is the sentence to keep — فَقَدْ سَوَّوْهُمْ بِاللهِ فِي المَحَبَّةِ لَا فِي الخَلْقِ وَالرِّزْقِ وَالتَّدْبِيرِ: they equated them with Allah in love, not in creating, providing and governing.

If that rings a bell, it should. It is exactly the explanation Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim gave for the confession in the Fire — إِذْ نُسَوِّيكُمْ بِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ (al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:98) — which you studied as the capstone of al-Qawāʿid al-Arbaʿ. Two different books, two different authors, the same analysis of the same error.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The shāhid of the section, and with it a rule for the whole subject: the āyāt affirm the Name of Allah and His magnification, and negate the namesake, the equal and the rival — and that negation is نَفْيٌ مُجْمَلٌ, a summary negation.

al-Fawzān then states the general principle: this is the way that comes in the Book and the Sunnah regarding what is negated of Allah — that there be negated of Him everything which contradicts His necessary perfection, of the kinds of defect and deficiency.

Hold that beside Lesson 6. The affirmation in the Qurʾān is detailed and specific — the Face, the Hands, the hearing, the mercy, each named. The negation is summary — no like, no equal, no rival, no defect. Detailed in what it affirms, general in what it denies. The muʿaṭṭilah inverted exactly that: general about the affirmation, exhaustively detailed about the denial.

١٧ · نَفْيُ الشَّرِيكِ عَنِ اللهِ تَعَالَى Section 17 — Negating the Partner

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَقُلِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلِيٌّ مِنَ الذُّلِّ وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا﴾.

وَقُلِ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلِيٌّ مِنَ الذُّلِّ وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا

"And say: all praise belongs to Allah, who has not taken a son, and who has no partner in dominion, and who has no helper out of weakness — and magnify Him greatly."

Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:111 · سورة الإسراء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الحَمْد is praise, and the article on it is لِلاسْتِغْرَاق, for totality: all praise belongs to Allah. Then three denials, and al-Fawzān names the party answered by each:

لَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًاHe has no son — against the Jews, the Christians, and some of the mushrikūn of the Arabs
وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي المُلْكِno partner in His dominion and Lordship — against the dualists and whoever holds a plurality of gods
وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ وَلِيٌّ مِنَ الذُّلِّno ally taken out of weakness — He needs no helper at all

And then the command that closes it: وَكَبِّرْهُ تَكْبِيرًا. The āyah does not end with the denials; it ends by telling you what to do once they are settled — magnify Him, and magnify Him greatly. Every section of this book has been building toward a disposition, not only a position.

That everything contradicting His necessary perfection is negated — the kinds of defect and deficiency, as a summary negation
That nothing may be negated of Him
That negation is left to the intellect

يُقَالُ فِي لُغَةِ العَرَبِ: جَبَلٌ سَمِيعٌ بَصِيرٌ. In Arabic one may say: a hearing, seeing mountain.
لَمْ يَصِفِ اللهُ نَفْسَهُ بِالمَكْرِ إِلَّا عَلَى وَجْهِ الجَزَاءِ. Allah described Himself with makr only by way of recompense.
لَفْظُ «تَبَارَكَ» لَا يُطْلَقُ إِلَّا عَلَى اللهِ. The word tabāraka is applied to none but Allah.
إِبْلِيسُ اسْتَثْنَى المُخْلَصِينَ مِنْ إِغْوَائِهِ. Iblīs excepted the sincere from those he would misguide.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَإِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ عَفُوًّا قَدِيرًا

Two names that must be read together. He pardons — and He was never unable to do otherwise. When you are wronged and have the upper hand, this is the āyah you are being measured against.
