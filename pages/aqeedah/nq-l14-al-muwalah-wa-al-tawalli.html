النَّاقِضُ الثَّامِن · المُوَالَاةُ وَالتَّوَلِّي

The Eighth Nullifier, Part 1 — Allying and Siding-With

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the أَصْل of this clause — what is obligatory toward the mushrikīn and toward the people of tawḥīd — and the verse it rests on.
  • Give the three divisions Ibn Bāz names, with the ruling on each and an example of each.
  • Define المُوَالَاة and التَّوَلِّي as he defines them, and state the ruling on each in his own six words.
  • Give his answer on raising the banner beside the cross and on cheering a disbelieving sportsman — and say what each one is instead.
  • Distinguish the state of war from the state of treaty in what may be given.
  • Explain why al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8 does not establish love of the peaceful disbeliever — «فَالبِرُّ شَيْء، وَالمَحَبَّةُ شَيْءٌ آخَر».
  • State the two obligations of الإِعْدَاد and الحَذَر, and the thing the sharḥ says the enmity does not prevent.
  • Say when ignorance is an excuse in this clause and when it is not.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٩٤–٢٠٤
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 194–204, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الثَّامِن: مُظَاهَرَةُ المُشْرِكِينَ وَمُعَاوَنَتُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين؛ وَالدَّلِيلُ قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ إِنَّ اللهَ لَا يَهْدِي القَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ﴾.

Read the clause’s own wording before anything else: it names مُظَاهَرَة and مُعَاوَنَة — backing and aiding — and it names what they are against: «عَلَى المُسْلِمِين».

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَّخِذُوا الْيَهُودَ وَالنَّصَارَىٰ أَوْلِيَاءَ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ وَمَن يَتَوَلَّهُم مِّنكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَهْدِي الْقَوْمَ الظَّالِمِينَ

O you who believe, do not take the Jews and the Christians as allies. They are allies of one another. And whoever among you allies with them — then he is one of them. Indeed, Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people.

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:51

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This clause has a distinction at its centre, and the sharḥ spends most of twenty-nine folios on it. Ibn Bāz states it in six words:

«المُوَالَاةُ غَيْرُ التَّوَلِّي؛ المُوَالَاةُ مَعْصِيَة، وَالتَّوَلِّي كُفْر.»

«Allying is not the same as siding-with; allying is a sin, and siding-with is disbelief» — and siding-with he defines as «مُسَاعَدَتُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين», aiding them against the Muslims. A reading that collapses the two has not taught this clause; it has manufactured a takfīr the source refuses.

أَصْلٌ أَصِيل The Foundation, Stated First

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«What is obligatory upon every Muslim is disownment of the mushrikīn, and disownment of their religion, and disbelief in that; and allegiance to the people of tawḥīd and love of them and love of their religion. هَذَا أَصْلٌ أَصِيل — this is a foundational foundation.»

And he states it in both directions, because it is one thing with two faces: «فَتَجِبُ المَحَبَّةُ لِلْمُؤْمِنِينَ وَمَحَبَّةُ دِينِهِمْ وَمُوَالَاتُهُمْ عَلَى ذَلِك… وَتَجِبُ المُعَادَاةُ لِلْكَافِرِينَ، وَالبَغْضُ لَهُمْ، وَتَجِبُ البَرَاءَةُ مِنْ دِينِهِمْ وَاعْتِقَادُ بُطْلَانِه.»

فَمَن يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالْعُرْوَةِ الْوُثْقَىٰ لَا انفِصَامَ لَهَا

So whoever disbelieves in false objects of worship and believes in Allah has grasped the most trustworthy handhold, which will not break.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:256

Note the order inside the verse, because the sharḥ leans on it: the disbelief in the ṭāghūt comes first, and the belief in Allah second. «فَلَا بُدَّ مِنَ الكُفْرِ بِالطَّاغُوت، وَالإِيمَانِ بِالله.» Which is why this clause is in a book of nullifiers at all: a man who grants allegiance where barāʾah is obliged has let go of one half of the handhold.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And one sentence here is easy to pass over and should not be: «and likewise love of disbelief and being pleased with it is an independent disbelief — وَإِنْ لَمْ يَفْعَلْهُ الإِنْسَان, even if the person does not do it.»

That is the matn of the seventh nullifier arriving here — «فَمَنْ فَعَلَهُ أَوْ رَضِيَ بِهِ» — and it means the clause is not only about deeds. But keep reading, because the next thing the sharḥ does is tell you what is NOT this.

الأَقْسَامُ الثَّلَاثَة Three Divisions, Named by Him

He sets them out himself and then counts them off — «المَقْصُود: أَنَّ هَذِهِ الأَقْسَامَ الثَّلَاثَةَ وَاقِعَة». This is the table to learn before anything else on the page.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

what it isthe ruling
القِسْمُ الأَوَّل«Being pleased with disbelief and not denouncing it and not disowning it, or aiding the disbelievers against the Muslims and giving the disbelievers victory over the Muslims» — and love of their religionرِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام — «وَمُظَاهَرَةُ المُشْرِكِينَ رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام»
الثَّانِي«Friendship with them or love of them for a worldly matter, and allying with them for that — not for love of their religion and not for giving them victory over the Muslims»: for trade, a partnership, money hoped for, kinshipمُنْكَرٌ وَمِنْ وَسَائِلِ الشِّرْك — forbidden, and a means to disbelief. NOT ridda.
الثَّالِث«That help be sought from them against the enemies, against other disbelievers, or in the interests of the Muslims, without allegiance»جَائِز — «فَهَذَا مِنَ القِسْمِ الجَائِز»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the third division is not a modern concession. He proves it entirely from the Prophet’s ﷺ own life and his Companions’.

The Jews of Khaybar, left for the tilling and cultivation; al-Muṭʿim ibn ʿAdī and others, to restrain the harm of other wrongdoing disbelievers; his uncle Abū Ṭālib, against the disbelievers; al-Ṣiddīq with Ibn al-Dughunnah, a disbeliever, against the disbelievers — «not for love of his religion, but to fight the other disbelievers»; and ʿAbdullāh ibn Urayqiṭ al-Dīlī, hired to show the way to Madīnah.

The sharḥ recounts all five as the Shaykh’s own recollection, without isnād or grading at this place, and the page reports them as he gives them.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Khaybar case is qualified in the footnote rather than left as a blank licence, which is the sort of thing this book does well: «he ﷺ kept the Jews at Khaybar for a temporary period for the tilling and cultivation, for the severe need, because of the Muslims being occupied with jihād. So the intent is that this and its like is what is permitted for a temporary, necessary, severe need — and it is not permissible to expand and be lax in these matters.»

And ʿUmar’s expulsion of them is explained rather than left as a contradiction: «ʿUmar expelled them on account of events they brought about, and in execution of the Prophet’s ﷺ command to remove them from the Peninsula; ʿUmar carried this out in his time because he had the leisure for it and his rule was long — unlike al-Ṣiddīq, whose rule was short and who was occupied with the people of apostasy.»

المُوَالَاةُ غَيْرُ التَّوَلِّي The Distinction, and Where Each Line Falls

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«As for taking him as a friend and a beloved, dealing with him as one deals with a friend — this is a kind of مُوَالَاة; and مُوَالَاة is not التَّوَلِّي. المُوَالَاة is a sin, and التَّوَلِّي is disbelief, and it is: aiding them against the Muslims. So المُوَالَاة is less and lower than التَّوَلِّي.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

المُوَالَاة — مَعْصِيَةالتَّوَلِّي — كُفْرٌ وَرِدَّة
his own examples«their love, or presenting a gift to them, or cheerfulness in their faces, or initiating the greeting with them — this is a kind of the forbidden muwālāh»; and attending their festivals, laughing with them, being at ease with them«giving them victory over the Muslims, and aiding them against the Muslims — this is apostasy and a nullifier of Islām»
its definitionFriendship and love for a worldly cause — «not for love of their religion, nor for being pleased with it, nor because it is true»Ibn Bāz, asked for the standard: «التَّوَلِّي هُوَ النَّصْر, as the scholars say… and its root is the love of hearts, then giving them victory and backing them against the Muslims points to it»
what it is called«وَتُسَمَّى مُوَالَاة» — and «وَسَائِلُ مِنْ وَسَائِلِ الكُفْر وَشَرٌّ عَظِيمٌ يَجِبُ الحَذَرُ مِنْهَا»«وَلَكِنَّ التَّوَلِّي كَوْنُهُ يَتَوَلَّاهُمْ وَيَنْصُرُهُمْ يُعِينُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين — هَذَا رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام»

«هَذَا شَيْءٌ وَهَذَا شَيْء» — this is one thing and that is another. He says it in those words, and the reason he insists is not leniency: it is that the second thing is the catastrophe — «حَتَّى لَا تَقَعَ الكَارِثَة», “so that the disaster does not occur.” The whole point of keeping the lesser thing lesser is that you can still be warned off it without being told you have left Islām.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he does not soften the lesser category while refusing to inflate it. What is obliged toward the disbeliever one deals with: «that there be in your heart his hatred for Allah’s sake and enmity to him for Allah’s sake, and that you keep away from his friendship and allegiance in any place, and that he know that of you — and that you are an enemy to him and he an enemy to you, and that you are free of him and he is free of you.»

And then the reason, which is the hinge: «حَتَّى لَا تَقَعَ الكَارِثَة، وَهُوَ المُوَالَاةُ الَّتِي تَجْعَلُكَ كَافِرًا بِمَحَبَّةِ دِينِهِ أَوْ نُصْرَتِهِ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين.» The sin is treated seriously precisely because it is the road to the nullifier — not because it is the nullifier.

قِسْمَانِ: مَعْصِيَةٌ وَمُكَفِّرَة Two Questions He Refuses to Inflate

Here is where the distinction stops being theory. Two concrete modern questions were put to him, of exactly the kind that get answered with takfīr by people quoting this clause — and he answered both the other way.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Raising the banner of tawḥīd beside the banner of the cross — is that of التَّوَلِّي?

Ibn Bāz — «كَلَّا — no. هَذَا نَوْعُ تَشَبُّه — this is a kind of imitation; because التَّوَلِّي, as I said, is giving them victory and backing them against the Muslims. We ask Allah for soundness.»

Q — Cheering a disbelieving footballer — is that of allegiance to him?

Ibn Bāz — «This has been researched, and it was made clear to them that the duty is not to cheer them, and that the duty is that this be confined to the Muslims, and that the disbelievers not be cheered. وَلَيْسَ هَذَا مِنَ المُوَالَاةِ المُكَفِّرَة؛ لَكِنَّهُ نَوْعُ مُوَالَاة، مَعْصِيَة.»

And in the same answer he names the two categories outright: «المُوَالَاةُ قِسْمَانِ: مَعْصِيَةٌ وَمُكَفِّرَة» — honouring him, softening speech with him, and the like, for the sake of coveting his wealth or something from him, is of the forbidden muwālāh; and the disbelief-entailing muwālāh is «أَنْ يَنْصُرَ الكُفَّارَ عَلَى المُسْلِمِينَ، وَأَنْ يُوَالِيَهُمْ ضِدَّ المُسْلِمِين».

And attending their festivals, or laughing with them and being at ease with them: «هَذِهِ مُوَالَاة، مَعْصِيَة، وَسِيلَةٌ لِلْكُفْر» — allegiance, a sin, a means to disbelief. Forbidden, and named a sin in the same sentence that forbids it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Count what he just declined to call apostasy: a national banner flown beside a cross; cheering a sportsman; a gift; a smile; greeting first; attending a festival; laughing in their company. Every one of these is forbidden in his answer, and not one of them is the nullifier. The nullifier has one shape — «نَصْرُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين» — and he will not let anything else wear it.

التَّوَلِّي العَامّ And the Worst of It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — التَّوَلِّي العَامّ?

Ibn Bāz — «التَّوَلِّي العَامُّ هَذَا أَشَرُّهَا — the general siding-with, this is the worst of them: giving them victory over the Muslims, and backing them against the Muslims, and aiding them against the Muslims — this is apostasy from Islām: «وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ».»

Q — Does «وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ» necessitate disbelief for whoever allies with them?

Ibn Bāz — «عَلَى حَسَبِ التَّوْلِيَة — according to the degree of the allying. So if he gave them victory and backed them against the Muslims, he was like them.»

«عَلَى حَسَبِ التَّوْلِيَة» is the answer to the verse being used as a blanket. The verse is not read as a flat equation; it is read as graduated, and the top of the scale is named. That is the same move as «فِيهِ التَّفْصِيل» in the seventh nullifier and «الفَرْدُ وَالفَرْدَانِ فِسْق» in the sixth.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also gives the clearest single case of the nullifier in commerce, and it is worth having because it is exact: «it is not permitted to cooperate with any human being upon sin and transgression — not by selling him grapes to make wine of, nor by selling him what aids him in gambling, nor by selling him what aids him in fighting the Muslims, as that he should sell the disbelievers weapons so that they may fight the Muslims with them — this is apostasy… this means تَوَلٍّ لِلْكُفَّار.»

And immediately the comparison that keeps it exact: «وَإِذَا بَاعَهُ عَلَى قُطَّاعِ الطَّرِيقِ أَوْ عَلَى البُغَاةِ صَارَ مُعَاوَنَةً عَلَى المَعَاصِي فَيَكُونُ مُحَرَّمًا وَمُنْكَرًا.» Same weapon, same seller — and the ruling turns entirely on who it is to be used against. Sold to disbelievers to fight Muslims: apostasy. Sold to bandits or rebels: forbidden and an abomination, and not apostasy.

حَالُ الحَرْبِ وَحَالُ السِّلْم War and Peace Are Not the Same Chapter

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The clause has a hinge that is not about the heart at all — it is about whether there is a war on.

«But it differs in the state of war and of peace. So in the state of war with the enemies, it is not permissible that they be aided with anything at all — not weapons, nor money, nor food, nor anything else; because giving them is an aid to them against the Muslims and a backing of them, so that becomes disbelief and apostasy from Islām, and a nullifier of its nullifiers.»

«As for in the state of peace, and of treaty, and of reconciliation — there is no objection to their being given: the relative gives his relative, and the Muslim gives the poor one, and the one in authority gives them what he sees benefit for the Muslims in, or exchanges gifts with them, or other than that in which he sees benefit — all of this there is no harm in, within the bounds of the Muḥammadan Sharīʿah.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And of the ḥarbī specifically: «as for the warring disbelievers, it is not permissible to assist them with anything; rather assisting them against the Muslims is of the nullifiers of Islām» — «وَمَظَاهَرَةُ الكُفَّارِ وَمُسَاعَدَتُهُمْ وَمُعَاوَنَتُهُمْ ضِدَّ المُسْلِمِينَ هَذَا مِنْ نَوَاقِضِ الإِسْلَامِ عِنْدَ جَمِيعِ العُلَمَاء».

And of the treaty state, the list is specific: «there is no bar to assisting them and doing good to them in what they need — as their poor and their disabled, and the like of that, and the ties of kinship and the womb.»

لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ

Allah does not forbid you from those who did not fight you over religion and did not expel you from your homes — that you should deal kindly and justly with them. Indeed, Allah loves the just.

سُورَةُ المُمْتَحَنَة · 60:8

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the case he brings for it is Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا: during the truce after the Prophet ﷺ made peace with the mushrikīn of Makkah on the laying down of war for ten years, «her mother came in that period as a visitor to Madīnah, seeking her daughter Asmāʾ for assistance and support; so she asked the Prophet ﷺ for a ruling on that, and he ﷺ gave her the fatwā that she should maintain the tie with her — meaning: that she should do good to her with money.»

The sharḥ gives that as the Shaykh’s own recounting, without isnād or grading at this place, and the page reports it as he gives it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then he gives a reason for the kindness that reframes the whole category.

«Then this doing of good, in the time of truce and the time of treaty, may be a path to guidance; it may be a cause of the Islām of the one given and the one treated well, and his entering into the religion of Allah. And for this Allah legislated, in the zakāh and in the treasury, a share for المُؤَلَّفَةُ قُلُوبُهُمْ — because a person may be given wealth while he is a mushrik, and Allah guides him to Islām by reason of the giving.»

And the case: Ṣafwān ibn Umayyah, who had held back from Islām, and the Prophet ﷺ kept giving him and treating him well until Islām entered upon him — and his own sentence afterwards: «مَا كَانَ أَهْلُ بَيْتٍ أَبْغَضَ إِلَيَّ مِنْ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ، فَلَمْ يَزَلْ يُعْطِينِي وَيُعْطِينِي حَتَّى صَارَ أَحَبَّ النَّاسِ إِلَيّ» — “there was no household more hateful to me than the Messenger of Allah’s ﷺ; and he kept on giving me and giving me until he became the most beloved of people to me.”

البِرُّ شَيْءٌ وَالمَحَبَّةُ شَيْءٌ آخَر What 60:8 Does and Does Not Establish

And the compiler’s footnote here is doing necessary work, because 60:8 is the verse most often stretched. Some use it to establish the permissibility of loving the peaceful disbeliever.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And this is an error, and the verse does not indicate it; because kindness toward a disbeliever does not entail love of the disbeliever. فَالبِرُّ شَيْء، وَالمَحَبَّةُ شَيْءٌ آخَر — kindness is one thing, and love is another. A person may be kind to an animal, or to one he hopes something from or fears, and love of it is not entailed by that.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three further objections are answered, each in a way worth having:

«إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ»al-Qaṣaṣ 28:56 is read as «you loved his guidance, not his person» — and the occasion of revelation shows it: he ﷺ was eager for him, loving his guidance; and when he was not guided, Allah sent down the verse. And even granting the other reading, there is no argument in it: the verse is Makkan, revealed about Abū Ṭālib; then Allah sent down in Madīnah «لَا تَجِدُ قَوْمًا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللهِ وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ يُوَادُّونَ مَنْ حَادَّ اللهَ وَرَسُولَه» and «قَدْ كَانَتْ لَكُمْ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ فِي إِبْرَاهِيم» — so if he had loved Abū Ṭālib, Allah forbade it to him after that
Ibrāhīm’s barāʾahSome claimed Ibrāhīm said «بَدَا بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمُ العَدَاوَةُ وَالبَغْضَاءُ أَبَدًا» only after they hated and fought him. «وَهَذَا إِفْكٌ مُبِين» — and the refutation is from the verse’s own ending: he made the limit at which the enmity ceases their believing in the tawḥīd of Allah — «حَتَّى تُؤْمِنُوا بِاللهِ وَحْدَه» — «and he did not say: until you leave off hating me and opposing me». And it entails belittling that noble prophet — as though he hated them in his own defence, and did not hate them for their violating Allah’s sanctities and associating partners with Him. «سَاءَ مَا يَحْكُمُون.»
the kitābiyyah wifeThe argument from marrying a disbelieving woman of the People of the Book, and the love and companionship it entails. Two answers: either it is a matter a text has come with, so it is particularised to it and the ruling confined to it; or love of a disbelieving wife is a love of appetite, like love of a disbeliever’s food and drink — not an essential love nor a religious one. And the binding consequence: on their own reasoning they would have to permit loving the warring disbelievers too, «because just as Allah made lawful to us the food and the women of the People of the Book of the people of dhimmah, so He permitted to us the food and women of the People of the Book who are at war»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the footnote closes with a sentence about method that is worth more than the three answers put together.

«And of the habit of the people of innovation is: objecting to the universals by the particulars, and striking the certainties with the probabilities, and overturning the consensuses by what is of the fallen among the possibilities. We seek refuge with Allah from ignorance.»

Three moves, and once you can name them you will see them everywhere — including, honestly, in arguments made on the stricter side of this very clause.

الإِعْدَادُ وَالحَذَر Preparation, Wariness — and Daʿwah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

None of the permitted kindness relaxes two standing obligations, and he names both as وَاجِب in war and peace alike:

«As for what pertains to preparation against them — this is obligatory; and likewise taking wariness of them is obligatory in war and peace together… so preparation against them and readiness for them and taking wariness of them, so that they do not seize us unawares — this is obligatory upon the guardians of the Muslims’ affairs: that they prepare for their enemies what they can of strength, and that they be ready for jihād, and that they beware the consequence of laxity; فَإِنَّ الأَعْدَاءَ لَا يُؤْتَمَنُونَ أَبَدًا — for the enemies are never to be trusted.»

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا خُذُوا حِذْرَكُمْ

O you who believe, take your precaution.

سُورَةُ النِّسَاء · 4:71

وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُم مَّا اسْتَطَعْتُم مِّن قُوَّةٍ

And prepare against them whatever you are able of strength.

سُورَةُ الأَنْفَال · 8:60

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then, in the next sentence, the thing that most readers of this clause never quote.

«Then this hatred and this enmity between us and them does not prevent calling them and being eager for their guidance; so the callers and those who direct to good are sent to them — perhaps Allah will bring them out of the darknesses into the light.»

Barāʾah and daʿwah in two consecutive sentences, by the same man, with no sense of strain — because they were never in tension. You do not call someone you have no hope for.

He adds the promise that makes the wariness bearable: «but whenever the Muslims are granted hating them for Allah’s sake and opposing them for Allah’s sake, and their patience and their taqwā — their plotting will not harm them» — on «وَإِنْ تَصْبِرُوا وَتَتَّقُوا لَا يَضُرُّكُمْ كَيْدُهُمْ شَيْئًا». And of the enemies in general, a sentence to hold without bitterness: «كُلُّهُمْ يَسُرُّهُمْ مَا يَضُرُّنَا وَيَسُوؤُهُمْ مَا يَنْفَعُنَا.»

الرُّكُون Leaning On Them — Two Kinds

وَلَا تَرْكَنُوا إِلَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا فَتَمَسَّكُمُ النَّارُ

And do not incline toward those who have done wrong, lest the Fire touch you.

سُورَةُ هُود · 11:113

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«This makes clear to us the danger of inclining, and that its affair is grave.» And then, as everywhere in this clause, two kinds:

الرُّكُونُ الأَوَّلInclining to shirk and its people — «هَذَا رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام»; «وَإِذَا نَاصَرَهُمْ وَعَاوَنَهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِينَ فَهُوَ رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام»
الرُّكُونُ الثَّانِيInclining to the oppressors — and aiding the oppressor in his oppression, the thief in his theft, the sinner in his sin — «كُلُّهُ دَاخِلٌ فِي المَعْصِيَة». A sin, not apostasy.

«فَالوَاجِبُ الحَذَرُ مِنَ الرُّكُونِ لَا إِلَى هَؤُلَاءِ وَلَا إِلَى هَؤُلَاء» — and the duty is wariness of inclining to either party. Both are forbidden; the rulings on them are not the same.

وَاتْلُ عَلَيْهِمْ نَبَأَ الَّذِي آتَيْنَاهُ آيَاتِنَا فَانسَلَخَ مِنْهَا فَأَتْبَعَهُ الشَّيْطَانُ فَكَانَ مِنَ الْغَاوِينَ

And recite to them the account of the one to whom We gave Our signs, but he sloughed them off; so Satan followed after him, and he became of those gone astray.

سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف · 7:175

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«“فَأَتْبَعَهُ الشَّيْطَان” means: the devil overtook him and the devil destroyed him, until he supplicated against Mūsā and his people; and this is of apostasy from Islām — we seek refuge with Allah — because he supplicated against the people of truth that they be forsaken, and that the people of shirk and falsehood be given victory.»

And asked about the soundness of the story of Balʿam itself, he said: «مِنْ أَخْبَارِ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيل، لَكِنَّ القُرْآنَ كَافٍ» — “of the reports of the Children of Isrāʾīl; but the Qurʾān is sufficient.”

The same discipline as the Hārūt and Mārūt stories in the last lesson: the lesson is taken from the verse, and the narrative is left where it belongs.

And notice what the verse is being cited for, because it is the clause’s deepest point. The man in it is not a disbeliever who stayed one. He is a man who was given the signs and sloughed them off — and the act by which he did it was a supplication: that the people of truth be defeated and the people of shirk be given victory. مُظَاهَرَة with no weapon in it at all.

التَّوَلِّي مِنَ المَسَائِلِ الظَّاهِرَة Where Ignorance Is No Excuse

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — One who does something of the nullifiers of Islām while ignorant of them — what is the ruling on his act? And what is the standard of ignorance in this question?

Ibn Bāz — «Whoever is among the Muslims — no leniency is shown him in this; meaning: the one who is among the Muslims knows these nullifiers and that they are contrary to Islām. As for one who is among the mushrikīn, or in lands where the matter may be hidden from him — he is warned; and if he then persists upon that, he disbelieves; and if he repents, then praise be to Allah.»

And then the category this clause belongs to: «as for the manifest matters, they do not need clarification — as his associating partners with Allah, or his holding that the ruling of Zayd and ʿAmr is better than the ruling of Allah and His Messenger, or his reviling Allah and His Messenger, or the like of that, or his giving the disbelievers victory over the Muslims and aiding them against a Muslim — هَذِهِ أُمُورٌ ظَاهِرٌ كُفْرُهَا.»

«لَكِنْ مَا قَدْ يَخْفَى يُسْأَلُ أَهْلُ العِلْم؛ وَإِذَا نُبِّهَ عَلَى ذَلِكَ وَتَابَ تَابَ اللهُ عَلَيْه» — but what may be obscure: the people of knowledge are asked; and if he is alerted to it and repents, Allah accepts his repentance.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Taymiyyah’s footnote here sets the frame exactly, and it cuts both ways: «and if it is in the hidden statements, it may be said that he is mistaken, astray — the proof that makes its holder a disbeliever has not been established against him; but that occurs among groups of them in the manifest matters, which the elite and the common people of the Muslims know to be of the religion of the Muslims — rather the Jews and the Christians and the mushrikīn know that Muḥammad ﷺ was sent with them and declared a disbeliever whoever opposed them: such as his command to worship Allah alone with no partner, and his prohibition of worshipping anyone besides Allah… and such as enmity to the Jews and the Christians and the mushrikīn, and such as the prohibition of the obscenities and ribā and wine and gambling.»

So the excuse of ignorance is real and has a field — «the hidden statements» — and this clause, in its core shape, is not in that field.

The general siding-with — «هَذَا أَشَرُّهَا», the worst of them: giving them victory over the Muslims and backing and aiding them against the Muslims
Imitation in dress and custom
Love of their religion only

Asked whether «وَمَنْ يَتَوَلَّهُمْ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنَّهُ مِنْهُمْ» necessitates disbelief for anyone who allies with them, he answered:

«Yes, without distinction»
«عَلَى حَسَبِ التَّوْلِيَة» — according to the degree of the allying; «so if he gave them victory and backed them against the Muslims, he was like them»
«The verse is abrogated»
«Only for those who fight»

Which earlier nullifiers use the same move as «عَلَى حَسَبِ التَّوْلِيَة»?

None of them
The seventh («فِيهِ التَّفْصِيل») and the sixth («الفَرْدُ وَالفَرْدَانِ فِسْق»)
The first and the second
The fifth alone

Selling weapons to the disbelievers so they may fight the Muslims with them?

Forbidden, and not apostasy
Apostasy — «this means تَوَلٍّ لِلْكُفَّار»
Permitted in the treaty state
A matter of ijtihād

And selling the same weapons to highway robbers or rebels?

Also apostasy
Aiding upon acts of disobedience — «فَيَكُونُ مُحَرَّمًا وَمُنْكَرًا» — forbidden and an abomination, not apostasy
Permitted
Fisq only

So what does the ruling on the sale turn on?

The price
WHO the weapon is to be used against
The seller’s intention alone
Whether war has been declared

In the state of WAR, what may the enemy be aided with?

Food and medicine only
Nothing at all — «لَا سِلَاحٌ وَلَا مَالٌ وَلَا طَعَامٌ وَلَا غَيْرُ ذَلِك»; and such giving «becomes disbelief and apostasy from Islām»
Money but not weapons
Whatever the ruler permits

And in the state of treaty and reconciliation?

The same prohibition applies
There is no objection to their being given — the relative gives his relative, the Muslim gives the poor, the one in authority gives what he sees benefit in, and gifts may be exchanged — «within the bounds of the Muḥammadan Sharīʿah»
Only kinship is excepted
Only the ruler may give

Which two groups does he name specifically as permitted recipients in the treaty state?

Their scholars and their rulers
Their poor and their disabled — «كَفَقِيرِهِمْ وَزَمِنِهِم» — along with the ties of kinship
Their traders and their envoys
Their children only

Which verse covers kindness to those who did not fight us?

al-Māʾidah 5:51
al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8 — «that you should deal kindly and justly with them; indeed Allah loves the just»
al-Baqarah 2:256
al-Anfāl 8:60

Which case does he bring for it?

The treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah itself
Asmāʾ bint Abī Bakr — whose mother came to Madīnah during the truce seeking assistance, and the Prophet ﷺ gave the fatwā that she should maintain the tie with her
Ṣafwān ibn Umayyah
The Jews of Khaybar

What reason does he give that reframes the whole category of kindness in the treaty state?

That it buys peace
That it «may be a path to GUIDANCE» — and for this Allah legislated a share in the zakāh for المُؤَلَّفَةُ قُلُوبُهُمْ
That it is required by the treaty
That it prevents retaliation

Ṣafwān ibn Umayyah’s own sentence afterwards?

«I accepted Islām for fear of the sword»
«There was no household more hateful to me than the Messenger of Allah’s ﷺ; and he kept on giving me and giving me until he became the most beloved of people to me»
«I was given and I was not guided»
«I hoped for the wealth and found the religion lacking»

Some use 60:8 to permit LOVING the peaceful disbeliever. The footnote’s answer?

That it is correct
«وَهَذَا خَطَأ» — because kindness toward a disbeliever does not entail love of him: «فَالبِرُّ شَيْء، وَالمَحَبَّةُ شَيْءٌ آخَر»
That the verse is abrogated
That it applies to relatives only

What illustration does it give for birr without love?

A neighbour one dislikes
A person may be kind to an ANIMAL, or to one he hopes something from or fears — and love is not entailed by that
A debtor
A guest

How is «إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ» answered?

It is accepted as proof of love
It is read as «you loved his GUIDANCE, not his person» — the occasion of revelation shows it; and even granting the other reading, the verse is Makkan about Abū Ṭālib, and al-Mujādilah and al-Mumtaḥanah came later in Madīnah
It is said to be weak
It is restricted to prophets

Some claimed Ibrāhīm disowned his people only AFTER they hated and fought him. The refutation?

That the verse is Madinan
«وَهَذَا إِفْكٌ مُبِين» — from the verse’s own ending: he made the limit at which the enmity ceases their BELIEVING in Allah’s tawḥīd, «and he did not say: until you leave off hating me»
That the story is isrāʾīlī
That it concerns Lūṭ, not Ibrāhīm

What does that claim entail about Ibrāhīm, in the footnote’s words?

Nothing objectionable
A belittling of that noble prophet — as though he hated them in his own defence, and did not hate them for violating Allah’s sanctities and associating partners with Him. «سَاءَ مَا يَحْكُمُون»
That he was ignorant of tawḥīd
That he recanted

The argument from marrying a kitābiyyah is answered how?

By forbidding such marriage
Either it is a matter a TEXT has come with, so the ruling is confined to it; or the love of a disbelieving wife is a love of APPETITE, like love of a disbeliever’s food — not an essential or religious love
By saying the marriage is for procreation only
By restricting it to the dhimmah

And what binding consequence does the footnote press on them?

That they must forbid all marriage
That on their reasoning they would have to permit loving the WARRING disbelievers too — since Allah permitted us the food and women of the People of the Book who are at war as well
That they must permit loving idolaters
That they must reject the zakāh share

Name the three moves the footnote attributes to ahl al-bidaʿ.

Innovation, exaggeration and neglect
Objecting to the universals by the particulars; striking the certainties with the probabilities; and overturning the consensuses by what is of the fallen among the possibilities
Takfīr, tafsīq and tabdīʿ
Taqlīd, tajahhul and taʾwīl

What two things are obligatory in war AND peace together?

Jihād and hijrah
الإِعْدَاد — preparation — and الحَذَر — wariness: «فَإِنَّ الأَعْدَاءَ لَا يُؤْتَمَنُونَ أَبَدًا»
Daʿwah and patience
Treaty and tribute

And what does he say in the very next breath that the enmity does NOT prevent?

Trade with them
Calling them and being eager for their guidance — «so the callers and those who direct to good are sent to them; perhaps Allah will bring them out of the darknesses into the light»
Treaties with them
Learning their languages

Which verse does he give as the promise that makes the wariness bearable?

«وَأَعِدُّوا لَهُمْ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُم»
«وَإِنْ تَصْبِرُوا وَتَتَّقُوا لَا يَضُرُّكُمْ كَيْدُهُمْ شَيْئًا»
«خُذُوا حِذْرَكُم»
«إِنَّ اللهَ يُحِبُّ المُقْسِطِين»

الرُّكُون in Hūd 11:113 — the two kinds?

Public and private
Inclining to shirk and its people → ridda if he aids them against the Muslims; and inclining to the OPPRESSORS, aiding the oppressor, thief or sinner → «كُلُّهُ دَاخِلٌ فِي المَعْصِيَة»
Of the heart and of the limbs
In war and in peace

In al-Aʿrāf 7:175, by what act did the man slough off the signs?

By selling his knowledge
By a SUPPLICATION — he supplicated against Mūsā and his people, that the people of truth be forsaken and the people of shirk be given victory
By fighting alongside Pharaoh
By concealing the revelation

Why is that the clause’s deepest point?

Because it concerns a prophet
Because it is مُظَاهَرَة with no weapon in it at all — and by a man who had BEEN GIVEN the signs
Because it proves the ruling on magic
Because it names the man

What did Ibn Bāz say about the soundness of the story of Balʿam itself?

That it is authentic
«مِنْ أَخْبَارِ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيل، لَكِنَّ القُرْآنَ كَافٍ» — of the reports of the Children of Isrāʾīl, but the Qurʾān is sufficient
That it is fabricated
That it is mutawātir

When is ignorance of this nullifier not an excuse?

Never; ignorance always excuses
For one who is AMONG THE MUSLIMS — «no leniency is shown him in this», because such a one knows these nullifiers
Only for scholars
Only after a judge rules

And for one among the mushrikīn, or in lands where the matter may be hidden?

He is excused permanently
He is WARNED; and if he then persists he disbelieves — «and if he repents, then praise be to Allah»
He is ruled a disbeliever at once
He must emigrate

Which category does giving the disbelievers victory over the Muslims fall into?

The hidden statements, where the proof may not be established
The MANIFEST matters — «هَذِهِ أُمُورٌ ظَاهِرٌ كُفْرُهَا» — which do not need clarification
A place of ijtihād
A matter for the ruler alone

What does Ibn Taymiyyah’s footnote establish about the excuse of ignorance?

That it does not exist
That it is real and has a field — «the HIDDEN statements», where it may be said the proof that makes its holder a disbeliever has not been established — but that the manifest matters are not in that field
That it applies only to new Muslims
That only a judge may apply it
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
المُوَالَاةُ وَالتَّوَلِّي شَيْءٌ وَاحِدٌ عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. Muwālāh and tawallī are one and the same for Ibn Bāz.
التَّوَلِّي هُوَ نَصْرُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِينَ وَهُوَ رِدَّة. Tawallī is giving them victory over the Muslims, and it is apostasy.
إِهْدَاءُ شَيْءٍ لِلْكَافِرِ أَوِ البَشَاشَةُ فِي وَجْهِهِ رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام. Presenting a gift to a disbeliever, or cheerfulness in his face, is apostasy from Islām.
رَفْعُ عَلَمِ التَّوْحِيدِ بِجَانِبِ عَلَمِ الصَّلِيبِ تَوَلٍّ عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. Raising the banner of tawḥīd beside the banner of the cross is tawallī for Ibn Bāz.
لَا يَجُوزُ فِي حَالِ الحَرْبِ إِعَانَتُهُمْ بِطَعَامٍ وَلَا مَال. In the state of war it is not permissible to aid them with food or money.
البِرُّ بِالكَافِرِ المُسَالِمِ يَلْزَمُ مِنْهُ مَحَبَّتُه. Kindness to a peaceful disbeliever entails loving him.
البَغْضَاءُ بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَهُمْ تَمْنَعُ مِنْ دَعْوَتِهِمْ. The enmity between us and them prevents calling them to Islām.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

المُوَالَاةُ غَيْرُ التَّوَلِّي؛ المُوَالَاةُ مَعْصِيَة، وَالتَّوَلِّي كُفْر

Ibn Bāz. Six words, and the whole eighth nullifier turns on them. And he meant them: asked about a banner raised beside a cross he said «كَلَّا، هَذَا نَوْعُ تَشَبُّه»; asked about cheering a disbelieving sportsman, «لَيْسَ هَذَا مِنَ المُوَالَاةِ المُكَفِّرَة؛ لَكِنَّهُ نَوْعُ مُوَالَاة، مَعْصِيَة». The nullifier has one shape — «نَصْرُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين» — and he will not let anything else wear it. And keep beside it the reason he nevertheless warns hard against the lesser thing: «حَتَّى لَا تَقَعَ الكَارِثَة» — so that the disaster does not occur. The sin is the road; it is not the destination.
