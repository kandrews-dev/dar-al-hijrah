النَّاقِضُ السَّادِس · مَسَائِلُ الاسْتِهْزَاء

The Sixth Nullifier, Part 2 — The Cases

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State what Ibn Bāz says the content of mockery is, and what he answers to «but making people laugh is not denial».
  • Give his ruling where the mocker was joking or meant to needle someone — and the proof the footnote gives from Pharaoh.
  • Distinguish, in his own words, ruling a man a disbeliever from killing him.
  • Give the two tiers of the ruling on reviling the Ṣaḥābah, with his own examples of the lower tier, and the reason he gives for the upper.
  • State the ruling on accusing ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا, and why.
  • Apply التَّفْصِيل to mocking knowledge and its people: which case is ridda and which is not.
  • State the ruling on sitting with those who mock, the two things that discharge the duty, and which verse carries it.
  • Explain the حُقُوقُ الكَلِمَة — and how a man may say it and still be ruined, or say it and be merely weak.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٥٢–١٥٨
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 152–158, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Part 1 established the ruling; these seven folios apply it. And they are a lesson of their own for one reason: the answers are not uniform.

Two of the rulings below are quoted constantly. Both of them are two-tiered in the source, and both are usually quoted without their lower tier. Reviling the Ṣaḥābah is not one ruling — it is two. Mocking the scholars is not one ruling — Ibn Bāz says in so many words «the correct position is to make the distinction». And the single sentence that governs all of it is his: ruling a man a disbeliever and killing him are two different things.

كَلِمَةٌ بِلِسَانِهِ دُونَ قَلْبِه A Word on the Tongue, Not in the Heart

The first question is about a phrase of the Imām Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb’s in Kashf al-Shubuhāt: «like a word he utters with his tongue without his heart.» Ibn Bāz is asked what it means, and his examples are deliberately the worst ones.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«An example of a word he utters with his tongue without his heart: meaning — he laughs, he says: “Muḥammad is a liar”, or “So-and-so”… and he disbelieves, even if he did not believe it. Or he says: “Allah is not above the heaven” — even if he is playing. We ask Allah for soundness.»

Hold the structure of that. The man does not hold the belief. He is laughing. And the ruling lands anyway — which is exactly what part 1’s argument predicted: if mockery is contempt, then the absence of the corresponding belief does not remove the contempt. It is the uttering of it in jest that is the act.

مَضْمُونُهُ التَّكْذِيب Its Content Is Denial — Even in Jest

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the exchange that answers the one objection everybody raises:

Ibn Bāz — «As for mockery: this, its content is denial. The mocker is in all of it — because his mockery indicates a disease in his heart and a doubt in his heart.»

The questioner — “The one who mocks is not denying. Like making people laugh.”

Ibn Bāz — «وَلَوْ — even so. It indicates making light of the religion, and he has no faith with him to restrain him. We ask Allah for soundness.»

And in the same place: «حَتَّى وَلَوْ كَانَ مَازِحًا أَوْ يَقْصِدُ مُضَايَقَةَ شَخْص» — “even if he was joking, or meant to needle someone.”

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the footnote’s proof for that is the thing worth taking away. Asked how a man can be ruled a denier when he believes the religion he is mocking, the footnote answers with the Qurʾān’s own cases: Pharaoh, and the ṭawāghīt of the Arabs who mocked their prophet while inwardly believing him.

«They rejected it while their own selves were convinced of it, out of injustice and arrogance.» — al-Naml 27:14

وَجَحَدُوا بِهَا وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا أَنفُسُهُمْ ظُلْمًا وَعُلُوًّا

And they rejected them, while their own selves were convinced of them — out of injustice and arrogance.

سُورَةُ النَّمْل · 27:14

So inward assent is not a defence, because the Qurʾān has already described disbelievers who had it. That is a serious point and it cuts against a common sentiment — “he believes really, he just has a bad mouth.” The verse is about men who were convinced, and it calls what they did جُحُود.

التَّكْفِيرُ شَيْءٌ وَالقَتْلُ شَيْءٌ آخَر The Sentence That Governs the Whole Clause

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«Whoever mocks the religion is declared a disbeliever specifically; everyone from whom a nullifier is found is declared a disbeliever specifically. As for the killing — that is another matter. The killing requires a demand for repentance.»

Keep that sentence. It is the one that stops this clause being read as a licence. Part 1 relayed an ijmāʿ on the killing of one who reviles the Prophet ﷺ, and relayed Aḥmad’s view that he is not even asked to repent. Here Ibn Bāz, stating the clause in his own voice, separates the two: the ruling and the execution of a sentence. And the fourth nullifier already told you who carries out a sentence — not an individual, and not a group.

كُفْرٌ عَمَلِيٌّ أَكْبَر Where It Sits in the Kinds of Kufr

الشَّرْح · the explanation

One more statement of principle before the cases, and it is useful because it places the clause:

«There is a practical disbelief that takes one out of the religion — such as prostrating to other than Allah, and slaughtering for other than Allah… likewise if he reviled the religion, or reviled the Messenger, or mocked Allah or the Messenger: a greater practical disbelief, with all of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah.»

Note what he lists it beside — the two acts from the first and second nullifiers. This is the same category as prostrating to an idol and slaughtering for a grave: an act that nullifies, not merely a belief. Which is why the mocker’s inward assent does not save him: nobody argues that a man who prostrates to an idol is excused by believing in his heart that Allah is one.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he states the particular case of the prayer, and then the full list:

«Mockery of the prayer is an independent disbelief, by the consensus of the Muslims.»

«Whoever mocks the religion — mocks what the Messenger ﷺ brought: derides the prayer, or the two testimonies, or the zakāh, or the fasting, or the ḥajj, or the commands of Allah regarding letting the beard grow and trimming the moustache, or the commands of Allah regarding goodness to parents and maintaining ties of kinship, or the commands of Allah regarding jihād in His path, or other than that — this one is a disbeliever in Allah, even if he fasts the day and stands the night, and even if he testifies that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ every hour.»

That closing clause is the same note the matn struck in the fifth nullifier with «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ». The deeds are not in dispute. They are simply not what is being weighed.

سَبُّ الصَّحَابَة Reviling the Ṣaḥābah — Two Tiers, Never One

This is given six separate times over these folios, and every single time it comes in two tiers. It is the most quoted material in the clause and the most often truncated, so the table below is built from his wordings rather than summarised.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

the casehis rulinghis reason
Reviling them in general / all of themكُفْر — «disbelief with all the people of knowledge»; «disbelief and apostasy from Islām»; and he calls it «كُفْرٌ مُسْتَقِلّ», an independent disbelief«because its meaning is faulting them, and that they are not fit for knowledge to be carried from them»; «because they are the transmitters of the religion»; «because if he reviles them, its meaning is that they are not trustworthy, and that what they transmitted of Islām has no soundness»
Reviling one or two of themفِسْق — «this has a tafṣīl: the one and the two are fisq»his own named examples: «as they revile Muʿāwiyah, or some of them revile ʿAlī — فِسْق»; and elsewhere «like reviling Muʿāwiyah and ʿĀʾishah — this is fisq»
Abū Bakr and ʿUmar specifically«الأَقْرَبُ عِنْدِي كُفْرُه» — “what is nearest, in my view, is his disbelief”«because Allah was pleased with them»; and «because no one in whose heart is a mustard-seed of faith reviles al-Ṣiddīq and ʿUmar and hates them — in what we believe»
Accusing ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا of zināكُفْرٌ أَكْبَر — «whoever accused her of zinā has disbelieved»«because he is a denier of Allah — for Allah declared her innocent». And he says that if they hold her innocence to be unestablished and that she is suspect, it becomes greater disbelief «because it is takdhīb of Allah».
Denying their companionship, or saying they were not Muslims, or that they apostatised«the apparent ruling is their disbelief»«because they have made the Companions of the Messenger ﷺ disbelievers, and made the bearers of Islām disbelievers — and the meaning of this is the nullification of Islām and the nullification of the religion entirely. If its bearers were apostate disbelievers, what is left for us?… and at their head al-Ṣiddīq, and ʿUmar, and ʿUthmān, and Ṭalḥah, and al-Zubayr, and Saʿīd ibn Zayd, and Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ and their like — so who remains?»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The reason he gives is more important than the ruling. Notice that he never grounds the upper tier in the honour of the Ṣaḥābah as individuals. He grounds it in transmission: they are نَقَلَةُ الدِّين — the carriers of the religion. To impugn them as a body is to say the chain by which the religion reached us is unsound, which is why he calls it «the nullification of the religion entirely» and asks «so who remains?»

And that is precisely why the lower tier is a different ruling. A man who reviles Muʿāwiyah has not said the chain is broken. He has wronged a Muslim, gravely — فِسْقٌ وَظُلْم — and that is a different charge.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also runs the argument through the Prophet’s ﷺ own testimony: «the scholars are agreed that whoever faults them is a disbeliever… because its meaning is denying the Prophet — the Prophet says they are خَيْرُ النَّاس, and this one faults them.»

Ibn Bāz quotes those words of the Prophet ﷺ loosely here, inside the argument, and the sharḥ gives them at this place with no attribution and no grading — so this page reports them as his wording and supplies neither.

الاسْتِهْزَاءُ بِالعِلْمِ وَأَهْلِه «The Correct Position Is to Make the Distinction»

This one he answers with the word «التَّفْصِيل» in his own mouth, and it is the single most useful ruling on these folios, because the case is so common.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And the correct position is to make the distinction. If the mockery is at the Sharʿī knowledge, or at the scholars on account of it — then there is no doubt that that is apostasy from Islām, because it is a belittling of what Allah has magnified, and a making-light of it, and within that is contempt for it and denial of it.

But if the mockery at the scholars goes back to another matter — such as their clothes, or the eagerness of some of them for the dunyā, or their being accustomed to other than what people are accustomed to, of customs having no connection to the Sharīʿah, or what resembles that — then this and its like is not apostasy from Islām, because it does not go back to the religion; rather it goes back to other matters. And Allah knows best.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

what is mockedthe rulingthe test
Sharʿī knowledge itselfرِدَّةit is belittling what Allah has magnified
A scholar on account of his knowledgeرِدَّة — «no doubt»the knowledge is the real object; the man is the occasion
A scholar’s clothes, worldliness, or unusual habits with no Sharʿī connectionNOT ridda«because it does not go back to the religion» — and note he does not call it permissible either

The footnote gives the Lajnah’s worked example, and it is the whole distinction in two words. On a man who says «يَا ذَقَن» — “hey, beard” — «if the speaker intended by his saying it ridicule, then that is disbelief; and if he intended identification, then it is not disbelief.» Same syllables, two rulings, and the difference is what the words were aimed at.

مَحَلُّ نَظَر Where He Declines to Rule

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Placing the muṣḥaf behind the rows in the mosque — where people lean back against it.

Ibn Bāz — «This is a place of consideration, and what is nearest — and Allah knows best — is that there is no harm upon him; because he does not intend to degrade them or hold them in contempt, and his need is only to lean back; and the one who placed them there intends to bring them near the people so that they may obtain copies when they wish to read. But if they were placed in front, that would be more prudent.»

Read that against part 1. There, treading on a muṣḥaf is disbelief by consensus and the qualifier «degrading it» was said to be clarification rather than a condition. Here, leaning on one is «no harm». The two are consistent, and the footnote in part 1 told you how: the one exclusion it preserved was the unintending. This is that exclusion in practice, from his own mouth, on a real case in a real mosque.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — A man who praises Allah at the moment of committing something forbidden — smoking, drinking, fornication — as mockery. Does he disbelieve?

Ibn Bāz — «This needs reflection. If his intent is mockery of the religion, and that there is no difference between praising at sins and praising at blessings, and that the religion has no value — then this is greater disbelief; mockery of the religion is disbelief: «قُلْ أَبِاللهِ وَآيَاتِهِ وَرَسُولِهِ كُنْتُمْ تَسْتَهْزِئُون». But if he says: it is an affliction, and praise be to Allah upon the affliction, and I repent to Allah and turn back to Him — but it is an affliction, and I praise Allah at afflictions as I praise Him at blessings, and I ask Him to pardon me and to accept my repentance — that is not mockery; there is nothing in it.»

«هَذَا يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى تَأَمُّل» is the answer to notice. The same words — «al-ḥamdu lillāh» at the moment of a sin — are two completely different acts, and he will not rule on them until he knows which one happened. And the second case he describes is not merely excused; it is a man repenting mid-sin. Nothing on these folios is read off the surface of an utterance.

الجُلُوسُ مَعَ المُسْتَهْزِئِين Sitting With Them

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«If he sits and does not forbid them and stays silent, then its apparent import is agreement. Otherwise — it is obligatory upon whoever sits with those who speak falsehood and reject the truth and mock the truth that he not sit; rather he should get up and depart, if he is unable to forbid with the tongue. Otherwise, let him forbid and make clear the error of the one who spoke falsehood. But if he sits and stays silent, then its meaning is agreement, and there is no power and no strength except by Allah… the apparent sense of the Qurʾān is that there is no excuse — rather either he speaks or he gets up. And if he forbade and made the falsehood clear, he has discharged what was upon him.»

He says «the apparent sense of the Qurʾān» without naming the verse at this place. The verse that carries it is al-Nisāʾ 4:140 — and it is worth having in front of you, because the ending is severe.

وَقَدْ نَزَّلَ عَلَيْكُمْ فِي الْكِتَابِ أَنْ إِذَا سَمِعْتُمْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ يُكْفَرُ بِهَا وَيُسْتَهْزَأُ بِهَا فَلَا تَقْعُدُوا مَعَهُمْ حَتَّىٰ يَخُوضُوا فِي حَدِيثٍ غَيْرِهِ إِنَّكُمْ إِذًا مِّثْلُهُمْ

And it has already been revealed to you in the Book that when you hear the verses of Allah being disbelieved in and mocked, do not sit with them until they turn to another subject — for then you would be like them.

سُورَةُ النِّسَاء · 4:140

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two ways out, and he names both: speak, or leave. And he puts them in an order — the leaving is for the one who is unable to forbid with his tongue. What there is not, is a third option of staying and saying nothing, and that is what «ظَاهِرُ القُرْآنِ عَدَمُ العُذْر» means.

This is also the one ruling on the page that most people will actually need, and it asks nothing of anyone that is beyond him. Standing up is available to everybody.

حُقُوقُ الكَلِمَة The Rights of the Word — the Whole Book in a Paragraph

The clause closes on something larger than itself, and it is the right place to end the Nawāqiḍ’s middle section. The question underneath every one of these ten clauses is: a man said «lā ilāha illā Allāh» — what does that get him?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And he may revile his religion, and he may mock his religion, while he is saying “there is no god but Allah” — and this word will not benefit him, because he has not discharged its right. For of its right is that you worship Allah alone, and that you magnify His sanctities, and that you hold to His right, and that you reject what is worshipped besides Him. So if you said it while you are not holding to its right, then its presence is like its absence.»

«So the point is that this word has rights, and these rights must be discharged. Some rights, [failing them,] make their owner as though he never said it at all — he remains in his disbelief and misguidance. And some rights, [failing them,] diminish its meaning and weaken its meaning, but their owner does not become a disbeliever.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

the first casethe second case
what he didHe said it, then reviled Allah and His Messenger — «he has disbelieved by that»He said it but does not hold to its completing rights — «he did not discharge its right fully; rather he discharged it with deficiency»
what it is worth«Saying “lā ilāha illā Allāh” did not benefit him, nor his prayer, nor his fasting, nor his ḥajj, nor his zakāh… because he came with a nullifier from among the nullifiers of Islām»«So he is weak in faith, and deserving of punishment, and in danger of entering the Fire on the Day of Resurrection if he dies upon that»
the name for itنَاقِض — it breaksنَقْص — it diminishes

That distinction — نَاقِض against نَقْص — is the entire book, and it is why the book exists. Ten clauses that break, set apart from everything that merely damages. And the second column is nobody’s comfort: weak in faith, deserving punishment, in danger of the Fire. It is simply not the same ruling.

Six of the ten nullifiers are now done. The seventh is السِّحْر — and the matn names with it الصَّرْف and العَطْف — beginning at printed folio ١٥٩.

Ibn Bāz’s example of «a word uttered with the tongue without the heart»:

A slip in recitation
A man laughing and saying «Muḥammad is a liar» — «and he disbelieves, even if he did not believe it»; or «Allah is not above the heaven» — «even if he is playing»
A vow not intended
An oath taken in anger

Why does the ruling land although the man holds no such belief?

Because the words are blasphemous in form
Because mockery IS contempt — so the absence of the corresponding belief does not remove the contempt; the uttering of it in jest is the act
Because he may come to believe it later
Because the listeners may be misled

What does Ibn Bāz say the content of mockery is?

Ignorance
Denial — التَّكْذِيب
Anger
Weakness of faith

The questioner objected: «the one who mocks is not denying — like making people laugh.» The answer?

That the objection is correct in that case
«وَلَوْ» — even so; it indicates making light of the religion, and he has no faith to restrain him
That it depends on the audience
That it is fisq only

And where the mocker was joking, or meant to needle someone?

He is excused
«حَتَّى وَلَوْ كَانَ مَازِحًا أَوْ يَقْصِدُ مُضَايَقَةَ شَخْص» — the ruling stands
He owes expiation only
The matter is referred to a judge

What proof does the footnote give that inward assent is no defence?

The ḥadīth of intentions
Pharaoh and the ṭawāghīt of the Arabs, who mocked their prophet while inwardly believing him — «وَجَحَدُوا بِهَا وَاسْتَيْقَنَتْهَا أَنْفُسُهُمْ», al-Naml 27:14
The consensus of the Mālikīs
The verse of al-Nisāʾ 4:140

Ibn Bāz on the mocker and the killing:

Both follow automatically
He is declared a disbeliever specifically — «as for the killing, that is another matter; the killing requires a demand for repentance»
Neither may be stated
The killing is immediate without demand for repentance

Why does that sentence matter for the whole clause?

It softens the ruling
It separates the RULING from the EXECUTION of a sentence — and the fourth nullifier already settled who carries out a sentence
It transfers the ruling to the scholars
It restricts the clause to public mockery

Beside which two acts does he place mockery, as كُفْرٌ عَمَلِيٌّ أَكْبَر?

Abandoning prayer and abandoning zakāh
Prostrating to other than Allah, and slaughtering for other than Allah
Magic and soothsaying
Hypocrisy and doubt

What does that placement explain?

Why the ruling is disputed
Why inward assent does not save the mocker — nobody argues that one who prostrates to an idol is excused by believing in his heart that Allah is one
Why the killing is obligatory
Why the clause follows the fifth

«Mockery of the prayer is…»

a major sin
an independent disbelief, by the consensus of the Muslims
fisq
a matter of ijtihād

In his long list of what mocking makes a man a disbeliever for, how does he close it?

«unless he repents at once»
«even if he fasts the day and stands the night, and even if he testifies that there is no god but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah every hour»
«unless he is ignorant»
«and the scholars differ in this»

Reviling the Ṣaḥābah in general — the ruling?

Fisq
Disbelief and apostasy from Islām, «with all the people of knowledge» — he calls it an independent disbelief
A matter of ijtihād
Prohibited but not sinful

And reviling one or two of them?

The same ruling
فِسْق — «this has a tafṣīl: the one and the two are fisq» — and he names Muʿāwiyah, ʿAlī and ʿĀʾishah as the examples
Disbelief if repeated
No ruling is given

What reason does he give for the UPPER tier?

Their precedence in Islām
That they are نَقَلَةُ الدِّين — the transmitters; so reviling them as a body means they are not trustworthy and what they transmitted «has no soundness»
Their participation at Badr
The verse of al-Fatḥ

How does he press that point rhetorically?

By citing the consensus
«If its bearers were apostate disbelievers, what is left for us?… and at their head al-Ṣiddīq, ʿUmar, ʿUthmān, Ṭalḥah, al-Zubayr, Saʿīd ibn Zayd, Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ — so who remains?»
By quoting Ibn Taymiyyah
By listing the ten promised Paradise

On reviling Abū Bakr and ʿUmar specifically, what is his wording?

«He has disbelieved by consensus»
«الأَقْرَبُ عِنْدِي كُفْرُه» — what is nearest, in my view, is his disbelief — «because Allah was pleased with them»
«This is fisq»
«The matter requires research»

And he adds about such a person:

That he must be exiled
That «no one in whose heart is a mustard-seed of faith» reviles al-Ṣiddīq and ʿUmar and hates them — «in what we believe»
That he is to be asked to repent three times
That his testimony is rejected only

Accusing ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا of zinā:

Fisq, like reviling Muʿāwiyah
He has disbelieved — «because he is a denier of Allah, for Allah declared her innocent»; and holding her innocence unestablished is greater disbelief, «because it is takdhīb of Allah»
A major sin requiring the ḥadd of slander only
A matter of ijtihād

On mocking knowledge and its people, what does Ibn Bāz say the correct position is?

Takfīr in every case
التَّفْصِيل — making the distinction
Withholding judgement entirely
Fisq in every case

Mockery at Sharʿī knowledge, or at a scholar ON ACCOUNT OF it:

Fisq
Apostasy from Islām, «no doubt» — because it belittles what Allah has magnified, and within it is contempt and denial
Prohibited but not apostasy
A matter for the ruler

And mockery at a scholar’s clothes, his worldliness, or customs with no Sharʿī connection?

Also apostasy
NOT apostasy from Islām — «because it does not go back to the religion»; and note he does not thereby call it permissible
Permissible
Fisq only if public

The Lajnah’s worked example on «يَا ذَقَن»:

It is disbelief in every case
If the speaker intended RIDICULE, that is disbelief; if he intended IDENTIFICATION, it is not
It is permitted among friends
It requires the ḥadd of slander

On placing the muṣḥaf behind the rows in the mosque, Ibn Bāz said:

It is disbelief by consensus
It is a place of consideration, and «what is nearest… is that there is no harm upon him» — because he does not intend to degrade them; though placing them in front would be more prudent
It is forbidden outright
It is obligatory to remove them

How is that consistent with part 1, where treading on a muṣḥaf is disbelief by consensus?

It is not consistent
Part 1’s footnote preserved exactly one exclusion — the UNINTENDING — and this is that exclusion applied to a real case
The two concern different copies
The earlier ruling was retracted

A man praises Allah at the moment of a forbidden act, as mockery. What did Ibn Bāz answer?

That he disbelieves outright
«This needs reflection» — then he distinguishes: if he intends mockery of the religion and that it has no value, greater disbelief; if he means «it is an affliction, and I praise Allah at afflictions and repent to Him», «there is nothing in it»
That it is fisq in both cases
That the question may not be answered

What does that answer show about reading utterances?

That the surface wording decides
That nothing on these folios is read off the surface of an utterance — the same words can be two completely different acts
That intention is never relevant
That only scholars may speak

A man sits with people mocking the religion and stays silent. Ibn Bāz:

He is excused if he dislikes it inwardly
«Its apparent import is agreement» — it is obligatory that he not sit; rather he should get up and depart if unable to forbid with the tongue
He must report them to the authority
He may remain if he is a scholar

What are the two things that discharge the duty?

Dislike in the heart, or supplication
Forbidding and making the falsehood clear — or getting up and leaving
Leaving, or informing the ruler
Silence, or changing the subject

What does «ظَاهِرُ القُرْآنِ عَدَمُ العُذْر» rule out?

Forbidding with the tongue
A third option of staying and saying nothing
Leaving the gathering
Rebuking in private afterwards

Which verse carries that ruling?

al-Tawbah 9:65
al-Nisāʾ 4:140 — «do not sit with them until they turn to another subject; for then you would be like them». The sharḥ says «the apparent sense of the Qurʾān» without naming it at this place
al-Anʿām 6:68
al-Naml 27:14

What does Ibn Bāz say of a man who says «lā ilāha illā Allāh» while reviling or mocking his religion?

That the word intercedes for him
That the word «will not benefit him», because he has not discharged its right — «its presence is like its absence»
That he is weak in faith only
That he must be asked to renew it

What are the two kinds of failure in the rights of the kalimah?

Public and private
Some, failing them, make their owner «as though he never said it» — he remains in disbelief; others diminish and weaken its meaning, «but their owner does not become a disbeliever»
Those of the heart and those of the limbs
Those before Islām and those after

What does he say of the second kind of man?

That he is safe
That he is weak in faith, deserving of punishment, and in danger of entering the Fire on the Day of Resurrection if he dies upon that
That he is a hypocrite
That nothing is upon him

And what is that distinction, in the book’s own terms?

Kufr and īmān
نَاقِض against نَقْص — what breaks, against what merely diminishes
Major and minor shirk
Obligation and recommendation
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
مَنْ قَالَ كَلِمَةَ الكُفْرِ مَازِحًا فَلَا شَيْءَ عَلَيْهِ عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. For Ibn Bāz, one who utters a word of disbelief in jest has nothing against him.
التَّصْدِيقُ البَاطِنُ عُذْرٌ لِلْمُسْتَهْزِئ. Inward assent is an excuse for the mocker.
التَّكْفِيرُ وَالقَتْلُ شَيْءٌ وَاحِدٌ عِنْدَ ابْنِ بَاز. For Ibn Bāz, declaring a man a disbeliever and killing him are one thing.
سَبُّ الصَّحَابَةِ عُمُومًا كُفْرٌ لِأَنَّهُمْ نَقَلَةُ الدِّين. Reviling the Ṣaḥābah in general is disbelief because they are the transmitters of the religion.
سَبُّ الوَاحِدِ وَالاثْنَيْنِ مِنَ الصَّحَابَةِ كُفْرٌ أَكْبَر. Reviling one or two of the Ṣaḥābah is greater disbelief.
الاسْتِهْزَاءُ بِلِبَاسِ العَالِمِ رِدَّةٌ عَنِ الإِسْلَام. Mocking a scholar’s clothes is apostasy from Islām.
مَنْ جَلَسَ مَعَ المُسْتَهْزِئِينَ وَسَكَتَ فَظَاهِرُهُ المُوَافَقَة. Whoever sits with the mockers and stays silent — its apparent import is agreement.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَمَّا القَتْلُ فَشَيْءٌ آخَر، القَتْلُ يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى اسْتِتَابَة

Ibn Bāz, in the same breath as «يَكْفُرُ بِعَيْنِه». Of everything on these two lessons, this is the sentence to carry. It does not soften the ruling by one word — he has just said the mocker is declared a disbeliever specifically. It separates the ruling from the sentence, and the fourth nullifier already told you whose the sentence is. And beside it keep the two he will not flatten: «وَالصَّوَابُ: التَّفْصِيل» on mocking the scholars, and «الفَرْدُ وَالفَرْدَانِ فِسْق» on reviling the Ṣaḥābah. Three sentences, and together they are the difference between learning this clause and misusing it.
