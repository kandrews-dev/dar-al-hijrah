إِحَاطَةُ العِلْمِ وَالسَّمْعُ وَالبَصَرُ وَالمَشِيئَة

His Knowledge, His Hearing and Sight, His Will

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٣٩–٤٠
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 39–40.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • List the five مَفَاتِحُ الغَيْب and give the ḥadīth that names them.
  • State the ruling on one who claims to know any of them.
  • Explain al-Shawkānī’s remark on al-Shūrā 42:11 and what he says it demolishes.
  • Give the proofs for المَشِيئَةِ وَالإِرَادَة, and what a man should say when something pleases him.
٣ · إِحَاطَةُ عِلْمِهِ بِجَمِيعِ مَخْلُوقَاتِه Section 3 — His Knowledge Encompasses All He Created

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنْزِلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَعِنْدَهُ مَفَاتِحُ الْغَيْبِ لَا يَعْلَمُهَا إِلَّا هُوَ﴾.

يَعْلَمُ مَا يَلِجُ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا وَمَا يَنْزِلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا

"He knows what penetrates into the earth and what comes out of it, what descends from the heaven and what ascends into it."

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd 57:4 · سُورَةُ الحَدِيد

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān fills in each of the four, and the list is worth reading slowly because it turns an abstract claim into an inventory:

  • مَا يَلِجُ فِي الأَرْضِ — what enters the earth: rain, seeds, buried treasure, the dead, and what else.
  • وَمَا يَخْرُجُ مِنْهَا — what comes out of it: plants, minerals, and what else.
  • وَمَا يَنْزِلُ مِنَ السَّمَاءِ — what descends from the sky: rain, the angels, and what else.
  • وَمَا يَعْرُجُ فِيهَا — what ascends into it: angels, deeds, and what else.

Your buried dead and your ascending deeds are in the same sentence as the rain and the minerals. Nothing has been left out of the traffic that Allah is watching.

وَعِنْدَهُ مَفَاتِحُ الْغَيْبِ لَا يَعْلَمُهَا إِلَّا هُوَ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا فِي الْبَرِّ وَالْبَحْرِ وَمَا تَسْقُطُ مِنْ وَرَقَةٍ إِلَّا يَعْلَمُهَا وَلَا حَبَّةٍ فِي ظُلُمَاتِ الْأَرْضِ وَلَا رَطْبٍ وَلَا يَابِسٍ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ

"And with Him are the keys of the unseen; none knows them but He. He knows what is in the land and the sea. Not a leaf falls but He knows it, nor a grain in the darknesses of the earth, nor anything moist or dry, but it is in a clear record."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:59 · سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

مَفَاتِحُ الغَيْبِ are the storehouses of the unseen — or what gives access to knowing it. And al-Fawzān states the ruling plainly: whoever claims knowledge of any of them has disbelieved.

They are not left unspecified. The Prophet ﷺ named them — the ḥadīth of Ibn ʿUmar in the Ṣaḥīḥayn — and then recited the āyah that lists them:

مَفَاتِحُ الغَيْبِ خَمْسٌ لَا يَعْلَمُهُنَّ إِلَّا اللهُ

"The keys of the unseen are five; none knows them but Allah." — and then he ﷺ recited Luqmān 31:34.

عَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ عُمَرَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا · رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ · al-Bukhārī and Muslim

إِنَّ اللَّهَ عِنْدَهُ عِلْمُ السَّاعَةِ وَيُنَزِّلُ الْغَيْثَ وَيَعْلَمُ مَا فِي الْأَرْحَامِ وَمَا تَدْرِي نَفْسٌ مَاذَا تَكْسِبُ غَدًا وَمَا تَدْرِي نَفْسٌ بِأَيِّ أَرْضٍ تَمُوتُ

"Indeed with Allah is knowledge of the Hour; He sends down the rain; and He knows what is in the wombs. No soul knows what it will earn tomorrow, and no soul knows in what land it will die."

Sūrat Luqmān 31:34 · سُورَةُ لُقْمَان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

A point of Arabic al-Fawzān does not pass over: وَلَا رَطْبٍ وَلَا يَابِسٍ — “nor anything moist or dry” — takes in all existing things, and comes as عُمُومٌ بَعْدَ خُصُوصٍ, a generality after a specificity. The āyah names the leaf and the grain first, then sweeps everything in.

And كِتَابٍ مُبِينٍ is al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ. Which means the āyah does three things at once, as al-Fawzān notes: it affirms that none knows the unseen but Allah, that His knowledge encompasses everything, and — because of that closing clause — al-qadar and the writing in the Preserved Tablet. An attribute lesson that is quietly also a qadar lesson.

وَمَا تَحْمِلُ مِنْ أُنْثَىٰ وَلَا تَضَعُ إِلَّا بِعِلْمِهِ

"And no female conceives, nor gives birth, except with His knowledge."

Sūrat Fāṭir 35:11 · سُورَةُ فَاطِر

لِتَعْلَمُوا أَنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ وَأَنَّ اللَّهَ قَدْ أَحَاطَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عِلْمًا

"…so that you may know that Allah is able over all things, and that Allah has encompassed all things in knowledge."

Sūrat al-Ṭalāq 65:12 · سُورَةُ الطَّلَاق

إِنَّ اللَّهَ هُوَ الرَّزَّاقُ ذُو الْقُوَّةِ الْمَتِينُ

"Indeed Allah — He is the Provider, the Possessor of strength, the Firm."

Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt 51:58 · سُورَةُ الذَّارِيَات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three short glosses on that last āyah. الرَّزَّاق — there is no provider besides Him; He provides for His creation and sees to what sets them right, abundant and wide in provision — so do not worship anyone else. ذُو القُوَّةِ — possessor of complete strength, which no weakness touches. المَتِين — one who has reached the utmost of strength and ability, so no hardship, burden or weariness overtakes Him in His acts; and المَتَانَة means intensity and strength.

٤ · إِثْبَاتُ السَّمْعِ وَالبَصَر Section 4 — Hearing and Sight

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَأْمُرُكُمْ أَنْ تُؤَدُّوا الْأَمَانَاتِ إِلَىٰ أَهْلِهَا... إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Kathīr reads the first āyah in its context — it opens with Allah mentioning that He made for us mates from among ourselves, and mates among the cattle. So: there is nothing like the Creator of all these pairs. And He is السَّمِيع, who hears all voices, البَصِير, who sees everything, nothing hidden from Him in earth or heaven.

And then al-Fawzān quotes al-Shawkānī on this āyah, in a passage worth reading twice:

“Whoever understands this noble āyah as it deserves to be understood, and reflects on it as it deserves to be reflected on, will walk by it — amid all the disputing of those who dispute about the attributes — upon a clear white road. And his insight will increase if he considers the meaning of and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing: for this affirmation, coming after that negation of any likeness, contains the coolness of certainty, the healing of breasts, and the settling of hearts.”

“So value, O seeker of truth, the worth of this radiant proof and this strong argument. For with it you will shatter a great deal of innovation, break the heads of misguidance, and humble the noses of whole parties of theologians — especially if you join to it the saying of Allah: and they encompass Him not in knowledge.”

That is the same āyah you met in Lesson 5 as the دُسْتُور of this subject. Here you are told what to do with it.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ كَانَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا

"Indeed Allah is ever Hearing, Seeing."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:58 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

And note where that closing comes: at the end of a command about returning trusts to their owners and judging between people with justice. The attribute is not left abstract — it is attached to the handling of other people's property and rights. He is Hearing of what you say, Seeing of what you do.

٥ · إِثْبَاتُ المَشِيئَةِ وَالإِرَادَة Section 5 — Will and Intent

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَلَوْلَا إِذْ دَخَلْتَ جَنَّتَكَ قُلْتَ مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا اقْتَتَلُوا وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَحْكُمُ مَا يُرِيدُ﴾.

وَلَوْلَا إِذْ دَخَلْتَ جَنَّتَكَ قُلْتَ مَا شَاءَ اللَّهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللَّهِ

"And why, when you entered your garden, did you not say: what Allah wills — there is no power except with Allah?"

Sūrat al-Kahf 18:39 · سُورَةُ الكَهْف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The meaning, al-Fawzān says: if He wills He keeps it, and if He wills He destroys it — said in acknowledgement of one's own inability, and that the power belongs to Allah.

And he records a practice of the Salaf from it that is easy to carry: مَنْ أَعْجَبَهُ شَيْءٌ فَلْيَقُلْ: مَا شَاءَ اللهُ لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللهِ — whoever is pleased by something, let him say: “what Allah wills; there is no power except with Allah.” The man in the sūrah lost his garden for want of that one sentence.

وَلَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا اقْتَتَلُوا وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَفْعَلُ مَا يُرِيدُ

"And had Allah willed, they would not have fought one another — but Allah does what He intends."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:253 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Had He willed that they not fight, they would not have fought — because nothing comes to pass in His dominion except what He intends. al-Fawzān puts it in two phrases worth memorising: لَا رَادَّ لِحُكْمِهِ, there is none to repel His judgement; وَلَا مُبَدِّلَ لِقَضَائِهِ, there is none to alter His decree.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَحْكُمُ مَا يُرِيدُ

"Indeed Allah judges as He intends."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:1 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَا شَاءَ اللهُ، لَا قُوَّةَ إِلَّا بِاللهِ

The sentence the owner of the garden did not say. Say it when something of yours pleases you — a child, a house, a piece of work — and you have acknowledged in seven words both where it came from and how easily it could go.

دَارُ الهِجْرَة — المَدْرَسَةُ السَّلَفِيَّةُ التَّأْسِيسِيَّةُ لِلْعُلُومِ الشَّرْعِيَّة

al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah · sharḥ of Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
