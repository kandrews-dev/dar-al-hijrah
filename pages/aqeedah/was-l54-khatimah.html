خَاتِمَةُ الرِّسَالَة

The Conclusion of the Treatise

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٢٠٢–٢٠٣
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 202–203.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the المَزِيَّةُ العُظْمَى — the greatest distinction — that the author closes the book with.
  • Explain the logic by which the saved sect is identified, and why the identification does not depend on counting the sects.
  • Give the clause of the ḥadīth that settles WHICH group is meant.
  • Say what الأَبْدَال means and the two explanations al-Fawzān reports for the name.
  • Give the four things the Prophet ﷺ said about الطَّائِفَةُ المَنْصُورَة.
  • Say what the author asks for in his closing duʿāʾ, and why he asks for that and not for more.
خَاتِمَةُ الرِّسَالَة The Conclusion of the Treatise

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَطَرِيقَتُهُمْ هِيَ دِينُ الإِسْلَامِ الَّذِي بَعَثَ اللهُ بِهِ مُحَمَّدًا ﷺ. لَكِنْ لَمَّا أَخْبَرَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ أَنَّ أُمَّتَهُ سَتَفْتَرِقُ عَلَى ثَلَاثٍ وَسَبْعِينَ فِرْقَةً، كُلُّهَا فِي النَّارِ إِلَّا وَاحِدَةً وَهِيَ الجَمَاعَةُ — وَفِي حَدِيثٍ عَنْهُ أَنَّهُ قَالَ: «هُمْ مَنْ كَانَ عَلَى مِثْلِ مَا أَنَا عَلَيْهِ اليَوْمَ وَأَصْحَابِي» — صَارَ المُتَمَسِّكُونَ بِالإِسْلَامِ المَحْضِ الخَالِصِ عَنِ الشَّوْبِ هُمْ أَهْلَ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ. وَفِيهِمُ الصِّدِّيقُونَ وَالشُّهَدَاءُ وَالصَّالِحُونَ؛ وَمِنْهُمْ أَعْلَامُ الهُدَى وَمَصَابِيحُ الدُّجَى، أُولُو المَنَاقِبِ المَأْثُورَةِ وَالفَضَائِلِ المَذْكُورَةِ. وَفِيهِمُ الأَبْدَالُ، وَفِيهِمْ أَئِمَّةُ الدِّينِ الَّذِينَ أَجْمَعَ المُسْلِمُونَ عَلَى هِدَايَتِهِمْ؛ وَهُمُ الطَّائِفَةُ المَنْصُورَةُ الَّذِينَ قَالَ فِيهِمُ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ: «لَا تَزَالُ طَائِفَةٌ مِنْ أُمَّتِي عَلَى الحَقِّ مَنْصُورَةً، لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ مَنْ خَالَفَهُمْ وَلَا مَنْ خَذَلَهُمْ، حَتَّى تَقُومَ السَّاعَةُ». فَنَسْأَلُ اللهَ أَنْ يَجْعَلَنَا مِنْهُمْ، وَأَنْ لَا يُزِيغَ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَانَا.

المَزِيَّةُ العُظْمَى · طَرِيقَتُهُمْ هِيَ دِينُ الإِسْلَام The Greatest Distinction — Their Way IS the Religion of Islam

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ continues the clarification of the distinctions of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah, and he made clear their greatest distinction, which is: that طَرِيقَتَهُمْ دِينُ الإِسْلَام — that is: it is their madhhab and their road to Allah.

Read what that sentence refuses to be. It does not say that their way is the best of the ways, or the safest, or the nearest to Islam. It says their way is Islam — the religion Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ with, and not a school within it.

And that at the splitting which the Prophet ﷺ informed would occur in this Ummah, they held firm upon Islam — upon مَا كَانَ عَلَيْهِ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ وَأَصْحَابُهُ, that is: the pure Islam, unmixed with admixtures; and for that reason they won the title Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah.

Follow the logic of the «لَكِنْ», because the whole passage is one argument and the particle is its hinge:

١  Their way is simply the religion of Islam — so in principle it needs no party name at all.
٢  But the Prophet ﷺ informed that the Ummah would split, and that of the sects only one is saved.
٣  Once a splitting has happened, the ones still holding the unmixed thing need a name to be told apart by.
٤  So those holding the pure Islam came to be — صَارَ — Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah.

The verb صَارَ is the point. The name is something they became, imposed by the splitting rather than chosen as a banner. Which is exactly the observation Lesson 2 made from the other direction: the sects are named after their innovation, their leader or their deeds, and this group alone is named after the thing everybody claims.

الفِرْقَةُ النَّاجِيَةُ وَمَا يُعَيِّنُهَا The Saved Sect, and What Identifies It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The matn alludes to the ḥadīth of the splitting of the Ummah, كُلُّهَا فِي النَّارِ إِلَّا وَاحِدَةً, and then — and this is the clause that does the work — it brings the wording that identifies which one:

هُمْ مَنْ كَانَ عَلَى مِثْلِ مَا أَنَا عَلَيْهِ اليَوْمَ وَأَصْحَابِي

"They are whoever is upon the like of what I am upon today, and my Companions."

يَذْكُرُهُ المُصَنِّفُ بِقَوْلِهِ: وَفِي حَدِيثٍ عَنْهُ — وَالمُصَنِّفُ لَمْ يَسُقْ لَهُ تَخْرِيجًا هُنَا

تَنْبِيهٌ — on the attribution of this clause.

The author introduces it with «وَفِي حَدِيثٍ عَنْهُ» — and in a ḥadīth from him, and al-Fawzān passes over it without stating a grading. Per the rule this track adopted in Lesson 22, this page reports the wording as the author gives it and supplies no grading of its own. Nothing in the section rests on it alone: the identification it states is established independently by the whole of Part 6 — the obligation toward the Companions — and by the ḥadīth of the victorious group below, which is in al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

And notice how the identification works, because it is the most practical sentence in the closing passage. It is not «they are the group with the correct opinions», which every sect would claim. It is a historical test: whoever is upon the like of what I am upon today, and my Companions. The standard is a state of affairs that actually existed at a datable moment, in front of named men — so the question «which group?» becomes a question about evidence rather than about self-description. Which is why the two preceding Parts had to establish the standing of the Companions before this clause could mean anything.

And it is worth seeing what the identification does not require. It does not require that the seventy-three be enumerated. A great deal of effort has been spent over the centuries trying to list them, and the matn spends none: once the saved group is fixed by what it is upon, the count of the others is of no practical use to a man deciding where to stand.

وَفِيهِمْ · مَنْ تَضُمُّهُ هَذِهِ الطَّائِفَة And Among Them — Whom This Group Contains

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And there came to be among them:

الصِّدِّيقُونthose who go to the utmost in truthfulness and in affirming
الشُّهَدَاءthose slain in the path of Allah
الصَّالِحُونthe people of righteous deeds
أَعْلَامُ الهُدَىthat is: among Ahl al-Sunnah are the eminent scholars, characterized by every praiseworthy description in knowledge and in deed
الأَبْدَالand they are the awliyāʾ and the devoted worshippers. They were named so — it is said: because whenever one of them dies, he is replaced (أُبْدِلَ) by another; and in a narration from Aḥmad, that they are أَصْحَابُ الحَدِيث, the people of ḥadīth
أَئِمَّةُ الدِّينthat is: among Ahl al-Sunnah are the scholars who are taken as a pattern — such as the four imāms and others

Three of those names repay a second look. الصِّدِّيقُون was defined in Lesson 48, of Abū Bakr and of ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا — so the word arrives here already weighted. And the two explanations of الأَبْدَال are not rivals so much as two different claims about who carries a religion. On the first, the awliyāʾ are replaced — the religion is never left without men upholding it, which is the same promise the next ḥadīth makes. On the second, reported from Imām Aḥmad, they are the people of ḥadīth: the men who carry the transmission itself. Both readings say that the thing which persists is not an institution but a line of men — and that is as true of this matn as of anything, since it reaches us only because such men copied it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And وَهُمُ الطَّائِفَةُ المَنْصُورَة — that is: Ahl al-Sunnah are the group mentioned in the ḥadīth:

لَا تَزَالُ طَائِفَةٌ مِنْ أُمَّتِي عَلَى الحَقِّ مَنْصُورَةً، لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ مَنْ خَالَفَهُمْ وَلَا مَنْ خَذَلَهُمْ، حَتَّى تَقُومَ السَّاعَةُ

"A group of my Ummah will not cease to be upon the truth, given victory. Neither the one who opposes them nor the one who forsakes them will harm them, until the Hour comes."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Four things are said in that sentence, and each one answers a fear a student of this creed will actually have:

لَا تَزَالُ طَائِفَةthat the truth is never left without people upon it — so a man standing on it is never the last
عَلَى الحَقِّ مَنْصُورَةthat they are upon the truth and given victory — and the order matters: the victory is predicated of them because they are upon the truth
لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ مَنْ خَالَفَهُمْthe harm of open opposition is ruled out
وَلَا مَنْ خَذَلَهُمْand the harm of being forsaken — by those who should have stood with them and did not

That fourth clause is the kindest thing in the ḥadīth. Opposition from an enemy is expected; خِذْلَان — abandonment by one's own — is the harder blow, and it is named separately and separately ruled out. And the promise runs حَتَّى تَقُومَ السَّاعَة, which is to say that there is no era in which a man can claim the thing has died out and he is excused.

الدُّعَاءُ الَّذِي خَتَمَ بِهِ The Duʿāʾ He Closed With

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the Shaykh sealed his blessed treatise with supplication, and with the ṣalāh and salām upon the Prophet ﷺ.

فَنَسْأَلُ اللهَ أَنْ يَجْعَلَنَا مِنْهُمْ، وَأَنْ لَا يُزِيغَ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَانَا

"So we ask Allah to make us of them, and not to turn our hearts aside after He has guided us."

Two requests, and it is worth asking why these two and not others. The first is admission — «make us of them». After fifty-three lessons of establishing what this group holds, the author does not claim membership in it; he asks for it. And the second is not a request for more knowledge, or for victory, or for the defeat of the sects he has spent the book answering. It is a request to be allowed to keep what he already has.

And the wording of the second is taken from the Qurʾān. «وَأَنْ لَا يُزِيغَ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَانَا» is the duʿāʾ of those of understanding in Āl ʿImrān, and the whole burden of this treatise sits inside it: a man does not hold his creed by having once understood it. Guidance is held by being held onto, and the hearts that turn aside are hearts that had been guided — which is precisely the kind of thing a book that has catalogued seventy-two ways of going wrong ought to end by admitting.

رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِنْ لَدُنْكَ رَحْمَةً إِنَّكَ أَنْتَ الْوَهَّابُ

"Our Lord, do not turn our hearts aside after You have guided us, and grant us mercy from Yourself. Indeed You are the Bestower."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:8 · سُورَةُ آلِ عِمْرَان

وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العَالَمِينَ، وَصَلَّى اللهُ وَسَلَّمَ عَلَى نَبِيِّنَا مُحَمَّدٍ، وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ وَسَلَّمَ.

With that, al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah is complete — fifty-four lessons, the full matn in Ibn Taymiyyah’s own order, with Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān’s sharḥ taken page by page from its opening to its last line. Nothing of the matn has been passed over.

Who are الأَبْدَال, and what are the two explanations of the name?

The four imāms; named for replacing one another in leadership
The awliyāʾ and devoted worshippers — named because whenever one dies he is replaced by another; and in a narration from Aḥmad, they are the people of ḥadīth
The Companions who outlived the Prophet ﷺ
Those who exchanged this world for the next

What do both explanations of الأَبْدَال have in common?

Both make them a fixed number in every age
Both say that what persists is not an institution but a line of men — the religion is never left without those upholding or transmitting it
Both restrict them to the Ḥijāz
Both identify them with the four imāms

What four things does the ḥadīth say about the victorious group?

That they are few, poor, patient and rewarded
That a group never ceases to exist; that they are upon the truth and given victory; that opposition does not harm them; and that being forsaken does not harm them either — until the Hour
That they are in the Ḥaramayn, are scholars, are obeyed and are victorious
That they fight, migrate, teach and are martyred

Why is the clause «وَلَا مَنْ خَذَلَهُمْ» significant?

Because it refers to the hypocrites of Madīnah
Because abandonment by one’s own is a harder blow than an enemy’s opposition — so it is named and ruled out separately
Because it abrogates the duty of mutual support
Because it limits the promise to times of war

What does the author ask for in his closing duʿāʾ?

Victory over the sects he has answered
To be made OF them, and that his heart not be turned aside after being guided — admission, and the keeping of what he already has
Knowledge of what he has not yet learnt
Forgiveness for errors in the treatise

Where is the wording of the second request from?

The ḥadīth of the victorious group
The duʿāʾ of those of understanding in Āl ʿImrān 3:8 — «Our Lord, do not turn our hearts aside after You have guided us»
Sūrat al-Fātiḥah
The khuṭbah at Ghadīr Khumm

What does that closing request concede about creed?

That it may be differed over
That a man does not hold his creed by having once understood it — the hearts that turn aside are hearts that HAD been guided
That knowledge of it is not obligatory
That it cannot be fully known in this world
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
طَرِيقَةُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ مَذْهَبٌ مِنْ مَذَاهِبِ الإِسْلَامِ لَا الإِسْلَامُ نَفْسُهُ. The way of Ahl al-Sunnah is one school within Islam rather than Islam itself.
تَعْيِينُ الفِرْقَةِ النَّاجِيَةِ مَوْقُوفٌ عَلَى عَدِّ الثَّلَاثِ وَالسَّبْعِينَ. Identifying the saved sect depends on enumerating the seventy-three.
الأَبْدَالُ قِيلَ إِنَّهُمْ أَصْحَابُ الحَدِيثِ فِي رِوَايَةٍ عَنْ أَحْمَدَ. It is said, in a narration from Aḥmad, that the abdāl are the people of ḥadīth.
حَدِيثُ الطَّائِفَةِ المَنْصُورَةِ رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ. The ḥadīth of the victorious group is narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim.
خَتَمَ المُؤَلِّفُ رِسَالَتَهُ بِسُؤَالِ النَّصْرِ عَلَى المُخَالِفِينَ. The author sealed his treatise by asking for victory over his opponents.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَأَنْ لَا يُزِيغَ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَانَا

The last words of the matn, and the right ones to end on. After a treatise that has established the attributes of Allah, the Last Day, the Decree, the Companions and the manners of the saved group, the author asks neither for more knowledge nor for victory. He asks to be allowed to keep what he has. Because the hearts that turn aside are hearts that had been guided — which is the one danger a book like this cannot protect its reader from.
