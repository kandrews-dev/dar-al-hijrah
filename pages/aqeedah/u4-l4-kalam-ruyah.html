كَلَامُ اللَّهِ وَرُؤْيَتُه

Allah's Speech & the Believers Seeing Him

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Affirm Allah's Speech (kalām) — real, with letter and sound, when He wills.
  • Know that the Qur'an is the uncreated Speech of Allah.
  • Affirm that the believers will see Allah on the Day of Judgement.
  • Know why these two issues split Ahl al-Sunnah from the deviant sects.

These two issues — al-Kalām and al-Ruʾyah — drew the historic line between Ahl al-Sunnah and the Muʿtazila & Jahmiyyah. Imam Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal was imprisoned and beaten for refusing to say "the Qur'an is created." Holding the truth here, gently and with proof, is part of the inheritance of Ahl al-Sunnah.

القِسْمُ الأَوَّل: كَلَامُ اللَّهPart 1 — Allah's Speech

Allah speaks in reality, with letter and sound, when He wills, how He wills, in a manner befitting His majesty. He has not ceased speaking and will not cease — "the words of your Lord do not run out" (al-Kahf 18:109). His speech is one of His essential and action-related attributes both: the capacity to speak is essential; particular speech acts are action-related.

وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَىٰ تَكْلِيمًا

"And Allah spoke to Mūsā directly."

al-Nisāʾ 4:164 — the verbal noun taklīm removes any room for metaphor

قُل لَّوْ كَانَ الْبَحْرُ مِدَادًا لِّكَلِمَاتِ رَبِّي لَنَفِدَ الْبَحْرُ قَبْلَ أَن تَنفَدَ كَلِمَاتُ رَبِّي

"Say: If the sea were ink for the words of my Lord, the sea would run out before the words of my Lord ran out."

al-Kahf 18:109

The great point — القرآن كلام الله غير مخلوق

The Qur'an is the Speech of Allah — not created. Recited by tongues, written in copies (maṣāḥif), memorized in hearts, heard with ears — yet in its origin it is from Allah, spoke by Him in reality, and brought down by Jibrīl to Muḥammad ﷺ. It is not the speech of Jibrīl, nor of Muḥammad ﷺ — they conveyed it.

The proof: «وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِّنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّىٰ يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ» — "If any of the polytheists seeks your protection, grant it him so that he may hear the speech of Allah" (al-Tawbah 9:6). Allah called the Qur'an His own speech, and "speech" cannot be separated from its speaker.

The Muʿtazila said: the Qur'an is created. The Jahmiyyah went further and denied Allah's speech altogether. The Ashāʿirah said Allah's "real speech" is an inner meaning (kalām nafsī) without letter or sound. Ahl al-Sunnah hold to what the Salaf held: Allah speaks in reality, with letter and sound, when He wills — and the Qur'an is His Speech, uncreated.

القِسْمُ الثَّانِي: رُؤْيَةُ اللَّهPart 2 — Seeing Allah on the Day of Judgement

The believers will see their Lord on the Day of Judgement and in Paradise — with their actual eyes, in a way befitting His majesty, without comprehending Him. This is the greatest gift of Paradise — the gaze upon His Face.

وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَّاضِرَةٌ ۝ إِلَىٰ رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

"On that Day faces will be radiant — looking at their Lord."

al-Qiyāmah 75:22–23 — the verb naẓara ilā with the preposition ilā can only mean visual sight

إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ كَمَا تَرَوْنَ هَٰذَا الْقَمَرَ، لَا تُضَامُّونَ فِي رُؤْيَتِهِ

"You will certainly see your Lord just as you see this moon — you will not be crowded in seeing Him."

al-Bukhārī & Muslim — the comparison ("just as") is in the clarity of seeing, not in the One seen

The Prophet ﷺ did not say Allah resembles the moon — he likened the seeing to the seeing of the moon: clear, certain, without crowding or doubt. The Muʿtazila denied the ruʾyah outright (and reinterpreted "looking at her Lord" as "waiting for her Lord's reward" — which is impossible Arabic: naẓara ilā with ilā is sight, not waiting). Ahl al-Sunnah affirm it as the texts state, and call it the greatest enjoyment of Paradise.

The lines that defined the Sunnah — الخلاصة

On al-Kalām: Allah really speaks — and the Qur'an is His Speech, uncreated. Imam Aḥmad held to this under whip and prison, and the Sunnah was preserved through him. On al-Ruʾyah: the believers will see Allah in Paradise — and this is the greatest reward. Hold these two beliefs, and you have caught the heart of what al-Wāsiṭiyyah teaches.
