مَرَاتِبُ الصَّحَابَةِ · الخِلَافَةُ وَالتَّفْضِيل

The Ranks of the Companions — the Caliphate and the Ranking

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٧٠–١٧٦
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 170–176.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the three sources from which the Companions’ excellence is taken.
  • Say which “Fatḥ” is meant in al-Ḥadīd 57:10, and why the Muhājirūn are ranked first.
  • Give Ibn al-Qayyim’s resolution of «do as you wish, I have forgiven you».
  • Separate the question of الخِلَافَة from the question of التَّفْضِيل, and say which one a man is declared astray for.
فَضْلُ الصَّحَابَةِ وَتَفَاضُلُهُمْ وَمَرَاتِبُهُم The Excellence of the Companions, and Their Ranks

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَيَقْبَلُونَ مَا جَاءَ بِهِ الكِتَابُ وَالسُّنَّةُ وَالإِجْمَاعُ مِنْ فَضَائِلِهِمْ وَمَرَاتِبِهِمْ. وَيُفَضِّلُونَ مَنْ أَنْفَقَ مِنْ قَبْلِ الفَتْحِ — وَهُوَ صُلْحُ الحُدَيْبِيَةِ — وَقَاتَلَ، عَلَى مَنْ أَنْفَقَ مِنْ بَعْدُ وَقَاتَلَ. وَيُقَدِّمُونَ المُهَاجِرِينَ عَلَى الأَنْصَارِ. وَيُؤْمِنُونَ بِأَنَّ اللهَ قَالَ لِأَهْلِ بَدْرٍ — وَكَانُوا ثَلَاثَمِائَةٍ وَبِضْعَةَ عَشَرَ: «اعْمَلُوا مَا شِئْتُمْ فَقَدْ غَفَرْتُ لَكُمْ». وَبِأَنَّهُ لَا يَدْخُلُ النَّارَ أَحَدٌ بَايَعَ تَحْتَ الشَّجَرَةِ كَمَا أَخْبَرَ بِهِ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ، بَلْ لَقَدْ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ، وَكَانُوا أَكْثَرَ مِنْ أَلْفٍ وَأَرْبَعِمِائَةٍ. وَيَشْهَدُونَ بِالجَنَّةِ لِمَنْ شَهِدَ لَهُ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ، كَالعَشَرَةِ وَثَابِتِ بْنِ قَيْسِ بْنِ شَمَّاسٍ وَغَيْرِهِمْ مِنَ الصَّحَابَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الكِتَابُ وَالسُّنَّةُ وَالإِجْمَاع — and these three sources suffice as witness to their excellence. Note the third: الإِجْمَاع. This is not a case built on reports alone; the Ummah's agreement is itself part of the evidence.

Then they are not upon one degree in excellence — rather according to their precedence to Islam, and the jihād, and the hijrah, and according to what they undertook of deeds toward their Prophet and their religion.

That is the shape of this whole section, and it is worth noticing before the details. Honouring them all does not mean levelling them. Lesson 46 forbade disparaging any of them; this lesson ranks them — and both are the same creed.

مَنْ أَنْفَقَ مِنْ قَبْلِ الفَتْح Those Who Spent before the Fatḥ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Which fatḥ? al-Fawzān: it is the treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah, because Allah named it a fatḥ in His saying إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُبِينًا; and that is the well-known view.

إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُبِينًا

"Indeed We have granted you a clear victory."

Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:1 · سُورَةُ الفَتْح

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And الحُدَيْبِيَة is a well near Makkah, at which the pledge took place beneath a tree that was there, when the mushrikūn barred the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his Companions from entering Makkah, so they pledged to him upon death. And this pledge was named a fatḥ, for the good and the victory for the Muslims that came about because of it.

There is a lesson in the naming by itself. Al-Ḥudaybiyah looked, at the time, like a defeat — turned back from the Kaʿbah, terms that seemed one-sided. The Qurʾān calls it a clear victory.

And the proof for preferring these is His saying:

لَا يَسْتَوِي مِنْكُمْ مَنْ أَنْفَقَ مِنْ قَبْلِ الْفَتْحِ وَقَاتَلَ أُولَئِكَ أَعْظَمُ دَرَجَةً مِنَ الَّذِينَ أَنْفَقُوا مِنْ بَعْدُ وَقَاتَلُوا وَكُلًّا وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْحُسْنَى

"Not equal among you are those who spent before the victory and fought — they are greater in degree than those who spent afterwards and fought. Yet to each Allah has promised the best."

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd 57:10 · سُورَةُ الحَدِيد

Read the last clause before drawing conclusions: وَكُلًّا وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْحُسْنَى — and to each Allah has promised the best. The āyah ranks them and then, in the same breath, refuses to disparage the lower rank. That is the exact balance of this section: a difference in degree is not a difference in standing.

وَالسَّابِقُونَ الْأَوَّلُونَ مِنَ الْمُهَاجِرِينَ وَالْأَنْصَارِ وَالَّذِينَ اتَّبَعُوهُمْ بِإِحْسَانٍ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ

"And the first forerunners among the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār, and those who followed them in excellence — Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him."

Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:100 · سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة

تَقْدِيمُ المُهَاجِرِينَ عَلَى الأَنْصَار The Muhājirūn Ranked before the Anṣār

الشَّرْح · the explanation

المُهَاجِرُون — the plural of مُهَاجِر; and what is meant by them is those who migrated from Makkah to Madīnah. And الهِجْرَة in the language is leaving; and in the Sharīʿah: the moving from a land of shirk to a land of Islam.

الأَنْصَار — that is: those who supported the Messenger ﷺ; and they are الأَوْس and الخَزْرَج; the Prophet ﷺ named them by this name.

And the proof for the ranking is a point of method worth having, because it is an argument from word order: the proof for preferring the Muhājirūn over the Anṣār is that Allah put them first in the mention — in al-Tawbah 9:100, in 9:117, and in al-Ḥashr 59:8–9, where the Muhājirūn are named before the Anṣār each time.

And for what they undertook of leaving their lands and their wealth and their children, seeking the reward and supporting Allah and His Messenger — and their truthfulness in that.

أَهْلُ بَدْر The People of Badr

الشَّرْح · the explanation

بَدْر — a well-known village about four stages from Madīnah, at which occurred the battle by which Allah gave Islam its might; and the day of Badr was named يَوْمُ الفُرْقَان — the Day of the Criterion.

And ثَلَاثَمِائَةٍ وَبِضْعَةَ عَشَرَ — thus their number came in صَحِيحُ البُخَارِيّ. And the ḥadīth itself comes in the two Ṣaḥīḥs, in the story of حَاطِبِ بْنِ أَبِي بَلْتَعَةَ — the Companion of Badr who sent word to Quraysh, and whose pardon is the occasion of these words.

اعْمَلُوا مَا شِئْتُمْ فَقَدْ غَفَرْتُ لَكُمْ

"Do as you wish, for I have already forgiven you."

فِي الصَّحِيحَيْنِ — in the story of Ḥāṭib ibn Abī Baltaʿah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn al-Qayyim said in «الفَوَائِد»: its meaning has been difficult for many people — and al-Fawzān quotes his resolution in full, because it is the one that neither empties the promise nor licenses anything:

— What we think concerning it, and Allah knows best, is that this is an address to a people whom Allah knew would not leave their religion, but would die upon Islam; and that they may commit what others commit of sins — but He will not leave them persisting upon them; rather He grants them success to a sincere repentance, and seeking forgiveness, and good deeds which erase the trace of that.

— And their being singled out by this rather than others is because that was realised in them, and that they are forgiven.

— And that does not prevent the forgiveness having come about by causes arising from them; just as it does not require that they abandon the obligations in reliance upon the forgiveness. For had it been obtained without their continuing to carry out the commands, they would not have needed, after that, a prayer, nor a ḥajj, nor a zakāh, nor a jihād — and this is impossible.

The last step is the one that settles it, and it is an argument from fact rather than from theory. The people of Badr went on praying, fasting, giving and fighting for the rest of their lives. So whatever the words meant, they plainly did not mean what the licensing reading says they meant — the men who heard them did not understand them that way.

بَيْعَةُ الرِّضْوَان The Pledge of Riḍwān

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This speech concerns the people of بَيْعَةِ الرِّضْوَان, the pledge that took place at al-Ḥudaybiyah. And al-Fawzān says the Shaykh mentioned two distinctions for them:

الأُولَىnot one of them enters the Fire — its proof being what is in Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim from the ḥadīth of Jābir رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ: «لَا يَدْخُلُ النَّارَ أَحَدٌ بَايَعَ تَحْتَ الشَّجَرَةِ»
الثَّانِيَةthat Allah was pleased with them — and this is the explicit text of the Qurʾān

لَقَدْ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ يُبَايِعُونَكَ تَحْتَ الشَّجَرَةِ فَعَلِمَ مَا فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ فَأَنْزَلَ السَّكِينَةَ عَلَيْهِمْ

"Allah was certainly pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you beneath the tree. He knew what was in their hearts, so He sent down tranquillity upon them."

Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:18 · سُورَةُ الفَتْح

Notice the clause between the pleasure and its reward: فَعَلِمَ مَا فِي قُلُوبِهِمْ — He knew what was in their hearts. The pleasure is not announced over an outward act whose sincerity was unexamined. It is announced over men whose hearts had already been inspected. Which is why a text of this strength can be given about a particular group of named people without being a general rule about anyone who takes a pledge.

الشَّهَادَةُ بِالجَنَّة Testifying to Paradise

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: Ahl al-Sunnah testify to Paradise for whomever the Messenger ﷺ testified to it. As for one whom the Messenger ﷺ did not testify to, they do not testify for him — because in that is speaking about Allah without knowledge; but they hope for the doers of good and fear for the doers of evil. And this is a foundation among the foundations of the creed.

That is a restraint worth dwelling on, because the natural impulse runs the other way. It is not piety to pronounce a righteous man into Paradise. The ruling belongs to Allah, and announcing it on His behalf is the fault — however good the person and however warm the intention. Hope and fear are what the believer is left with, which is exactly the position Lesson 36 described at the grave.

العَشَرَة — al-Fawzān names them: أَبُو بَكْرٍ · عُمَرُ · عُثْمَانُ · عَلِيٌّ · عَبْدُ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنُ عَوْفٍ · الزُّبَيْرُ بْنُ العَوَّامِ · سَعْدُ بْنُ أَبِي وَقَّاصٍ · سَعِيدُ بْنُ زَيْدٍ · أَبُو عُبَيْدَةَ بْنُ الجَرَّاحِ · طَلْحَةُ بْنُ عُبَيْدِ اللهِ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ — and the aḥādīth are authentic in testifying to Paradise for these.

وَثَابِتُ بْنُ قَيْسِ بْنِ شَمَّاسٍ — he was the orator of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and his glad tidings of Paradise is established in صَحِيحُ البُخَارِيّ. وَغَيْرُهُمْ مِنَ الصَّحَابَةِ — such as عُكَّاشَةُ بْنُ مِحْصَنٍ and عَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ سَلَامٍ and others.

مَسْأَلَتَانِ · الخِلَافَةُ وَالتَّفْضِيل Two Separate Questions — the Caliphate and the Ranking

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَيُقِرُّونَ بِمَا تَوَاتَرَ بِهِ النَّقْلُ عَنْ أَمِيرِ المُؤْمِنِينَ عَلِيِّ بْنِ أَبِي طَالِبٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ وَغَيْرِهِ: أَنَّ خَيْرَ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ بَعْدَ نَبِيِّهَا أَبُو بَكْرٍ ثُمَّ عُمَرُ، وَيُثَلِّثُونَ بِعُثْمَانَ وَيُرَبِّعُونَ بِعَلِيٍّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ… وَإِنْ كَانَتْ هَذِهِ المَسْأَلَةُ — مَسْأَلَةُ عُثْمَانَ وَعَلِيٍّ — لَيْسَتْ مِنَ الأُصُولِ الَّتِي يُضَلَّلُ المُخَالِفُ فِيهَا عِنْدَ جُمْهُورِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ، لَكِنِ الَّتِي يُضَلَّلُ فِيهَا مَسْأَلَةُ الخِلَافَةِ. وَمَنْ طَعَنَ فِي خِلَافَةِ أَحَدٍ مِنْ هَؤُلَاءِ فَهُوَ أَضَلُّ مِنْ حِمَارِ أَهْلِهِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

مَا تَوَاتَرَ بِهِ النَّقْلُ — that is, what is established by the route of التَّوَاتُر — and al-tawātur is the strongest of the chains. The student met its definition in Lesson 31: what a group transmitted whose collusion upon a lie is ruled out, at every link.

And note whose transmission it is. The report that Abū Bakr and then ʿUmar are the best of this Ummah is mutawātir from ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib himself — and in this mutawātir narration from ʿAlī is a refutation of the Rāfiḍah, who prefer ʿAlī over Abū Bakr and ʿUmar and advance him over them in the caliphate, and so impugn the caliphate of the two Shaykhs.

The man they claim to be defending is the man who narrated against them.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And this discussion comprises two questions. Keeping them apart is the whole point of the section:

مَسْأَلَةُ الخِلَافَةAhl al-Sunnah — including the Ṣaḥābah — reached CONSENSUS that the caliph after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is Abū Bakr, then ʿUmar, then ʿUthmān, then ʿAlī
مَسْأَلَةُ التَّفْضِيلthey reached consensus that the best after the Prophet ﷺ is Abū Bakr then ʿUmar — and DIFFERED over ʿUthmān and ʿAlī, which of them is more excellent

The Shaykh mentioned three statements on it: advancing ʿUthmān · advancing ʿAlī · withholding from advancing either over the other.

And the Shaykh indicated a preference for the first — advancing ʿUthmān — for three reasons:

١ — what the narrations concerning the virtues of ʿUthmān indicate.

٢ — the consensus of the Companions on advancing ʿUthmān in the pledge — and that was only because he was more excellent; so their ordering in excellence is like their ordering in the caliphate.

٣ — that the affair of Ahl al-Sunnah settled upon advancing ʿUthmān then ʿAlī.

And two statements from the Companions themselves. عَبْدُ الرَّحْمَنِ بْنُ عَوْفٍ said to ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ: I looked into the affair of the people, and I did not find them holding anyone equal to ʿUthmān. And أَبُو أَيُّوبَ: whoever does not advance ʿUthmān over ʿAlī has belittled the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār.

And ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ was among those who pledged to him, and used to carry out the prescribed punishments before him. Which is a quiet and complete answer to anyone claiming ʿAlī's cause against ʿUthmān: ʿAlī served in his administration.

أَيُّ المَسْأَلَتَيْنِ يُضَلَّلُ فِيهَا Which of the Two a Man Is Declared Astray For

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ set out a weighing between the two questions — the question of advancing ʿAlī over ʿUthmān in excellence, and the question of advancing ʿAlī over the others in the caliphate — in terms of the gravity that follows from each.

preferring ʿAlī over ʿUthmān in excellencehe is NOT declared astray — given the existence of the difference over it among Ahl al-Sunnah, even though the preponderant view is ʿUthmān
preferring ʿAlī in the caliphate, or over Abū Bakr and ʿUmar in excellencehe IS declared astray

And al-Fawzān gives the ground on which each caliphate rests, which is why the second row is settled: Abū Bakr — for his excellence, his precedence, the Prophet's ﷺ advancing him over all the Companions, and the Companions' consensus on pledging to him. Then ʿUmar — for his excellence, his precedence, Abū Bakr's covenant to him, and the Ummah's agreement upon him. Then ʿUthmān — for the people of the shūrā advancing him and the Ummah's agreement. Then ʿAlī — for his excellence and the consensus of the people of his age upon him.

And these are the four caliphs pointed to in the ḥadīth of العِرْبَاضِ بْنِ سَارِيَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, in his ﷺ saying: «عَلَيْكُمْ بِسُنَّتِي وَسُنَّةِ الخُلَفَاءِ الرَّاشِدِينَ المَهْدِيِّينَ مِنْ بَعْدِي» — hold to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly-guided, rightly-directed caliphs after me.

And this is why the Shaykh said: وَمَنْ طَعَنَ فِي خِلَافَةِ أَحَدٍ مِنْ هَؤُلَاءِ فَهُوَ أَضَلُّ مِنْ حِمَارِ أَهْلِهِ — because of his opposing the text and the consensus, with no proof and no evidence; and that is like the Rāfiḍah, who claim that the caliphate after the Prophet ﷺ belongs to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib.

The distinction this section draws is one of the most practically useful in the whole book, and it is worth stating plainly: Ahl al-Sunnah differ among themselves on one of these questions and do not declare each other astray for it; and they do not differ at all on the other. A settled matter and an open matter sit side by side here, and the author is careful to tell you which is which — which is itself a lesson in how to hold a position you believe is preponderant without making it a test of orthodoxy.

وَكُلًّا وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الْحُسْنَى

The clause that closes al-Ḥadīd 57:10, immediately after ranking one group above another. Learn the ranking and this clause together, or you will have half the verse: a difference in degree is not a difference in standing.
