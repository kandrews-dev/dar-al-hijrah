نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام · الدَّرْسُ الثَّامِنYear 6

النَّاقِضُ الخَامِس · بُغْضُ شَيْءٍ مِمَّا جَاءَ بِهِ ﷺ

The Fifth Nullifier — Hating Something of What He Brought

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Quote the fifth nullifier and say what the four words «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ» do to it.
  • Name the two kinds of بُغْض, give two examples of each, and state the ruling on each.
  • Give Ibn Bāz’s own application of the matn’s condition — the one about prayer and ḥajj.
  • Distinguish كَرَاهَةٌ طَبِيعِيَّة from إِكْرَاه, and define the second.
  • State the ruling on reviling the Ṣaḥābah in general and the ruling on rancour toward some of them — and why they differ.
  • Refute the claim that only hating all of the religion is disbelief, from the wording of the verse and by reductio.
  • Explain the Ṣaḥābah at al-Ḥudaybiyah without attributing to them hatred of anything the Prophet ﷺ brought.
  • State the rule «المَعَاصِي بَرِيدُ الكُفْر» and the two-sided warning the compiler closes with.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٣٦–١٤٥
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 136–145, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الخَامِس: مَنْ أَبْغَضَ شَيْئًا مِمَّا جَاءَ بِهِ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِه — كَفَرَ إِجْمَاعًا؛ وَالدَّلِيلُ قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿ذَلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَرِهُوا مَا أَنْزَلَ اللهُ فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ﴾.

Some copies of the matn read «مِنْ دِينِ الله» in place of «مِمَّا جَاءَ بِهِ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ» — the compiler notes the variant.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The whole clause turns on four words, and they are easy to read past: «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ» — “even if he acts upon it.”

A man prays and hates that prayer was legislated. A man makes ḥajj and hates that ḥajj was imposed. The deed is there and the nullifier is still there, which tells you immediately that what is being judged is not the limbs. And it tells you something else, which the footnote makes explicit: if the بُغْض that nullifies were dislike of the act itself, these four words would be a contradiction. So it is not. It is بُغْضُ التَّشْرِيع — dislike of the legislating.

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَرِهُوا مَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

That is because they hated what Allah sent down, so He rendered their deeds worthless.

سُورَةُ مُحَمَّد · 47:9

This is the same verse that closed the fourth nullifier, and it is carrying a different load here. There it answered the man who is not pleased with the ruling of the Messenger ﷺ. Here it is the daleel for the man who hates a thing of what he brought. And in both places the operative word is the heart’s, not the hand’s.

نَوْعَانِ مِنَ البُغْض Two Kinds, and Only One Is Disbelief

This is the distinction to carry out of the lesson, and it is the compiler’s footnote on «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ». Get it wrong in either direction and you have ruined the clause — collapse the two and you make a woman’s grief at her co-wife into apostasy; erase the first and you empty the nullifier out entirely.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

النَّوْعُ الأَوَّل · بُغْضٌ مُكَفِّرالنَّوْعُ الثَّانِي · بُغْضٌ طَبِيعِيّ
what is hatedThe legislating, or something of it — «so he hates that Allah legislated marriage, or hates that Allah sent Muḥammad ﷺ»The act, for its hardship or pain — while being pleased with the Sharīʿah of it
examplesHating that prayer, or zakāh, or tawḥīd, or fasting was legislatedA woman whose husband marries a second wife — «hating the particular decree, pleased with the Sharīʿah»; one who dislikes wuḍūʾ with cold water; one who dislikes fighting for its hardship on him
the rulingكُفْر — and Ibn Taymiyyah relays the ijmāʿ that whoever hates what the Messenger ﷺ brought is a disbeliever«فَهَذَا لَيْسَ مِنَ الكُفْرِ فِي شَيْء» — “this is not of disbelief in anything at all”

The verses given for the first kind are worth reading together, because each one names a different symptom of the same thing — and all three are describing hearts, not deeds.

بَلْ جَاءَهُم بِالْحَقِّ وَأَكْثَرُهُمْ لِلْحَقِّ كَارِهُونَ

Rather, he brought them the truth, and most of them are averse to the truth.

سُورَةُ المُؤْمِنُون · 23:70

وَمَن يُرِدْ أَن يُضِلَّهُ يَجْعَلْ صَدْرَهُ ضَيِّقًا حَرَجًا كَأَنَّمَا يَصَّعَّدُ فِي السَّمَاءِ

And whomever He wills to leave astray, He makes his breast tight and constricted, as though he were climbing into the sky.

سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام · 6:125

وَإِذَا ذُكِرَ اللَّهُ وَحْدَهُ اشْمَأَزَّتْ قُلُوبُ الَّذِينَ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِالْآخِرَةِ

And when Allah alone is mentioned, the hearts of those who do not believe in the Hereafter shrink with aversion.

سُورَةُ الزُّمَر · 39:45

And the verses given for the second kind are the ones that settle the matter beyond argument, because Allah describes the believers themselves this way.

كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْقِتَالُ وَهُوَ كُرْهٌ لَّكُمْ

Fighting has been enjoined upon you, while it is hateful to you.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:216

يُجَادِلُونَكَ فِي الْحَقِّ بَعْدَمَا تَبَيَّنَ كَأَنَّمَا يُسَاقُونَ إِلَى الْمَوْتِ وَهُمْ يَنظُرُونَ

They dispute with you concerning the truth after it had become clear, as though they were being driven to death while they looked on.

سُورَةُ الأَنْفَال · 8:6

The sharḥ pairs that with al-Anfāl 8:5 — «and indeed a party of the believers were averse» — and with al-Tawbah 9:38: «what is the matter with you that when you are told, “Go forth in the path of Allah,” you cling heavily to the earth?» These are believers. So a heaviness in the soul toward a hard command is not the nullifier; and anyone who tells you otherwise is reading past three verses and a ḥadīth of Muslim.

وَإِسْبَاغُ الوُضُوءِ عَلَى المَكَارِه

“…and performing wuḍūʾ thoroughly despite discomfort.”

صَحِيحُ مُسْلِم · عَنْ أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read that ḥadīth as a definition. The Prophet ﷺ names doing the act while it is disliked as a thing to be praised and counted among the means of raised ranks. It is not merely excused — it is rewarded.

The sharḥ also quotes «حُفَّتِ الجَنَّةُ بِالمَكَارِه» — paradise is encircled with things disliked — attributed to the Prophet ﷺ. No grading is given for it at this place, so none is supplied here.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ibn Taymiyyah is quoted on how the two kinds relate, in a sentence that is really about the mechanics of the heart:

«The one who is content — what carries him to bear the disliked things and the pains is what he finds of the pleasure of contentment and its sweetness. So when that sweetness and pleasure are lost, it becomes impossible for him to bear pain and bitterness. So how can he be imagined to be content when he has with him none of the sweetness of contentment by which he might carry the bitterness of what is disliked?» — al-Istiqāmah

Which is to say: the second kind is survivable because the first is absent. The co-wife bears her grief on the contentment underneath it. Remove the contentment and there is nothing left to bear it with. That is the clinical account of how type 2 turns into type 1 if it is fed instead of fought.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Imām of the matn himself, in his tafsīr of «وَاصْبِرْ نَفْسَكَ مَعَ الَّذِينَ يَدْعُونَ رَبَّهُم», draws exactly the conclusion a worried person needs: «in it is: that the believer is not harmed by his own soul’s dislike of that, when he struggles against it.»

So the obligation in the second kind is مُجَاهَدَة, not panic. And one sentence of Ibn Bāz’s later on these folios is the whole method: «if he trains his soul and struggles against it, these deeds become a bliss he finds in his heart, and his soul becomes a mount for him in this — it complies with him, because he has trained it and struggled against it.»

وَلَوْ صَلَّى، وَلَوْ حَجّ Ibn Bāz Applies the Matn’s Condition

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He states the clause first in general:

«And likewise whoever hates the law of Allah, or something of the law of Allah — so whoever hates something of the law of Allah: hates the prayer, or the zakāh, or the tawḥīd, or the fasting, and is averse to that — he becomes a disbeliever… So what is obligatory upon the believer is that he love what Allah has legislated, and that he ally upon that; and that he be wary of what Allah has forbidden, and ally upon that; and that he submit to the law of Allah wherever it is.»

Note «أَوْ شَيْئًا» — “or something of it.” That is the matn’s word, and it is the hinge of the long refutation further down these folios.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then he is asked the exact question the matn’s four words anticipate: what if he dislikes the thing but does not deny it — he dislikes, say, the prohibition of drinking wine?

«Whoever hates what Allah has legislated, his deed is nullified… Whoever hates the prayer — even if he prays — or hates the ḥajj — even if he makes ḥajj — or hates the prohibition of fornication, or of wine: he disbelieves, likewise.»

That is the matn’s «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ», in his own voice, with the examples filled in. And it is also the answer to anyone who imagines this nullifier is about weak feelings: the man in the example is praying. What is being judged is what he holds about the Legislator who imposed it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And one more distinction, from a different question — this one between natural dislike and compulsion, which are constantly confused because the Arabic shares a root:

On the pledge «بَايَعْنَا رَسُولَ اللهِ ﷺ عَلَى السَّمْعِ وَالطَّاعَةِ فِي المَنْشَطِ وَالمَكْرَه»: «its meaning is that they hear and obey even if this thing is something they dislike and it is hard upon them, because of their poverty or for other reasons — whenever the Messenger commands, they obey him, even if it is something that may harm them… So it is from the standpoint of natural dislike, from the standpoint of natural hardship — not from the standpoint of compulsion. Compulsion is his being forced to this thing, or threatened over a thing so that he does it, and it is not legislated — that is compulsion.»

The sharḥ quotes that ḥadīth here without a grading, so none is supplied. And keep the three apart: بُغْضُ التَّشْرِيع is hating the legislating — the nullifier. كَرَاهَةٌ طَبِيعِيَّة is finding the act hard — rewarded. إِكْرَاه is being forced to an unlegislated act — a different chapter entirely, with its own rulings.

مَسَائِل Eight Questions, and the Spread Across Them

What follows on these folios is a run of particular questions answered in Ibn Bāz’s own words. Read them as a set. The point is not any single ruling — it is that the answers are not all the same, and the ones that are not takfīr are as instructive as the ones that are.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three of those eight are not takfīr, and one of them is explicitly the opposite. A man who is wearied by a long recitation is told he is ignorant or weak in faith — not that he has left the religion. A man who hates a Companion is told to repent, not that he has apostatised. Rancour toward some of the Ṣaḥābah is named fisq and misguidance, and then the door is explicitly closed: «but it is not greater disbelief.»

Whoever quotes the beard answer and the polygyny answer from these folios while leaving out the other three has not quoted the folios. He has quoted the half he wanted.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The footnote on the beard answer is worth having, because «the least of its states» sounds like a hedge and is not one: «his saying “the least of its states” is not intended to mean that it is [merely] a sunnah, or that there is a disagreement in it; rather he means: even if the non-obligation of letting it grow were supposed, there is no doubt that letting it grow is a prophetic sunnah — so whoever hates the legislation of Allah in it has disbelieved, because it is of what Allah sent down. So a disagreement, were it supposed to exist, does not affect the ruling of disbelief on the one who hates it.»

That is a clean piece of reasoning and it generalises: the nullifier attaches to hating that Allah legislated a thing — so the fiqh rank of the thing is beside the point. Obligatory or recommended, the object of the hatred is the same.

هَلْ يُشْتَرَطُ بُغْضُ الدِّينِ كُلِّه؟ The Long Refutation — “Only If He Hates All of It”

Then the compiler takes up a position from a printed book, تَسْهِيلُ العَقِيدَةِ الإِسْلَامِيَّة, which held that hating what the Messenger ﷺ brought is only disbelief if he hates the whole religion of Allah; whoever hates part of it does not disbelieve. The argument offered was from this very verse: «Allah mentioned in it the nullifying of the deeds of one who hated all of what Allah sent down, because «مَا» is general.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The compiler’s reply is the best sustained reasoning in the book so far, and it is six answers to six claims. He opens flatly: «وَهَذَا خَطَأ» — and then works.

The objection — «مَا» in the verse means all of what Allah sent down.

The answer — Allah said «they hated what Allah sent down» — He did not say “all of what Allah sent down”, and the difference between the two is plain. «مَا» here is a relative pronoun meaning “that which”, not a word meaning “all”, and it takes in the individual instances of what Allah sent down. So of one who hates the Qurʾān, or hates the Sunnah, or hates the prophethood of Muḥammad ﷺ and is angered by it, or hates the ḥajj — it is sound to say: so-and-so hated what Allah sent down.

The objection — A general «مَا» must mean the totality.

The answer — Then test the same grammar on a sister verse. Of a man who eats one date it is sound to say he ate what Allah made lawful — and the verse is «كُلُوا مِن طَيِّبَاتِ مَا رَزَقْنَاكُم». So when we say whoever eats what Allah made lawful enters Paradise, or whoever commits what Allah forbade of the destructive sins enters the Fire, nobody understands that he must eat every lawful thing on earth first, or commit every forbidden thing.

The objection — And in any case the verse concerns only those who never entered Islām — and the disbeliever hates all of Allah’s religion.

The answer — This is not correct, and the list he gives is the refutation: among the disbelievers are those who love many of the Sharʿī rulings — maintaining ties of kinship, honouring the guest, the system of zakāh in Islām, chastity, aiding the wronged, truthfulness, trustworthiness — indeed some of them love Islām, and were it not for people’s blame he would accept it. And the disbelievers of Quraysh performed ḥajj and ʿumrah, and among them were those who fasted voluntarily and made vows to Allah. «So they are not all of them hating all of the religion of Allah and its rulings from first to last — and perhaps that is not even conceivable.»

The objection — If part-hatred were disbelief, the reductio would be absurd.

The answer — The compiler turns the reductio the other way and it lands: if the verse required hating the totality, we could not declare a disbeliever the man who hates Islām itself — not until he also hated every sound heavenly religion before its abrogation, and everything Allah sent down upon all of His prophets, «because all of it is of what Allah sent down — and nobody says this.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The objection — The Sunnah shows it is not disbelief: ʿUmar disliked the ruling of the Prophet ﷺ at the truce of al-Ḥudaybiyah — and the apparent state of the Ṣaḥābah that day is that they hated this truce, which is why they did not at first comply with his command ﷺ to shave.

The answer — No doubt many of the Ṣaḥābah did not desire the truce and were averse to it, and they reviewed it with the Prophet ﷺ. And that was before the ruling had settled with them — because they saw it as one of the political matters in which ijtihād is permitted, and they hoped only to change his ijtihād, since they supposed this was giving a humiliation in the religion, and held that the perfection of the religion lay in engaging the disbelievers of Makkah in battle, to humble them and make Islām manifest. So when they knew that this was of what the Prophet ﷺ brought, and the revelation settled it with the descent of Sūrat al-Fatḥ, they left off reviewing it with him and their souls were at peace with it. «So it is not permissible then to say that the Ṣaḥābah hated and were averse to something of what the Prophet ﷺ brought — we seek refuge with Allah.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That answer is worth more than the objection it answers. It gives you the whole method for reading the Ṣaḥābah’s reviewing of the Prophet ﷺ: a matter they took to be open to ijtihād, reviewed before the ruling was settled, and accepted the moment the revelation came. Not one of those three elements is dispensable. Take away the first and it is objection to a ruling; take away the third and it is persistence. It is also the exact shape of what the sharḥ said about Ḥunayn back in part 1 of the fourth nullifier.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He then answers the objection from the ḥadīth of the bedouin:

The Prophet ﷺ said to a man: «أَسْلِمْ» — “Accept Islām.” He said: «أَجِدُنِي كَارِهًا» — “I find myself averse.” He said: «أَسْلِمْ وَإِنْ كُنْتَ كَارِهًا» — “Accept Islām, even if you are averse.”

The grading is given, and named. The compiler cites it from Aḥmad’s Musnad by two chains from Ḥumayd from Anas and says «وَسَنَدُهُ صَحِيح» — its chain is authentic; and he reports al-Dhahabī’s judgement in Muʿjam Shuyūkhihi: «this is an authentic, singular ḥadīth».

And then the answer: «there is not in this that if he accepted Islām while hating the religion his Islām would be valid. For this bedouin had made clear to the Prophet ﷺ that in his soul there was a remnant of dislike for the religion which had not yet expanded to it — so the Prophet ﷺ guided him and directed him to accepting the truth and compelling the soul to accept Islām even if his soul disliked it; because faith, when its pleasantness mixes with the hearts, no one is displeased with it.»

So the ḥadīth is about a man being led out of the dislike, not a man being admitted with it. And the last sentence is the promise: the aversion does not survive contact with the faith itself.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The objection — And Thaqīf: the Prophet ﷺ accepted them although they stipulated that there be no ṣadaqah upon them and no jihād — «and the apparent state of them is that they entered Islām while hating these two rulings.» (Abū Dāwūd; the compiler grades the chain ḥasan.)

The answer — This is borne on dislike of acting on the two, for their hardship on them at the outset — and there is no text in it that they hated their legislation; rather their apparent state is that they disliked performing them. So the Prophet ﷺ was gentle with them, knowing that once they believed they would come to know the obligation of zakāh and jihād upon them and would comply, and he did not press them, lest they be driven away from Islām; and knowing that this corrupt condition has no weight and no effect — as in the Ṣaḥīḥayn in the story of Barīrah, when he said «and stipulate the right of inheritance for them», meaning: it has no effect. And the same ḥadīth itself says it: the Prophet ﷺ said afterwards, «they will give charity and they will fight, once they accept Islām.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the compiler seals that with a quotation from the ḥāfiẓ Abū Aḥmad al-Qaṣṣāb — whom Ibn Taymiyyah calls «the well-known imām» — in Nukat al-Qurʾān:

«And I do not know of any disagreement among the Ummah that the one coming out of disbelief into faith, were he to say: “I believe in Allah, and I believe that the prayer and the zakāh are a right — but I will not perform them,” and confine himself to pronouncing the testimony — that it is not accepted from him, and that he is a disbeliever as he was.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The objection — The last objection, and the most tempting: the mere commission of sins carries the sinner — willing or not — into hating some acts of obedience. The man given to drink or to fornication hates that the law of Allah be applied in his town so he is not prevented, and hates the ones who enjoin good; yet Ahl al-Sunnah are agreed that committing them does not take him out of Islām. So hating some acts of obedience is not disbelief.

The answer — «وَهَذَا لَيْسَ بِشَيْء، وَالمُلَازَمَةُ مُنْتَفِيَة» — the implication simply does not hold. Contradictory wills can sit together in one heart — the soul’s appetite for the sin and the heart’s hatred of the sin; so all the more does committing a sin not entail hating the obedience. And the claim is contradicted by the evidence: many of the people of major sins love the people of ḥisbah, and love that Islām be ruled by in their lands, and praise their rulers for establishing the ḥudūd — even while they want concealment for themselves; many of them love the people of obedience and uprightness and wish Allah would help them against their own desires so they might repent; and the drinkers and smokers are not angered to see the scholars of Islām warning against it — rather you find them warning their own children off it and striking them over it. And such-and-such of the Ṣaḥābah came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to have the ḥadd established upon them, and did not hate him ﷺ.

لَا تَلْعَنْهُ، فَإِنَّهُ يُحِبُّ اللهَ وَرَسُولَه

“Do not curse him — for he loves Allah and His Messenger.”

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيّ · said by the Prophet ﷺ of a man brought repeatedly for drink

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That ḥadīth is the whole answer in one sentence, and the compiler uses it as such. The man is a repeat offender, brought for the ḥadd again, and he is being cursed by others — and the Prophet ﷺ stops them with what is in the man’s heart.

So, the compiler concludes: «they love the legislation of Allah and love that the law of Allah be carried out on the criminals; and whoever’s faith among them is strong steps forward to the imām to have the ḥadd established on him; and of them is one who shelters under Allah’s concealment; and of them is one who hates that the ḥadd be established upon him — not because he hates the legislation, but for what is in it of pain to him, or his killing, or the tearing of his covering. So this is of the natural dislike.»

Which returns the whole refutation to the distinction it started from. Type 2, again — and now in the hardest case, the habitual sinner.

المَعَاصِي بَرِيدُ الكُفْر And Yet — the Two-Sided Warning

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Having refused the inference, the compiler refuses to leave the sinner comfortable either:

«Yes — immersion in the major sins and turning away from the obligations may lead its author in the future to aversion to the religion and hatred of it. And for this the scholars said: المَعَاصِي بَرِيدُ الكُفْر — the acts of disobedience are the courier of disbelief. So this divine punishment may descend on some of the disobedient who contravene His command; and for this Allah warned them lest a trial befall them — which shows the obligation of fearing sins, and that they are among the causes of falling into apostasy, we seek refuge with Allah from it.»

So both things are true at once, and the lesson is in holding them together. Committing a sin does not entail hating the obedience — and living in sin is the road by which that hatred arrives. The first is a rule of judgement; the second is a warning about oneself. Apply the first to other people and the second to yourself, and you have read the folios correctly.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the clause closes with something nobody would predict from its subject.

«When that is clear: it is obligatory upon every Muslim to beware lest there fall into his heart an aversion to the religion, and he be destroyed; and it is upon the scholar to carry the people upon the known middle way — so he does not take them the way of severity, nor incline them to the side of dissolution; for going out to the extremes is a departure from justice, and the welfare of the creation is not established by it. As for the side of dissolution — it is the likely place of walking with caprice and appetite, and Allah has forbidden us to follow them. And as for the side of harshness — it is a destroyer: it makes the religion hateful to the common people.»

Read that twice. A clause whose subject is hating the religion ends by naming harshness in teaching it as one of the things that produces that hatred — and it names it as a مُهْلِكَة, a destroyer, the same weight of word used for dissolution on the other side. The man who takes the nine folios above and uses them to make the religion heavy on the common people is, by the compiler’s own closing sentence, doing the thing the clause warns against.

فَهَذَا لَيْسَ مِنَ الكُفْرِ فِي شَيْء

The footnote, on natural dislike. Keep it beside the matn’s own four words, «وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِهِ», and you have both halves of the fifth nullifier in two short phrases. One says: the deed does not answer for the heart. The other says: the heart’s heaviness under a hard command is not the thing being judged. And beside both keep the compiler’s last sentence, because it is aimed at whoever teaches this clause: «فَإِنَّهَا مُهْلِكَةٌ تُبَغِّضُ لِلْعَامَّةِ الدِّين.»
