مُقَدِّمَةُ القَوَاعِدِ الأَرْبَع

The Author’s Introduction

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Recite the author’s opening duʿāʾ and name the ثَلَاث that are عُنْوَانُ السَّعَادَة.
  • Define الحَنِيفِيَّة as the author defines it, and give its proof.
  • Explain the العِبَادَة–الطَّهَارَة analogy in your own words.
  • State what shirk does to a deed, and why the author calls it شَبَكَة — a net.
  • Distinguish الشُّكْر from الحَمْد as Ibn Taymiyyah does.
  • Say why abandoning istighfār is dangerous even to the obedient.
  • Explain the kalimah as نَفْيٌ وَإِثْبَاتٌ using al-Zukhruf 43:26–27.
  • Tell a شَرْطُ قَبُولٍ from a شَرْطُ صِحَّةٍ, and say which tawḥīd is.
  • Use حَقُّ اللهِ and حَقُّ المَخْلُوقِ to explain why this ruling unsettles people.
الدُّعَاءُ الافْتِتَاحِيُّ The Opening Duʿāʾ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَسْأَلُ اللَّهَ الكَرِيمَ، رَبَّ العَرْشِ العَظِيمِ، أَنْ يَتَوَلَّاكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ، وَأَنْ يَجْعَلَكَ مُبَارَكًا أَيْنَمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَنْ يَجْعَلَكَ مِمَّنْ إِذَا أُعْطِيَ شَكَرَ، وَإِذَا ابْتُلِيَ صَبَرَ، وَإِذَا أَذْنَبَ اسْتَغْفَرَ؛ فَإِنَّ هَؤُلَاءِ الثَّلَاثَ عُنْوَانُ السَّعَادَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author does not open with a lecture. He opens by making duʿāʾ for you — and that tells you what kind of book this is: a short treatise written out of concern for the reader’s salvation, not a display of learning.

Then he names three states, and between them they cover every condition a servant can ever be in:

  • إِذَا أُعْطِيَ شَكَرَ — when he is given, he is grateful. The response to blessing is الشُّكْر.
  • وَإِذَا ابْتُلِيَ صَبَرَ — when he is tried, he is patient. The response to hardship is الصَّبْر.
  • وَإِذَا أَذْنَبَ اسْتَغْفَرَ — when he sins, he seeks forgiveness. The response to falling is الاسْتِغْفَار.

A عُنْوَان is the title on a book or the address on a letter — the thing written on the outside by which you know what is inside. So whoever is granted these three has been given the address of happiness: look at a man’s response to gift, to trial and to his own sin, and you can read what is inside him.

الحَنِيفِيَّةُ مِلَّةُ إِبْرَاهِيم The Ḥanīfiyyah — the Way of Ibrāhīm

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

اعْلَمْ — أَرْشَدَكَ اللَّهُ لِطَاعَتِهِ — أَنَّ الحَنِيفِيَّةَ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ مُخْلِصًا لَهُ الدِّينَ، وَبِذَلِكَ أَمَرَ اللَّهُ جَمِيعَ النَّاسِ وَخَلَقَهُمْ لَهَا.

Two things are packed into this definition, and the student must not lose either. التَّوْحِيد — أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ, that the One worshipped be Allah alone; and الإِخْلَاص — مُخْلِصًا لَهُ الدِّينَ, that the worship itself be purely for Him. A man may direct his worship to Allah and still ruin it by seeking the praise of people.

وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنْسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ

"And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me."

Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt 51:56 · سورة الذاريات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The āyah settles the question of purpose. You were not created to be merely moral, or merely useful — you were created to worship. And since worship is the purpose, getting worship right is the whole of the matter. That is why the author moves straight to the next point.

العِبَادَةُ وَالطَّهَارَة Worship and Purification — the Author’s Analogy

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَإِذَا عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ خَلَقَكَ لِعِبَادَتِهِ، فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّ العِبَادَةَ لَا تُسَمَّى عِبَادَةً إِلَّا مَعَ التَّوْحِيدِ، كَمَا أَنَّ الصَّلَاةَ لَا تُسَمَّى صَلَاةً إِلَّا مَعَ الطَّهَارَةِ؛ فَإِذَا دَخَلَ الشِّرْكُ فِي العِبَادَةِ فَسَدَتْ، كَالحَدَثِ إِذَا دَخَلَ فِي الطَّهَارَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the hinge of the whole treatise, and it is worth slowing down over. Every Muslim already understands the second half of the comparison perfectly — so the author uses what you know to teach you what you don’t.

الصَّلَاة — the prayerالعِبَادَة — worship
is valid only withالطَّهَارَة — purityالتَّوْحِيد — tawḥīd
and is ruined byالحَدَث — impurityالشِّرْك — shirk

Notice what the analogy claims. A man in a state of حَدَث who prays has not prayed a weak prayer — he has not prayed at all; the act does not even earn the name. So too the act of worship mixed with shirk is not poor worship. It is not worship. And a small amount of impurity is enough: the impurity does not have to outweigh the water.

الشَّبَكَة The Net — What Is at Stake

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَإِذَا عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ الشِّرْكَ إِذَا خَالَطَ العِبَادَةَ أَفْسَدَهَا، وَأَحْبَطَ العَمَلَ، وَصَارَ صَاحِبُهُ مِنَ الخَالِدِينَ فِي النَّارِ — عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ أَهَمَّ مَا عَلَيْكَ: مَعْرِفَةُ ذَلِكَ، لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُخَلِّصَكَ مِنْ هَذِهِ الشَّبَكَةِ، وَهِيَ الشِّرْكُ بِاللَّهِ.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَنْ يُشْرَكَ بِهِ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَلِكَ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ

"Indeed Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whomever He wills."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:48 · سورة النساء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author’s word for shirk is شَبَكَة — a net, the kind set for birds and fish. The image is exact: a net is not visible to the one walking into it, it is set deliberately, and the creature caught in it believes itself free until it tries to leave. This is why the four principles are needed at all. Nobody falls into shirk intending to; they fall in because they cannot see it.

And note how he words the rescue: لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُخَلِّصَكَ — perhaps Allah will free you. Knowledge is the means; the deliverance itself is from Allah, and is asked for, not assumed.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَذَلِكَ بِمَعْرِفَةِ أَرْبَعِ قَوَاعِدَ ذَكَرَهَا اللَّهُ تَعَالَى فِي كِتَابِهِ.

And with that sentence the four principles begin — each one, as the author insists, drawn from the Book of Allah itself.

لِمَاذَا بَدَأَ بِالدُّعَاءِ؟ Why Open with a Du‘ā’?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Shaykh Ṣāliḥ Āl al-Shaykh draws attention to something easy to read past. The Imām does not begin by condemning anyone. He begins by praying for the reader — and he cites Ibn al-Qayyim on the state of the one who calls to Allah among people turned away from the truth: he looks on the one who has earned punishment with mercy, the way you would look on a captive from among those you love, fallen into the hands of the enemy.

That image sets the tone of the whole treatise. What follows is severe in its rulings precisely because the author considers the people he is describing to be captives worth rescuing, not enemies worth defeating. Keep it in mind when you reach the fourth foundation.

الشُّكْرُ: بِاللِّسَانِ وَبِالعَمَلِ Gratitude — with the Tongue and with the Deed

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Gratitude is not only something said. The sharḥ shows it in the Qur’ān under both halves — speech and action — and Allah names each:

  • أَنِ اشْكُرْ لِي وَلِوَالِدَيْكَ — “be grateful to Me and to your parents” (Luqmān 31:14): with the tongue and the deed together.
  • اعْمَلُوا آلَ دَاوُودَ شُكْرًا — “work, O family of Dāwūd, in gratitude” (Sabaʾ 34:13): gratitude named as work.
  • وَاشْكُرُوا لِي وَلَا تَكْفُرُونِ — “and be grateful to Me and do not deny Me” (al-Baqarah 2:152): speech and deed again.

So gratitude by the tongue is to attribute the gift to the One who gave it, to praise Him for it, and not to turn to anyone else regarding it. Gratitude by action is to use the blessing in what the One who gave it loves.

Shukr is not the same as ḥamd. Ibn Taymiyyah settles the difference: الحَمْد is broader in its causes — it may be for a blessing or for no particular blessing at all, as unprompted praise; while الشُّكْر is broader in its kinds — it is with the heart, the tongue and the hand. Shukr answers a blessing; ḥamd may simply answer who Allah is.

ذُرِّيَّةَ مَنْ حَمَلْنَا مَعَ نُوحٍ إِنَّهُ كَانَ عَبْدًا شَكُورًا

"…descendants of those We carried with Nūḥ. Indeed he was a grateful servant."

Sūrat al-Isrā’ 17:3 · سورة الإسراء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Why was Nūḥ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ named عَبْدًا شَكُورًا — a grateful servant — as though it were his title? The mufassirūn answer, as Ibn Kathīr relates: when he ate he thanked Allah for it, when he drank he thanked Allah for it, when he was clothed he thanked Allah for that. Gratitude was not an occasion in his life; it was the texture of it.

And Allah says of how rare this is: وَقَلِيلٌ مِنْ عِبَادِيَ الشَّكُورُ — “and few of My servants are grateful” (Sabaʾ 34:13).

الصَّبْرُ وَالاسْتِغْفَارُ Patience, and Seeking Forgiveness

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The muwaḥḥid will be tried — that is treated as certain, not possible. The sharḥ notes the forms trial takes: through words directed at him, through his body, through his wealth, and otherwise.

And he will sin — minor sins or major ones. Allah is الغَفُور, and the effect of that name must appear among His creation; so He loves from His sincere servant that he be constant in seeking forgiveness.

Then comes the sentence worth copying out: when a servant abandons much istighfār, arrogance comes to him — and arrogance destroys a great deal of his deeds. Seeking forgiveness is not only how a sin is repaired; it is what keeps a worshipper from thinking well of himself.

And the measure of it: the more a servant knows his Lord, the greater these three grow in him. Which is why the one who needed them least did them most —

وَاللَّهِ إِنِّي لَأَسْتَغْفِرُ اللَّهَ وَأَتُوبُ إِلَيْهِ فِي الْيَوْمِ أَكْثَرَ مِنْ سَبْعِينَ مَرَّةً

"By Allah, I seek Allah's forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times a day."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī, from Abū Hurayrah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ — and Muslim records from al-Aghar al-Muzanī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ that he ﷺ sought forgiveness more than a hundred times in a day and a night

الشَّرْح · the explanation

A warning the sharḥ puts bluntly. The muwaḥḥid is in danger from الغُرُور — conceit. He may say that he is of the people of tawḥīd, or of those who truly follow the salaf, or that he is a student of knowledge — while there is not in his heart the humility and lowliness before Allah that would make this very means accepted. The claim can hollow out the thing claimed.

الحَنِيفِيَّةُ وَأَعْظَمُ تَفْسِيرٍ لِلْكَلِمَةِ The Ḥanīfiyyah, and the Greatest Explanation of the Kalimah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The word itself carries the meaning. حَنَفَ in the language is to incline, to turn away (Lisān al-ʿArab, Mukhtār al-Ṣiḥāḥ): الحَنِيف is the one who inclines away from the other religions toward the truth — and it is also said to mean the one who makes his religion purely for Allah. So the ḥanīfiyyah is the way that turned away from every falsehood toward the truth.

Allah commanded His Prophet ﷺ to hold to it: أَنِ اتَّبِعْ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ حَنِيفًا — “follow the way of Ibrāhīm, inclining to the truth” (al-Naḥl 16:123); and He described Ibrāhīm as شَاكِرًا لِأَنْعُمِهِ — “grateful for His favours” (al-Naḥl 16:120–121), which joins this lesson's two themes in one man.

And then the sharḥ makes a claim worth pausing on: the greatest explanation of the kalimah in the whole Qur’ān is Ibrāhīm's own words —

وَإِذْ قَالَ إِبْرَاهِيمُ لِأَبِيهِ وَقَوْمِهِ إِنَّنِي بَرَاءٌ مِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ إِلَّا الَّذِي فَطَرَنِي فَإِنَّهُ سَيَهْدِينِ

"And when Ibrāhīm said to his father and his people: I am free of what you worship, except the One who originated me — for indeed He will guide me."

Sūrat al-Zukhruf 43:26–27 · سورة الزخرف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Lay the kalimah over the āyah and they match piece for piece:

The kalimahIbrāhīm's wordsWhat it does
لَا إِلَٰهَإِنَّنِي بَرَاءٌ مِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَالنَّفْي — disowning every object of worship besides Allah
إِلَّا اللَّهُإِلَّا الَّذِي فَطَرَنِيالإِثْبَات — establishing worship for Him alone

This is why the scholars say the kalimah is نَفْيٌ وَإِثْبَاتٌ — a denial and an affirmation. Half of it is a no, and a man who only ever says the yes has not said the kalimah. Ibn al-Qayyim treats this in Badāʾiʿ al-Fawāʾid, and notes the same two movements in Sūrat al-Kāfirūn.

شَرْطُ القَبُولِ وَشَرْطُ الصِّحَّةِ A Condition of Acceptance, a Condition of Validity

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ sharpens the analogy into exact terms. Ṭahārah is a شَرْطُ صِحَّةٍ — a condition for the prayer's validity. Tawḥīd and ikhlāṣ are a شَرْطُ قَبُولٍ — a condition for worship's acceptance.

And it holds no matter how much worship there is. Though the mark of sujūd be on his forehead, though he fast the day and stand the night — the condition of acceptance is that he be a muwaḥḥid, sincere. Allah said to His own Prophet ﷺ:

بَلِ اللَّهَ فَاعْبُدْ وَكُنْ مِنَ الشَّاكِرِينَ

"Rather, worship Allah, and be among the grateful."

Sūrat al-Zumar 39:66 · سورة الزمر

وَقَدِمْنَا إِلَىٰ مَا عَمِلُوا مِنْ عَمَلٍ فَجَعَلْنَاهُ هَبَاءً مَنْثُورًا

"And We shall turn to whatever deeds they did, and make them scattered dust."

Sūrat al-Furqān 25:23 · سورة الفرقان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Picture the comparison the sharḥ actually uses. A man prays a magnificent prayer — long standing, long bowing, long prostration, beautifully performed — but he entered it without purity. By the agreement of the scholars that prayer is not accepted:

لَا تُقْبَلُ صَلَاةٌ بِغَيْرِ طُهُورٍ، وَلَا صَدَقَةٌ مِنْ غُلُولٍ

"No prayer is accepted without purity, and no charity from what was taken wrongfully."

رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ · Muslim, from Ibn ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا — and in the Ṣaḥīḥayn from Abū Hurayrah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ: «لَا يَقْبَلُ اللَّهُ صَلَاةَ أَحَدِكُمْ إِذَا أَحْدَثَ حَتَّى يَتَوَضَّأَ»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the asymmetry, which is the point of the whole comparison. The condition of ikhlāṣ for worship is weightier than the condition of purity for the prayer. One who prays deliberately in a state of ḥadath — the scholars differ over whether he is a disbeliever. But one who worships Allah while committing major shirk is, by agreement, not accepted, and by agreement, a disbeliever. The analogy understates its own case.

حَقُّ اللهِ وَحَقُّ المَخْلُوقِ The Right of Allah, the Right of the Creature

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ ends the introduction by naming honestly how hard this ruling sits with people, and it is worth hearing in full. Someone says lā ilāha illā Allāh, Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah; he prays, gives zakāh, fasts, makes ḥajj, devotes himself to worship; people consider him righteous. And then it is said that his deeds are scattered dust. “This is a grave thing — and how is it to settle in people's souls?”

The resolution is a single distinction. The matter is judged by حَقُّ اللهِ — the right of Allah. The error enters when people look instead at حَقُّ المَخْلُوقِ — the right of the creature, the state the creature is in. Look at the worshipper and you see effort, sincerity of feeling, a life of devotion. Look at the right of Allah — who created man and shaped him, who raised the heavens in this astonishing order, who set the proofs of His oneness in the soul and in the horizons — and no mushrik has any argument left against Allah at all.

And even so, the sharḥ adds, Allah sent the messengers as a mercy: to establish the proof and to deliver the warning. That is why these four rules exist, and why a short treatise about them opens with a du‘ā’ for the reader.
