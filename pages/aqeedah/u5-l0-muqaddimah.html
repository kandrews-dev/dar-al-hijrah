مُقَدِّمَةُ القَوَاعِدِ الأَرْبَع

The Author’s Introduction

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Recite the author’s opening duʿāʾ and name the ثَلَاث that are عُنْوَانُ السَّعَادَة.
  • Define الحَنِيفِيَّة as the author defines it, and give its proof.
  • Explain the العِبَادَة–الطَّهَارَة analogy in your own words.
  • State what shirk does to a deed, and why the author calls it شَبَكَة — a net.
الدُّعَاءُ الافْتِتَاحِيُّ The Opening Duʿāʾ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَسْأَلُ اللَّهَ الكَرِيمَ، رَبَّ العَرْشِ العَظِيمِ، أَنْ يَتَوَلَّاكَ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ، وَأَنْ يَجْعَلَكَ مُبَارَكًا أَيْنَمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَنْ يَجْعَلَكَ مِمَّنْ إِذَا أُعْطِيَ شَكَرَ، وَإِذَا ابْتُلِيَ صَبَرَ، وَإِذَا أَذْنَبَ اسْتَغْفَرَ؛ فَإِنَّ هَؤُلَاءِ الثَّلَاثَ عُنْوَانُ السَّعَادَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author does not open with a lecture. He opens by making duʿāʾ for you — and that tells you what kind of book this is: a short treatise written out of concern for the reader’s salvation, not a display of learning.

Then he names three states, and between them they cover every condition a servant can ever be in:

  • إِذَا أُعْطِيَ شَكَرَ — when he is given, he is grateful. The response to blessing is الشُّكْر.
  • وَإِذَا ابْتُلِيَ صَبَرَ — when he is tried, he is patient. The response to hardship is الصَّبْر.
  • وَإِذَا أَذْنَبَ اسْتَغْفَرَ — when he sins, he seeks forgiveness. The response to falling is الاسْتِغْفَار.

A عُنْوَان is the title on a book or the address on a letter — the thing written on the outside by which you know what is inside. So whoever is granted these three has been given the address of happiness: look at a man’s response to gift, to trial and to his own sin, and you can read what is inside him.

الحَنِيفِيَّةُ مِلَّةُ إِبْرَاهِيم The Ḥanīfiyyah — the Way of Ibrāhīm

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

اعْلَمْ — أَرْشَدَكَ اللَّهُ لِطَاعَتِهِ — أَنَّ الحَنِيفِيَّةَ مِلَّةَ إِبْرَاهِيمَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ مُخْلِصًا لَهُ الدِّينَ، وَبِذَلِكَ أَمَرَ اللَّهُ جَمِيعَ النَّاسِ وَخَلَقَهُمْ لَهَا.

Two things are packed into this definition, and the student must not lose either. التَّوْحِيد — أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللَّهَ وَحْدَهُ, that the One worshipped be Allah alone; and الإِخْلَاص — مُخْلِصًا لَهُ الدِّينَ, that the worship itself be purely for Him. A man may direct his worship to Allah and still ruin it by seeking the praise of people.

وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنْسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ

"And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me."

Sūrat al-Dhāriyāt 51:56 · سورة الذاريات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The āyah settles the question of purpose. You were not created to be merely moral, or merely useful — you were created to worship. And since worship is the purpose, getting worship right is the whole of the matter. That is why the author moves straight to the next point.

العِبَادَةُ وَالطَّهَارَة Worship and Purification — the Author’s Analogy

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَإِذَا عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ اللَّهَ خَلَقَكَ لِعِبَادَتِهِ، فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّ العِبَادَةَ لَا تُسَمَّى عِبَادَةً إِلَّا مَعَ التَّوْحِيدِ، كَمَا أَنَّ الصَّلَاةَ لَا تُسَمَّى صَلَاةً إِلَّا مَعَ الطَّهَارَةِ؛ فَإِذَا دَخَلَ الشِّرْكُ فِي العِبَادَةِ فَسَدَتْ، كَالحَدَثِ إِذَا دَخَلَ فِي الطَّهَارَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the hinge of the whole treatise, and it is worth slowing down over. Every Muslim already understands the second half of the comparison perfectly — so the author uses what you know to teach you what you don’t.

الصَّلَاة — the prayerالعِبَادَة — worship
is valid only withالطَّهَارَة — purityالتَّوْحِيد — tawḥīd
and is ruined byالحَدَث — impurityالشِّرْك — shirk

Notice what the analogy claims. A man in a state of حَدَث who prays has not prayed a weak prayer — he has not prayed at all; the act does not even earn the name. So too the act of worship mixed with shirk is not poor worship. It is not worship. And a small amount of impurity is enough: the impurity does not have to outweigh the water.

الشَّبَكَة The Net — What Is at Stake

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَإِذَا عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ الشِّرْكَ إِذَا خَالَطَ العِبَادَةَ أَفْسَدَهَا، وَأَحْبَطَ العَمَلَ، وَصَارَ صَاحِبُهُ مِنَ الخَالِدِينَ فِي النَّارِ — عَرَفْتَ أَنَّ أَهَمَّ مَا عَلَيْكَ: مَعْرِفَةُ ذَلِكَ، لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُخَلِّصَكَ مِنْ هَذِهِ الشَّبَكَةِ، وَهِيَ الشِّرْكُ بِاللَّهِ.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يَغْفِرُ أَنْ يُشْرَكَ بِهِ وَيَغْفِرُ مَا دُونَ ذَلِكَ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ

"Indeed Allah does not forgive that partners be associated with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whomever He wills."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:48 · سورة النساء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author’s word for shirk is شَبَكَة — a net, the kind set for birds and fish. The image is exact: a net is not visible to the one walking into it, it is set deliberately, and the creature caught in it believes itself free until it tries to leave. This is why the four principles are needed at all. Nobody falls into shirk intending to; they fall in because they cannot see it.

And note how he words the rescue: لَعَلَّ اللَّهَ أَنْ يُخَلِّصَكَ — perhaps Allah will free you. Knowledge is the means; the deliverance itself is from Allah, and is asked for, not assumed.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَذَلِكَ بِمَعْرِفَةِ أَرْبَعِ قَوَاعِدَ ذَكَرَهَا اللَّهُ تَعَالَى فِي كِتَابِهِ.

And with that sentence the four principles begin — each one, as the author insists, drawn from the Book of Allah itself.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَإِنَّ هَؤُلَاءِ الثَّلَاثَ عُنْوَانُ السَّعَادَةِ

"For these three are the address of happiness." Test yourself against them tonight: what came to you today that you did not thank Allah for? What was taken from you that you met with complaint instead of ṣabr? What did you do that you have not yet asked forgiveness for?
