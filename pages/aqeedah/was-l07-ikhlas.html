الرَّفِيقُ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ وَسُورَةُ الإِخْلَاص

The Company on the Path, and Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the four categories of الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِمْ and define each.
  • Explain why the ṣirāṭ is ascribed sometimes to Allah and sometimes to His servants.
  • Give al-Fawzān’s two reasons the sūrah is named الإِخْلَاص.
  • Say why it equals a third of the Qurʾān, and give the ḥadīth.
  • Split Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ into its affirmations and its negations.
الرَّفِيقُ عَلَى الصِّرَاط The Company on the Path

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِمْ مِنَ النَّبِيِّينَ، وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ، وَالشُّهَدَاءِ، وَالصَّالِحِينَ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The straight path the messengers brought — in creed and in everything else — and which Ahl al-Sunnah walk, is the path of those Allah has favoured. And al-Fawzān is precise about which favour: الإِنْعَامُ المُطْلَقُ التَّامُّ المُتَّصِلُ بِسَعَادَةِ الأَبَدِ — the absolute, complete favour, joined to everlasting happiness. Not every blessing qualifies. These four do:

النَّبِيُّونthose Allah singled out with His prophethood and His message
الصِّدِّيقُونthose who went far in truthfulness and in affirming the truth — that is, far in submitting to the Messenger ﷺ, with complete sincerity to Allah
الشُّهَدَاءthose killed in the path of Allah — named so because Paradise is testified for them, and because the angels of mercy witness them
الصَّالِحُونthose who fulfil the rights of Allah and the rights of His servants

A detail worth noticing: the ṣirāṭ is sometimes ascribed to Allah — وَأَنَّ هَٰذَا صِرَاطِي مُسْتَقِيمًا فَاتَّبِعُوهُ, “and this is My path, straight, so follow it” (al-Anʿām 6:153) — because He is the One who legislated it and set it up. And sometimes to the servants — صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ — because they are the ones who walked it. The road is His; the footprints are theirs.

وَأَنَّ هَٰذَا صِرَاطِي مُسْتَقِيمًا فَاتَّبِعُوهُ وَلَا تَتَّبِعُوا السُّبُلَ فَتَفَرَّقَ بِكُمْ عَنْ سَبِيلِهِ

"And this is My path, straight — so follow it, and do not follow the other ways, lest they scatter you from His way."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:153 · سورة الأنعام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then al-Fawzān draws out something from the wording that is pastoral rather than technical, and it is one of the kindest observations in the sharḥ. In saying صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ, there is تَنْبِيهٌ عَلَى الرَّفِيقِ فِي هَذَا الطَّرِيقِ — a pointer to the company on this road.

— so that the loneliness of standing apart from the people of his own time leaves the one walking this path, when he realises that his companions on this ṣirāṭ are the prophets, the ṣiddīqūn, the martyrs and the righteous.

Read that against Ibn Masʿūd's line from Lesson 2 — the jamāʿah is whatever agrees with the truth, even if you are alone. This is its consolation. You are not alone on the road; you are only alone in your century. And this is the path we ask Allah to guide us to in every rakʿah of every prayer.

القِسْمُ الأَوَّل · الأَدِلَّةُ مِنَ القُرْآن Part One — the Evidence from the Qurʾān

From here the author begins producing the evidence. He has stated the method — that Allah joined negation and affirmation in what He named and described Himself with — and now he brings example after example, first from the Qurʾān and then from the Sunnah. This is the longest part of the book, and the first witness he calls is the shortest sūrah he could have chosen.

١ · سُورَةُ الإِخْلَاص Section 1 — Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَدْ دَخَلَ فِي هَذِهِ الجُمْلَةِ مَا وَصَفَ اللهُ بِهِ نَفْسَهُ فِي سُورَةِ الإِخْلَاصِ الَّتِي تَعْدِلُ ثُلُثَ القُرْآنِ، حَيْثُ يَقُولُ: ﴿قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ﴾.

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

"Say: He is Allah, One. Allah, the Self-Sufficient Master. He neither begets nor was begotten. And there is none comparable to Him."

Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1–4 · سورة الإخلاص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Why begin here? al-Fawzān says: لِفَضْلِهَا — for its excellence. And then he gives two reasons for its name, both worth keeping:

  • Because it is purely about the attributes of Allah — أَخْلَصَتْ فِي صِفَاتِ اللهِ.
  • Because it frees its reciter from shirk — تُخَلِّصُ قَارِئَهَا مِنَ الشِّرْكِ.

The second is the more striking. The sūrah is not only about sincerity; it works sincerity in the one who recites it.

And why does it equal a third of the Qurʾān? Because, al-Fawzān explains, the meanings of the Qurʾān are of three kinds — تَوْحِيدٌ، وَقَصَصٌ، وَأَحْكَامٌ: tawḥīd, narratives, and rulings. This sūrah is tawḥīd alone. So it comes to a third.

وَالَّذِي نَفْسِي بِيَدِهِ، إِنَّهَا لَتَعْدِلُ ثُلُثَ القُرْآنِ

"By the One in whose Hand is my soul, it equals a third of the Qurʾān." — a man heard another reciting Qul huwa Allāhu Aḥad, repeating it; in the morning he came to the Prophet ﷺ and mentioned it, as though he thought little of it. So the Prophet ﷺ said this.

أَبُو سَعِيدٍ الخُدْرِيُّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ · رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī — and Ibn al-Qayyim notes that the reports of it equalling a third are so many they nearly reach tawātur

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the words themselves, as al-Fawzān takes them one by one:

  • قُلْ — “Say,” addressed to Muḥammad ﷺ. And al-Fawzān draws an argument out of that single word: in it is a proof that the Qurʾān is the speech of Allah — for had it been the speech of Muḥammad or of anyone else, He would not have said قُلْ.
  • أَحَدٌ — One: with no peer, no deputy, no like and no partner.
  • الصَّمَدُ — the Master perfect in His sovereignty, His honour and His greatness, in whom are all the attributes of perfection; and the One whom the creation turns to and seeks in all its needs and its gravest affairs.
  • لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ — He has no child and no parent. And in it is a refutation of the Christians, and of the mushrikūn of the Arabs, who ascribed a child to Allah.
  • وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ — He has no match, no likeness and no peer.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the الشَّاهِد — the point for which the author cited the sūrah at all. It joined negation and affirmation, and you can split it down the middle:

الإِثْبَاتالنَّفْي
اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ		لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

Two āyahs affirming, two negating — and the negations come after the affirmations, not instead of them. Four short lines, and the method of the whole book is already complete inside them. This is why the author began here.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ

You have known it since childhood. Read it once more with the table in front of you: two affirmations, then two negations — in that order. A third of the Qurʾān, and the method of this entire book, in four lines.
