هُوَ الأَوَّلُ وَالآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالبَاطِن

The First, the Last, the Manifest, the Hidden

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Recite the ḥadīth in which the Prophet ﷺ explained al-Ḥadīd 57:3 himself.
  • Pair the four names: which two are for أَزَلِيَّتِهِ وَأَبَدِيَّتِهِ, and which two for عُلُوِّهِ وَقُرْبِهِ.
  • Define التَّوَكُّل in the language and in the Sharīʿah, and say why it does not cancel taking means.
  • Give the two meanings of الحَكِيم and the derivation of الخَبِير.
٢ · الجَمْعُ بَيْنَ عُلُوِّهِ وَقُرْبِهِ وَأَزَلِيَّتِهِ وَأَبَدِيَّتِه Section 2 — His Highness and Nearness, His Beginninglessness and Endlessness

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ سُبْحَانَهُ: ﴿هُوَ الْأَوَّلُ وَالْآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالْبَاطِنُ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ﴾.

هُوَ الْأَوَّلُ وَالْآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالْبَاطِنُ وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ

"He is the First and the Last, the Manifest and the Hidden; and He is Knowing of all things."

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd 57:3 · سورة الحديد

الشَّرْح · the explanation

There is no need to reason about this āyah, because the Prophet ﷺ explained it himself — and al-Fawzān brings his explanation from Muslim. Four names, four clauses, each one a denial of anything beyond Allah in that direction:

اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ الأَوَّلُ فَلَيْسَ قَبْلَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الآخِرُ فَلَيْسَ بَعْدَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ البَاطِنُ فَلَيْسَ دُونَكَ شَيْءٌ

"O Allah, You are the First — there is nothing before You. You are the Last — there is nothing after You. You are the Manifest — there is nothing above You. You are the Hidden — there is nothing nearer than You."

رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ · Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān points out the structure the ḥadīth reveals. The four names give His encompassing from every direction:

الأَوَّلُ وَالآخِرإِحَاطَتُهُ الزَّمَانِيَّة — His encompassing in time
الظَّاهِرُ وَالبَاطِنإِحَاطَتُهُ المَكَانِيَّة — His encompassing in place

And Ibn al-Qayyim unfolds it further — four names in two facing pairs, two for His beginninglessness and endlessness, and two for His highness and His nearness:

  • His firstness precedes the firstness of everything else — it is a precedence over all things.
  • His lastness stands after the lastness of everything else — it is His remaining after all things.
  • His manifestness is His aboveness and highness over everything: the meaning of appearing requires highness, and the outward of a thing is what is uppermost in it.
  • His hiddenness is His encompassing of everything, such that He is nearer to it than it is to itself — and this is the nearness of general encompassing.

Note what that last line does. البَاطِن is not a name of obscurity — it is a name of nearness. He is hidden because nothing is closer; what is closest to you is what you are least able to stand back and look at.

And وَهُوَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمٌ: His knowledge has encompassed everything — past, present and future; the upper world and the lower; the outward and the inward. Not the weight of an atom in the earth or in the heaven escapes His knowledge.

The الشَّاهِد: the āyah affirms these names, which entail His encompassing of everything — in time, in place, in awareness, in decree and in management.

الحَيُّ الَّذِي لَا يَمُوتُ The Living Who Does Not Die

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ سُبْحَانَهُ: ﴿وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى الْحَيِّ الَّذِي لَا يَمُوتُ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَهُوَ الْحَكِيمُ الْخَبِيرُ﴾.

وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى الْحَيِّ الَّذِي لَا يَمُوتُ

"And put your trust in the Living who does not die."

Sūrat al-Furqān 25:58 · سورة الفرقان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّوَكُّل in the language is handing over — you say وَكَّلْتُ أَمْرِي إِلَى فُلَانٍ, meaning I handed my affair over to him. And in the Sharīʿah it is اعْتِمَادُ القَلْبِ عَلَى اللهِ: the heart's reliance upon Allah in bringing about what benefits and repelling what harms.

al-Fawzān makes two rulings explicit, and the second settles a question students ask constantly: reliance is a kind of worship, and it is obligatory; and it does not contradict taking the means — rather it agrees with them completely. A man who ties his camel and trusts Allah has done both; a man who leaves it loose has done neither.

And then the reason the āyah names this particular attribute. Why say “the Living who does not die” when commanding reliance? Because:

— the attribute of life was singled out as a pointer that it is the living who can be depended on to secure what one needs. And there is no permanent life except for Allah. As for the living whose lives are cut off — when they die, whoever relied on them is lost.

That is a complete argument for tawḥīd of reliance, made from one word. Every other object of reliance has an expiry date.

The shāhid: it affirms the perfect life of Allah and negates death of Him — so here again is the joining of negation and affirmation.

وَهُوَ الْحَكِيمُ الْخَبِيرُ

"And He is the All-Wise, the All-Aware."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:18 · سورة الأنعام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الحَكِيم carries two meanings, and al-Fawzān gives both:

  • The Judge — who rules between His creation by His universal command and His legislated command, in this world and the next.
  • The One who perfects — from الحِكْمَة, which is putting things in their places. So He did not create anything in vain, and did not legislate anything but what is pure benefit.

الخَبِير is from الخِبْرَة, which is encompassing the insides of things as well as their outsides. You say خَبَرْتُ الشَّيْءَ when you have known a thing as it really is. So He is the One whose awareness has encompassed the hidden depths of things and their secrets, as it has encompassed their surfaces.

The shāhid: two of His names affirmed, and they contain two attributes — الحِكْمَة and الخِبْرَة.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَنْتَ الأَوَّلُ فَلَيْسَ قَبْلَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الآخِرُ فَلَيْسَ بَعْدَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ البَاطِنُ فَلَيْسَ دُونَكَ شَيْءٌ

A duʿāʾ you can say tonight, and a tafsīr of an āyah by the one who received it. Four directions, four denials — and nothing of creation left standing in any of them.
