مَكَانَةُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ بَيْنَ فِرَقِ الأُمَّة

The Standing of Ahl al-Sunnah among the Sects of the Ummah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٠٩–١١٣
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 109–113.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say what الوَسَط means here, and the āyah al-Fawzān grounds it in.
  • Name both extremes in each of the five bābs, and trace each name to its origin.
  • Give the one-line summary of how Ahl al-Sunnah settle the question of الوَعْد وَالوَعِيد.
  • State the definition of الصَّحَابِيّ that al-Fawzān gives.
القِسْمُ الثَّالِث · مَكَانَةُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ بَيْنَ فِرَقِ الأُمَّة Part Three — The Standing of Ahl al-Sunnah among the Sects of the Ummah

al-Fawzān explains why the author turns here, and the reasoning is a piece of pedagogy worth noting: when the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ had made clear the position of Ahl al-Sunnah toward the texts of the attributes, he wanted to make clear their standing among the sects of the Ummah, so that their worth and their excellence would be known by comparing them with others — for الضِّدَّ يُظْهِرُ حُسْنَهُ الضِّدُّ, the opposite is what shows up the goodness of its opposite, and بِضِدِّهَا تَتَبَيَّنُ الأَشْيَاءُ, by their contraries things are made clear.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

بَلْ هُمُ الوَسَطُ فِي فِرَقِ الأُمَّةِ، كَمَا أَنَّ الأُمَّةَ هِيَ الوَسَطُ فِي الأُمَمِ. فَهُمْ وَسَطٌ فِي بَابِ صِفَاتِ اللهِِ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى بَيْنَ أَهْلِ التَّعْطِيلِ الجَهْمِيَّةِ وَأَهْلِ التَّمْثِيلِ المُشَبِّهَةِ. وَهُمْ وَسَطٌ فِي بَابِ أَفْعَالِ اللهِِ بَيْنَ الجَبْرِيَّةِ وَالقَدَرِيَّةِ وَغَيْرِهِمْ. وَفِي بَابِ وَعِيدِ اللهِِ بَيْنَ المُرْجِئَةِ وَالوَعِيدِيَّةِ مِنَ القَدَرِيَّةِ وَغَيْرِهِمْ. وَفِي بَابِ أَسْمَاءِ الإِيمَانِ وَالدِّينِ بَيْنَ الحَرُورِيَّةِ وَالمُعْتَزِلَةِ وَبَيْنَ المُرْجِئَةِ وَالجَهْمِيَّةِ. وَفِي أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ بَيْنَ الرَّافِضَةِ وَالخَوَارِجِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

First the word. al-Fawzān quotes al-Miṣbāḥ al-Munīr: الوَسَط, vowelled with the fatḥah on the sīn, is the balanced; and what is intended by al-wasaṭ here is العَدْلُ الخِيَار — the just, the choicest.

That is the correction of a modern misreading before it starts. الوَسَط does not mean lukewarm, or halfway, or moderate in the sense of diluted. It means the best part — as the centre of a thing is its choicest part. And the author did not coin the word; it is the Qurʾān's own description of this Ummah:

وَكَذَلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ

"And thus We made you a middlemost community, that you may be witnesses over mankind."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:143 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So they are a middle between the sects that ascribe themselves to Islam, just as the Islamic Ummah is a middle between the nations: for this Ummah is a middle between the nations that incline to excess and immoderation and the nations that incline to negligence and laxity. And Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah, of this Ummah, are a middle between the innovating sects of the Ummah, which deviated from the straight path — some of them going to excess and becoming extreme, and some of them being lax and so deviating.

Note the shape of the claim: the same pattern repeats at two scales. The Ummah among the nations, and Ahl al-Sunnah within the Ummah. And note that both kinds of deviation are named deviation — the lax are not merely weaker versions of the correct position; they are off the road in the other direction.

أَوَّلًا · فِي بَابِ صِفَاتِ اللهِ First — In the Chapter of Allah’s Attributes

الشَّرْح · the explanation

١ · بَابُ صِفَاتِ اللهِ
الجَهْمِيَّةthe muʿaṭṭilah, named after الجَهْمُ بْنُ صَفْوَانَ التِّرْمِذِيّ. They went to excess and were immoderate in declaring Allah free (tanzīh) until they negated His names and His attributes, in wariness of likening Him — so they claimed. And by that they were called muʿaṭṭilah, because they stripped Allah of His names and attributes.
المُشَبِّهَةthe people of tamthīl. They went to excess and were immoderate in affirming the attributes until they likened Allah to His creation and compared His attributes to theirs — تَعَالَى اللهُ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ.
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةThey took the middle between the two extremes: they affirmed Allah’s attributes in the manner befitting His majesty, without likening and without comparison. So they did not go to excess in tanzīh, and they did not go to excess in affirmation — rather:
نَزَّهُوا اللهَ بِلَا تَعْطِيلٍ، وَأَثْبَتُوا لَهُ الأَسْمَاءَ وَالصِّفَاتِ بِلَا تَمْثِيلٍ

That closing pair of clauses is the whole of Parts 1 and 2 in eleven words. Both extremes agreed on one false premise — that affirming an attribute means likening — and then split over what to do about it. The Jahmiyyah dropped the attribute to escape the likeness; the Mushabbihah accepted the likeness in order to keep the attribute. Ahl al-Sunnah denied the premise, which is what al-Shūrā 42:11 does in its two halves, as Lesson 5 showed.

ثَانِيًا · فِي بَابِ أَفْعَالِ اللهِ Second — In the Chapter of Allah’s Actions

الشَّرْح · the explanation

٢ · بَابُ أَفْعَالِ اللهِ
الجَبْرِيَّةnamed after الجَبْر, compulsion, because they say the servant is compelled in his action. They went to excess in affirming Allah’s actions until they negated the actions of the servants, and claimed that they do nothing at all, but that Allah is the doer and the servant is compelled — so his movements and his actions are all involuntary, like the movements of a man with a tremor, and ascribing the action to the servant is figurative.
القَدَرِيَّةnamed after القَدَر. They went to excess in affirming the actions of the servants, and said: the servant creates his own action, without Allah’s will or volition; so the actions of the servants do not fall under Allah’s will and volition — Allah neither decreed them nor willed them, and they did them independently.
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةThey took the middle, and said: the servant has choice, and volition, and an action that issues from him — but he does nothing without Allah’s will, His volition and His decree.

And al-Fawzān grounds each half of that in an āyah. For the deed:

وَاللَّهُ خَلَقَكُمْ وَمَا تَعْمَلُونَ

"And Allah created you and what you do."

Sūrat al-Ṣāffāt 37:96 · سُورَةُ الصَّافَّات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So He affirmed for the servants a deed which is of Allah's creating and His decreeing. Read it carefully: the āyah does not say Allah created you instead of your deeds, nor that you do them outside His creating. It names both in one breath — which is exactly the position that neither extreme can state.

And for the volition:

وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ رَبُّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"And you do not will, except that Allah — Lord of the worlds — wills."

Sūrat al-Takwīr 81:29 · سُورَةُ التَّكْوِير

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So He affirmed for the servants a volition which comes after the volition of Allah تَعَالَى. And more clarification of this is to come إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ in the discussion of al-qadar.

That is the precise formulation, and the word to hold is بَعْدَ — after. The servant's will is not denied (against the Jabriyyah) and not independent (against the Qadariyyah). It is real, and it is downstream.

ثَالِثًا · فِي بَابِ وَعِيدِ اللهِ Third — In the Chapter of Allah’s Threat

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الوَعِيد is frightening and threatening; and what is intended here is the texts in which there is a threatening of the disobedient with punishment and exemplary chastisement.

٣ · بَابُ وَعِيدِ اللهِ
المُرْجِئَةfrom الإِرْجَاء, postponement — they were named so because they postponed the deeds out of the definition of īmān, claiming that the one who commits a major sin is not sinful. And they said: no sin harms alongside faith, just as no obedience benefits alongside disbelief. So with them, the committer of a major sin is complete in faith and not exposed to the threat — they were lax in the ruling on the disobedient and went to excess in laxity, until they claimed that acts of disobedience do not decrease faith.
الوَعِيدِيَّةthose who held the carrying out of the threat upon the disobedient, and were severe in that — until they said: the committer of a major sin, if he dies without repenting, abides forever in the Fire, and they ruled that he leaves faith in this world.
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةThey took the middle between the two sides and said: the committer of a major sin is sinful, exposed to the threat, deficient in faith, and is ruled to be a fāsiq — not as the Murjiʾah say, that he is complete in faith and not exposed — but he does not leave faith, and does not abide forever in the Fire if he enters it. He is under the will of Allah: if He wills He pardons him, and if He wills He punishes him to the measure of his sin, then he comes out of the Fire and enters the Garden — not as the Waʿīdiyyah say.

And then al-Fawzān states it in a single line that is the best thing in this section, and which generalises far past this question:

فَالمُرْجِئَةُ أَخَذُوا بِنُصُوصِ الوَعْدِ، وَالوَعِيدِيَّةُ أَخَذُوا بِنُصُوصِ الوَعِيدِ، وَأَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ جَمَعُوا بَيْنَهُمَا

— the Murjiʾah took the texts of the promise; the Waʿīdiyyah took the texts of the threat; and Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah gathered the two together.

Which is the same diagnosis as the first bāb, in a different subject. Each sect is right about the half of the revelation it is holding. The error is not in what they affirmed but in what they had to discard to keep it — and al-wasaṭ turns out to mean the position that needs to discard nothing.

رَابِعًا · فِي بَابِ أَسْمَاءِ الإِيمَانِ وَالدِّين Fourth — In the Chapter of the Names of Faith and Religion

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here the author names four parties, two on each side, and al-Fawzān traces both names on the severe side to the incidents that produced them:

الحَرُورِيَّةthey are the Khawārij, named in relation to Ḥarūrā, a village in ʿIrāq in which they gathered when they went out against ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ.
المُعْتَزِلَةthe followers of وَاصِلُ بْنُ عَطَاءٍ, who withdrew from the gathering of al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, and his followers went off with him, because of a disagreement that occurred between the two of them over the ruling on the Muslim who commits a major sin. So al-Ḥasan رَحِمَهُ اللهُ said of this Wāṣil: «he has withdrawn from us» — so they were called Muʿtazilah, the withdrawers.

So the madhhab of the Khawārij and the Muʿtazilah regarding the ruling on the Muslim who commits a major sin is a severe madhhab: they ruled that he leaves Islam. Then the Muʿtazilah said: he is neither a Muslim nor a disbeliever, but at المَنْزِلَةِ بَيْنَ المَنْزِلَتَيْن — the station between the two stations; and the Khawārij said: he is a disbeliever. And they agreed that if he dies in that state he abides forever in the Fire.

And the Murjiʾah and the Jahmiyyah stood opposite them and were lax in the ruling on him, going to excess in laxity: they said no act of disobedience harms alongside faith — because faith with them is the assent of the heart alone, or along with the utterance of the tongue (with a disagreement among them), and the deeds do not enter into it; so it does not increase by obedience nor decrease by disobedience.

That clause is the root of the whole dispute, and it is worth isolating: the question is whether deeds are part of what the word īmān names. Say no, and no sin can touch your faith. Say yes but make every deed essential, and one sin destroys it. Ahl al-Sunnah say the deeds are part of it and that it has degrees — which is why Lesson 28's small observation, that afḍal al-īmān implies faith admits of degrees, was load-bearing after all.

And Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah took the middle between the two groups, and said: the disobedient one does not leave faith by the mere act of disobedience, and he is under the will — if Allah wills He pardons him, and if He wills He punishes him in the Fire, but he does not abide in it forever, as the Khawārij and the Muʿtazilah say. And the acts of disobedience decrease faith, and their doer deserves to enter the Fire unless Allah pardons him; and the committer of a major sin is a fāsiq, deficient in faith — not as the Murjiʾah say, that he is complete in faith. وَاللهُ تَعَالَى أَعْلَم.

خَامِسًا · فِي أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ Fifth — Regarding the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And first the definition, which the student should learn exactly as given: الصَّحَابِيُّ: هُوَ مَنْ لَقِيَ النَّبِيَّ ﷺ مُؤْمِنًا بِهِ وَمَاتَ عَلَى ذَلِكَ — the Companion is whoever met the Prophet ﷺ as a believer in him, and died upon that.

Three conditions in one line: the meeting · the faith at the meeting · and dying upon it.

٥ · أَصْحَابُ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ
الرَّافِضَةa name taken from الرَّفْض, which is abandoning. They were named so because they said to زَيْدِ بْنِ عَلِيِّ بْنِ الحُسَيْنِ: disown the two Shaykhs, Abū Bakr and ʿUmar — and he refused, and said: «مَعَاذَ اللهِ», Allah forbid. So they abandoned him, and were called the Rafīḍah.
Their madhhab: they went to excess regarding ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ and Ahl al-Bayt and preferred them over everyone else, and set up enmity toward the rest of the Companions — especially the three caliphs Abū Bakr, ʿUmar and ʿUthmān رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمْ — and reviled them and cursed them, and may even have declared them disbelievers, or some of them.
الخَوَارِجAnd the Khawārij stood opposite them: they declared ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ a disbeliever, and with him many of the Companions, and fought them and held their blood and their wealth lawful.
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةAhl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah opposed all of them: so they took all of the Companions as allies, and did not go to excess regarding any one of them, and acknowledged the excellence of all of the Companions, and that they are the best of this Ummah after its Prophet ط. And more clarification of this is to come.

Notice how exactly the two errors mirror each other, and that both are expressed as loyalty. One party loved ʿAlī so much that it cursed his companions; the other opposed ʿAlī so far that it killed them. And the middle position is not a lukewarm affection for everyone — it is loyalty to all of them with excess toward none, which is harder than either extreme and is the only one that leaves the generation intact.

خُلَاصَةُ البَاب The Pattern of the Five

Set the five side by side and one shape repeats. In every bāb, two parties each seize one set of texts and must discard the other — the attributes against the likeness, Allah's acts against the servant's, the promise against the threat, the assent against the deeds, ʿAlī against the Companions. And in every bāb Ahl al-Sunnah hold both sets. That is what the word wasaṭ has meant the whole time: not a cautious middle between two bold positions, but the only position with nothing left over.

Check Your Understanding · MCQ أَسْئِلَة

What does الوَسَط mean here, per al-Fawzān?

Lukewarm, halfway between two positions
The balanced — and specifically العَدْلُ الخِيَار, the just and the choicest
The majority
The latest of the generations

What false premise did the Jahmiyyah and the Mushabbihah share?

That the Qurʾān is created
That affirming an attribute means likening Allah to creation — they only differed over what to do about it
That the attributes are unknowable
That reason precedes revelation

What did the Jabriyyah say about ascribing an action to the servant?

That it is real but incomplete
That it is figurative — his movements being involuntary, like those of a man with a tremor
That it is forbidden to speak of
That it applies only to the disobedient

What does وَمَا تَشَاءُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَشَاءَ اللَّهُ establish, in al-Fawzān’s reading?

That the servant has no volition at all
That the servant has a real volition which comes after Allah’s volition
That volition belongs to Allah alone and is ascribed to man figuratively
That the servant wills independently

How does al-Fawzān summarise the dispute over the promise and the threat?

Both parties erred in reading the texts
The Murjiʾah took the texts of the promise, the Waʿīdiyyah took the texts of the threat, and Ahl al-Sunnah gathered the two together
The texts of the threat abrogate those of the promise
The question is left to Allah without a position

Why were the Muʿtazilah so called?

Because they withdrew from the army of ʿAlī
Because Wāṣil ibn ʿAṭāʾ withdrew from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī’s gathering over the ruling on the major sinner, and al-Ḥasan said: he has withdrawn from us
Because they isolated themselves from the people in worship
Because they withdrew the deeds from faith

What is the root of the dispute in the chapter of the names of faith?

Whether Allah punishes the believer at all
Whether the deeds are part of what the word īmān names
Whether repentance is accepted after death
Whether the Fire has an end

What is al-Fawzān’s definition of a Companion?

Anyone who lived in the Prophet’s ﷺ lifetime
Whoever met the Prophet ﷺ as a believer in him, and died upon that
Anyone who narrated a ḥadīth from him ﷺ
Those who made hijrah with him ﷺ

What was the position of Ahl al-Sunnah toward the Companions?

They preferred Ahl al-Bayt and withheld judgement on the rest
They took all of them as allies, went to excess regarding none, and held them the best of this Ummah after its Prophet ﷺ
They declared the disputes among them to be sins
They refrained from speaking of them at all
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
المُرْجِئَةُ أَخَّرُوا الأَعْمَالَ عَنْ مُسَمَّى الإِيمَانِ. The Murjiʾah postponed the deeds out of the definition of īmān.
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ غَلَوْا فِي التَّنْزِيهِ كَالجَهْمِيَّةِ. Ahl al-Sunnah went to excess in tanzīh as the Jahmiyyah did.
مُرْتَكِبُ الكَبِيرَةِ عِنْدَ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ فَاسِقٌ نَاقِصُ الإِيمَانِ. For Ahl al-Sunnah the committer of a major sin is a fāsiq, deficient in faith.
الخَوَارِجُ كَفَّرُوا عَلِيًّا رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ. The Khawārij declared ʿAlī رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ a disbeliever.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَالمُرْجِئَةُ أَخَذُوا بِنُصُوصِ الوَعْدِ، وَالوَعِيدِيَّةُ أَخَذُوا بِنُصُوصِ الوَعِيدِ، وَأَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ جَمَعُوا بَيْنَهُمَا

Memorize it as a diagnostic tool, not only as a fact about two old sects. Nearly every deviation you will meet is a half of the revelation held tightly and the other half let go. When you hear a position that has to explain away a text, ask which half is being dropped.
