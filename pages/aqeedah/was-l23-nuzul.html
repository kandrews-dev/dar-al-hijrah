ثُبُوتُ النُّزُولِ الإِلَهِيّ

The Divine Descent to the Lowest Heaven

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the six attributes this one ḥadīth establishes.
  • Explain the construction in سَمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا and where you have met it before.
  • Give both of al-Fawzān’s grounds for rejecting “the descent of His mercy or His command”.
  • Say why النُّزُول proves العُلُوّ as well as صِفَاتِ الأَفْعَال.
١ · ثُبُوتُ النُّزُولِ الإِلَهِيِّ إِلَى سَمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا Section 1 — The Divine Descent to the Lowest Heaven

The author opens Part 2 with the ḥadīth that has drawn more reinterpretation than any other text of the attributes — and he opens with it deliberately. It is مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْه, agreed upon by al-Bukhārī and Muslim, so the condition he set in the previous lesson is met at the highest level before the argument even begins.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَمِنْ ذَلِكَ مِثْلُ قَوْلِهِ ﷺ: «يَنْزِلُ رَبُّنَا إِلَى سَمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ حِينَ يَبْقَى ثُلُثُ اللَّيْلِ الآخِرُ، فَيَقُولُ: مَنْ يَدْعُونِي فَأَسْتَجِيبَ لَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْأَلُنِي فَأُعْطِيَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْتَغْفِرُنِي فَأَغْفِرَ لَهُ؟» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

يَنْزِلُ رَبُّنَا إِلَى سَمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا كُلَّ لَيْلَةٍ حِينَ يَبْقَى ثُلُثُ اللَّيْلِ الآخِرُ، فَيَقُولُ: مَنْ يَدْعُونِي فَأَسْتَجِيبَ لَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْأَلُنِي فَأُعْطِيَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْتَغْفِرُنِي فَأَغْفِرَ لَهُ؟

"Our Lord descends to the lowest heaven every night, when the last third of the night remains, and says: who is calling upon Me, that I may answer him? Who is asking of Me, that I may give him? Who is seeking My forgiveness, that I may forgive him?"

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

يَنْزِلُ رَبُّنَا — that is: a descent befitting His majesty. We believe in it, and we do not liken it to the descent of the created thing, because He سُبْحَانَهُ is لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ — «there is nothing like Him» (al-Shūrā 42:11).

That is the whole method of Lesson 6 compressed into one sentence, and note its order: affirm, then refuse the likeness — not refuse the likeness by cancelling the affirmation. The āyah of al-Shūrā is doing the same work here that it did over all of Part 1.

إِلَى سَمَاءِ الدُّنْيَا — that is: السَّمَاءُ الدُّنْيَا — of the kind where the thing described is annexed to its own description (إِضَافَةُ المَوْصُوفِ إِلَى صِفَتِه).

You met this construction two lessons ago in رُوحُ القُدُسِ — «the Spirit of Holiness», meaning the purified Spirit. Here it means not «the heaven of the world» but the lowest heaven. Reading the grammar correctly settles which heaven is meant.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two points of vowelling, and they are not pedantry — each one carries a ruling:

الآخِرُ — بِالرَّفْعِa description of ثُلُثُ, not of اللَّيْلِ — “the last third”. And in this is a specifying of the time of the divine descent.
فَأَسْتَجِيبَ — بِالنَّصْبِmanṣūb as the answer to the question (جَوَابُ الاسْتِفْهَامِ) — “that I may answer him”. Likewise فَأُعْطِيَهُ and فَأَغْفِرَ لَهُ.

The second one matters for how the ḥadīth is heard. The naṣb makes each clause consequent on the asking: not «I answer» as a bare statement, but «so that I may answer him». The descent is described as being for the one who calls. That is why this ḥadīth lives in the chapter of duʿāʾ as much as in the chapter of attributes.

سِتُّ صِفَاتٍ فِي حَدِيثٍ وَاحِد Six Attributes in One Ḥadīth

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān does not stop at the descent. He counts what else the ḥadīth establishes, and the total is striking for a text of three lines:

النُّزُولthe descent — of the attributes of the Actions
العُلُوّthe highness — for the descending is from a height
الكَلَامthe speech — since there comes in it فَيَقُولُ
الإِجَابَةthe answering
الإِعْطَاءthe giving
المَغْفِرَةthe forgiving — and these last three are also attributes of Actions

And the second row is the one to keep. فَإِنَّ النُّزُولَ يَكُونُ مِنَ العُلُوِّ — for the descending is from a height. This is the same move as Lesson 20, where the inzāl of the Qurʾān proved the ʿuluww. Every text that says He comes down says, by saying it, that He is above. So the one who reinterprets this ḥadīth to escape affirming the descent has not escaped the ʿuluww; he has only moved the problem.

الرَّدُّ عَلَى مَنْ أَوَّلَ الحَدِيث The Refutation of Those Who Reinterpret It

The taʾwīl is well known: what descends is not Allah, but His mercy, or His command, or an angel sent by Him. al-Fawzān answers it on two grounds — one from the principles of language, and one from the ḥadīth's own words.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الأَوَّل · Because the default is the real meaning, and that nothing has been omitted (الأَصْلُ الحَقِيقَةُ وَعَدَمُ الحَذْفِ). To read “our Lord descends” as “our Lord’s mercy descends” is to insert a word that is not in the text. The one who inserts it owes a reason, and there is none here.

الثَّانِي · And because he ﷺ said: «who is calling upon Me, that I may answer him?» — so is it conceivable that His mercy, or His command, should say this statement?!

The second ground needs no technical training at all, which is why al-Fawzān puts it last and in the form of a question. Mercy does not speak. A command does not say «who is asking of Me, that I may give him». The sentence has a speaker who says Me and I four times over, and offers to answer, to give and to forgive — and none of those is something an abstraction or a messenger does on its own account.

So the taʾwīl does not merely soften the ḥadīth; it has to cut the ḥadīth in half. It takes the first clause figuratively and then has nowhere to put the second, because whatever descended is immediately quoted speaking as the Lord. A reading that cannot survive the next line of its own proof-text is not a reading.

And this is the discipline the author laid down in Lesson 4, meeting its first test in Part 2: taḥrīf was defined there as turning a text from its apparent meaning without a warrant from the Lawgiver. Here is what that looks like in practice.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَنْ يَدْعُونِي فَأَسْتَجِيبَ لَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْأَلُنِي فَأُعْطِيَهُ؟ مَنْ يَسْتَغْفِرُنِي فَأَغْفِرَ لَهُ؟

Three offers, every night, in the hour when almost nobody is awake to take them. Learn it as a creed and use it as an invitation — and notice that the clause which refutes the reinterpreters is the same clause that is calling you.
