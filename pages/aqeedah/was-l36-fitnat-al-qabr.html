فِتْنَةُ القَبْرِ وَعَذَابُهُ وَنَعِيمُه

The Examination of the Grave, Its Punishment and Its Bliss

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٢٧–١٢٩
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 127–129.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the author’s comprehensive definition of belief in the Last Day.
  • Name the two questions put to the dead, and what القَوْلُ الثَّابِت means in Ibrāhīm 14:27.
  • Distinguish the two kinds of punishment of the grave.
  • Answer the Muʿtazilah’s objection to the punishment of the grave.
القِسْمُ الرَّابِع · مَا يَدْخُلُ فِي الإِيمَانِ بِاليَوْمِ الآخِر Part Four — What Enters into Belief in the Last Day

الشَّرْح · the explanation

اليَوْمُ الآخِر — is the Day of Rising; and belief in it is one of the pillars of faith. Reason and the fiṭrah have indicated it, all the revealed Books stated it plainly, and all the prophets and messengers called to it. And it is named the Last Day because it comes after this world.

And then al-Fawzān points out something about the author's method here, which is worth noticing before the details begin: the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ mentioned here a comprehensive rule for the meaning of belief in the Last Day —

that it is belief in everything the Prophet ﷺ informed of, of what occurs after death.

So there enters into it belief in everything the texts have indicated: the state of dying, the state of the dead man in the grave, the resurrection from the graves, and what occurs after it. Then the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ pointed to some of those things.

That is a rule rather than a list, and it has a practical use. A student who memorises five events is left defenceless before a sixth he has not heard of. The author's formulation covers whatever the texts contain, including what this book does not have room to mention.

١ · مَا يَكُونُ فِي القَبْر First — What Occurs in the Grave

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمِنَ الإِيمَانِ بِاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ: الإِيمَانُ بِكُلِّ مَا أَخْبَرَ بِهِ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ مِمَّا يَكُونُ بَعْدَ المَوْتِ؛ فَيُؤْمِنُونَ بِفِتْنَةِ القَبْرِ، وَبِعَذَابِ القَبْرِ وَنَعِيمِهِ. فَأَمَّا الفِتْنَةُ فَإِنَّ النَّاسَ يُفْتَنُونَ فِي قُبُورِهِمْ، فَيُقَالُ لِلرَّجُلِ: مَنْ رَبُّكَ؟ وَمَا دِينُكَ؟ وَمَنْ نَبِيُّكَ؟ فَيُثَبِّتُ اللهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِالقَوْلِ الثَّابِتِ فِي الحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الآخِرَةِ؛ فَيَقُولُ المُؤْمِنُ: رَبِّيَ اللهُ، وَالإِسْلَامُ دِينِي، وَمُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ نَبِيِّي. وَأَمَّا المُرْتَابُ فَيَقُولُ: هَاهْ هَاهْ، لَا أَدْرِي، سَمِعْتُ النَّاسَ يَقُولُونَ شَيْئًا فَقُلْتُهُ. فَيُضْرَبُ بِمِرْزَبَّةٍ مِنْ حَدِيدٍ، فَيَصِيحُ صَيْحَةً يَسْمَعُهَا كُلُّ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا الإِنْسَانَ. وَلَوْ سَمِعَهَا الإِنْسَانُ لَصَعِقَ. ثُمَّ بَعْدَ هَذِهِ الفِتْنَةِ إِمَّا نَعِيمٌ وَإِمَّا عَذَابٌ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الأَمْرُ الأَوَّل · فِتْنَةُ القَبْر. And the word first: al-fitnah, in the language, is الامْتِحَانُ وَالاخْتِبَار — testing and examination; and what is intended by it here is the questioning of the dead by the two angels.

A useful correction of the English instinct. Fitnah here is not «tribulation» in the loose sense; it is an examination — a thing with questions and answers and a result.

And a point of precision on the matn's wording: فَيُقَالُ لِلرَّجُلِ — that is: the dead person, whether a man or a woman; and perhaps the mention of «the man» is التَّغْلِيب — by way of the masculine standing for both.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the obligation, with its basis: belief in the questioning of the two angels is obligatory, because of its being established from the Prophet ﷺ in aḥādīth whose total reaches the degree of التَّوَاتُر. That is the term defined in Lesson 31 — and note the form of the claim: not one mutawātir ḥadīth, but a body of reports whose sum reaches that degree.

And the Noble Qurʾān indicates it in His saying:

يُثَبِّتُ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِالْقَوْلِ الثَّابِتِ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ وَيُضِلُّ اللَّهُ الظَّالِمِينَ وَيَفْعَلُ اللَّهُ مَا يَشَاءُ

"Allah makes firm those who believe with the firm word, in the life of this world and in the Hereafter; and Allah lets the wrongdoers go astray — and Allah does what He wills."

Sūrat Ibrāhīm 14:27 · سُورَةُ إِبْرَاهِيم

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the link from the āyah to the grave is not an inference; it is the Prophet's ﷺ own: the two Shaykhs reported, from the ḥadīth of البَرَاءِ بْنِ عَازِبٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا, that the Prophet ﷺ said concerning His saying يُثَبِّتُ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِالْقَوْلِ الثَّابِتِ: «it was sent down concerning the punishment of the grave».

And Muslim added: «so it is said to him: who is your Lord? And he says: my Lord is Allah, and my prophet is Muḥammad ﷺ» — and that is His saying عَزَّ وَجَلَّ: يُثَبِّتُ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِالْقَوْلِ الثَّابِتِ.»

القَوْلُ الثَّابِت — is the word of tawḥīd, which became firm in the heart of the believer by proof and evidence. And then al-Fawzān divides the āyah's two places, which is the finest thing in the section:

فِي الحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَاthat they hold fast to it, even if there reaches them in its path what reaches them of harm and torture
وَفِي الآخِرَةِthat they are granted success in the answer at the questioning of the two angels

One āyah, two makings-firm, and the second is earned by the first. The man who held the word fast under pressure in this life is the man who finds it on his tongue in the grave. Which is why the answer cannot be rehearsed: it is not a password but a report of what a life was actually built on.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And وَأَمَّا المُرْتَاب — that is: the doubter; فَيَقُولُ, when asked — هَاهْ هَاهْ, a word of hesitation and of pain — لَا أَدْرِي، سَمِعْتُ النَّاسَ يَقُولُونَ شَيْئًا فَقُلْتُهُ; because he is not a believer in what the Prophet ﷺ came with. And of such Allah said وَيُضِلُّ اللَّهُ الظَّالِمِينَ — «and Allah lets the wrongdoers go astray».

Read what he actually confesses, because it is the most chilling line in the matn and it is not a denial. «I heard the people saying something, so I said it.» He had the right words. He simply never had them as his own — and the examination is of the second thing, not the first.

فَيُضْرَبُ بِمِرْزَبَّةٍ مِنْ حَدِيدٍ — and it is the great hammer — فَيَصِيحُ صَيْحَةً يَسْمَعُهَا كُلُّ شَيْءٍ إِلَّا الإِنْسَانَ. Then he explained the wisdom in man's not hearing it, with his saying وَلَوْ سَمِعَهَا الإِنْسَانُ لَصَعِقَ — that is: he would fall down dead, or lose consciousness.

And al-Fawzān adds a second wisdom of his own, which answers a question a bereaved person asks: and of the wisdom of Allah also is that what happens to the dead in his grave is not perceived by those who loved him — because Allah تَعَالَى made it of the unseen; and had He made it apparent, the intended wisdom would have been lost, which is belief in the unseen.

الأَمْرُ الثَّانِي · النَّعِيمُ أَوِ العَذَاب Second — the Bliss, or the Punishment

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author's clause ثُمَّ بَعْدَ هَذِهِ الفِتْنَةِ إِمَّا نَعِيمٌ وَإِمَّا عَذَابٌ — in this is the affirming of the punishment of the grave or its bliss.

And the madhhab of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah is that the dead, when he dies, is in bliss or in punishment; and that this occurs to his soul AND his body, as the aḥādīth from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ have come mutawātir. So belief in it is obligatory — and its manner and description are not spoken of, because that is not grasped by the intellects, being of the matters of the Hereafter; and the matters of the Hereafter none knows but Allah and those whom Allah has acquainted with something of them — and they are the messengers صَلَوَاتُ اللهِ وَسَلَامُهُ عَلَيْهِمْ.

Affirm, and do not ask how. The same discipline as the attributes, applied now to an event rather than to a description — and for the same reason: no one has the access that answering «how» would require.

And the two kinds, which resolve the obvious question of whether a sinning believer is punished forever in the grave:

النَّوْعُ الأَوَّلa lasting punishment, and it is the punishment of the disbeliever — as He said: النَّارُ يُعْرَضُونَ عَلَيْهَا غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا (Ghāfir 40:46)
النَّوْعُ الثَّانِيfor a term, then it is cut off — the punishment of some of the disobedient among the believers: he is punished to the measure of his crime, then it is lightened for him. And the punishment may be cut off from him by reason of a supplication, or a charity, or a seeking of forgiveness

That last clause is the one to sit with. A duʿāʾ made for a dead person can end a punishment that had begun. It is the strongest argument in the whole section for why this doctrine is not morbid: it tells the living that there is still something they can do.

النَّارُ يُعْرَضُونَ عَلَيْهَا غُدُوًّا وَعَشِيًّا وَيَوْمَ تَقُومُ السَّاعَةُ أَدْخِلُوا آلَ فِرْعَوْنَ أَشَدَّ الْعَذَابِ

"The Fire — they are exposed to it morning and evening; and on the Day the Hour comes: enter the family of Pharaoh into the severest punishment."

Sūrat Ghāfir 40:46 · سُورَةُ غَافِر

شُبْهَةُ المُعْتَزِلَةِ وَالجَوَابُ عَنْهَا The Muʿtazilah’s Objection, and the Answer to It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Muʿtazilah denied the punishment of the grave; and their objection in that is: that they do not perceive it, and do not see the dead being punished or being questioned.

The answer: that our not perceiving a thing and not seeing it does not indicate its non-existence and its not occurring. How many things are there that we do not see, and they exist — and of that is the punishment of the grave, or its bliss.

And that Allah تَعَالَى made the matter of the Hereafter, and what is connected to it, unseen, and veiled it from the grasp of the intellects in this abode — so that those who believe in the unseen might be distinguished from others. And the matters of the Hereafter are not measured by the matters of this world. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

Notice that the answer has two layers, and the second is the stronger. The first is merely logical: absence of perception is not evidence of absence. The second says the concealment is deliberate and is the point — the unseen is hidden in order to sort those who will believe a report from those who will only believe their eyes. So the Muʿtazilī demand to see it before believing it is not an objection to the doctrine; it is the very test the doctrine describes.

And note the family resemblance to Lesson 21. There, the deniers of the ruʾyah reasoned from what seeing is like here; here, the deniers of the punishment reason from what death looks like here. Both measure the Hereafter by this world, and both are answered by refusing the measurement.

يُثَبِّتُ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا بِالْقَوْلِ الثَّابِتِ فِي الْحَيَاةِ الدُّنْيَا وَفِي الْآخِرَةِ

The Prophet ﷺ said this āyah was sent down about the punishment of the grave. Learn it with al-Fawzān's two places attached: the firmness you are given there is the firmness you held to here. The answer in the grave is not recalled — it is reported.
