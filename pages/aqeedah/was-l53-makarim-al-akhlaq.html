مَكَارِمُ الأَخْلَاقِ وَمَحَاسِنُ الأَعْمَال

Noble Character and Beautiful Deeds

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٩٩–٢٠١
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 199–201.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Distinguish الخُلْق from الخَلْق, and say which one the ḥadīth is about.
  • Give the two things al-Fawzān says the ḥadīth of the best character establishes about īmān itself.
  • State the three magnanimities the matn commends, and what each one inverts.
  • Name the eight classes whose rights the matn commands, and define اليَتِيم, المِسْكِين and ابْنُ السَّبِيل.
  • Explain al-Fawzān’s argument for why الاسْتِطَالَة is forbidden whether it is with a right or without one.
  • Say what السَّفْسَاف originally means, and why the image is apt.
الدَّعْوَةُ إِلَى مَكَارِمِ الأَخْلَاقِ وَمَحَاسِنِ الأَعْمَال The Call to Noble Character and Beautiful Deeds

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَيَدْعُونَ إِلَى مَكَارِمِ الأَخْلَاقِ وَمَحَاسِنِ الأَعْمَالِ، وَيَعْتَقِدُونَ مَعْنَى قَوْلِهِ ﷺ: «أَكْمَلُ المُؤْمِنِينَ إِيمَانًا أَحْسَنُهُمْ خُلُقًا»؛ وَيَنْدُبُونَ إِلَى أَنْ تَصِلَ مَنْ قَطَعَكَ، وَتُعْطِيَ مَنْ حَرَمَكَ، وَتَعْفُوَ عَمَّنْ ظَلَمَكَ؛ وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِبِرِّ الوَالِدَيْنِ، وَصِلَةِ الأَرْحَامِ، وَحُسْنِ الجِوَارِ، وَالإِحْسَانِ إِلَى اليَتَامَى وَالمَسَاكِينِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ، وَالرِّفْقِ بِالمَمْلُوكِ؛ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الفَخْرِ وَالخُيَلَاءِ وَالبَغْيِ وَالاسْتِطَالَةِ عَلَى الخَلْقِ بِحَقٍّ أَوْ بِغَيْرِ حَقٍّ. وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِمَعَالِي الأَخْلَاقِ، وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنْ سَفْسَافِهَا. وَكُلُّ مَا يَقُولُونَهُ وَيَفْعَلُونَهُ مِنْ هَذَا وَغَيْرِهِ، فَإِنَّمَا هُمْ فِيهِ مُتَّبِعُونَ لِلْكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ahl al-Sunnah give attention to character, so they adorn themselves with excellent character and make others desire it. So they يَدْعُونَ إِلَى مَكَارِمِ الأَخْلَاق — that is: the best of them.

Then a distinction that rests on one vowel, and which the matn’s own wording depends on later in this same passage:

الخُلُق
ḍammah on the khāʾ and the lām		plural أَخْلَاق — and it is الصُّورَةُ البَاطِنَة, the inward form; and it is the religion, the disposition and the nature
الخَلْق
fatḥah on the khāʾ, sukūn on the lām		it is الصُّورَةُ الظَّاهِرَة, the outward form

So the same three letters name a man’s face or a man’s soul, and only the vowelling tells you which. Which is why the matn can say, a few lines later, «overbearing toward الخَلْق» using the other word — and why a student who reads unvowelled Arabic has to decide, at each occurrence, which of the two is meant.

And they call to مَحَاسِنِ الأَعْمَال — such as generosity, courage, truthfulness and trustworthiness.

أَكْمَلُ المُؤْمِنِينَ إِيمَانًا أَحْسَنُهُمْ خُلُقًا

"The most complete of the believers in īmān are the best of them in character."

رَوَاهُ أَحْمَدُ وَالتِّرْمِذِيُّ — وَقَالَ التِّرْمِذِيُّ: حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And وَيَعْتَقِدُونَ مَعْنَى قَوْلِهِ ﷺ — that is: they believe in it and act by what it requires. And his words أَحْسَنُهُمْ خُلُقًا mean: the most gentle of them, the most courteous of them, and the most beautiful of them.

So in the ḥadīth is an urging toward being characterized by the best of character; and in it is that deeds enter into the name of īmān, and that īmān admits of degrees.

That second sentence is why this ḥadīth is in a book of creed at all, and it quietly closes a question that Part 6 opened. Lesson 45 defined īmān as قَوْلٌ بِاللِّسَانِ وَاعْتِقَادٌ بِالقَلْبِ وَعَمَلٌ بِالجَوَارِح, and set Ahl al-Sunnah against those who exclude deeds from it or deny that it increases and decreases.

This ḥadīth proves both points at once, and proves them from a ḥadīth about manners. «The most complete of the believers in īmān» presupposes that īmān has degrees of completeness; and the thing that raises a man in those degrees here is character — which is a deed of the limbs and the tongue. So the doctrinal claim of Part 6 and the ethical claim of Part 7 turn out to be one claim. This is why the two sections sit together at the close of the book.

المَكَارِمُ الثَّلَاث The Three Magnanimities

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And they يَنْدُبُونَ — that is, they call to:

أَنْ تَصِلَ مَنْ قَطَعَكthat is: you do good to the one who did evil to you
وَتُعْطِيَ مَنْ حَرَمَكthat is: you extend the gift — and it is the freely given and the present and the like — to the one who withheld that from you; because that is of excellence (الإِحْسَان)
وَتَعْفُوَ عَمَّنْ ظَلَمَكthat is: you forgive the one who transgressed against you in property, or blood, or honour — because that is of what draws love and earns reward and recompense

Set the three side by side and the pattern appears: each one takes the natural response and inverts it. Severing is answered with joining, withholding with giving, wronging with pardon. And al-Fawzān does not justify them by sentiment — he gives a reason for each: the second is iḥsān, and the third draws love and earns reward. The three magnanimities are profitable as well as noble, which is a thing the Sharīʿah is content to say out loud.

الحُقُوقُ المَأْمُورُ بِهَا The Rights That Are Commanded

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And they command — that is, Ahl al-Sunnah — what Allah commanded of giving those who have rights their rights:

بِرُّ الوَالِدَيْنthat is: obeying them in other than disobedience, and doing good to them by word and by deed
صِلَةُ الأَرْحَامdoing good to the near kin; and الأَرْحَام is the plural of رَحِم, and it is whoever a kinship gathers you with
حُسْنُ الجِوَارdoing good to whoever dwells beside you — by extending what is good and withholding harm
اليَتَامَىplural of يَتِيم; لُغَةً المُنْفَرِد, the solitary one; شَرْعًا one whose father died before he reached puberty — and doing good to them is by tending their circumstances and their property, and by compassion toward them
المَسَاكِينplural of مِسْكِين, and he is the needy one whom need and poverty have made still; and doing good to them is by giving them charity and being gentle with them
ابْنُ السَّبِيلand he is the traveller cut off, whose provision has run out, or been lost, or been stolen; and it is said: he is the guest
الرِّفْقُ بِالمَمْلُوكand he is the owned servant; and the owned among the animals enters into it. And الرِّفْق is the opposite of violence, and it is softness of side

مِسْكِين is from السُّكُون — stillness. al-Fawzān’s gloss is not a flourish: the word names a man whom need has made motionless. Poverty has taken away his capacity to move, to act, to change his circumstance — and that is what the Arabic is describing when it calls him a miskīn.

Which tells you what doing good to him has to consist of. Charity and gentleness, in that order — the first because he cannot move himself, the second because a man held still by need is already humiliated and does not need a benefactor’s roughness on top of it.

وَاعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَلَا تُشْرِكُوا بِهِ شَيْئًا وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا وَبِذِي الْقُرْبَى وَالْيَتَامَى وَالْمَسَاكِينِ وَالْجَارِ ذِي الْقُرْبَى وَالْجَارِ الْجُنُبِ وَالصَّاحِبِ بِالْجَنْبِ وَابْنِ السَّبِيلِ وَمَا مَلَكَتْ أَيْمَانُكُمْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ مَنْ كَانَ مُخْتَالًا فَخُورًا

"And worship Allah and associate nothing with Him; and to parents, excellence; and to the near of kin, and the orphans, and the needy, and the near neighbour, and the distant neighbour, and the companion at your side, and the wayfarer, and those your right hands possess. Indeed Allah does not love one who is arrogant and boastful."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:36 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

Compare that āyah with the matn’s list and the matn turns out to be a paraphrase of it. Parents, kin, neighbours, orphans, the needy, the wayfarer, those the right hands possess — in the same order. And the āyah does two further things the matn then copies. It opens with worship Allah and associate nothing with Him, putting the whole list of manners downstream of tawḥīd; and it closes by forbidding exactly what the matn forbids next — one who is arrogant and boastful. So the structure of this closing section is the structure of a single āyah of al-Nisāʾ.

المَنْهِيُّ عَنْه What Is Forbidden

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الفَخْرand it is vaunting with merits and distinctions, of standing and of lineage
الخُيَلَاءwith a ḍammah on the khāʾ: arrogance and self-admiration
البَغْيand it is aggression against people
الاسْتِطَالَةُ عَلَى الخَلْقthat is: raising oneself above them, and holding them in contempt, and disparaging them

Then the clause that looks strange until you see the argument: «بِحَقٍّ أَوْ بِغَيْرِ حَقّ» — whether with a right or without one. How can being overbearing «with a right» be forbidden?

— because the one who is overbearing, if he is overbearing WITH a right, then he has boasted (فَقَدِ افْتَخَرَ); and if he is overbearing WITHOUT a right, then he has transgressed (فَقَدْ بَغَى) — and neither this nor that is permitted.

That is a small piece of reasoning worth keeping, because of its shape. The matn has just forbidden فَخْر and بَغْي by name; the overbearing man must fall under one or the other, and so the prohibition is already complete before the exception can be raised. Having a right on your side changes what you are owed; it does not license contempt for the person who owes it. Which is the hardest case in the section, and the reason it is given.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And وَيَأْمُرُونَ بِمَعَالِي الأَخْلَاق — that is: Ahl al-Sunnah command the lofty character, and it is the excellent character. وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنْ سَفْسَافِهَا — that is: its base and contemptible kind.

And السَّفْسَاف is the contemptible and base in anything; and it is the opposite of المَعَالِي and المَكَارِم. And its origin is: what flies up of the dust of flour when it is sifted, and of the earth when it is stirred up.

That etymology is the best image in the section, so do not read past it. Sifting flour or sweeping a floor throws up a fine dust — the lightest part of the material, the part that rises because it is worth nothing and is thrown away. So سَفْسَافُ الأَخْلَاق is the chaff of character: the petty, weightless conduct that a man gives off as a by-product and that settles on everything around him. And the opposite is مَعَالِي — the high things. The Arabic sets a lightness that merely floats against a height that is climbed.

الخِتَامُ · وَإِنَّمَا هُمْ فِيهِ مُتَّبِعُون The Close — They Are Only Following in It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: everything Ahl al-Sunnah say and do, and command and forbid, of what has preceded being mentioned in this treatise and of what has not been mentioned — they have derived it from the Book of their Lord and the Sunnah of their Prophet. They did not innovate it from themselves, and they did not imitate anyone else in it.

That sentence is the author’s last word on the whole treatise, and it is a claim of a particular kind. Having spent the book establishing the attributes of Allah, the Last Day, the Decree, the Companions and now the manners of this group, he ends by saying that none of it is his. «They did not innovate it from themselves, and they did not imitate anyone else in it» — the two denials together rule out both originality and partisanship.

So the Wāsiṭiyyah closes by withdrawing any authority of its own. It is not offered as Ibn Taymiyyah’s creed, and not as one school’s creed against another’s — which is also why, as Lesson 51 noted, it carries only what is agreed and leaves the disputed to the books of uṣūl. And the ḥadīths in this are many; of them is what the Shaykh mentioned.

Check Your Understanding · MCQ أَسْئِلَة

What is the difference between الخُلُق and الخَلْق?

One is singular and the other plural
الخُلُق (ḍammah) is الصُّورَةُ البَاطِنَة, the inward form — the disposition and nature; الخَلْق (fatḥah, sukūn) is الصُّورَةُ الظَّاهِرَة, the outward form
One refers to Allah’s creating and the other to His decreeing
They are two dialectal forms of one word

What two things does al-Fawzān say the ḥadīth establishes about īmān?

That it is in the heart alone, and that it cannot be lost
That deeds enter into the name of īmān, and that īmān admits of degrees
That it is identical with Islam, and that it is one indivisible thing
That it is complete at the moment of testimony

Why does that matter for Part 6 of this creed?

Because it names the sects
Because Lesson 45 defined īmān as statement, belief and deed against those who exclude deeds or deny that it increases — and this ḥadīth proves both points from a ḥadīth about manners
Because it settles the ruling on the one who commits a major sin
Because it establishes the ranks of the Companions

What pattern do the three magnanimities share?

Each concerns property
Each inverts the natural response — severing answered with joining, withholding with giving, wronging with pardon
Each is obligatory rather than recommended
Each applies only to relatives

What reason does the matn give for pardoning the one who wronged you?

That vengeance is forbidden outright
That it is of what draws love and earns reward and recompense
That the wrongdoer may repent
That the ruler alone may exact punishment

What does بِرُّ الوَالِدَيْن consist of?

Financial support alone
Obeying them in other than disobedience, and doing good to them by word and by deed
Obeying them in everything without exception
Living with them until their death

What is a يَتِيم in the Sharīʿah, as against in the language?

In the language, one who lost both parents; in the Sharīʿah, one who lost his mother
In the language, المُنْفَرِد, the solitary; in the Sharīʿah, one whose father died before he reached puberty
In the language, a child; in the Sharīʿah, anyone in need
The two meanings are identical

What does the word مِسْكِين describe about the man?

That he begs from people
That need and poverty have made him STILL — robbed of the capacity to move or change his circumstance
That he owns no dwelling
That he is a traveller

Who is ابْنُ السَّبِيل?

Anyone born on a journey
The traveller cut off, whose provision has run out or been lost or stolen — and it is said: the guest
A beggar in the marketplace
One who has no family

Who falls under الرِّفْقُ بِالمَمْلُوك?

Owned servants only
The owned servant — and owned animals enter into it as well
Employees and labourers
Prisoners of war

What does al-Nisāʾ 4:36 do that the matn then copies?

It lists the five pillars
It puts the whole list of manners downstream of «worship Allah and associate nothing with Him», and closes by forbidding the arrogant and boastful — exactly what the matn forbids next
It abrogates an earlier command about neighbours
It restricts the rights to the believers among them

How is الاسْتِطَالَة forbidden even «with a right»?

Because a right must be waived to be valid
Because the overbearing man with a right has boasted (فَخْر), and without a right has transgressed (بَغْي) — and both were already forbidden by name
Because rights belong to the ruler to enforce
Because claiming a right publicly is prohibited

What does السَّفْسَاف originally mean?

A worn-out garment
The dust that flies up when flour is sifted or earth is stirred — the lightest part, which rises because it is worthless
A broken vessel
Chatter and idle speech

What is the author’s last word on everything in the treatise?

That it is the creed of the Ḥanbalī school
That they derived all of it from the Book and the Sunnah — they did not innovate it from themselves, and did not imitate anyone else in it
That it is his own ijtihād and may be differed with
That it is established by the consensus of the four imāms
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
الخُلُقُ بِضَمِّ الخَاءِ هُوَ الصُّورَةُ البَاطِنَةُ. الخُلُق with a ḍammah is the inward form.
الحَدِيثُ يَدُلُّ عَلَى أَنَّ الأَعْمَالَ تَدْخُلُ فِي مُسَمَّى الإِيمَانِ وَأَنَّ الإِيمَانَ يَتَفَاضَلُ. The ḥadīth indicates that deeds enter into the name of īmān and that īmān admits of degrees.
طَاعَةُ الوَالِدَيْنِ وَاجِبَةٌ فِي المَعْصِيَةِ أَيْضًا. Obeying parents is obligatory in disobedience as well.
الاسْتِطَالَةُ عَلَى الخَلْقِ بِحَقٍّ جَائِزَةٌ. Being overbearing toward people with a right is permitted.
الرِّفْقُ مَأْمُورٌ بِهِ حَتَّى فِي البَهَائِمِ المَمْلُوكَةِ. Gentleness is commanded even regarding owned animals.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَكْمَلُ المُؤْمِنِينَ إِيمَانًا أَحْسَنُهُمْ خُلُقًا

This is why a book about the attributes of Allah ends with manners. «The most complete in īmān» presupposes that īmān has degrees; and the thing that raises a man through them, in this ḥadīth, is his character. So the doctrinal claim of Part 6 — that deeds are part of īmān and that īmān increases — and the ethical claim of Part 7 are not two claims. A creed is measured by what it produces in a man.
