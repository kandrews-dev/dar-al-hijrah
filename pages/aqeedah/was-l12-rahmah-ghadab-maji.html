الرَّحْمَةُ وَالغَضَبُ وَالمَجِيءُ لِفَصْلِ القَضَاء

Mercy, Anger, and His Coming to Judge

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give Ibn al-Qayyim on the difference between الرَّحْمَن and الرَّحِيم.
  • Explain كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ and why that writing is not an obligation imposed by anyone.
  • Name the four attributes of §8 and give an āyah for each.
  • State what the deniers do with وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَ, and why it is taḥrīf.
٧ · الرَّحْمَةُ وَالمَغْفِرَة Section 7 — Mercy and Forgiveness

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ﴾، ﴿وَكَانَ بِالْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا﴾، ﴿وَرَحْمَتِي وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ﴾، ﴿كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ﴾، ﴿وَهُوَ الْغَفُورُ الرَّحِيمُ﴾، ﴿فَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ حَافِظًا وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here Ibn al-Qayyim makes a grammatical observation that settles the difference between the two names — and he proves it from the Qurʾān's own usage:

الرَّحْمَٰن indicates the attribute standing with Him.

الرَّحِيم indicates its attachment to the one shown mercy.

The proof: Allah said وَكَانَ بِالمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا — and never once said رَحْمَٰنًا بِهِم. The first is for the description; the second is for the act.

That is the kind of argument that cannot be waved away. The two names are not stylistic variants: one names the mercy as His own attribute, the other names it reaching you.

وَكَانَ بِالْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا

"And He is ever Merciful to the believers."

Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:43 · سورة الأحزاب

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And al-Fawzān spells out what that mercy to the believers consists of, in both worlds. In this world: He guided them to the truth that others remained ignorant of, and showed them the road that others strayed from. In the Hereafter: He secures them from the Great Terror and admits them to Paradise.

Note that the first mercy named is not ease or wealth. It is guidance — and it is named as mercy precisely by contrast with those who did not receive it.

كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ

"Your Lord has written mercy upon Himself."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:54 · سورة الأنعام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He made it obligatory upon His own noble Self, as a favour from Him and an act of excellence. And al-Fawzān guards the point from misunderstanding: this writing is universal and decreed — no one imposed it upon Him.

The distinction matters. A creditor can oblige a debtor; nobody can oblige Allah. When He binds Himself, the binding is itself a gift.

فَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ حَافِظًا وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ

"But Allah is the best guardian, and He is the most merciful of the merciful."

Sūrat Yūsuf 12:64 · سورة يوسف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān gives the setting, which makes the words land differently. These are Yaʿqūb عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ, when his sons asked him to send their brother with them and pledged to guard him. He answered that Allah's guarding of him is better than your guarding — تَفْوِيضٌ, a handing-over by Yaʿqūb to Allah of his son's keeping.

A father who had already lost one son, told by the same sons that they would keep this one safe. He does not refuse them; he transfers the trust upward. And among Allah's names is الحَفِيظ: the One who guards His servants with His general guarding from destruction and ruin, and who guards their deeds for them.

٨ · الرِّضَا وَالغَضَبُ وَالسَّخَطُ وَالكَرَاهِيَة Section 8 — Pleasure, Anger, Displeasure and Dislike

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ﴾، ﴿وَغَضِبَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَلَعَنَهُ﴾، ﴿ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمُ اتَّبَعُوا مَا أَسْخَطَ اللَّهَ وَكَرِهُوا رِضْوَانَهُ﴾، ﴿فَلَمَّا آسَفُونَا انْتَقَمْنَا مِنْهُمْ﴾، ﴿وَلَٰكِنْ كَرِهَ اللَّهُ انْبِعَاثَهُمْ فَثَبَّطَهُمْ﴾.

رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ وَرَضُوا عَنْهُ

"Allah is pleased with them, and they are pleased with Him."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:119 · سورة المائدة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He is pleased with them for the obedience they did sincerely for Him; and they are pleased with Him for what He recompensed them with of bliss. And al-Fawzān adds: His pleasure is the highest degree of the bliss itself — وَرِضْوَانٌ مِنَ اللَّهِ أَكْبَرُ, “and the pleasure of Allah is greater” (al-Tawbah 9:72).

And their pleasure with Him he describes beautifully: each of them is content with his own station, until he supposes that no one was given anything better than what he was given. In a place where ranks differ, nobody looks sideways.

وَمَنْ يَقْتُلْ مُؤْمِنًا مُتَعَمِّدًا فَجَزَاؤُهُ جَهَنَّمُ خَالِدًا فِيهَا وَغَضِبَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَلَعَنَهُ

"And whoever kills a believer intentionally — his recompense is Hell, abiding therein; and Allah is angry with him and has cursed him."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:93 · سورة النساء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān notes the two guards built into the wording: by saying مُؤْمِنًا the āyah excludes the killing of a disbeliever, and by saying مُتَعَمِّدًا it excludes killing by mistake.

فَلَمَّا آسَفُونَا انْتَقَمْنَا مِنْهُمْ

"So when they angered Us, We took retribution from them."

Sūrat al-Zukhruf 43:55 · سورة الزخرف

آسَفُونَا means they angered Us; انْتَقَمْنَا means We punished them. And al-Fawzān marks the force of the word chosen: الانْتِقَام is the most severe of punishment.

وَلَٰكِنْ كَرِهَ اللَّهُ انْبِعَاثَهُمْ فَثَبَّطَهُمْ

"But Allah disliked their going forth, so He held them back."

Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:46 · سورة التوبة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This āyah is worth pausing on, because it is the two wills of the last lesson caught in a single verse. Allah disliked that these men go out to the campaign, and so held them back — hindering them by decree and destiny.

And yet, al-Fawzān points out: He had commanded them to the campaign legislatively, and had given them the physical ability for it — but He did not aid them in it, for a wisdom He knows. Commanded by the legislated will; prevented by the universal. Both at once, in one event.

كَبُرَ مَقْتًا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ أَنْ تَقُولُوا مَا لَا تَفْعَلُونَ

"It is greatly hateful to Allah that you say what you do not do."

Sūrat al-Ṣaff 61:3 · سورة الصف

المَقْت is hatred, and the āyah says this grew great in it. al-Fawzān records the occasion: before fighting was prescribed, some of the believers used to say we wish Allah would tell us which deeds He loves most, so that we might do them — and then, when told, fell short. The warning is aimed at the enthusiasm that outruns the man.

٩ · المَجِيءُ لِفَصْلِ القَضَاء Section 9 — His Coming to Judge

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿هَلْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ فِي ظُلَلٍ مِنَ الْغَمَامِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ وَقُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿كَلَّا إِذَا دُكَّتِ الْأَرْضُ دَكًّا دَكًّا ۝ وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَ وَالْمَلَكُ صَفًّا صَفًّا﴾.

هَلْ يَنْظُرُونَ إِلَّا أَنْ يَأْتِيَهُمُ اللَّهُ فِي ظُلَلٍ مِنَ الْغَمَامِ وَالْمَلَائِكَةُ وَقُضِيَ الْأَمْرُ

"Are they waiting for anything but that Allah should come to them in canopies of cloud, with the angels, and the matter be decided?"

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:210 · سورة البقرة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

يَنْظُرُونَ here means are they waiting, and al-Fawzān reads the āyah as a threat to the disbelievers who refuse to enter into submission and follow the footsteps of the devil. الظُّلَل is the plural of ظُلَّة — whatever shades you.

كَلَّا إِذَا دُكَّتِ الْأَرْضُ دَكًّا دَكًّا وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَ وَالْمَلَكُ صَفًّا صَفًّا

"No! When the earth is pounded to dust, and your Lord comes, and the angels, rank upon rank…"

Sūrat al-Fajr 89:21–22 · سورة الفجر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

دُكَّتِ — shaken and moved, shaking after shaking, until every building on it is brought down and it returns to scattered dust. وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَ — by His Essence, to decide the judgement between His servants. And صَفًّا صَفًّا: the angels drawn up rank after rank, having encircled the jinn and mankind — the people of each heaven forming one rank, so that with the earth and those on it they come to seven ranks.

The shāhid, and the author's whole purpose in the section: the āyāt affirm coming and arrival for Allah on the Day of Resurrection, by His Essence, in the manner that befits His majesty, to decide the judgement. These are among صِفَاتِهِ الفِعْلِيَّة — His attributes of action — and they must be affirmed upon their reality.

And then the warning, which closes the circle back to Lesson 4: it is not permitted to interpret them away as the coming of His command, as the deniers of the attributes do, saying that وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَ means “His command came.” وَهَذَا مِنْ تَحْرِيفِ آيَاتِ اللهِ — and this is from the distortion of the āyāt of Allah.

You met that exact example in Lesson 4 as al-Fawzān's second illustration of تَحْرِيفِ اللَّفْظِ — a word added to the revealed wording. Here it returns, in its place, as a live error being answered rather than a textbook case.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

كَتَبَ رَبُّكُمْ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ الرَّحْمَةَ

He bound Himself, and no one bound Him. Hold that beside the fourth section of this lesson — the anger, the displeasure, the retribution — and you will have both halves of how Allah deals with His servants, neither one cancelling the other.

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
