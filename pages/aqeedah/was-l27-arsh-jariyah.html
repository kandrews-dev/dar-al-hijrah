العَرْشُ فَوْقَ المَخْلُوقَاتِ · وَحَدِيثُ الجَارِيَة

The Throne above Creation, and the Ḥadīth of the Slave-Girl

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say what فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ refers to in the ḥadīth of the Throne.
  • State the three things al-Fawzān derives from that ḥadīth.
  • Give the ruling this lesson establishes about asking أَيْنَ اللهُ؟.
  • Name the two rulings al-Fawzān draws from أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ.
«وَالعَرْشُ فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ، وَاللهُ فَوْقَ العَرْش» "And the Throne Is above That, and Allah Is above the Throne"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «وَالعَرْشُ فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ، وَاللهُ فَوْقَ العَرْشِ، وَهُوَ يَعْلَمُ مَا أَنْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ» حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ رَوَاهُ أَبُو دَاوُدَ وَغَيْرُهُ.

وَالعَرْشُ فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ، وَاللهُ فَوْقَ العَرْشِ، وَهُوَ يَعْلَمُ مَا أَنْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ

"And the Throne is above that, and Allah is above the Throne — and He knows what state you are in."

حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ — graded ḥasan by the author; reported by Abū Dāwūd and others

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَالعَرْشُ فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ — al-Fawzān refers back: the explanation of العَرْش came before, at the āyāt of the istiwāʾ. That was Lesson 16: in the language it is the couch of the king; and what is intended here, as the totality of the texts indicates, is a couch having legs, borne by the angels, like a dome over the world, and it is the roof of the created things.

And فَوْقَ ذَلِكَ — that is: above the created things which the Messenger ﷺ set out for his Companions in the ḥadīth in which he mentioned the distance between the heaven and the earth, and what is between each heaven and the next, and the thickness of each heaven, and the sea which is above the seventh heaven and what is between its lowest part and its highest, and what is above that sea of the eight mighty mountain-goats; and then above that is the Throne.

The point of reciting that ladder is not the astronomy. It is that «above» in this ḥadīth is the last term of a series which the Prophet ﷺ had just laid out, rung by rung, for the people listening. Whatever is said about the particulars of each rung, the sentence is plainly locating the Throne at the top of everything created, and Allah above the Throne.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَاللهُ فَوْقَ العَرْشِ — that is: established over it with an istiwāʾ befitting His majesty. The same formula as Lesson 16, and the same restraint: the fact affirmed, the manner not described.

وَهُوَ يَعْلَمُ مَا أَنْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ — by His encompassing knowledge, from which nothing is hidden.

And that final clause is doing something the student should notice, because it is the whole answer to an objection that has not yet been raised. Someone might hear «Allah is above the Throne» and conclude that He is therefore far from us. The ḥadīth closes the door in its own last four words: He is above the Throne and He knows what state you are in. The height and the knowledge arrive in one breath — which is exactly the reconciliation Lesson 17 made for the maʿiyyah, and §7 will make again.

And the shāhid — three things:

— the affirming of Allah’s highness over His Throne; and that His Throne is above all of the created things; and that Allah’s knowledge encompasses the deeds of the servants, nothing of which is hidden from Him.

حَدِيثُ الجَارِيَة · «أَيْنَ اللهُ؟» The Ḥadīth of the Slave-Girl — "Where Is Allah?"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ لِلْجَارِيَةِ: «أَيْنَ اللهُ؟» قَالَتْ: فِي السَّمَاءِ. قَالَ: «مَنْ أَنَا؟» قَالَتْ: أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللهِ. قَالَ: «أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ» رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ.

al-Fawzān gives the story in full, and it is worth having, because the creedal point is carried by an everyday incident. She was the slave-girl of Muʿāwiyah ibn al-Ḥakam. Her master Muʿāwiyah became angry with her and struck her on the face. Then he regretted it, and informed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and said: shall I not free her? So the Prophet ﷺ said: «yes — bring her to me.» So he brought her to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

أَيْنَ اللهُ؟ — قَالَتْ: فِي السَّمَاءِ. قَالَ: مَنْ أَنَا؟ — قَالَتْ: أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللهِ. قَالَ: أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ

"Where is Allah? She said: above the heaven. He said: who am I? She said: you are the Messenger of Allah. He said: free her, for she is a believer."

رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَيْنَ اللهُ؟ — and al-Fawzān draws the ruling straight out of the fact that the Prophet ﷺ asked it: in it is a proof for the permissibility of asking about Allah with أَيْنَ.

That is a larger point than it looks. The deniers of the ʿuluww hold that where? is an improper question about Allah — that it presupposes a place and so presupposes a body. But the Prophet ﷺ asked it. He asked it of an uneducated slave-girl, as the test of whether she was a believer, and he accepted her one-word answer. A question the Messenger of Allah ﷺ put to a servant cannot be a question that misrepresents Allah.

قَالَتْ: فِي السَّمَاءِ — that is: Allah سُبْحَانَهُ is above the heaven; and the explanation of this phrase came before — the previous lesson, where either reading of the فِي yields the height.

قَالَ: مَنْ أَنَا؟ — he asked her about her belief concerning him. قَالَتْ: أَنْتَ رَسُولُ اللهِ — so she affirmed the messengership for him.

Two questions, and between them they are the two testimonies. He examined her on the Lord she worshipped and on the Messenger she followed — and the first of the two was answered with a statement about where Allah is.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَعْتِقْهَا فَإِنَّهَا مُؤْمِنَةٌ — he said this to her master. And al-Fawzān takes two rulings from it:

١ that whoever bears this testimony is a believer
٢ and that the freeing (of a slave, as an expiation) has īmān as a condition for it

The second is why the question was asked at all. Muʿāwiyah did not come for a theology lesson; he came to free a girl he had wronged. The Prophet ﷺ had to establish that she was a believer before the freeing would serve — and the examination he used was two questions long.

And the shāhid from the ḥadīth: that in it is a proof for Allah's highness above His creation, above His heavens; and that He is pointed to in the direction of height with a perceptible pointing (يُشَارُ إِلَيْهِ فِي جِهَةِ العُلُوِّ إِشَارَةً حِسِّيَّةً).

Keep that last phrase beside Lesson 21, where the objection from الجِهَة was answered by splitting the word: indwelling in creation is false, above creation is true. Here is the true sense, confirmed by a gesture — and by the one person in the room who had never studied a book.

It is worth stopping on who the witness is. Of the four texts in this section, three are words of the Prophet ﷺ. The fourth is the one-word answer of a slave-girl who was asked a question and said the first true thing — and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ pronounced her a believer on the strength of it. Whatever this doctrine is, it is not a subtlety reserved for specialists. That is the author's point in including her at all.

العَرْشُ فَوْقَ المَخْلُوقَاتِ كُلِّهَا. The Throne is above all of the created things.
لَا يَجُوزُ السُّؤَالُ عَنِ اللهِ بِـ«أَيْنَ». It is not permissible to ask about Allah with "where".

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَاللهُ فَوْقَ العَرْشِ، وَهُوَ يَعْلَمُ مَا أَنْتُمْ عَلَيْهِ

Learn the two clauses together and you will never need to choose between them. He is above the Throne — and He knows the state you are in right now. Neither half cancels the other, and the ḥadīth put them in one sentence so that nobody would try.
