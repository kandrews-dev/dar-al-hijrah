حَقِيقَةُ الإِيمَانِ وَحُكْمُ مُرْتَكِبِ الكَبِيرَة

The Reality of Īmān, and the Ruling on the Major Sinner

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٦١–١٦٦
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 161–166.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the definition of الإِيمَان with its four parts.
  • Distinguish أَقْوَالُ القَلْب from أَعْمَالُ القَلْب, and say which produces which.
  • Name the five positions on the definition of īmān — and say what separates the Muʿtazilah from Ahl al-Sunnah, given that their definitions agree.
  • Explain the difference between الإِيمَانُ المُطْلَق and مُطْلَقُ الإِيمَان.
القِسْمُ السَّادِس · حَقِيقَةُ الإِيمَانِ وَحُكْمُ مُرْتَكِبِ الكَبِيرَة Part Six — The Reality of Īmān, and the Ruling on the Major Sinner

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ: أَنَّ الدِّينَ وَالإِيمَانَ قَوْلٌ وَعَمَلٌ: قَوْلُ القَلْبِ وَاللِّسَانِ، وَعَمَلُ القَلْبِ وَاللِّسَانِ وَالجَوَارِحِ. وَأَنَّ الإِيمَانَ يَزِيدُ بِالطَّاعَةِ وَيَنْقُصُ بِالمَعْصِيَةِ. وَهُمْ مَعَ ذَلِكَ لَا يُكَفِّرُونَ أَهْلَ القِبْلَةِ بِمُطْلَقِ المَعَاصِي وَالكَبَائِرِ كَمَا يَفْعَلُهُ الخَوَارِجُ؛ بَلِ الأُخُوَّةُ الإِيمَانِيَّةُ ثَابِتَةٌ مَعَ المَعَاصِي. وَلَا يَسْلُبُونَ الفَاسِقَ المِلِّيَّ الإِسْلَامَ بِالكُلِّيَّةِ، وَلَا يُخَلِّدُونَهُ فِي النَّارِ كَمَا تَقُولُهُ المُعْتَزِلَةُ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَمِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ — that is: the foundations upon which their creed is built. So what follows is not a detail of the subject but one of its load-bearing walls.

الدِّين — is, in the language, submission and compliance; and in the Sharīʿah: what Allah commanded. الإِيمَان — in the language is affirming as true; and in the Sharīʿah it is what the author states:

قَوْلٌ وَعَمَلٌ: قَوْلُ القَلْبِ وَاللِّسَانِ، وَعَمَلُ القَلْبِ وَاللِّسَانِ وَالجَوَارِحِ

And al-Fawzān unpacks it into its four parts:

قَوْلُ القَلْبالاعْتِقَاد — the creed held
قَوْلُ اللِّسَانالتَّكَلُّمُ بِكَلِمَةِ الإِسْلَام — uttering the word of Islam
عَمَلُ القَلْبنِيَّةٌ وَإِخْلَاص — intention and sincerity
عَمَلُ الجَوَارِحthe limbs — such as the prayer, the ḥajj, the jihād

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then a distinction most students never get told, which is the hinge of the whole definition. الفَرْقُ بَيْنَ أَقْوَالِ القَلْبِ وَأَعْمَالِه: its sayings are the creedal matters it acknowledges and holds. As for its actions, they are its movement which Allah and His Messenger love — and that is the love of good and the firm resolve upon it, and the hatred of evil and the determination to leave it.

So the heart does two different things: it holds a creed, and it moves. And then the causal claim that makes the rest of the definition inevitable:

— And the actions of the heart are what the actions of the limbs and the sayings of the tongue ARISE FROM. And from there, the sayings of the tongue and the actions of the limbs became part of īmān.

That is the argument, and it is worth seeing that it is an argument rather than an assertion. The deeds are not bolted onto faith as an extra requirement; they are what faith in the heart does when it is there. A heart that genuinely loves good and resolves upon it produces prayer the way a root produces a branch — which is why removing the deeds from the definition removes the evidence that the root exists.

أَقْوَالُ النَّاسِ فِي تَعْرِيفِ الإِيمَان Five Positions on the Definition of Īmān

الشَّرْح · the explanation

١ أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةbelief in the heart, utterance with the tongue, and action with the limbs
٢ المُرْجِئَةbelief in the heart and utterance with the tongue only
٣ الكَرَّامِيَّةutterance with the tongue only
٤ الجَبْرِيَّةacknowledgement in the heart, or mere knowledge in the heart
٥ المُعْتَزِلَةbelief of the heart, utterance of the tongue, and action of the limbs — the same three

Look at row 5 and row 1. The Muʿtazilah define īmān exactly as Ahl al-Sunnah do. So al-Fawzān has to say where the parting is, and it is not in the definition at all:

— the difference between them is that with the Muʿtazilah the committer of a major sin is stripped of the name of īmān ENTIRELY and abides forever in the Fire; and with Ahl al-Sunnah he is not stripped of īmān entirely — rather he is a believer deficient in īmān, and does not abide forever in the Fire if he enters it.

Same premise, opposite conclusion. Which is the most useful thing in this lesson for reading modern arguments: it shows that «deeds are part of īmān» does not by itself settle anything. The question that divides is what follows when a deed is missing — and the answer turns on whether īmān has degrees, which is the next clause of the matn.

And all of these are false statements, and the truth is what Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah said, for many proofs.

يَزِيدُ بِالطَّاعَةِ وَيَنْقُصُ بِالمَعْصِيَة It Increases with Obedience and Decreases with Disobedience

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: and of the foundations of Ahl al-Sunnah is that īmān admits of degrees by increase and decrease — obedience increases it and disobedience diminishes it; and many proofs indicate that. The student met this in Lesson 28, drawn out of the single word afḍal; here it is stated as a principle with its texts.

إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ الَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ اللَّهُ وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَإِذَا تُلِيَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُهُ زَادَتْهُمْ إِيمَانًا وَعَلَى رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ

"The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts tremble; and when His āyāt are recited to them, they increase them in faith; and upon their Lord they rely."

Sūrat al-Anfāl 8:2 · سُورَةُ الأَنْفَال

لِيَزْدَادُوا إِيمَانًا مَعَ إِيمَانِهِمْ

"That they might increase in faith, along with their faith."

Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:4 · سُورَةُ الفَتْح

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān glosses the first: إِذَا ذُكِرَ اللَّهُ — that is: His greatness and His power are mentioned, and what He warned of to whoever disobeys Him; وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ — that is: they feared; وَإِذَا تُلِيَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُهُ — that is: His revealed āyāt are recited, or His universal signs are called to mind; زَادَتْهُمْ إِيمَانًا — that is: their faith increased by reason of that; وَعَلَى رَبِّهِمْ يَتَوَكَّلُونَ — that is: they commit all their affairs to Him, not to another.

لَا يُكَفِّرُونَ أَهْلَ القِبْلَةِ بِمُطْلَقِ المَعَاصِي They Do Not Declare the People of the Qiblah Disbelievers for Sins

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: Ahl al-Sunnah, although they hold that the deeds enter into what is named by īmān, and that it increases with obedience and decreases with disobedience — they nevertheless do not rule with disbelief upon one who claims Islam and faces the Kaʿbah, merely for his committing sins which are short of shirk and disbelief — كَمَا يَفْعَلُهُ الخَوَارِجُ, who said: whoever commits a major sin is, in this world, a disbeliever, and in the Hereafter abides forever in the Fire, never coming out of it.

And then the author's proof that الأُخُوَّةُ الإِيمَانِيَّةُ ثَابِتَةٌ مَعَ المَعَاصِي — the brotherhood of faith stands together with the sins; so the disobedient one is our brother in faith.

فَمَنْ عُفِيَ لَهُ مِنْ أَخِيهِ شَيْءٌ فَاتِّبَاعٌ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَأَدَاءٌ إِلَيْهِ بِإِحْسَانٍ

"And whoever is pardoned something by his brother — then a fair demand, and payment to him in good measure."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:178 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The meaning: that when the one who committed the offence is pardoned by the victim or his guardian from the retaliation, and he is content to take the wealth as blood-money — then it is upon the one entitled to the money to demand it in a fair way, without harshness; and upon the one who owes it to pay it without delay.

And now the point of evidence, which is the whole reason the author quoted a verse about blood-money in a chapter on faith: and the manner of the proof from the āyah is that He named the killer a BROTHER of the one killed — even though killing is one of the major sins — and even so, the brotherhood of faith did not depart from him.

That is as strong a case as the question admits. If any sin were going to sever the brotherhood, it would be the one where a man has killed the brother in question — and the āyah, legislating for exactly that situation, still calls him أَخِيهِ.

وَإِنْ طَائِفَتَانِ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ اقْتَتَلُوا فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَهُمَا فَإِنْ بَغَتْ إِحْدَاهُمَا عَلَى الْأُخْرَى فَقَاتِلُوا الَّتِي تَبْغِي حَتَّى تَفِيءَ إِلَى أَمْرِ اللَّهِ

"And if two parties of the believers fight each other, make peace between them. But if one of them transgresses against the other, then fight the one that transgresses until it returns to the command of Allah."

Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:9 · سُورَةُ الحُجُرَات

إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَ أَخَوَيْكُمْ

"The believers are but brothers, so make peace between your two brothers."

Sūrat al-Ḥujurāt 49:10 · سُورَةُ الحُجُرَات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the manner of the proof from the two noble āyāt is that He named them BELIEVERS despite the existence of the fighting and the transgression between them, and named them BROTHERS of the believers with His saying فَأَصْلِحُوا بَيْنَ أَخَوَيْكُمْ.

A point of Arabic on the last clause: and the singling out of the two for mention is to establish the obligation of making peace in what is more than two, by way of the stronger case — if peace must be made between two, then all the more between twenty.

إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ إِخْوَةٌ — a fresh sentence, confirming what came before it of the command to make peace; and the meaning is that they return to one single matter, which is faith — so they are brothers in religion.

الفَاسِقُ المِلِّيّ The Fāsiq Who Is Still of the Millah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الفَاسِق — al-fisq is going outside the obedience of Allah; and what is meant by the fāsiq here is the one who commits some of the major sins — such as drinking wine, fornication and theft — while holding that these are forbidden.

That qualification is doing essential work. The man described here agrees that what he did is forbidden. Someone who held it lawful would be in a different case entirely — and that is the line between a sinner and an apostate, stated in four words.

المِلِّيّ — that is: the one who is upon the millah of Islam and has not committed of the sins what necessitates his disbelief. So Ahl al-Sunnah do not strip him of Islam entirely and rule him a disbeliever, as the Khawārij say of him in this world; and they do not rule him to abide forever in the Fire in the Hereafter, as the Muʿtazilah say — and the Khawārij. For the Muʿtazilah hold that the fāsiq is called neither a Muslim nor a disbeliever, but is with them بِالمَنْزِلَةِ بَيْنَ المَنْزِلَتَيْن; that is his ruling with them in this world, and in the Hereafter he abides forever in the Fire.

الإِيمَانُ المُطْلَقُ وَمُطْلَقُ الإِيمَان Absolute Īmān, and Īmān Unrestrictedly

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the resolution, and it turns on a pair of phrases that differ only in word order. al-Fawzān states the author's two halves and then defines the terms at the very end — so take the definitions first:

الإِيمَانُ المُطْلَقthe COMPLETE īmān — īmān without qualification
مُطْلَقُ الإِيمَانthe DEFICIENT īmān — some īmān, unspecified

Two words, reversed, and two different doctrines. The fāsiq has muṭlaq al-īmān and not al-īmān al-muṭlaq — he has faith, and he does not have faith entire.

And each half has its proof. The fāsiq DOES enter under the name of īmān unrestrictedly — the one that takes in both the complete and the deficient — as in His saying فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ مُؤْمِنَةٍ. For whoever frees a believing slave, even if the one freeing is himself a fāsiq, in what requires the freed slave's faith — as in the expiation for ẓihār and for killing — that freeing suffices him, by the agreement of the scholars, because it enters into the generality of the āyah even if the one freed is not of the people of complete faith.

وَمَنْ قَتَلَ مُؤْمِنًا خَطَأً فَتَحْرِيرُ رَقَبَةٍ مُؤْمِنَةٍ وَدِيَةٌ مُسَلَّمَةٌ إِلَى أَهْلِهِ

"And whoever kills a believer by mistake — then the freeing of a believing slave, and blood-money handed over to his family."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:92 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he may NOT enter under the name of absolute īmān — that is, when what is intended by īmān is the complete — as in His saying إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ الَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ اللَّهُ وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ; because what is meant by the īmān mentioned in that noble āyah is the complete īmān, so the fāsiq does not enter into it, because his īmān is deficient.

إِنَّمَا there is a particle of restriction, which establishes the ruling for the one mentioned and negates it of everyone else — which is exactly why the āyah can exclude a man the other āyah includes. Two āyāt, two senses of one word, and no conflict.

لَا يَزْنِي الزَّانِي حِينَ يَزْنِي وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ، وَلَا يَسْرِقُ السَّارِقُ حِينَ يَسْرِقُ وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ، وَلَا يَشْرَبُ الخَمْرَ حِينَ يَشْرَبُهَا وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ، وَلَا يَنْتَهِبُ نُهْبَةً ذَاتَ شَرَفٍ يَرْفَعُ النَّاسُ إِلَيْهِ فِيهَا أَبْصَارَهُمْ حِينَ يَنْتَهِبُهَا وَهُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ

"The fornicator does not fornicate while he is a believer; the thief does not steal while he is a believer; the wine-drinker does not drink it while he is a believer; nor does he seize a plunder of note, at which people raise their eyes to him, while he is a believer."

رَوَاهُ الشَّيْخَان — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: complete in īmān. So what is negated here of the fornicator, the thief and the drinker is the COMPLETENESS of īmān, not the whole of it — and al-Fawzān gives a proof for that reading which settles the matter from an unexpected direction:

— by the proof of the consensus on the inheritance of the fornicator, the thief and the wine-drinker.

A Muslim does not inherit from a disbeliever. So the agreed-upon fact that these men still inherit proves that the īmān the ḥadīth denies them cannot be the īmān that makes a man a Muslim. The fiqh settles the ʿaqīdah.

So the ḥadīth indicated that these people, at the time of their doing the sin, have had the complete īmān negated of them; and the many texts of the Book and the Sunnah have indicated that they are not apostates by that. So it is known that the īmān negated in this ḥadīth is only the completeness of the obligatory īmān.

And the vocabulary: النُّهْبَة — with a ḍammah on the nūn, is the thing plundered; and al-nahb is the taking of wealth by overpowering and force. ذَاتَ شَرَفٍ — that is: of consequence; and it has been said: one at which people crane to look, raising their eyes to it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the author's verdict, which al-Fawzān calls الحُكْمُ العَادِل — the just ruling, joining together the texts which negated īmān of him — like «the fornicator does not fornicate while a believer» — and the texts which affirmed īmān for him — the āyah of retaliation and the āyah on the transgressing parties:

هُوَ مُؤْمِنٌ نَاقِصُ الإِيمَانِ، أَوْ مُؤْمِنٌ بِإِيمَانِهِ فَاسِقٌ بِكَبِيرَتِهِ

— he is a believer deficient in faith; or: a believer by his faith, a fāsiq by his major sin.

And upon that basis: فَلَا يُعْطَى الاسْمَ المُطْلَقَ — that is, the name of complete īmān — وَلَا يُسْلَبُ مُطْلَقَ الاسْمِ — that is, the deficient īmān.

Read the second formula slowly, because it is the finest thing in the lesson: a believer by his faith, a fāsiq by his major sin. Each name is given on the ground that earns it, and neither cancels the other. The Khawārij gave him only the second name; the Murjiʾah gave him only the first; and the author gives him both, each with its own cause attached.

That is the pattern of Lesson 30 one last time, and it has never varied: each sect kept the set of texts it could hold and discarded the rest, and the middle position is the one that has to throw nothing away.

Check Your Understanding · MCQ أَسْئِلَة

What are the four parts of īmān in the author’s definition?

Belief, utterance, prayer and charity
Saying of the heart (belief), saying of the tongue (the word of Islam), action of the heart (intention and sincerity), and action of the limbs
Knowledge, action, calling to it and patience
Islam, īmān, iḥsān and taqwā

What is the difference between the sayings of the heart and its actions?

The sayings are spoken aloud, the actions are hidden
Its sayings are the creedal matters it holds; its actions are its movement — love of good and resolve upon it, hatred of evil and determination to leave it
Its sayings are obligatory and its actions recommended
There is no difference; they are two names for one thing

What follows from the actions of the heart, per al-Fawzān?

They are the whole of īmān
The actions of the limbs and the sayings of the tongue ARISE from them — and from there the limbs and the tongue became part of īmān
They are known only to Allah and so are not part of īmān
They vary from person to person

How do the Muʿtazilah define īmān?

Belief in the heart only
Belief of the heart, utterance of the tongue and action of the limbs — the same three as Ahl al-Sunnah
Utterance of the tongue only
Mere knowledge in the heart

So what separates them from Ahl al-Sunnah?

Their definition of al-dīn
Not the definition but the consequence: with them the major sinner is stripped of the name of īmān entirely and abides forever in the Fire
Their position on the Companions
Their denial that īmān increases

How does the āyah of retaliation prove the brotherhood of faith survives sin?

Because it commands forgiveness
Because it names the killer a BROTHER of the one killed, though killing is a major sin
Because it mentions blood-money
Because it addresses the believers

What qualification does al-Fawzān attach to the fāsiq in this discussion?

That his sins are few
That he commits the major sins while holding that they are forbidden
That he has not been warned
That he repents afterwards

What is the difference between الإِيمَانُ المُطْلَق and مُطْلَقُ الإِيمَان?

They are two names for the same thing
Al-īmān al-muṭlaq is the COMPLETE īmān; muṭlaq al-īmān is the DEFICIENT īmān — the fāsiq has the second and not the first
One is the faith of the prophets, the other of the common people
One is inward and the other outward

What proof settles that the ḥadīth negates only the completeness of īmān?

The consensus of the mufassirūn
The consensus on the INHERITANCE of the fornicator, thief and wine-drinker — a Muslim does not inherit from a disbeliever
That the ḥadīth is reported by both Shaykhs
That the sins named are only three
