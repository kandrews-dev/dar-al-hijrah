الإِيمَانُ بِاللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِه

Belief in Allah & the Angels

Year 2+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • State the four matters included in belief in Allah
  • Describe the nature of the angels — created from light, never disobeying
  • Name key angels and their appointed duties
  • Explain how belief in the angels affects a Muslim's behaviour
الرُّكْنُ الأَوَّل: الإِيمَانُ بِاللَّه Pillar 1 — Belief in Allah

We have spent two whole units on this pillar already — so here we simply organise it. The scholars say that true belief in Allah includes four matters, and you have studied them all:

1. His existence — وجوده

Proven by the fiṭrah, the creation, and revelation.

2. His Lordship — ربوبيته

He alone creates, owns, and controls. (Unit 1, L.0)

3. His worship — ألوهيته

He alone deserves worship. (Units 1–2)

4. Names & Attributes — أسماؤه وصفاته

Affirmed without taḥrīf or tamthīl. (Unit 2)

These four are simply the three categories of tawhid (Unit 1) plus belief in His existence. With that pillar secured, we now turn to the unseen world Allah has told us about — beginning with the angels.

الرُّكْنُ الثَّانِي: الإِيمَانُ بِالمَلَائِكَة Pillar 2 — Belief in the Angels

The angels (الملائكة) are a vast world of creation that we cannot normally see. Belief in them is the second pillar of īmān. To believe in them means: to be certain they exist, that they are honoured servants of Allah, and to believe in those named for us specifically, and the rest in general.

بَلْ عِبَادٌ مُّكْرَمُونَ ۝ لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ وَهُم بِأَمْرِهِ يَعْمَلُونَ

"Rather, they are honoured servants. They do not precede Him in speech, and they act by His command."

Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ 21:26–27 · سورة الأنبياء

طَبِيعَةُ المَلَائِكَة The Nature of the Angels

💡

خُلقوا من نور

Created from light

"The angels were created from light." (Muslim) — not from clay like humans nor fire like jinn.

🕊️

لا يعصون الله

They never disobey

"They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them, and they do as commanded." (66:6)

🔢

عددهم عظيم

Immense in number

The Bayt al-Maʿmūr is entered by 70,000 angels daily, never to return. (Bukhārī & Muslim)

🪽

لهم أجنحة

They have wings

"...messengers with wings — two, three, or four." (35:1) Jibrīl has 600 wings.

Important: Angels are not male or female, they are not Allah's "children" (a claim the Quran strongly refutes, 43:19), and they have no share in Lordship or worship. They are pure servants who carry out His commands. We respect them, but we never worship them — that would be the shirk of Unit 1.

مَلَائِكَةٌ سُمُّوا لَنَا وَوَظَائِفُهُم Named Angels & Their Duties

جِبْرِيل

Jibrīl (Gabriel) — the Angel of Revelation

The greatest of the angels, entrusted with bringing revelation (waḥy) from Allah to the prophets. Called الرُّوحُ الأَمِينُ (the Trustworthy Spirit, 26:193). He came to the Prophet ﷺ in the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl from L.0.

مِيكَائِيل

Mīkāʾīl (Michael) — entrusted with the rain & provision

Charged with the rain and vegetation by which Allah provides sustenance to creation, by Allah's command.

إِسْرَافِيل

Isrāfīl — entrusted with the Trumpet (al-Ṣūr)

Will blow the Trumpet to begin the Last Day — the first blast of terror, and the second of resurrection (connects to L.3).

مَلَكُ الْمَوْتِ

The Angel of Death — entrusted with taking souls

Takes the souls at the appointed time: "Say: the Angel of Death, who is set over you, will take you." (32:11)

مَالِك · رِضْوَان

Mālik & the keepers of Hell and Paradise

Mālik is the keeper of the Hellfire (43:77). Other angels are the keepers (khazana) of Paradise who greet its people (39:73).

الكِرَامُ الكَاتِبُونَ · مُنكَر ونَكِير

The honourable scribes & the questioners of the grave

Two angels record every deed (82:10–12). In the grave, two angels question the deceased about their Lord, religion, and prophet (connects to L.3).

ثَمَرَاتُ الإِيمَانِ بِالمَلَائِكَة Fruits of Believing in the Angels
  • Awareness of being recorded: knowing the noble scribes write every word and deed makes a believer careful and conscientious.
  • Comfort and companionship: the believer is never truly alone — angels seek out gatherings of dhikr and pray for those who teach good.
  • Glory of Allah's power: the vastness of the angelic world increases awe of the One who created and commands them all.
