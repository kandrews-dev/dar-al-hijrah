السُّنَّةُ وَشَرْطُ الاسْتِدْلَالِ بِالحَدِيث

The Sunnah, and the Condition for Using a Ḥadīth

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define السُّنَّة linguistically and technically.
  • Give the four things the author says the Sunnah does for the Qurʾān.
  • List the five conditions of a حَدِيثٌ صَحِيح and name what each one excludes.
  • Explain what تَلَقَّاهَا أَهْلُ المَعْرِفَةِ بِالقَبُولِ adds beyond mere authenticity.
القِسْمُ الثَّانِي · الأَدِلَّةُ مِنَ السُّنَّة Part Two — The Evidence from the Sunnah

Part 1 is finished: twenty-three sections of Qurʾānic evidence, and the author's own verdict that he gave only a selection. He now turns to the second source. But he does not simply start quoting aḥādīth — he first states what the Sunnah is, what it does, and on what condition a ḥadīth may be used in this subject at all. That preliminary is this lesson, and it is the methodological heart of Part 2.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

ثُمَّ فِي سُنَّةِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ، فَالسُّنَّةُ تُفَسِّرُ القُرْآنَ وَتُبَيِّنُهُ وَتَدُلُّ عَلَيْهِ وَتُعَبِّرُ عَنْهُ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first locates the sentence grammatically, because it matters: ثُمَّ فِي سُنَّةِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ is conjoined to his earlier statement — «and there has entered into this totality what Allah described Himself with in Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ…» — that is: and there has entered into it what the Messenger ﷺ described his Lord with, in what has come in the authentic Sunnah.

So this is not a new topic. It is the same sentence from the beginning of the book, still running: what Ahl al-Sunnah believe about Allah is what He said of Himself and what His Messenger ﷺ said of Him. Part 1 and Part 2 are two halves of one clause.

And why the Sunnah at all? Because the Sunnah is the second foundation to which recourse must be had after the Book of Allah عز وجل.

فَإِنْ تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ

"And if you differ in anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:59 · سورة النساء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān draws the referral out into its two halves, and the second is the one that establishes the Sunnah: the referral to Allah is the returning to His Book; and the referral to the Messenger ﷺ, after his death, is the returning to his Sunnah.

The āyah commands two referrals, not one. And since the Messenger ﷺ is no longer present to be asked, obeying the second half of the command is consulting the Sunnah. Whoever will not refer to it has not obeyed the āyah — and the āyah ties the obedience to belief in Allah and the Last Day.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the definition, in the two registers the ʿulamāʾ always give:

لُغَةً — in the languageالطَّرِيقَة — the way, the road
اصْطِلَاحًا — technicallywhat has come from the Messenger of Allah ٞط of a statement, or an act, or an approval

Hold the lexical sense in view, because it explains the name of the group this whole book is about. أَهْلُ السُّنَّة means the people of the way — not the people of a theory. Lesson 2 made the same point from the other side: the sects are named after their innovation, their founder or their deeds, and these are named after the road.

مَكَانَةُ السُّنَّة The Standing of the Sunnah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author gives the Sunnah four functions in one line, and al-Fawzān takes them one at a time. فَالسُّنَّةُ تُفَسِّرُ القُرْآنَ — that is: it makes clear its meanings and its aims, for the Prophet ﷺ explains to the people what was sent down to him:

وَأَنْزَلْنَا إِلَيْكَ الذِّكْرَ لِتُبَيِّنَ لِلنَّاسِ مَا نُزِّلَ إِلَيْهِمْ

"And We sent down to you the Reminder, that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:44 · سورة النحل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَتُبَيِّنُهُ — that is: it clarifies what comes in it in summary form; and al-Fawzān gives the obvious examples: such as the prayer, the fast, the ḥajj, the zakāh, and most of the rulings, which come in the Qurʾān in summary and the Prophetic Sunnah clarifies them.

That single sentence is why the Sunnah is not optional. The Qurʾān commands the prayer and does not say how many, when, or in what form. A person holding the Qurʾān alone could not pray.

وَتَدُلُّ عَلَيْهِ وَتُعَبِّرُ عَنْهُ — that is: it indicates what the Qurʾān indicated, and expresses what the Qurʾān expressed; so it comes out in agreement with the Qurʾān, and the ruling is then from what the Book and the Sunnah have indicated — as with the names of Allah and His attributes.

Note where al-Fawzān chooses to point that last function: at this very subject. The attributes are the case where the Sunnah is not adding rulings the Qurʾān left summary, and not explaining a difficult word — it is saying the same thing the Qurʾān said. Which is why Part 2 will feel like Part 1 continued rather than a fresh argument.

شَرْطُ الاسْتِدْلَالِ بِالحَدِيث The Condition for Using a Ḥadīth as Proof

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمَا وَصَفَ الرَّسُولُ بِهِ رَبَّهُ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ مِنَ الأَحَادِيثِ الصِّحَاحِ الَّتِي تَلَقَّاهَا أَهْلُ المَعْرِفَةِ بِالقَبُولِ وَجَبَ الإِيمَانُ بِهَا كَذَلِكَ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Again the grammar first: وَمَا وَصَفَ… is the mubtadaʾ, and its khabar is وَجَبَ الإِيمَانُ بِهَا كَذَلِكَ — that is: just as it is obligatory to believe in what Allah described Himself with in the Noble Qurʾān.

The same obligation, on the same footing. And the ground for that is that the speech is not the Prophet's ﷺ own:

وَمَا يَنْطِقُ عَنِ الْهَوَى إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا وَحْيٌ يُوحَى

"Nor does he speak from desire. It is nothing but a revelation revealed."

Sūrat al-Najm 53:3–4 · سورة النجم

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So the Sunnah which the Messenger ﷺ uttered is a revelation from Allah — and al-Fawzān brings a second proof for it from the pairing of the two things sent down upon him:

وَأَنْزَلَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْكَ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَةَ — “and Allah sent down upon you the Book and the Wisdom” (al-Nisāʾ 4:113). So the Book is the Qurʾān, and the Wisdom is the Sunnah. Both are sent down.

So it is obligatory to believe in what has come in the Sunnah — especially in the chapter of belief:

وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانْتَهُوا

"And whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it."

Sūrat al-Ḥashr 59:7 · سورة الحشر

الشُّرُوطُ الخَمْسَةُ لِلْحَدِيثِ الصَّحِيح The Five Conditions of an Authentic Ḥadīth

الشَّرْح · the explanation

But there is no avoiding, in the accepting of a ḥadīth and the believing in it, its being established from the Prophet ﷺ; and this is why the Shaykh رحمه الله said مِنَ الأَحَادِيثِ الصِّحَاحِ.

This is the clause that separates this book from the method of those who argue creed from whatever story supports them. الصِّحَاح is the plural of صَحِيح, and al-Fawzān then gives the definition the ḥadīth scholars use:

الحَدِيثُ الصَّحِيحُ: هُوَ مَا نَقَلَهُ رَاوٍ عَدْلٌ تَامُّ الضَّبْطِ عَنْ مِثْلِهِ، مِنْ غَيْرِ شُذُوذٍ وَلَا عِلَّةٍ.

— that which an upright narrator of complete precision transmitted from one like himself, without irregularity and without a hidden defect.

So it is that in which five conditions have come together:

١ عَدَالَةُ الرُّوَاةِthe uprightness of the narrators — excludes the untrustworthy
٢ تَمَامُ الضَّبْطِcomplete precision — excludes the honest but unreliable, who would not lie but misremembers
٣ اتِّصَالُ السَّنَدِthe connectedness of the chain — excludes a chain with a gap in it, however sound its named links
٤ سَلَامَتُهُ مِنَ الشُّذُوذِits freedom from shudhūdh — excludes the reliable narrator who contradicts those more reliable than him
٥ سَلَامَتُهُ مِنَ العِلَّةِits freedom from a hidden ʿillah — excludes the defect that only a specialist detects, in a chain that looks sound

Four of the five are about the people, and the last two are the ones that make this a science rather than a background check. A ḥadīth can pass every test of honesty and memory and connection, and still be rejected because a stronger narrator said otherwise (شُذُوذ), or because something is wrong in it that does not show on the surface (عِلَّة).

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the author's second clause, which is additional to authenticity: تَلَقَّاهَا أَهْلُ المَعْرِفَةِ — that is: the people of knowledge of ḥadīth accepted it and took it.

Notice what the author has done. He has not written «the aḥādīth which I consider authentic», nor «the aḥādīth whose chains look sound to the reader». He has handed the judgement to the specialists of the field — and in a book of creed, where the temptation is to grade a ḥadīth by whether it suits one's position, that is the whole safeguard.

This is also the rule this track works under. Every ḥadīth in the lessons that follow carries a grading attributed to a named scholar — al-Bukhārī, Muslim, or an imām who graded it — and where the sharḥ gives an attribution without a grading, the page says so plainly rather than supplying one.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَمَا يَنْطِقُ عَنِ الْهَوَى إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا وَحْيٌ يُوحَى

The āyah on which the whole of Part 2 rests. If what he ﷺ said about his Lord is revelation, then the aḥādīth of the attributes are not a secondary class of evidence to be weighed against the Qurʾān — they are the same evidence, reaching us by the other route.
