البَسْمَلَةُ وَخُطْبَةُ الافْتِتَاح

The Basmalah and the Opening Address

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why the author opens with the basmalah and a khuṭbah at all.
  • Give al-Fawzān’s derivation of the name الله, and the difference between الرَّحْمَن and الرَّحِيم.
  • Distinguish the two kinds of هُدَى — and say which one is denied of the Messenger ﷺ.
  • Explain how عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ refutes two opposite errors about the Prophet ﷺ at once.
بَسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيم The Basmalah

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

بِسْمِ اللهِ الرَّحْمَنِ الرَّحِيمِ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Why begin here? al-Fawzān gives two reasons, and both are about following: in imitation of the Book of Allah, where the basmalah opens every sūrah but Barāʾah; and in imitation of the Prophet ﷺ, who began his letters with it.

The بَاء is for seeking help — by the name of Allah, I begin. And اللهُ is a proper name of the sacred Essence. al-Fawzān derives it: it means the One possessing divinity and the right to be worshipped over all His creation, taken from أَلَهَ يَأْلَهُ أُلُوهَةً in the sense of عَبَدَ يَعْبُدُ عِبَادَةً — to worship. So اللهُ means مَأْلُوهٌ: the One who is worshipped.

That derivation is not a grammatical aside. The very name of Allah, unpacked, already contains the creed of this book: the One who is worshipped.

And the two names that follow are not synonyms. الرَّحْمَن is the possessor of the general mercy, embracing all creatures. الرَّحِيم is the possessor of the mercy particular to the believers — which is why Allah closes an āyah about His sending blessings upon the believers with وَكَانَ بِالْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا.

هُوَ الَّذِي يُصَلِّي عَلَيْكُمْ وَمَلَائِكَتُهُ لِيُخْرِجَكُمْ مِنَ الظُّلُمَاتِ إِلَى النُّورِ وَكَانَ بِالْمُؤْمِنِينَ رَحِيمًا

"It is He who sends blessings upon you, and His angels, to bring you out of the darknesses into the light — and He is ever Merciful to the believers."

Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:43 · سورة الأحزاب

خُطْبَةُ الافْتِتَاح The Opening Address

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي أَرْسَلَ رَسُولَهُ بِالهُدَى وَدِينِ الحَقِّ، لِيُظْهِرَهُ عَلَى الدِّينِ كُلِّهِ، وَكَفَى بِاللهِ شَهِيدًا. وَأَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَحْدَهُ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ، إِقْرَارًا بِهِ وَتَوْحِيدًا. وَأَشْهَدُ أَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ، صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَسَلَّمَ تَسْلِيمًا مَزِيدًا.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ. The definite article here is لِلاسْتِغْرَاق — for totality: all praise, every last part of it, belongs to Allah both in ownership and by right. And al-Fawzān distinguishes the word's two senses: in the language, الحَمْد is praise for beautiful qualities and good deeds; by convention, it is an act that announces the magnifying of the Benefactor because He is the Benefactor — and its opposite is blame.

بِالهُدَى — “with guidance.” Here is a distinction worth carrying through the whole of your studies. الهُدَى is of two kinds:

KindWhat it isWho does it
هُدَى الدَّلَالَةِ وَالبَيَانshowing and making clearthe Messenger ﷺ does this — وَإِنَّكَ لَتَهْدِي إِلَىٰ صِرَاطٍ مُسْتَقِيمٍ (al-Shūrā 42:52)
هُدَى التَّوْفِيقِ وَالإِلْهَامputting guidance into a heartdenied of the Messenger ﷺ; none but Allah is able — إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ (al-Qaṣaṣ 28:56)

Hold those apart and a great deal becomes clear at once: why the Prophet ﷺ grieved over those who refused him, and why your own duty toward others ends at the first kind.

إِنَّكَ لَا تَهْدِي مَنْ أَحْبَبْتَ وَلَٰكِنَّ اللَّهَ يَهْدِي مَنْ يَشَاءُ

"You do not guide whom you love, but Allah guides whom He wills."

Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ 28:56 · سورة القصص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَكَفَى بِاللهِ شَهِيدًا. A decisive proof of this Messenger's truthfulness, al-Fawzān notes: Allah is witness that he is His messenger, aware of all his deeds, and his helper against his enemies. Had he been a fabricator, Allah would have hastened his punishment — and Allah said exactly that:

وَلَوْ تَقَوَّلَ عَلَيْنَا بَعْضَ الْأَقَاوِيلِ لَأَخَذْنَا مِنْهُ بِالْيَمِينِ ثُمَّ لَقَطَعْنَا مِنْهُ الْوَتِينَ

"And had he invented about Us some sayings, We would have seized him by the right hand, then severed from him the aorta."

Sūrat al-Ḥāqqah 69:44–46 · سورة الحاقة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The two testimonies. أَشْهَدُ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ means: I acknowledge and affirm that there is none rightfully worshipped except Allah. And al-Fawzān shows that the two phrases following it are not padding — they are a double reinforcement of the kalimah's two halves: وَحْدَهُ reinforces the affirmation, and لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ reinforces the negation.

عَبْدُهُ وَرَسُولُهُ. Why both words? Because each closes a different door, and al-Fawzān names them: the people of الإِفْرَاط, excess, who went beyond bounds concerning him and raised him above the station of servitude; and the people of التَّفْرِيط, neglect, who threw what he brought behind their backs as though he were no messenger at all.

  • Testifying he is Allah’s servant — عَبْدُهُ — denies raising him above his station.
  • Testifying he is Allah’s messenger — رَسُولُهُ — requires believing in him, obeying what he commanded, accepting what he reported, avoiding what he forbade, and following what he legislated.

Two words, and both ditches are closed. The whole of the Prophet's ﷺ right is held between them.

صَلَّى اللهُ عَلَيْهِ. And the soundest thing said about the meaning of Allah's ṣalāh upon His Messenger is what al-Bukhārī records in his Ṣaḥīḥ from Abū al-ʿĀliyah: “Allah's ṣalāh upon His Messenger is His praise of him in the highest assembly.” As for آلِهِ here, the best of what is said is that it means his followers upon his religion.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا صَلُّوا عَلَيْهِ وَسَلِّمُوا تَسْلِيمًا

"O you who believe, send blessings upon him and greetings of peace."

Sūrat al-Aḥzāb 33:56 · سورة الأحزاب

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
البَسْمَلَةُ جَاءَتْ فِي أَوَّلِ كُلِّ سُورَةٍ إِلَّا سُورَةَ بَرَاءَةَ. The basmalah comes at the start of every sūrah except Barāʾah.
الرَّسُولُ ﷺ يَقْدِرُ عَلَى هُدَى التَّوْفِيقِ وَالإِلْهَامِ. The Messenger ﷺ is able to grant the guidance of enabling.
مَعْنَى «لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ»: لَا مَعْبُودَ بِحَقٍّ إِلَّا اللهُ. The meaning of lā ilāha illā Allāh is: there is none rightfully worshipped except Allah.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَكَفَى بِاللهِ شَهِيدًا

"And Allah suffices as a witness." The argument behind those four words is worth keeping: had he been inventing it, Allah would not have left him to it. The survival and spread of this message is itself the testimony.
