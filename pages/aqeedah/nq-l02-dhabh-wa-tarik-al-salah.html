النَّاقِضُ الأَوَّل · الذَّبْحُ وَتَارِكُ الصَّلَاة

The First Nullifier — Slaughter, and the One Who Leaves the Prayer

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the two āyāt and the ḥadīth that make slaughtering an act of worship.
  • Apply the sharḥ’s تَفْصِيل to the slaughter at a new house — and say what turns it from permitted to shirk.
  • Distinguish the اسْتِغَاثَة that is allowed from the kind that is shirk.
  • State Ibn Bāz’s ruling on تَارِكُ الصَّلَاة, and give the grammatical argument it rests on.
  • Give the standard (ضَابِط) by which a sin is a major sin.
  • Distinguish the two kinds of خُلُود, and say which sins fall under the second.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ٣٧–٦٧
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 37–67, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

الذَّبْحُ لِغَيْرِ اللهِ Slaughtering for Other Than Allah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The matn's own example, now with its proofs. The argument has one step: because slaughtering for Allah is an act of worship of Him — so directing it to another is shirk with Him. Everything else follows from establishing that first clause, and the Qurʾān establishes it twice.

قُلْ إِنَّ صَلَاتِي وَنُسُكِي وَمَحْيَايَ وَمَمَاتِي لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ وَبِذَلِكَ أُمِرْتُ وَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ

"Say: my prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds. He has no partner; with that I was commanded, and I am the first of those who submit."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:162–163 · سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام

إِنَّا أَعْطَيْنَاكَ الْكَوْثَرَ فَصَلِّ لِرَبِّكَ وَانْحَرْ

"Indeed We have given you al-Kawthar, so pray to your Lord and sacrifice."

Sūrat al-Kawthar 108:1–2 · سُورَةُ الكَوْثَر

Both āyāt put the sacrifice beside the prayer — «my prayer and my sacrifice», «pray to your Lord and sacrifice». Nobody argues that prayer may be offered to a saint. The pairing is the argument: whatever the prayer is, the slaughter is, and the two are commanded in one breath to one recipient.

لَعَنَ اللهُ مَنْ ذَبَحَ لِغَيْرِ اللهِ

"Allah has cursed whoever slaughters for other than Allah."

أَخْرَجَهُ مُسْلِمٌ فِي صَحِيحِهِ مِنْ حَدِيثِ عَلِيٍّ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So it is not permissible to slaughter for al-Khiḍr, nor for al-Badawī, nor for al-Ḥusayn, nor for anyone else of mankind, nor for idols, nor for the jinn. Rather the slaughter is for Allah alone, and drawing near by sacrifices is for Him alone — as with the sacrificial animals and the offerings.

Notice who is named. Not foreign gods — al-Ḥusayn رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, the grandson of the Prophet ﷺ, and figures revered as saints. The nullifier is written for people who love these names, which is exactly why it is a nullifier and not a warning about idolaters elsewhere.

And drawing near to them with sacrifices so that they may intercede for you, or cure your child — all of this is of the major shirk, and we seek refuge in Allah. Read the two motives named. Neither is contempt for Allah; both are a man in need, reaching for help. That is the shape the first nullifier actually takes.

مَسْأَلَةٌ: ذَبِيحَةُ البِنَاء A Case: the Slaughter at a New House

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz was asked about a custom: a man, when he begins building a house for himself, slaughters an animal when the building reaches halfway — or the slaughter is delayed until the building is finished and he intends to live in it — and the relatives and neighbours are invited to it.

He answered: «This practice has a detail in it» — and the detail is the whole lesson:

لِاتِّقَاءِ الجِنّ
to ward off the jinn		شِرْكٌ أَكْبَر — «if it is for the jinn then it is major shirk, because it is worship of other than Allah»
لِتَحْصِيلِ السَّلَامَة
so that the slaughter itself secures the house and its people		لَا يَجُوز — «فَهُوَ مِنَ البِدَع», it is of the innovations
شُكْرًا لِلَّه
thanking Allah for the blessing of reaching the roof		permitted — it is from the door of gratitude for what He has favoured him with

One animal, one knife, one day — and three different rulings. Nothing outward distinguishes them. What moves the act between permitted, innovated and major shirk is entirely what the man intends by it — which is the practical form of the definition in Lesson 1: worship is a posture, and the same outward act is worship or is not depending on where that posture is directed.

And note the middle row, because students skip it. A thing can be forbidden as a bidʿah without being shirk at all. Believing the slaughter itself secures the house is not directing worship to a creature; it is inventing a cause Allah did not make. The categories are not a single slope.

أَيُّ اسْتِغَاثَةٍ تَجُوز؟ Which Seeking of Aid Is Allowed?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Before the heavier question, a distinction that prevents a real error in the other direction. Ibn Bāz cites the story of Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام:

فَاسْتَغَاثَهُ الَّذِي مِنْ شِيعَتِهِ عَلَى الَّذِي مِنْ عَدُوِّهِ

"And the one from his own party cried out to him for help against the one from his enemy."

Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ 28:15 · سُورَةُ القَصَص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And of that is a person seeking the aid of his companions in jihād and in war, and the like.

So the word اسْتِغَاثَة is not itself the problem. A man calling to another man, present and able, for something a man can do, is ordinary — the Qurʾān reports it without censure. What makes it shirk is asking of one who cannot be asked: the absent, the dead, or for what only Allah gives. The nullifier is about who is being asked and for what, never about the asking.

مَسْأَلَةٌ: تَارِكُ الصَّلَاة A Case: the One Who Abandons the Prayer

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«But is it said of one who abandons the prayer that he is a mushrik? Yes — he is called a mushrik, and he is called a kāfir, upon the sounder of the two statements of the scholars.» And the proof is a ḥadīth of Jābir:

بَيْنَ الرَّجُلِ وَبَيْنَ الشِّرْكِ وَالكُفْرِ تَرْكُ الصَّلَاة

"Between a man and shirk and disbelief is the abandonment of the prayer."

رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ فِي الصَّحِيحِ مِنْ حَدِيثِ جَابِرٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

Now the argument — and it is grammatical, which is why it is worth slowing down for:

«So he named it disbelief and shirk; and the disbelief and the shirk DEFINED by the article «الْ» is the major disbelief and the major shirk.»

The ḥadīth says الشِّرْك and الكُفْر, with the definite article — the shirk, the disbelief. And a definite noun points to the full, known reality of the thing, not to some lesser instance of it. So the wording itself carries the weight of the ruling.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the same rule answers the texts pulled in the other direction. The ḥadīths in which disbelief is applied to one who disowns his father, to the one who fights a Muslim, to the wailing woman, and to one who attacks lineages — this is كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر, disbelief below disbelief, with the people of knowledge, without doubt in it.

And the reason: because it is كُفْرٌ مُنَكَّر — an INDEFINITE disbelief — not كُفْرٌ مُعَرَّف, a definite one.

الكُفْر / الشِّرْك
with «الْ»		the major — as in the ḥadīth of abandoning the prayer
كُفْرٌ
indefinite		كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر — a sin among sins, not an exit from the religion

One article decides between a sin and an apostasy. That is the kind of thing this track exists to teach: the ruling was always in the wording, and a reader who does not notice the article will read the two sets of texts as contradicting each other and have to explain one of them away.

So what some went to, of analogising the abandonment of the prayer to these things, is not apparent — because the texts it is being analogised to are the indefinite kind, and this one is not.

الفَرْقُ بَيْنَ خُلُودِ الكَافِرِ وَخُلُودِ العَاصِي The Eternity of the Disbeliever and That of the Sinner

الشَّرْح · the explanation

A difficulty the student will meet as soon as he reads the Qurʾān on the major sins: eternity in the Fire is stated for the murderer and for the one who returns to ribā. Ibn Bāz first fixes what a major sin is:

ضَابِطُ الكَبِيرَة — every sin in which there came a threat of the Fire, or of Allah's anger, or of the curse, or in which there is a prescribed punishment in this world — that is a major sin. And what has nothing of that coming in it is called a disobedience, and is not called a major sin.

And of the major sins, eternity in the Fire has come in some — as in the one who kills, and in the one who returns to ribā:

وَمَنْ يَقْتُلْ مُؤْمِنًا مُتَعَمِّدًا فَجَزَاؤُهُ جَهَنَّمُ خَالِدًا فِيهَا وَغَضِبَ اللَّهُ عَلَيْهِ وَلَعَنَهُ

"And whoever kills a believer deliberately, his recompense is Hell, abiding therein; and Allah is angry with him and has cursed him."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:93 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«The eternity that has come in these sins is not the eternity Allah promised the disbelievers. For eternity is of two kinds…»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

١ خُلُودٌ مُؤَبَّد
no end at all		Everlasting, with no end — rather for ever and ever. This is the eternity of the disbelievers.
٢ خُلُودٌ طَوِيل
long, but it ends		Because it has a long duration — but it comes to an end. This is the eternity of the one who kills himself, and the one who kills people, and the one who dies upon ribā.

يُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يَخْرُجُوا مِنَ النَّارِ وَمَا هُمْ بِخَارِجِينَ مِنْهَا وَلَهُمْ عَذَابٌ مُقِيمٌ

"They will wish to come out of the Fire, but they will not come out of it; and theirs is an enduring punishment."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:37 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

That āyah is cited for the first kind, and see what it adds. It does not merely say they remain — it says they will want to leave and will not. The wanting is part of the punishment, and it is what «مُقِيم», enduring, is doing at the end of the verse.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And as for the second: «this one abides the measure of a year, and this one the measure of a hundred years, and this one the measure of a thousand years» — and more or less; an eternity with an end that Allah knows, سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى.

So the word خَالِدًا does not carry one fixed length. In Arabic it names a long staying; the Qurʾān uses it of both, and what separates them is not the word but who it is said of. The disbeliever's is sealed by other texts — «they will not come out of it»; the sinner's is not.

And that is the point at which this nullifier connects to everything after it. A man who dies upon the major shirk is in the first column; a Muslim who dies upon a major sin is in the second. The whole reason for studying the ten is to know which column a thing puts you in — and, as the overview says, to guard your own Islam rather than to throw takfīr at others.

ذَبِيحَةُ البِنَاءِ شُكْرًا لِلَّهِ شِرْكٌ أَكْبَرُ. A slaughter at a new house, out of gratitude to Allah, is major shirk.
الاسْتِغَاثَةُ بِالحَيِّ الحَاضِرِ فِيمَا يَقْدِرُ عَلَيْهِ جَائِزَةٌ. Seeking the aid of a living, present person in what he is able to do is permitted.
الكُفْرُ المُعَرَّفُ بِـ«الْ» هُوَ الكُفْرُ الأَصْغَرُ. Disbelief made definite by the article is the minor disbelief.
خُلُودُ العَاصِي فِي النَّارِ لَهُ نِهَايَةٌ يَعْلَمُهَا اللهُ. The sinner’s abiding in the Fire has an end that Allah knows.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

بَيْنَ الرَّجُلِ وَبَيْنَ الشِّرْكِ وَالكُفْرِ تَرْكُ الصَّلَاة

Keep the article in view when you recite it. الشِّرْك and الكُفْر, definite, are the major — and the texts that call a wailing woman or one who disowns his father a kāfir use the word indefinite, which is كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر. One article separates a sin from an apostasy, and a reader who does not notice it will think the two sets of texts contradict.
