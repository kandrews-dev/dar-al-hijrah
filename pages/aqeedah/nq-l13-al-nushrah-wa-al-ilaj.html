النَّاقِضُ السَّابِع · العِلَاجُ وَالنُّشْرَة

The Seventh Nullifier, Part 3 — The Harm and the Remedy

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State exactly what the magic affected in the Prophet ﷺ and what it did not.
  • Answer the objection from «وَاللهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاس» — and say what العِصْمَة actually guarantees.
  • Give the answer to «a khabar āḥād is not taken in ʿaqīdah», including the point about الأُصُول and الفُرُوع.
  • Define النُّشْرَة and give its ruling, with the reason stated as a principle.
  • Demolish the necessity argument for treating magic with magic — Ibn Bāz’s sentence, Ibn Taymiyyah’s reasons, and the condition inside the qāʿidah.
  • Explain why a recitation that does not heal is no argument for going to a magician — and the proof from the Prophet’s ﷺ own final illness.
  • Say what the sharḥ concludes about Hārūt and Mārūt, and what it does with the stories of al-Zuhrah.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٧٩–١٩٣
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 179–193, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This closes the longest clause in the book. Part 1 was what magic is, part 2 the ruling on the magician — and these fifteen folios are the ones an ordinary Muslim will actually need, because they are about being harmed and what may be done about it.

They also contain the best sustained piece of apologetics in the book: six objections to the ḥadīth that the Prophet ﷺ was bewitched, answered one at a time.

سِحْرُ لَبِيدِ بْنِ الأَعْصَم What Happened, and What It Did Not Touch

The question put to him is the whole difficulty in one breath: how can the Messenger ﷺ be bewitched when Allah says «وَاللهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاس»? And how, when he receives revelation and conveys it — how does he convey it while bewitched? And what of the disbelievers’ saying «you follow none but a bewitched man»?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«This is established in the authentic ḥadīth. And when the revelation had settled and the message had settled, and the proofs of prophethood and the truthfulness of the message had been established, and Allah had given His Prophet victory over the mushrikīn and humbled them — a person of the Jews called Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam went against him and worked magic for him in a comb and combings and the sheath of a male date-palm spathe. So it came to be imagined to him that he had done a certain thing with his wife when he had not done it.»

«But there did not depart from him — and praise be to Allah — his intellect, nor his awareness, nor his discernment, in what he spoke to the people of; and he spoke to the people with the truth which Allah had revealed to him; but he sensed something that affected him in some measure with his wives.»

Read the timing first, because the sharḥ puts it first and it is not decoration. This happened after the message was established and its proofs had been given. Whatever it was, it was not an event that could have put the mission in doubt — the mission was already proved.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then what was done about it: «so the revelation came to him from his Lord by means of Jibrīl عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام, and informed him of what had occurred; so he sent one who extracted that thing from a well belonging to one of the Anṣār, and he destroyed it; and that effect departed from him — and praise be to Allah; and He sent down upon him the two sūrahs of refuge, and he recited them, and every affliction departed from him.»

And the Prophet’s ﷺ words «مَا تَعَوَّذَ المُتَعَوِّذُونَ بِمِثْلِهِمَا» — given here with no grading, so none is supplied.

And the conclusion, stated flatly: «and there did not follow from that anything of what harms people, or impairs the message or the revelation; and Allah — glorified and exalted — protected him from the people from what prevents the arrival of the message and its conveyance.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That last clause is the key to the whole section, and it is a definition of العِصْمَة. He does not say Allah protected him from harm. He says Allah protected him «from what prevents the arrival of the message and its conveyance».

And then the list, so that nobody can pretend otherwise: «as for what befalls the messengers of kinds of affliction — he was not protected from it; rather something of that befell him: he was wounded on the day of Uḥud, and the helmet was broken on his head, and some of the rings of the mail entered into his cheeks, and he fell into one of the pits that were there; and they constrained him in Makkah severely… and Allah raised his ranks by it, and elevated his station, and multiplied his good deeds by it — but Allah protected him from them, so they were not able to kill him, nor to prevent him from conveying the message… and he conveyed the message and discharged the trust.»

So the ʿiṣmah is specific, and the specificity is the answer. «وَلَيْسَ مِنَ العِصْمَةِ عَدَمُ ابْتِلَائِهِم، بَلْ هُمْ أَشَدُّ النَّاسِ بَلَاءً.» A man who holds that prophets are shielded from pain has to explain Uḥud, and cannot.

الشُّبُهَاتُ السِّتّ Six Objections, Answered in Order

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ الأُولَى — The ḥadīth is not sound, because Hishām ibn ʿUrwah who narrated it was mukhtaliṭ — his memory confused — as Ibn al-Qaṭṭān mentioned.

الجَوَاب — al-Dhahabī in al-Mīzān: «لَمْ يَخْتَلِطْ أَبَدًا — he was never confused — and no weight is given to what Abū al-Ḥasan ibn al-Qaṭṭān said»; and Ibn Ḥajar in al-Tahdhīb: «and we have not seen anyone preceding him in that». And then, granting it for argument: «what is the proof that he narrated it after the confusion and not before — and a great multitude narrated it from him?» — Egyptians, Meccans, Baṣrans and Madīnans, «and the generality of them are of the trustworthy and firm». And Ibn Bāz’s own principle, said of Abū Isḥāq al-Sabīʿī: «الأَصْلُ عَدَمُ الغَلَطِ بِسَبَبِ الاخْتِلَاط — the default is the absence of error on account of confusion; because the imāms narrated from him and paid no heed to his confusion.» Plus: the ḥadīth has corroborating witnesses; the people of ḥadīth were agreed on its authenticity and received it with acceptance; and neither al-Dāraquṭnī nor al-Jayyānī nor Abū Masʿūd al-Dimashqī criticised it — of those who did criticise some chains in the Ṣaḥīḥayn. Ibn al-Qayyim: «established with the people of knowledge of ḥadīth; they do not differ about its authenticity… and not one of the people of ḥadīth spoke about it with a single word; and the story is well known among the people of tafsīr and the sunan and ḥadīth and history and the jurists — and these know the states of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his days better than the theologians.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ الثَّانِيَة — The ḥadīth of the magic is a khabar āḥād, and solitary reports are not taken in matters of belief — only the Qurʾān and the mutawātir.

الجَوَاب — Three answers. «The statement that a solitary report is not taken in ʿaqīdah is an innovation in the religion, and it is a false statement of the statements of the people of innovation, contrary to the Book and the Sunnah and the consensus of the Salaf.» Second: «this report yields knowledge, by the consensus of the imāms of criticism on its establishment.» And third — the one worth keeping: «believing in the possibility of magic affecting the Prophet ﷺ in what does not touch the Sharīʿah is of the branches of belief, not of its foundations.» Which is to say the objection does not even reach its target: nobody is founding a doctrine on this. It is a report about an event in a life.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ الثَّالِثَة — Labīd did not bewitch him; he only attempted it, and Allah informed him of it, and the magic had no effect on him — just as the magic of Pharaoh’s magicians had no effect on Mūsā.

الجَوَاب — False on both halves. As to the first: the wording of ʿĀʾishah’s ḥadīth is «سُحِرَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ» — he WAS bewitched — «so it indicates the occurrence of the magic, not merely the attempt»; and the word «مَطْبُوب» means bewitched; and her words «until it would be imagined to him». As to the second: «the verses speak plainly that they bewitched the eyes of the people, and awe came over them from the staffs and ropes which they saw moving and which were not so in reality; and what occurred to the eyes of the people occurred to Mūsā likewise — when he conceived fear within himself; and this is of the effect of the magic upon his eyes, for otherwise why did he see the ropes and staffs moving?»

فَأَوْجَسَ فِي نَفْسِهِ خِيفَةً مُّوسَىٰ

And Mūsā conceived fear within himself.

سُورَةُ طَه · 20:67

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ الرَّابِعَة — The ḥadīth contradicts Allah’s saying «وَاللهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاس».

الجَوَاب — «That verse is among the last to be revealed, as Ibn Kathīr mentioned; and the Prophet ﷺ had been bewitched, and his tooth broken, and his head wounded, BEFORE its revelation.» And on the meaning: «and it is said: He protects you from killing, and captivity, and destruction. And the protection is established for the prophets of Allah — but it is not of the protection that they not be tried; rather they are the most severely tried of people; so what is intended by the protection is not their not being exposed to bodily harms.» And Ibn Bāz, as above: «Allah protected him from the people in what prevents the conveying of the message» — not from illness and injury.

وَاللَّهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاسِ

And Allah will protect you from the people.

سُورَةُ المَائِدَة · 5:67

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ الخَامِسَة — If the harm of magic reached the Messenger ﷺ, then the disbelievers would have been right in their saying «إِنْ تَتَّبِعُونَ إِلَّا رَجُلًا مَسْحُورًا» — “you follow none but a bewitched man”.

الجَوَاب — «The disbelievers intended by that only that he was insane, his mind gone by means of magic, and that for this he abandoned their religion; and it is said: مَسْحُورًا meaning a magician, bewitching people; and it is said: you follow none but a man who has a lung» — i.e. a mere mortal, «eating food and drinking drink, not an angel of the angels»; «and it is said: مَخْدُوعًا — deceived. So the disbelievers’ intent goes back to the disorder of his mind by magic, or to deception, or that he is a human being, or that he is a magician — and the ḥadīth indicates none of these corrupt meanings; and there is in the verse no indication of the impossibility of magic befalling him which does not affect his heart, nor his belief, nor his mind, but affects his body only.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then a precision about the effect itself which settles the objection completely.

«And the kind of effect has been specified: that he would imagine coming to his wives when he did not come to them — and he, along with that, was certain that the act had not occurred; so the matter was not confused for him such that he would believe the non-occurrence to be an occurrence. Rather that was a passing thought; and were it supposed that it reached the level of supposition, it was in one of the affairs of the world, and did not pass beyond it to the rest of the affairs of the world — let alone to the affairs of the religion.»

And the evidential clincher is an absence: «it has not been reported that he deputed anyone to lead the people in prayer, nor to manage their affairs; so the ḥadīth does not indicate that he ﷺ was disordered in his mind, such that it could be said it contradicts the verse.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشُّبْهَةُ السَّادِسَة — Magic affecting the Messenger ﷺ leads to the absence of trust in everything he brought from Allah.

الجَوَاب — «A man is doubted only when his affair is hidden, or when he is known to have erred and denied it; as for one for whom there is decisive proof of his truthfulness — the possibility of doubt does not arise upon any matter of the matters the ḥadīth brings; because what befell him ﷺ was in his noble body, and in a particular matter also — not in his discernment nor his belief; so it did not prevent him from conveying the message and discharging it; and it has not been reported that he said a statement and then retracted it.»

إِلْزَامُ السَّاحِرِ بِإِزَالَةِ مَا فَعَل Made to Undo It First

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«The magician, when he is known, is compelled to remove what he has done; so it is said to him: either you remove what you did, or your neck is struck. Then, when he has removed that thing, the one in authority kills him — because the magician is killed, upon the correct view, without being asked to repent, as ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه did.»

Note the order, which is a mercy toward the victim rather than toward the magician: the harm is undone first. The sentence does not take precedence over the Muslim who is still suffering.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He grounds it in Ḥafṣah’s deed — «and when Ḥafṣah the Mother of the Believers knew that a slave girl of hers had bewitched her… she killed her» — and the compiler’s footnote on it is doing two jobs at once.

the chainʿAbdullāh ibn Aḥmad in al-Masāʾil and al-Bayhaqī, from ʿUbaydullāh ibn ʿUmar from Nāfiʿ from Ibn ʿUmar from Ḥafṣah: a slave girl of hers bewitched her, and she confessed to the magic and brought it out, so she killed her. «وَسَنَدُهُ صَحِيح»; and Ibn Kathīr: «it is authentic that Ḥafṣah…»; and Mālik has it in al-Muwaṭṭaʾ as a balāgh. A third route, al-Ṭabarānī’s, al-Haythamī weakens through Ismāʿīl ibn ʿAyyāsh’s narrations from the Madīnans, «and the rest of its men are trustworthy».
the governanceThe same chain carries what happened next: «that reached ʿUthmān رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه and he became angry; so Ibn ʿUmar came to him and said: her slave girl bewitched her, she confessed to the magic and brought it out — and ʿUthmān desisted.» And the narrator’s own reading of it: «and it is as though his anger was only at her killing her without his command»

Read that second row twice. A Mother of the Believers, with a confession and the magic physically produced, acted — and the Caliph was angry, and the anger is reported to have been about acting without the authority. That is the fourth nullifier’s rule showing up as a fact of the first generation’s history, and it is on this page for that reason.

Note also the sequence in her case: the slave girl confessed and produced the magic — i.e. the harm was undone — and only then was she killed, which is exactly the rule Ibn Bāz stated above.

حُكْمُ النُّشْرَة Undoing Magic With Magic

This is the practical heart of the lesson, and the sharḥ gives it its own heading. A Muslim has been harmed. May he go to a magician to have it undone?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And whoever is afflicted with magic, it is not for him to treat himself with magic — for evil is not removed by evil, and disbelief is not removed by disbelief; rather evil is removed by good. And for this, when he — upon him be peace and blessings — was asked about النُّشْرَة, he said: «هِيَ مِنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَان» — it is of the work of the devil.»

«And the nushrah mentioned in the ḥadīth is: the undoing of magic from the bewitched person BY MAGIC.» — the ḥadīth is given here without a grading, so none is supplied.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«الشَّرُّ لَا يُزَالُ بِالشَّرّ» is the principle, and he gives three separate reasons under it:

it is shirk«as for treating it by the work of the magicians and their like, of what they draw near to the jinn by, by slaughter or other acts of devotion — this is not permitted, because it is of the work of the devil; rather it is of the GREATER SHIRK»
the mechanism«the undoing of magic at the hand of the magician is of the work of the devil, because he undoes it by supplication to other than Allah, and seeking rescue from other than Allah, and doing what Allah has forbidden»
what it leads to«and it leads to being pleased with what the magician said and with what he does of magic» — which is the matn’s own «أَوْ رَضِيَ بِهِ»

And likewise forbidden is «treating it by asking the soothsayers and fortune-tellers and charlatans, and using what they say; because they are not believed, and because they are lying reprobates who claim knowledge of the unseen and confuse people» — which is part 2’s material arriving where it bites.

لَيْسَ مِنَّا مَنْ سَحَرَ أَوْ سُحِرَ لَه

“He is not of us who performs magic, or has magic performed for him.”

قَالَ ابْنُ بَاز: «الحَدِيثُ صَحِيحٌ، رَوَاهُ الإِمَامُ أَحْمَدُ وَأَبُو دَاوُدَ بِإِسْنَادٍ جَيِّد» · a named grading

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And look at what that ḥadīth rules out by its second clause. «أَوْ سُحِرَ لَه» — or had magic performed for him. The man commissioning it is in the ḥadīth, not only the man performing it. Which is precisely the case of the person who goes to a magician to have his own magic undone.

Ibn Bāz uses it exactly so, and adds an a fortiori: «وَإِذَا كَانَ الكَاهِنُ فَالسَّاحِرُ مِنْ بَابِ أَوْلَى» — if the soothsayer carries the forty-day threat, then the magician all the more — «فَكَيْفَ بِالسَّاحِرِ الَّذِي يَعْبُدُ الشَّيَاطِينَ وَيَعْبُدُ الجِنّ؟»

العِلَاجُ مَا هُوَ بِضَرُورَة The Necessity Argument, Taken Apart

This is the argument everyone reaches for, and the sharḥ spends two folios on it. «They argue by necessity» — the questioner says it outright.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — They argue by necessity.

Ibn Bāz — «The medicine is not a necessity. Treatment is not a necessity. Treatment is a sunnah — it is recommended that one treat himself. It is not a necessity.»

Q — The statement of some of the jurists that it is permitted to undo magic with magic like it — and Imām Aḥmad and Saʿīd ibn al-Musayyib are said to have allowed it?

Ibn Bāz — «غَلَط — a mistake.» and: «هِيَ مِنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَان.»

That answer is short because the work is done underneath it, and the compiler supplies the work.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Taymiyyah, on why medical treatment is not a necessity at all — five reasons:

١«Many of the sick, or most of the sick, are healed without treatment» — especially country people and those in the far reaches of the earth, «Allah heals them by what He created in them of the natural powers in their bodies… and by what He makes easy for them of a kind of movement and work, or an answered supplication, or a beneficial recitation, or a strength of heart and good reliance». Whereas eating is necessary — «were he not to eat he would die»
٢Eating at necessity is obligatory; Masrūq: «whoever is compelled to the carrion and does not eat, and dies, enters the Fire». But treatment is not obligatory — and the proof is the black woman whom the Prophet ﷺ gave the choice between patience upon the affliction and entering the Garden, and supplication for health — «فَاخْتَارَتِ البَلَاءَ وَالجَنَّة»; «and had the removal of illness been obligatory, there would have been no place for the choice… and I do not know of any predecessor who made treatment obligatory»
٣The medicine is not certain — «in many illnesses it is not even supposed that it will repel the illness; for were that consistent, no one would die» — unlike food against hunger, which is certain
٤An illness has many medicines — «so if it is not repelled by the forbidden one, he moves to the lawful one; and it is impossible that there be no cure or medicine for it among the lawful; and He who sent down the disease sent down for every disease a cure, except death» — and it is not permissible that the cures be in the forbidden category, «and He is the Kind, the Merciful»
٥«and in it is the fiqh of the chapter» — hunger has one remover; illness is removed by many kinds of causes, outward and inward, spiritual and bodily — «so the medicine is not determined as the remover», and even the specific remedy «is hidden from most people», so that a specialist may spend much of his life learning it and still the kind of the illness and its cure be hidden from him. «So the causes removing illness part from the causes removing hunger in these plain realities — and so their rulings parted.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Permanent Committee names the condition inside the qāʿidah itself, which is what the argument from necessity always skips:

«The rule «الضَّرُورَاتُ تُبِيحُ المَحْظُورَات»… it is a condition of this rule that the forbidden thing be LESS than the necessity, as the scholars of uṣūl have established. And since magic is disbelief and shirk, it is a greater harm… and magic can be treated by the legislated causes, so there is no compulsion to treat it by what is disbelief and shirk.»

And ʿAllāmah Muḥammad ibn Ibrāhīm, on those who permitted the nushrah for necessity, in one sentence: «And magic is forbidden, and disbelief. So is disbelief to be committed so that sick or afflicted souls may live? — along with the fact that in most cases the bewitched person dies or his mind is disordered. So the Messenger ﷺ forbade and shut the door, and did not make a distinction — not in the work of the devil, and not in the bewitched person.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the two prophetic wordings he cites for the general rule: «عِبَادَ اللهِ تَدَاوَوْا، وَلَا تَدَاوَوْا بِحَرَام» — O slaves of Allah, treat yourselves, and do not treat yourselves with what is forbidden — and «إِنَّ اللهَ لَمْ يَجْعَلْ شِفَاءَكُمْ فِيمَا حَرَّمَ عَلَيْكُم» — Allah has not placed your cure in what He has forbidden you. The sharḥ gives the first as «قَالَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ» and the second as «رُوِيَ عَنْه», with no gradings at this place, so none are supplied.

وَإِذَا لَمْ يُشْفَ بِالقِرَاءَة And When the Recitation Does Not Heal

This is the pastoral half, and it is the reason the page is not merely polemic. The real situation is not a man choosing between two treatments. It is a man who has tried the lawful one, and nothing happened, and is now desperate.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And if the sick person was not healed by the recitation — medicine likewise does not necessarily entail healing; because not every treatment benefits and achieves the intended end. Allah may defer the healing to a long term, and a person may die of this illness; and it is not a condition of treatment that the person be healed.»

«And that is not an excuse — if he was treated by someone with recitation and no healing appeared to him — to turn to the magicians; because the one under obligation is commanded to take up the legislated and permitted causes, and forbidden from taking up the forbidden causes.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the sentence that reframes the whole expectation: «and many people are not benefited by the causes, nor by the recitation with the Qurʾān nor anything else, for want of the conditions being met and the impediments being absent. And were every sick person to be healed by the recitation or the medicine, no one would die; but Allah — glorified is He — is the One in whose hand is the healing: if He wills it, He makes its causes easy; and if He does not will it, the causes do not benefit him.»

And then he proves it from the one case nobody can answer. «And in his final illness ﷺ, ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا used to recite these three sūrahs [al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and al-Nās] into his hands ﷺ, then wipe with them his head and his face and his chest, hoping for their blessing and what had occurred in them of recitation — and he ﷺ passed away in that illness of his; because Allah — glorified is He — did not will his healing from that illness.»

The best recitation there has ever been, over the best man there has ever been, by the hand of his wife — and he died of it. If that is not a failure of the ruqyah, then nothing in your house is either.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — A matter by which the trial has become general and whose affair has grown grave — going to the magicians to undo magic.

Ibn Bāz — «هَذَا مُنْكَر.»

Q — Many of them go to the magicians and come out with no result, so they bear the sin [for nothing].

Ibn Bāz — He affirmed it, and said: «Yes. And some of them — whisperings, and things he imagined that are not real; and this one is being tried with the devil’s play and the devil’s whisperings.»

That is a remarkable thing to find in this chapter and it is kindly meant. Much of what a frightened person is certain is magic is not magic at all — it is waswasah and imagination. The man who said magic is real and that denying it is kufr is the same man saying most of what you are afraid of is not it.

أَفَلَا تَنَشَّرْت The One Report Used Against All of This

The objection is from al-Bukhārī, in the Book of Medicine and the Book of Adab, in the story of the magic: the thing was extracted at the Prophet’s ﷺ command, and ʿĀʾishah said — «O Messenger of Allah, so why did you not…?» and the wording that follows is the whole dispute.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The Prophet ﷺ answered: «أَمَّا اللهُ فَقَدْ شَفَانِي، وَأَمَّا أَنَا فَأَكْرَهُ أَنْ أُثِيرَ عَلَى النَّاسِ شَرًّا» — “As for Allah, He has healed me; and as for me, I dislike to stir up evil against the people.” And of the well: «this is the well I was shown — and it was as though its water were the steeping of henna, and as though its palms were the heads of devils.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The compiler’s answer has four steps, and the third is the finding.

١«There is nothing in the ḥadīth indicating the permissibility of the nushrah which is of the work of the devil; and whoever says that ʿĀʾishah’s intent was this — i.e. why did you not go to a magician to have the magic undone — upon him is the proof.»
٢The word is an insertion. «Then his saying “تَنَشَّرْت” is مُدْرَجَة, from the words of Ibn ʿUyaynah, as is clear from the context of the ḥadīth in al-Ḥumaydī’s narration.» Ibn Ḥajar in al-Fatḥ: «so the one who explained what was intended by her saying “أَفَلَا” — it is as though he did not recall the exact word, so he gave it by its meaning»
٣And it may not be from النُّشْرَة at all. Ibn Ḥajar: «and it is possible that it is from النَّشْر meaning extraction, so it agrees with the narration of whoever narrated it with the wording “فَهَلَّا أَخْرَجْتَه” — why did you not bring it out»; and he reconciles the two wordings: «the extraction that occurred was of the root of the magic, and the extraction denied was of the parts of the magic»
٤And even granting the word, it carries three meanings: the shayṭānī nushrah; the legislated nushrah; or النَّشْر in the sense of making a thing public and bringing it out. «وَلَيْسَ فِي الحَدِيثِ مَا يُرَجِّح» — and there is nothing in the ḥadīth to make one of them preponderant, so there is no proof in it

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ibn Bāz was asked about ʿĀʾishah’s words directly, and answered with a distinction in three words: «النَّشْرُ غَيْرُ السِّحْر» — undoing is not the same as magic.

A questioner then put it as a matter of propriety — is it conceivable that ʿĀʾishah would propose to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that he be treated by the work of a magician? I hold this to be impossible with respect to the Messenger ﷺ. And the Shaykh affirmed him and said: «لَا يُمْكِن» — it is not possible — citing «لَيْسَ مِنَّا مَنْ سَحَرَ أَوْ سُحِرَ لَه».

مَسَائِلُ خَاتِمَة Three Closing Questions

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — The difference between the one bewitched and the insane?

Ibn Bāz — «The insane is the one whom a jinnī enters into, only, until his mind is altered. And the bewitched is the one to whom harm occurs — meaning: the magicians from among mankind harm him by what they have gained from the jinn, as happened with Labīd ibn al-Aʿṣam when he bewitched the Prophet ﷺ.»

Two different things with two different agents. In possession, the jinnī acts on the man directly. In magic, a human being acts, using what he has bought from the jinn. Which is why one of them has a criminal and the other does not.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Is the magician burned with fire?

Ibn Bāz — «No one is burned with fire, because the Messenger ﷺ forbade that, and said: «إِنَّ النَّارَ لَا يُعَذِّبُ بِهَا إِلَّا الله» — none punishes with fire but Allah. Rather he is killed with the sword.»

The sharḥ gives that prophetic wording here without a grading, so none is supplied. And notice the shape of the answer in a chapter this severe: the ruling is death, and even so the manner is restrained by a prohibition — because a mode of punishment belongs to Allah.

هَارُوتُ وَمَارُوت Two Angels, or Two Kings?

وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَى الْمَلَكَيْنِ بِبَابِلَ هَارُوتَ وَمَارُوتَ

…and what was sent down upon the two angels at Bābil, Hārūt and Mārūt.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:102

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — Are Hārūt and Mārūt two angels or two human beings? We hope for a statement of the preponderant view.

Ibn Bāz — «The scholars differed in this, and the more apparent is that they are two angels, sent down as a trial and a test, as Allah said… And some of the people of knowledge said: they are two kings from the children of Ādam, by whom the people were tried. And the first statement is the more apparent; and the recitation upon this — on the first statement — is with fatḥ of the lām [المَلَكَيْن, the two angels], and on the second with kasr [المَلِكَيْن, the two kings].»

That is a precise thing and worth seeing. The difference of opinion is not merely about history — it is carried by a single vowel, and each reading belongs to one of the two views.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the stories. A large body of reports circulates about Hārūt and Mārūt and al-Zuhrah — the woman they are said to have desired, who was turned into the planet. The compiler’s verdict: «وَلَا يَصِحُّ فِي البَابِ شَيْءٌ مَرْفُوع» — nothing in the chapter is authentic as a prophetic report.

Ibn Kathīr traces it: the nearest it comes to is a report of Ibn ʿUmar from Kaʿb al-Aḥbār — «so the ḥadīth revolved and returned to the transmission of Kaʿb al-Aḥbār from the books of the Children of Isrāʾīl». The marfūʿ routes: Abū Ḥātim — «هَذَا حَدِيثٌ مُنْكَر»; al-Bazzār on Zuhayr — «he was not a ḥāfiẓ»; and the later routes rest on narrators the compiler shows to be مَسْتُورُ الحَال or «very weak».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ibn Bāz’s own verdict, which is a model of how to handle an isrāʾīlī report:

«ʿAbd al-Razzāq’s chain here is good — and this is of what [Ibn ʿUmar] narrated from Kaʿb al-Aḥbār; and Kaʿb al-Aḥbār narrates from the Children of Isrāʾīl the lean and the fat, the false and the sound — so he is not relied upon and not depended upon. As for [its being] from the Prophet ﷺ — it is a weak ḥadīth.»

«So the upshot is that it is not established from the Prophet ﷺ; rather it is of the reports of the Children of Isrāʾīl, in which is the lean and the fat and the false and the sound and the lie — so they are not depended upon.» And of the whole business: «and these reports of theirs are differing, various and mutually contradictory — so no reliance is placed on them; reliance is placed only on the text of the Mighty Book, and Allah knows best what He intended… and there is in them no text from the Messenger explaining that.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then two things which show what the discipline is actually for.

First, he says what matters instead: «and the important thing in this is that He made clear — glorified is He — that it is disbelief, and that the knowledge of magic and its teaching is disbelief… and it is a trial and a test.» Which returns the Hārūt and Mārūt verse to the one thing it is cited in the matn for.

Second — and this is the part worth imitating — he says what the story would teach if it were sound, without accepting it: «then this, if it were authentic, in it is the indication of the foulness of wine, and that it is a means to every evil — we seek refuge with Allah: a means to killing, a means to shirk, a means to fornication, a means to every corruption. It is أُمُّ الخَبَائِث — the mother of all foulness.»

And he refuses one more report in passing. Asked about what is related from Ibn ʿUmar, that whenever he saw al-Zuhrah he cursed it: «لَيْسَ بِصَحِيح» — it is not authentic.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

النَّاقِضُ السَّابِع is closed — thirty-five folios, printed ١٥٩–١٩٣, the longest clause in the book.

Part 1 — what magic is: named for concealment; teaching and learning both kufr by the daleel; the kawnī permission and «نُعَالِجَ قَدَرَ اللهِ بِقَدَرِه»; and why it is shirk — the slaughter, vows and prostration it is bought with.
Part 2 — the ruling: two grounds, the تَفْصِيل he grants and the أَصْل he keeps, killed as a disbeliever and not as a ḥadd, the repentance that is refused in court and accepted by Allah, and the famous ḥadīth of the ḥadd graded weak by seven named scholars.
Part 3 — the harm and the remedy: six objections answered, النُّشْرَة forbidden with its three reasons, the necessity argument dismantled, the recitation that does not heal, and the isrāʾīlī stories refused.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
أَثَّرَ السِّحْرُ فِي عَقْلِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ وَتَمْيِيزِه. The magic affected the Prophet’s ﷺ intellect and discernment.
العِصْمَةُ المَذْكُورَةُ فِي الآيَةِ تَعْنِي أَنَّهُ لَا يُصِيبُهُ أَذًى بَدَنِيّ. The protection in the verse means no bodily harm befalls him.
قَوْلُهُمْ: خَبَرُ الآحَادِ لَا يُؤْخَذُ بِهِ فِي العَقِيدَةِ بِدْعَة. The claim that a solitary report is not taken in ʿaqīdah is an innovation.
النُّشْرَةُ هِيَ حَلُّ السِّحْرِ عَنِ المَسْحُورِ بِالسِّحْر. Al-nushrah is the undoing of magic from the bewitched by magic.
العِلَاجُ ضَرُورَةٌ تُبِيحُ المَحْظُور. Treatment is a necessity that permits the forbidden.
إِذَا لَمْ تَنْفَعِ الرُّقْيَةُ جَازَ الذَّهَابُ إِلَى السَّحَرَة. If the recitation does not benefit, going to the magicians becomes permitted.
لَا يَصِحُّ فِي قِصَّةِ الزُّهَرَةِ شَيْءٌ مَرْفُوعٌ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ. Nothing in the story of al-Zuhrah is authentic as a report traced to the Prophet ﷺ.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

الشَّرُّ لَا يُزَالُ بِالشَّرّ، وَالكُفْرُ لَا يُزَالُ بِالكُفْر، وَإِنَّمَا يُزَالُ الشَّرُّ بِالخَيْر

Ibn Bāz, on undoing magic by magic. It is the whole of these fifteen folios in one line, and it is the answer to the argument from desperation — which these folios take seriously rather than brushing aside: «العِلَاجُ مَا هُوَ بِضَرُورَة، العِلَاجُ سُنَّة». And keep beside it the sentence that makes the whole page bearable, because it is about the Prophet ﷺ himself: ʿĀʾishah recited the three sūrahs over his hands in his final illness, «وَتُوُفِّيَ ﷺ فِي مَرَضِهِ ذَلِك؛ لِأَنَّ اللهَ سُبْحَانَهُ لَمْ يُرِدْ شِفَاءَهُ مِنْ ذَلِكَ المَرَض.» A ruqyah that does not heal is not a failed ruqyah. It is a decree.
