الإيمان بالكتب والرسل

Belief in the Books & the Messengers

Year 2+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • Name the revealed Books mentioned in the Quran and who received them
  • Explain the Quran's unique status as preserved and abrogating
  • State what belief in the Messengers requires
  • Distinguish a nabī from a rasūl, and name the five Ulū al-ʿAzm
الركن الثالث: الإيمان بالكتب Pillar 3 — Belief in the Revealed Books

Allah did not leave creation without guidance. He revealed Books (الكُتُب) to His messengers — the speech of Allah, sent down as light and guidance. Belief in them means: to be certain they truly came from Allah, to believe in those named for us specifically, and to believe in the rest in general.

لَقَدْ أَرْسَلْنَا رُسُلَنَا بِالْبَيِّنَاتِ وَأَنزَلْنَا مَعَهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْمِيزَانَ لِيَقُومَ النَّاسُ بِالْقِسْطِ

"We sent Our messengers with clear proofs, and sent down with them the Book and the Balance, so that people may uphold justice."

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd 57:25 · سورة الحديد

الكتب التي سُمّيت في القرآن The Books Named in the Quran
الصُّحُف

The Scriptures of Ibrāhīm & Mūsā (Ṣuḥuf)

Mentioned in Sūrat al-Aʿlā 87:19 — "the scriptures of Ibrāhīm and Mūsā."

التَّوْرَاة

The Tawrāt — given to Mūsā (Moses) ﷺ

"Indeed We sent down the Tawrāt, in which was guidance and light." (5:44)

الزَّبُور

The Zabūr — given to Dāwūd (David) ﷺ

"...and We gave Dāwūd the Zabūr." (4:163)

الإِنْجِيل

The Injīl — given to ʿĪsā (Jesus) ﷺ

"And We gave him the Injīl, in which was guidance and light." (5:46)

القُرْآن

The Qurʾān — given to Muhammad ﷺ

The final revelation, confirming and superseding what came before.

Crucial point: We believe the original Tawrāt, Zabūr, and Injīl were true revelation from Allah. But the books in people's hands today have been changed (taḥrīf) over time — added to, altered, and lost. So we do not take our religion from them. The Quran alone remains perfectly preserved.

تميّز القرآن What Makes the Quran Unique

محفوظ

Divinely preserved

"Indeed We sent down the Reminder, and indeed We are its Guardian." (15:9)

مُهيمن

Confirming & superseding

It confirms the truth in earlier books and is the final authority over them. (5:48)

للناس كافة

For all humanity

Earlier books were for specific nations; the Quran is for all people until the Last Day.

الركن الرابع: الإيمان بالرسل Pillar 4 — Belief in the Messengers

Allah chose the best of humanity to carry His message. Belief in the Messengers means: to believe Allah sent a messenger to every nation, all calling to the same core message — tawhid (back to Unit 1!); to believe in those named for us and the rest in general; and to know that the final messenger is Muhammad ﷺ, the seal of the prophets.

وَلَقَدْ بَعَثْنَا فِي كُلِّ أُمَّةٍ رَّسُولًا أَنِ اعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ وَاجْتَنِبُوا الطَّاغُوتَ

"We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, saying: Worship Allah and avoid false gods."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:36 · سورة النحل

The Quran names 25 prophets, though the total is far greater. They are human beings — they eat, sleep, and die — given no share of divinity. We must believe in all of them; rejecting even one messenger is to reject them all (26:105).

الفرق بين النبي والرسول · أولو العزم Prophet vs. Messenger · The Five of Firm Resolve

نَبِيّ

Nabī — Prophet

One given revelation, often to continue and uphold the law of a messenger before him.

رَسُول

Rasūl — Messenger

One given revelation and a new message/law to convey to a people. Every rasūl is a nabī, but not every nabī is a rasūl.

The greatest of the messengers are the five Ulū al-ʿAzm (those of firm resolve), named together in 33:7 and 42:13:

نُوح إِبْرَاهِيم مُوسَى عِيسَى مُحَمَّد ﷺ

Nūḥ · Ibrāhīm · Mūsā · ʿĪsā · Muhammad ﷺ — Muhammad ﷺ being the final and greatest, the seal of the prophets (33:40).
