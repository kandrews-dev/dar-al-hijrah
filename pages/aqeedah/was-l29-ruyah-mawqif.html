الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ التَّاسِعُ وَالعِشْرُونYear 3

الرُّؤْيَةُ فِي السُّنَّةِ · وَمَوْقِفُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّة

The Seeing in the Sunnah, and the Position of Ahl al-Sunnah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٠٦–١٠٨
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 106–108.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State precisely what the comparison with the full moon is comparing — and what it is not.
  • Give both narrations of لَا تُضَامُّونَ and the meaning they share.
  • Say why those two prayers in particular are named in a ḥadīth about the seeing.
  • Reproduce the four denials in the author’s statement of the position of Ahl al-Sunnah.
٨ · إِثْبَاتُ رُؤْيَةِ المُؤْمِنِينَ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَوْمَ القِيَامَة Section 8 — The Believers Seeing Their Lord on the Day of Rising

Part 1 ended on the ruʾyah, and so does Part 2 — the same doctrine, reached by the other route. Lesson 21 took it from four āyāt and answered the three objections. Here one ḥadīth does the work, and it adds something the āyāt did not: a comparison, and a prescription.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ كَمَا تَرَوْنَ القَمَرَ لَيْلَةَ البَدْرِ، لَا تُضَامُّونَ فِي رُؤْيَتِهِ. فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَنْ لَا تُغْلَبُوا عَلَى صَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَصَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا فَافْعَلُوا» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ كَمَا تَرَوْنَ القَمَرَ لَيْلَةَ البَدْرِ، لَا تُضَامُّونَ فِي رُؤْيَتِهِ. فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَنْ لَا تُغْلَبُوا عَلَى صَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ وَصَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا فَافْعَلُوا

"You will surely see your Lord as you see the moon on the night it is full; you will not be wronged in seeing Him. So if you are able not to be overcome regarding a prayer before the rising of the sun and a prayer before its setting, then do so."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ — the address is to the believers; and the السِّين is the sīn of futurity, and emphasis is intended by it. تَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ — that is: you will behold Him with your eyes.

And the aḥādīth that have come affirming the believers' seeing of their Lord are مُتَوَاتِرَة — mass-transmitted. That is the strongest category in the science of ḥadīth, above even the single ṣaḥīḥ report, and the student met the term in Lesson 21 where al-Fawzān used it of this same doctrine.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

كَمَا تَرَوْنَ القَمَرَ لَيْلَةَ البَدْرِ — that is: the night of its completeness, and it is the fourteenth night of the month, for on that night it has been filled with light.

And now the sentence that the entire comparison hangs on, and which a careless reader will get backwards. What is intended by this comparison is the establishing of the seeing and the emphasising of it, and the negating of figurativeness from it —

وَهُوَ تَشْبِيهٌ لِلرُّؤْيَةِ بِالرُّؤْيَةِ، لَا تَشْبِيهٌ لِلْمَرْئِيِّ بِالمَرْئِيِّ

— it is a likening of the seeing to the seeing, not a likening of the One seen to the thing seen — because He سُبْحَانَهُ is لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ.

Read it slowly. The ḥadīth does not say your Lord is like the moon. The kāf attaches to the verb, not to the object: you will see Him as you see the moon — that is, with your eyes, with certainty, with no crowding and no dispute about it. The comparison is between two acts of seeing, not between two things seen.

And notice what that does to the usual objection. The muʿaṭṭilah hear a comparison and conclude tamthīl. But the thing compared here is our vision, and our vision really is like our vision. The ḥadīth likens the clarity, and al-Shūrā 42:11 governs the rest — which is precisely the discipline of Lesson 6, now applied to a simile rather than to an attribute.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

لَا تُضَامُّونَ فِي رُؤْيَتِهِ — and al-Fawzān gives both ways it is transmitted, because the vowelling changes the picture:

لَا تُضَامُونَ
بِضَمِّ التَّاءِ وَتَخْفِيفِ المِيمِ		from الضَّيْم, wrong — no wrong will befall you, such that some of you see Him and others do not
لَا تَضَامُّونَ
بِفَتْحِ التَّاءِ وَتَشْدِيدِ المِيمِ		from التَّضَامّ, crowding together — you will not press up against one another in order to see Him; you will not be gathered into one place for it so that there is a crush

And the meaning upon both narrations is: that you will see Him with a seeing that is realised — each one of you seeing Him while he is in his own place.

Which is the answer to a question nobody dares ask out loud: how will everyone get a view? The ḥadīth settles it in a single word, on either reading. No queue, no obstruction, no one at the back.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتُمْ أَنْ لَا تُغْلَبُوا — that is: that you do not become overcome — عَلَى صَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ طُلُوعِ الشَّمْسِ, and it is the Fajr prayer; وَصَلَاةٍ قَبْلَ غُرُوبِهَا, and it is the ʿAṣr prayer; فَافْعَلُوا — that is: guard these two prayers in congregation, in their times.

And why these two, in a ḥadīth about seeing Allah? al-Fawzān gives the chain: and he singled out these two prayers because of the gathering of the angels at them, so they are the most excellent of the prayers — so it was fitting that whoever guards them should be recompensed with the most excellent of gifts, which is the looking at the Face of Allah تَعَالَى.

The most excellent prayers → the most excellent gift. And notice where the two prayers fall: the one before the sun rises and the one before it sets — the two hardest to reach, at the two ends of the day. The ḥadīth does not merely report a doctrine; it names the price and tells you to pay it.

And the shāhid from the ḥadīth: the affirming of the believers' seeing of their Lord with their own eyes on the Day of Rising — and the mention of who opposed in that, along with the refutation of him, has already come, at the āyāt of the ruʾyah in Lesson 21.

مَوْقِفُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ مِنْ هَذِهِ الأَحَادِيث The Position of Ahl al-Sunnah toward These Aḥādīth

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

إِلَى أَمْثَالِ هَذِهِ الأَحَادِيثِ الَّتِي يُخْبِرُ فِيهَا رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ عَنْ رَبِّهِ بِمَا يُخْبِرُ بِهِ. فَإِنَّ الفِرْقَةَ النَّاجِيَةَ أَهْلَ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِذَلِكَ كَمَا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِمَا أَخْبَرَ اللهُ بِهِ فِي كِتَابِهِ، مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍ وَلَا تَعْطِيلٍ، وَمِنْ غَيْرِ تَكْيِيفٍ وَلَا تَمْثِيلٍ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is a statement of the position of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah toward the aḥādīth of the attributes that have come from the Messenger ﷺ — that it is like their position toward the āyāt of the attributes in the Qurʾān, exactly the same.

And that equivalence is the point of the whole of Part 2. The author did not need a second methodology for the Sunnah. The same four doors are closed, in the same order — and the student met them in Lesson 4, before a single proof-text had been brought.

And it is: believing in them, and holding as creed what they indicate, upon its reality —

مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍthey do not turn them away from their apparent meaning by the kinds of false taʾwīl
وَلَا تَعْطِيلٍand they do not negate what they indicate, thereby stripping them
وَلَا تَكْيِيفٍand they do not ask how — which L04 showed is not merely forbidden but impossible
وَلَا تَمْثِيلٍand they do not liken the attributes mentioned in them to the attributes of the created — because Allah is لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then al-Fawzān names the two parties this position stands between — the same two that the next part of the book will set out in full:

— And by that they differ from the way of the innovators, of the Jahmiyyah, the Muʿtazilah and the Ashāʿirah, whose position toward these texts was the position of one who denies them, or one who reinterprets what they indicate.

— And contrary to the Mushabbihah, who went to excess in the affirmation until they likened Allah to His creation — تَعَالَى اللهُ عَمَّا يَقُولُونَ عُلُوًّا كَبِيرًا.

Two errors in opposite directions, and the position of Ahl al-Sunnah is not a midpoint arrived at by splitting the difference. It is what you are left holding when you accept every text and add nothing. The deniers subtracted; the likeners added; and the people of the Sunnah reported.

With this the evidence from the Sunnah is complete — eight sections, and the position toward them stated. What follows in Part 3 is the author's account of why this middle standing is where the people of the Sunnah are found on every question, not only this one: between the Jabriyyah and the Qadariyyah on Allah's actions, between the Murjiʾah and the Waʿīdiyyah on His threat, and between the Rāfiḍah and the Khawārij on the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

إِنَّكُمْ سَتَرَوْنَ رَبَّكُمْ كَمَا تَرَوْنَ القَمَرَ لَيْلَةَ البَدْرِ

Memorize the ḥadīth with al-Fawzān's clause attached, or you will misread it the first time someone presses you: a likening of the seeing to the seeing, not of the One seen to the thing seen. And then notice what the ḥadīth asks of you in return — two prayers, at the two hardest hours of the day.
