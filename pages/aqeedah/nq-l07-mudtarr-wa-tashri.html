النَّاقِضُ الرَّابِع · المُضْطَرُّ وَالتَّشْرِيع

The Fourth Nullifier, Part 3 — Necessity, Contentment, Legislation

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the ruling on a man who cannot reach his right except by litigating before a court that judges by man-made codes — and the three limits attached to it in the same answer.
  • Quote Ibn Bāz’s two sentences on the story of ʿUmar — «وَفِي القِصَّتَيْنِ نَظَر» and «فَالمَعْنَى صَحِيحٌ مِنْ حَيْثُ المَعْنَى» — and say what each one does.
  • Give the verse that actually carries the ruling on one who hates what Allah sent down.
  • Say why the compiler warns against one printed edition of شَرْحُ كِتَابِ التَّوْحِيد.
  • Explain what Ibn Bāz says the words «إِنَّكَ لَتُعْطِي مَنْ شِئْت» amounted to, and why he reads them as the speech of the munāfiqūn.
  • State what التَّشْرِيع is and is not: who has it, and whether الشَّارِع is a name of Allah.
  • Use the principle الرَّسُولُ مُبَلِّغ to explain how the Messenger ﷺ can be called a legislator without the tawḥīd being touched.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٣٢–١٣٥
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 132–135, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الرَّابِع: مَنِ اعْتَقَدَ أَنَّ غَيْرَ هُدَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ أَكْمَلُ مِنْ هُدَاه، أَوْ أَنَّ حُكْمَ غَيْرِهِ أَحْسَنُ مِنْ حُكْمِه — كَالَّذِينَ يُفَضِّلُونَ حُكْمَ الطَّوَاغِيتِ عَلَى حُكْمِه — فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ.

The clause itself, for the third and last time. Part 1 established the ruling, part 2 gave the تَفْصِيل that governs how it is applied, and these four folios close it with three narrower questions the book heads itself.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Four folios, three headings, and the book sets them. This lesson follows the source’s own order rather than imposing one: the man under compulsion, the man who is not pleased with the ruling of the Messenger ﷺ, and legislation.

Read in that order they do something deliberate. The clause began at its most severe, spent twelve folios on restraint, and ends by naming the one man it does not reach at all — and then returns the whole subject to where it started, which is not politics but tawḥīd.

حُكْمُ المُضْطَرِّ لِلتَّحَاكُمِ إِلَى القَانُون The Man Who Cannot Reach His Right Any Other Way

The question put to him is specific, and it is the ordinary situation of most Muslims on earth: a man has a right — a debt owed him, a property taken from him — and the only court that will hear him judges by a man-made code. He cannot reach his right unless he goes to it. Is he a disbeliever?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«So I refer to your request for a fatwā… in which you ask about the ruling on one who refers his case to one who judges by man-made codes, and who cannot reach his right unless he refers it to them — is he a disbeliever? And I inform you that if he is compelled to that, he is not a disbeliever. But it is not for him to refer his case to them except at the point of necessity, when obtaining his right is not made possible for him except by that; and it is not for him to take other than what the purified Sharīʿah makes lawful. May Allah grant all of us success.»

That is the whole answer, and notice how much of it is condition. One sentence relieves him; three qualify it. The relief is for the man who is compelled — not for the man who finds that court convenient, and not for the man who prefers it, who is the subject of the clause in the first place.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

the rulingHe is not a disbeliever — «لَا يَكُونُ كَافِرًا».
limit ١Only at necessity — «إِلَّا عِنْدَ الضَّرُورَة». Not as a first resort and not as a preference.
limit ٢Only where obtaining his right is not possible otherwise — if another route exists, the necessity does not.
limit ٣He may not take what the Sharīʿah does not make lawful for him. The court’s permission is not a title. If it awards him ribā, or a share the Sharīʿah does not give him, the necessity that let him enter does not let him collect.

Limit ٣ is the one that is usually dropped, and it is the one that keeps the answer honest. A man may go in to recover what is his. He does not come out with more than his.

حُكْمُ مَنْ لَمْ يَرْضَ بِحُكْمِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ Not Being Pleased With His Ruling

The second heading is where this clause meets the heart. The sharḥ brings it through a story — ʿUmar killing a man who refused arbitration before the Prophet ﷺ — and what Ibn Bāz does with that story is the most instructive thing on these four folios.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And in the two stories there is something to be examined — but they are two witnesses to the deed of the munāfiqūn… and for this reason, in this narration from ʿUmar, he rebuked this man who sought arbitration before other than the Prophet ﷺ, and held that he had apostatised by that, and for this he killed him. So the meaning is sound from the standpoint of the meaning — for whoever is not pleased with the ruling of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is a disbeliever. So what is obligatory is contentment with the law of Allah and contentment with the ruling of Allah. And whoever hates the ruling of Allah is a disbeliever.»

Then the verse.

ذَٰلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ كَرِهُوا مَا أَنزَلَ اللَّهُ فَأَحْبَطَ أَعْمَالَهُمْ

That is because they hated what Allah sent down, so He rendered their deeds worthless.

سُورَةُ مُحَمَّد · 47:9

Read the two sentences in order, because the order is the lesson. He says the story has something in it to be examined — «وَفِي القِصَّتَيْنِ نَظَر» — and then he says the meaning is sound. The ruling is not resting on the story. It is resting on the verse, and the story is brought as an illustration of what the munāfiqūn did.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is worth taking as a habit, not just a datum. A weak or questioned report that illustrates a ruling established elsewhere is used as an illustration. It does not become the proof. Quoting «the meaning is sound» while dropping «there is something to be examined in it» turns a shaykh’s careful sentence into a licence he did not give — and this clause, of all the ten, is the one where that is done most often.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the compiler attaches a warning here that belongs on this page. One printed edition of Ibn Bāz’s شَرْحُ كِتَابِ التَّوْحِيد — the Dār al-Ḍiyāʾ print, with the taḥqīq of al-ʿAlāwī — carries at this place the sentence «and the story of ʿUmar demonstrates that referring judgement to other than the law of Allah is disbelief and apostasy». The compiler says plainly: «I did not find this quotation in the recorded lecture that they relied upon in printing the sharḥ; and in any case this edition has deficiency and distortion in the wordings, and perhaps in the meanings also, so it ought not to be relied upon.»

A sentence that strong, attributed to Ibn Bāz, on this clause of all clauses — and the book’s own compiler says the edition carrying it is unreliable and the recording does not have it. This is the discipline of ḥadīth applied to a modern print, and it is why the pages of this site name their folios.

كَلَامُ المُنَافِقِينَ عِنْدَ القَسْم Two Remarks, and What They Really Said

Having said the stories are witnesses to the deed of the munāfiqūn, the sharḥ gives two incidents of exactly that kind. Both are about distribution of wealth, and in both the words sound like complaint until Ibn Bāz unpacks what they assert.

فَوَجَدْتُ عِنْدَهُ رَجُلًا يَسْأَلُهُ وَرَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ يَقُول: «لَا أَجِدُ مَا أُعْطِيك»، فَتَوَلَّى الرَّجُلُ عَنْهُ وَهُوَ مُغْضَبٌ وَهُوَ يَقُول: لَعَمْرِي إِنَّكَ لَتُعْطِي مَنْ شِئْت. فَقَالَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ: «يَغْضَبُ عَلَيَّ أَنْ لَا أَجِدَ مَا أُعْطِيَه!»

“I found with him a man asking him, and the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was saying, ‘I do not have anything to give you.’ So the man turned away from him angry, saying: by my life, you give to whomever you wish! And the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: ‘He is angry with me that I do not have anything to give him!’”

يُفَسِّرُهُ الشَّيْخُ فِي شَرْحِ سُنَنِ أَبِي دَاوُد · the sharḥ gives no grading at this place, so none is supplied here

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz’s reading of it: «this shows that some people, in most cases, if they are not given what they want, will speak — even if the one withholding is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ.» Then he takes the man’s actual sentence apart:

«Look at this man saying “you give to whomever you wish” — after the Prophet ﷺ had excused himself to him. Its meaning is: you are not truthful. You give to whom you wish — but not because of a lack of what to give; rather you give to whom you wish and leave whom you wish. Its meaning is accusing the Prophet ﷺ of not being truthful in his saying that he does not have what to give him; and nobody says this but a hypocrite, of the people of hypocrisy well known in Madīnah.»

That is the move worth learning. The surface of the sentence is a grievance about money. Its content is a denial of the Prophet’s ﷺ truthfulness — and that, not the anger, is what the ruling attaches to. The same clause runs through the whole nullifier: what is being asserted, not how loudly.

لَمَّا قَسَمَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ قِسْمَةَ حُنَيْنٍ قَالَ رَجُلٌ مِنَ الأَنْصَار: مَا أَرَادَ بِهَا وَجْهَ اللهِ.

“When the Prophet ﷺ made the division of Ḥunayn, a man from the Anṣār said: he did not intend the Face of Allah by it.”

حَدِيثُ أَبِي وَائِلٍ عَنْ عَبْدِ اللهِ · the sharḥ quotes it here without a grading, and none is supplied here

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz on it, in four words: «هَذَا مِنَ المُنَافِقِين» — “this is from the munāfiqūn.”

And the reason is the same as the first. To say of the Prophet ﷺ «he did not intend the Face of Allah by it» is not a complaint about a share. It is a verdict passed on his intention — which is the one thing no created person may pass a verdict on, and the thing the munāfiqūn of Madīnah were marked by.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he draws something out of the first incident that has nothing to do with takfīr at all. «The intent is that in this there is a consolation for one who sees in the state of people what he sees — of their agitation, and their greed, and their failure to excuse the one who excuses himself to them.»

A man was ungrateful to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to his face, over nothing. So if you are treated that way, you are not seeing something new under the sun. That is a ʿaqīdah book handing you something to live with.

تَوْحِيدُ الحُكْم Ruling Belongs to Him As Worship Does

Then the sharḥ returns the whole subject to where the matn put it. Not to constitutions — to tawḥīd. And it does so with a sentence that puts worship and ruling in one breath.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«So the intent is: there is no avoiding the tawḥīd of Allah, by singling Him out for worship and disowning the worship of other than Him and those who worship other than Him; and there is no avoiding the belief that shirk is false, and that it is obligatory upon all the slaves, of jinn and men, to devote worship to Allah alone, and to discharge the right of this tawḥīd by referring judgement to the law of Allah — for Allah is the Ruler; and of His tawḥīd is faith and affirmation of that. He is the Ruler in this world by His Sharīʿah, and in the Hereafter by Himself.»

And the explicit parallel: «just as worship is for Allah alone, likewise ruling is His alone.»

The verses he cites, in his order. The first is the clause he repeats most — «إِنِ الْحُكْمُ إِلَّا لِلَّهِ», from al-Anʿām 6:57 and from Yūsuf 12:40 and 12:67, which is why he gives three references for one sentence.

فَالْحُكْمُ لِلَّهِ الْعَلِيِّ الْكَبِيرِ

So judgement belongs to Allah, the Most High, the Most Great.

سُورَةُ غَافِر · 40:12

وَمَا اخْتَلَفْتُمْ فِيهِ مِن شَيْءٍ فَحُكْمُهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ

And in whatever you differ, its judgement is referred to Allah.

سُورَةُ الشُّورَى · 42:10

Keep the shape of the argument. The matn’s clause was about a man preferring another guidance. The sharḥ closes by showing why that preference is a nullifier and not merely a sin: because ruling is an attribute singled out for Allah in the same way worship is, so granting it elsewhere is of the same kind as granting worship elsewhere. That is the thread back to nullifiers one, two and three.

التَّشْرِيع Legislating, and Who Does It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

On governments, one sentence:

«They have no authority of legislation. Legislation belongs to Allah alone, in His Mighty Book, or His revelation to His Messenger ﷺ.»

And on people in general: «النَّاسُ لَا يُشَرِّعُون، المُشَرِّعُ هُوَ اللهُ عَلَى يَدِ رَسُولِهِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ — people do not legislate. The Legislator is Allah, by the hand of His Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then a precision about the words themselves, which is the kind of thing this book is good at:

«الشَّارِع and المُشَرِّع are applied to Allah — exalted is He — and to His Messenger ﷺ by the command of Allah; but the name الشَّارِع is not among the names of Allah — exalted is He.»

Two separate claims, and both matter. The word may be used of Allah — we say «the Lawgiver» and mean Him. It is not one of His names, because His names are taken from revelation and not coined from His acts. And the same word is used of the Messenger ﷺ — by Allah’s command, not in his own right, which is the point the next section settles.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And one more, on titles:

«No one is to be named with anything that shares with the Lord in His greatness — such as مَلِكُ المُلُوك, حَاكِمُ الحُكَّام, سُلْطَانُ السَّلَاطِين and the like; because this is false and a lie, and because it is a defect in the tawḥīd and a defect in the īmān, and because that will lead to self-admiration, arrogance and self-aggrandisement.»

Note the three reasons, because they are three different kinds of reason: it is untrue; it damages the tawḥīd; and it harms the man himself. The clause that spent twenty-eight folios on the gravest political question in the book ends up objecting to a business card — and that is not a change of subject. It is the same tawḥīd, met at the scale where most people will actually meet it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The compiler adds the consensus in his own words: “The scholars are agreed that legislating for people is of the exclusive characteristics of Allah — exalted is He — and that Allah is the Legislator; and that whoever says ‘I am the legislator for people’, or ‘I contend with Allah in His legislation’, or legislates alongside Allah, or legislates what contradicts the law of Allah, has contended with Allah in an attribute He has singled Himself out by; and that what is obligatory is to single Him out in ruling and in legislation.”

Read against part 2: this is the claim stated at its clearest — and part 2 is where you learn that establishing it of a particular man is «أَمْرٌ وَرَاءَ ذَلِك».

وَالرَّسُولُ مُبَلِّغ How the Messenger ﷺ Can Be Called a Legislator

That precision leaves a question, and the sharḥ answers it with three ḥadīth wordings in a row. If legislation is Allah’s alone, how does the Sharīʿah itself ascribe making-unlawful to the Messenger ﷺ? The answer is one word: مُبَلِّغ — he conveys.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — The ḥadīth «Ibrāhīm made Makkah inviolable, and I make Madīnah inviolable as Ibrāhīm made Makkah inviolable» — and here it is Allah who made it inviolable?

Ibn Bāz — «Both of these came. Allah made it inviolable on the day He created the heavens and the earth; and Ibrāhīm made it inviolable when Allah sent him — meaning: he manifested its prohibition and clarified it. And conveying — it was manifested by the hand of the Prophet ﷺ, its prohibition; and he did not speak of his own accord: it is from the Lawgiver, and He made it inviolable by the knowledge of Allah and the law of Allah.»

The questioner there had in mind the ḥadīth of Khuwaylid — «Makkah: Allah made it inviolable… and the people did not make it inviolable» — which the compiler attributes to al-Bukhārī and Muslim, and that attribution is given here as the sharḥ gives it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The second wording makes the grammar carry the doctrine:

On the ḥadīth «إِنَّ اللهَ وَرَسُولَهُ حَرَّمَ بَيْعَ الخَمْر»: «he used the verb in the singular, because the making-unlawful in reality belongs to Allah, and the Messenger is a conveyer. For this reason the singular verb ḥarrama came. And the commentator mentioned a narration with ḥarramā [dual] — and that is not to be denied either, because they are two: so Allah is the One who makes unlawful, and the Messenger is a conveyer.»

And the third, from the Ṣaḥīḥayn — «Allah and His Messenger forbid you [dual] the flesh of domestic donkeys», the ḥadīth of Anas: «so Allah is the One who forbids, and that is the foundation; and the Messenger is a conveyer.» The sharḥ places it فِي الصَّحِيحَيْن, and that is how it is reported here.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So the dual and the singular are both correct, and they are correct for different reasons. The singular is correct because the origin of the ruling is one. The dual is correct because «they are two» — Allah ruling, and the Messenger ﷺ conveying that ruling, which is a real and obligatory role, not a nominal one. Reject the dual and you have cut off obedience to the Messenger ﷺ; take the dual as partnership and you have cut into the tawḥīd. The word مُبَلِّغ holds both.

خُلَاصَةُ النَّاقِضِ الرَّابِع The Fourth Nullifier, Closed

الشَّرْح · the explanation

part 1 · ص ١٠٨–١١٩The ruling. The ijmāʿ is reported on the man who claims a superiority; تَوْحِيدُ الحُكْم; al-Tawbah 9:31 and the mechanism of obedience in making lawful and unlawful; and the three states — holding it better, holding it equal, or permitting it to replace the Sharīʿah while privately granting Allah’s law is better.
part 2 · ص ١٢٠–١٣١التَّفْصِيل. Greater and lesser ṭāghūt; istiḥlāl against the bribe and the kinship; Ibn ʿAbbās’s «كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْر» on 5:44; the six wordings of the one condition; «لَازِمُ الحُكْمِ لَيْسَ بِحُكْم»; and who fights, which is a state and never a man.
part 3 · ص ١٣٢–١٣٥The man under compulsion, with his three limits; contentment with his ruling ﷺ, on the verse and not on the story; the munāfiq’s two remarks and what they asserted; and التَّشْرِيع, closing on وَالرَّسُولُ مُبَلِّغ.

Twenty-eight folios, and the longest of the ten. Of those, twelve established the ruling and sixteen governed its application. If you remember the clause by its proportions you will remember it correctly.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

النَّاسُ لَا يُشَرِّعُون، المُشَرِّعُ هُوَ اللهُ عَلَى يَدِ رَسُولِهِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ

Ibn Bāz. The whole fourth nullifier in one sentence, and both of its halves are in it. «People do not legislate» is the ruling that part 1 established. «by the hand of His Messenger Muḥammad ﷺ» is مُبَلِّغ — the reason the Messenger’s ﷺ share in it takes nothing from Allah. And beside it keep the sentence that governs how any of this is applied to a particular man, from part 2: «لَكِنَّ الحُكْمَ بِالرِّدَّةِ أَمْرٌ وَرَاءَ ذَلِكَ.»
