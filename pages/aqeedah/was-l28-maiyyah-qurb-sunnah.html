المَعِيَّةُ وَالقُرْبُ فِي السُّنَّة

The Maʿiyyah and the Nearness in the Sunnah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٠٣–١٠٥
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 103–105.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say what أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ teaches about īmān itself, besides the maʿiyyah.
  • Explain how al-Fawzān keeps فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ from implying mixture with the creation.
  • Divide the four names of the duʿāʾ into their two pairs, and say what each pair affirms.
  • Give the structure of the answer in لَا تَدْعُونَ أَصَمَّ وَلَا غَائِبًا — what is denied and what is affirmed.
٧ · إِثْبَاتُ مَعِيَّةِ اللهِ لِخَلْقِهِ وَأَنَّهَا لَا تُنَافِي عُلُوَّه Section 7 — The Maʿiyyah, and That It Does Not Contradict His Highness

The author arranges Part 2 exactly as he arranged Part 1: the ʿuluww and the istiwāʾ first, and then immediately the texts of nearness — because these are the texts the muʿaṭṭilah use to cancel the ones before them. Lesson 17 settled the question from the Qurʾān. Here the same resolution arrives four times over from the Sunnah, and one of the four states it in a single sentence.

١ · «أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ أَنْ تَعْلَمَ أَنَّ اللهَ مَعَكَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْت» First — "The Best of Faith Is to Know That Allah Is with You Wherever You Are"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ أَنْ تَعْلَمَ أَنَّ اللهَ مَعَكَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ» حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ، أَخْرَجَهُ الطَّبَرَانِيُّ مِنْ حَدِيثِ عُبَادَةَ بْنِ الصَّامِتِ.

أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ أَنْ تَعْلَمَ أَنَّ اللهَ مَعَكَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ

"The best of faith is that you know Allah is with you wherever you are."

حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ — graded ḥasan in the matn; al-Ṭabarānī in al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr, from ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ — that is: of the best of its qualities. And al-Fawzān pauses on that, because a second doctrine falls out of the wording: and in this is a proof that īmān admits of degrees (أَنَّ الإِيمَانَ يَتَفَاضَلُ).

If faith had no degrees there would be no «best» of it. That is the position against the Murjiʾah, who held that faith is one indivisible thing which does not increase or decrease — and the author will return to it in Part 6. Here it arrives in passing, out of a single word.

أَنَّ اللهَ مَعَكَ — that is: by His knowledge and His observing; حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ — in whatever place you are found. This is the general maʿiyyah of Lesson 17, whose entailment is knowledge.

And then the point of the whole ḥadīth, which is not information but effect: whoever knows that, his outward and his inward become equal, and he stands in awe of Him in every place.

اسْتَوَتْ عَلَانِيَتُهُ وَسَرِيرَتُهُ — a man whose private conduct matches his public conduct. That is the definition of ikhlāṣ stated from the other end, and the ḥadīth makes it the product of one piece of knowledge. And in the ḥadīth is a proof for the affirming of Allah's maʿiyyah with His creation by His knowledge and His encompassing of their deeds, and that it is obligatory upon the servant to recall that constantly, so that he does his work well.

٢ · «فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِه» Second — "Allah Is before His Face"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «إِذَا قَامَ أَحَدُكُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَلَا يَبْصُقْ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ، فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ، وَلَا عَنْ يَمِينِهِ، وَلَكِنْ عَنْ يَسَارِهِ أَوْ تَحْتَ قَدَمِهِ» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

إِذَا قَامَ أَحَدُكُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ فَلَا يَبْصُقْ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ، فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ، وَلَا عَنْ يَمِينِهِ، وَلَكِنْ عَنْ يَسَارِهِ أَوْ تَحْتَ قَدَمِهِ

"When one of you stands to pray, let him not spit before his face, for Allah is before his face; nor to his right; but to his left, or beneath his foot."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

إِذَا قَامَ أَحَدُكُمْ إِلَى الصَّلَاةِ — that is: when he has begun it. قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ — that is: in front of him — and قِبَلَ is vowelled with a kasrah on the ق and a fatḥah on the ب.

فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ — this is the reason given for the prohibition of spitting in the direction of the one praying's qiblah: that Allah سُبْحَانَهُ is مُوَاجِهُهُ — facing him.

And then al-Fawzān guards it in the same breath, because this is precisely the wording an opponent would seize: and this facing is as befits Allah سُبْحَانَهُ. It does not follow from it that He سُبْحَانَهُ is mixed with His creation — rather He is above His heavens, established upon His Throne, and He is near to His creation, encompassing them.

That single sentence holds all three together: above His heavens · established upon His Throne · near to His creation and encompassing them. Ahl al-Sunnah are not balancing two claims against each other; they are reporting all the texts.

وَلَا عَنْ يَمِينِهِ — out of honouring the right side, and because the two angels are on his right, as comes in a narration of al-Bukhārī. وَلَكِنْ عَنْ يَسَارِهِ أَوْ تَحْتَ قَدَمِهِ — but let him spit in the direction of his left, or beneath his foot.

And the shāhid: the affirming of Allah's nearness to His praying servant and His turning toward him — while He سُبْحَانَهُ is above him.

٣ · الأَوَّلُ وَالآخِرُ وَالظَّاهِرُ وَالبَاطِن Third — the First, the Last, the Outermost, the Innermost

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «اللَّهُمَّ رَبَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ السَّبْعِ وَرَبَّ العَرْشِ العَظِيمِ، رَبَّنَا وَرَبَّ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ، فَالِقَ الحَبِّ وَالنَّوَى، مُنْزِلَ التَّوْرَاةِ وَالإِنْجِيلِ وَالقُرْآنِ، أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ نَفْسِي، وَمِنْ شَرِّ كُلِّ دَابَّةٍ أَنْتَ آخِذٌ بِنَاصِيَتِهَا. أَنْتَ الأَوَّلُ فَلَيْسَ قَبْلَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الآخِرُ فَلَيْسَ بَعْدَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ البَاطِنُ فَلَيْسَ دُونَكَ شَيْءٌ، اقْضِ عَنِّي الدَّيْنَ وَأَغْنِنِي مِنَ الفَقْرِ» رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

اللَّهُمَّ — its origin is يَا اللهُ; the mīm is a substitute for the yāʾ of calling.

رَبَّ السَّمَاوَاتِ السَّبْعِ — their Creator and their Owner; وَرَبَّ العَرْشِ العَظِيمِ — the Great, whose measure none can measure but Allah, for it is the greatest of the created things. رَبَّنَا وَرَبَّ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ — our Creator and our Provider, and the Creator of everything and its Owner — so in it is the affirming of His lordship over everything.

فَالِقَ الحَبِّ وَالنَّوَى — the Splitter of the grain of food and the date-stone, for sprouting. مُنْزِلَ التَّوْرَاةِ — upon Mūsā, and وَالإِنْجِيلِ — upon ʿĪsā, and وَالقُرْآنِ — upon Muḥammad ﷺ. And in that is a proof of the excellence of these Books, and that they are sent down from Allah تَعَالَى.

أَعُوذُ — I take refuge and I hold fast. مِنْ شَرِّ كُلِّ دَابَّةٍ — every creature that crawls upon the face of the earth. أَنْتَ آخِذٌ بِنَاصِيَتِهَا — al-nāṣiyah is the forelock, the front of the head; that is: it is beneath Your overpowering and Your authority; You dispose of it however You will — so that You may turn its harm away from me.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the heart of it — the four names, which the student met in Lesson 9 from al-Ḥadīd 57:3. Here the Prophet ﷺ supplies the commentary himself, a clause for each. These four names are two pairs:

الأَوَّل · الآخِرtwo names for His beginninglessness and His endlessness (أَزَلِيَّتِهِ وَأَبَدِيَّتِهِ) — nothing before You, nothing after You
الظَّاهِر · البَاطِنtwo names for His highness and His nearness (عُلُوِّهِ وَقُرْبِهِ) — nothing above You, nothing nearer than You

And these two are the place of the shāhid from the ḥadīth, because in them is the affirming of Allah's highness and His nearness, and that the two do not conflict and do not contradict.

And then al-Fawzān states it in the sentence this whole question has been working toward:

فَهُوَ قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِ، عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ

— He is near in His highness, high in His nearness.

Note that the resolution is not a compromise between two attributes, and not a ranking of one over the other. Each is asserted in the other. And it is not a formula a theologian devised afterwards to patch a difficulty — it is the structure of a duʿāʾ the Prophet ﷺ taught, in which the two names sit side by side, each with its own explanatory clause attached by him.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

اقْضِ عَنِّي الدَّيْنَ — that is: what is upon me of the rights of Allah and the rights of the creation; and in this is a disavowal of one's own might and power.

وَأَغْنِنِي مِنَ الفَقْرِ — al-faqr is need; and al-faqīr is one who finds nothing, or finds some part of sufficiency.

And in the ḥadīth also is the legislated practice of seeking nearness to Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى by His names and His attributes, in the fulfilling of a need and the answering of a supplication. Which is what the duʿāʾ demonstrates rather than states: eight lines of naming Him, and two lines of asking.

٤ · «ارْبَعُوا عَلَى أَنْفُسِكُم» Fourth — "Go Easy on Yourselves"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ لَمَّا رَفَعَ الصَّحَابَةُ أَصْوَاتَهُمْ بِالذِّكْرِ: «أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ، ارْبَعُوا عَلَى أَنْفُسِكُمْ، فَإِنَّكُمْ لَا تَدْعُونَ أَصَمَّ وَلَا غَائِبًا، إِنَّمَا تَدْعُونَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا قَرِيبًا، إِنَّ الَّذِي تَدْعُونَهُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَى أَحَدِكُمْ مِنْ عُنُقِ رَاحِلَتِهِ» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ، ارْبَعُوا عَلَى أَنْفُسِكُمْ، فَإِنَّكُمْ لَا تَدْعُونَ أَصَمَّ وَلَا غَائِبًا، إِنَّمَا تَدْعُونَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا قَرِيبًا، إِنَّ الَّذِي تَدْعُونَهُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَى أَحَدِكُمْ مِنْ عُنُقِ رَاحِلَتِهِ

"O people, go easy on yourselves. You are not calling upon one who is deaf, nor one who is absent. You are only calling upon One who hears, sees, and is near. The One you are calling upon is nearer to each of you than the neck of his riding-beast."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān gives the occasion: and that was in the expedition of Khaybar, as comes in some of the routes of the ḥadīth; and the dhikr with which they raised their voices was the takbīr: اللهُ أَكْبَرُ، لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ.

So this is not a rebuke of people doing something wrong. They were magnifying Allah on a march. The correction is to the volume only, and the reason given for it is a point of creed.

ارْبَعُوا — that is: be gentle. فَإِنَّكُمْ — a reason given for the command to gentleness.

Now watch the structure of the sentence, because al-Fawzān points it out and it is the cleanest example in the book of the method from Lesson 6 — negate the deficiency, affirm its opposite:

النَّفْيلَا تَدْعُونَ أَصَمَّ وَلَا غَائِبًا — He negated the defect preventing hearing and the defect preventing sight
الإِثْبَاتإِنَّمَا تَدْعُونَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا قَرِيبًا — and he affirmed their opposites

So there is no cause for raising the voice. إِنَّ الَّذِي تَدْعُونَهُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَى أَحَدِكُمْ مِنْ عُنُقِ رَاحِلَتِهِ — so He is near to whoever calls upon Him and remembers Him. So there is no need for raising the voices, when He is near, hearing them when they are lowered just as He hears them when they are raised.

And note the comparison he ﷺ chose. A man on a journey is sitting on the animal; its neck is the nearest thing in the world to him, right under his hands. Nearer than that — said to men shouting across a desert.

حَصِيلَةُ هَذِهِ الأَحَادِيث What These Aḥādīth Yield Together

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So all of these aḥādīth together conveyed: the affirming of Allah's maʿiyyah with His creation, and His nearness to them, and His hearing of their voices, and His seeing of their movements — and that does not contradict His highness and His istiwāʾ upon His Throne. And the discussion of the maʿiyyah, its kinds and its evidences from the Noble Qurʾān, along with the explanation of those evidences, has already come.

That closing cross-reference is the author's own: he is pointing the reader back to Lesson 17. Part 1 established the two kinds of maʿiyyah and the moon analogy; Part 2 brings four Prophetic texts that say the same, and one of them — near in His highness, high in His nearness — states the resolution as a formula. The Sunnah here is doing its fourth function from Lesson 22: indicating what the Qurʾān indicated, and expressing what the Qurʾān expressed.

What second doctrine does al-Fawzān derive from the words أَفْضَلُ الإِيمَانِ?

That faith is only belief in the heart
That īmān admits of degrees — for if there is a "best" of it, it is not one indivisible thing
That faith is complete only with the Sunnah
That faith can be lost entirely

What effect does the ḥadīth say this knowledge has on a person?

It increases his dhikr
His outward and his inward become equal, and he stands in awe of Him in every place
It makes him fear travelling alone
It makes him pray at night

How does al-Fawzān keep فَإِنَّ اللهَ قِبَلَ وَجْهِهِ from implying mixture with creation?

By saying the ḥadīth is about the qiblah only
By saying the facing is as befits Him: He is above His heavens, established on His Throne, and near to His creation, encompassing them
By reading "Allah" as "the angel of Allah"
By restricting it to the Prophet ﷺ

Why may the one praying not spit to his right?

Because the qiblah is to the right
Out of honouring the right side, and because the two angels are on his right
Because it is the direction of the imām
Because the ground is harder there

Into what two pairs does al-Fawzān divide the four names of the duʿāʾ?

Names of the Essence and names of the Actions
الأَوَّل and الآخِر for His beginninglessness and endlessness; الظَّاهِر and البَاطِن for His highness and His nearness
Names of mercy and names of might
Names known to all and names known only to Him

What is al-Fawzān’s formula resolving the highness and the nearness?

He is high, and His nearness is His knowledge only
فَهُوَ قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِ، عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ — near in His highness, high in His nearness
He is near to the believers and high above the disbelievers
The two are affirmed without any relation between them

What is the structure of لَا تَدْعُونَ أَصَمَّ وَلَا غَائِبًا followed by إِنَّمَا تَدْعُونَ سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا قَرِيبًا?

A rebuke followed by a consolation
The negation of the defects preventing hearing and sight, then the affirming of their opposites
A general statement then a specific one
A command then its reason
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
الإِيمَانُ يَتَفَاضَلُ. Īmān admits of degrees.
مُوَاجَهَةُ اللهِ لِلْمُصَلِّي تَقْتَضِي أَنَّهُ مُخْتَلِطٌ بِخَلْقِهِ. Allah’s facing the one praying entails that He is mixed with His creation.
«الظَّاهِرُ وَالبَاطِنُ» اسْمَانِ لِعُلُوِّهِ وَقُرْبِهِ. Al-Ẓāhir and al-Bāṭin are two names for His highness and His nearness.
نَهَى النَّبِيُّ ﷺ الصَّحَابَةَ عَنِ الذِّكْرِ نَفْسِهِ. The Prophet ﷺ forbade the Companions the dhikr itself.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَنْتَ الظَّاهِرُ فَلَيْسَ فَوْقَكَ شَيْءٌ، وَأَنْتَ البَاطِنُ فَلَيْسَ دُونَكَ شَيْءٌ

Two names and two clauses, and the Prophet ﷺ attached the clauses himself. Nothing is above Him, and nothing is nearer than He is. Carry al-Fawzān's summary with it: قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِ، عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ.
