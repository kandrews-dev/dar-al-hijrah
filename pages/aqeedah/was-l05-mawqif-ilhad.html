مَوْقِفُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالإِلْحَادُ فِي الأَسْمَاء

The Position of Ahl al-Sunnah, and Deviating in the Names

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٨–٢٢
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 18–22.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State how Ahl al-Sunnah answer the charge that affirming the attributes means likening Allah to His creation.
  • Define الإِلْحَاد and name al-Fawzān’s five kinds of it.
  • Explain why سَمِيّ, كُفْو and نِدّ are each denied of Allah.
  • Say why أَعْلَمُ بِنَفْسِهِ is the reason the attributes must be affirmed as they came.
مَوْقِفُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ مِنَ الصِّفَات The Position of Ahl al-Sunnah

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

بَلْ يُؤْمِنُونَ بِأَنَّ اللهَ سُبْحَانَهُ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ البَصِيرُ، فَلَا يَنْفُونَ عَنْهُ مَا وَصَفَ بِهِ نَفْسَهُ، وَلَا يُحَرِّفُونَ الكَلِمَ عَنْ مَوَاضِعِهِ، وَلَا يُلْحِدُونَ فِي أَسْمَاءِ اللهِ وَآيَاتِهِ، وَلَا يُكَيِّفُونَ وَلَا يُمَثِّلُونَ صِفَاتِهِ بِصِفَاتِ خَلْقِهِ.

Having named the four exclusions in the last lesson, the author now states what Ahl al-Sunnah do — and he states it as a single balanced act, not as four avoidances.

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."

Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:11 · سُورَةُ الشُّورَى

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān calls this āyah دُسْتُورٌ وَاضِحٌ فِي بَابِ الأَسْمَاءِ وَالصِّفَاتِ — a clear constitution for the whole subject of the names and attributes. And he shows why, by splitting it in two:

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌa refutation of المُمَثِّلَة — those who liken
وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ البَصِيرُa refutation of المُعَطِّلَة — those who divest; because it affirms hearing and sight

One āyah, both ditches. It is the shortest statement of the creed there is: affirmation of the attributes, with negation of likeness — and the two halves arrive in a single breath so that neither can be taken alone.

«فَلَا يَنْفُونَ عَنْهُ مَا وَصَفَ بِهِ نَفْسَهُ» The Charge, and the Answer

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here al-Fawzān states the objection the muʿaṭṭilah actually make, and it is worth stating fairly because it sounds pious: if you affirm that Allah hears, sees, is pleased, descends — have you not made Him like us, who also hear and see? Surely safety lies in denying all of it.

Those who argued this way, he says, went to excess in التَّنْزِيه until they divested Him of His attributes — on the pretext of fleeing from likening Him to the attributes of creatures. So their motive was reverence; their result was emptying.

And the answer of Ahl al-Sunnah is one sentence that dissolves the whole objection:

Allah has attributes that belong to Him and befit Him; creatures have attributes that belong to them and befit them; and there is no resemblance between the attributes of the Creator and the attributes of the creature.

So the dreaded consequence does not follow. Sharing a name is not sharing a reality. That a man hears and Allah hears no more makes his hearing like Allah's than that a man exists and Allah exists makes his existence like Allah's. The muʿaṭṭil pays an enormous price — the attributes themselves — to avoid a problem he never had.

الإِلْحَادُ فِي أَسْمَاءِ اللهِ Deviating in the Names of Allah

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَلَا يُلْحِدُونَ فِي أَسْمَاءِ اللهِ وَآيَاتِهِ، وَلَا يُكَيِّفُونَ وَلَا يُمَثِّلُونَ صِفَاتِهِ بِصِفَاتِ خَلْقِهِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الإِلْحَاد in the language is inclining, turning aside from a thing — and al-Fawzān gives an etymology that will fix the word in your memory for good: it is the same root as اللَّحْد, the niche cut in the side of a grave, named for its leaning away from the line of the digging, toward the qiblah.

So deviating in the names of Allah is turning them aside from their realities and their sound meanings, toward falsehood. And it comes in five kinds:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

  1. Naming the idols with them. اللَّات derived from الإِلَه, العُزَّى from العَزِيز, مَنَاة from المَنَّان — the names of Allah bent into names for stones.
  2. Naming Allah with what does not befit Him. As the Christians name Him “father,” and the philosophers name Him مُوجِبًا or عِلَّةً فَاعِلَةً — a necessitating cause, as though He were a mechanism rather than One who wills.
  3. Describing Him with the deficiencies He is free of. As the Jews said إِنَّ اللَّهَ فَقِيرٌ وَنَحْنُ أَغْنِيَاءُ (Āl ʿImrān 3:181), and يَدُ اللَّهِ مَغْلُولَةٌ (al-Māʾidah 5:64), and that He rested on the Sabbath — exalted is Allah above what they say.
  4. Denying their meanings and realities. As the Jahmiyyah say: they are bare words containing no attribute and no meaning — so السَّمِيع indicates no hearing, البَصِير indicates no sight, الحَيّ indicates no life.
  5. Likening His attributes to the attributes of His creation. As the likener says: His hand is like mine — exalted is Allah.

Look at how wide that list is. The fourth kind is committed by a man who recites the names with perfect reverence and means nothing by any of them; the first by a man who worships a rock. Both are إِلْحَاد. The word does not only cover the crude error.

وَلِلَّهِ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ فَادْعُوهُ بِهَا وَذَرُوا الَّذِينَ يُلْحِدُونَ فِي أَسْمَائِهِ سَيُجْزَوْنَ مَا كَانُوا يَعْمَلُونَ

"And to Allah belong the most beautiful names, so call upon Him by them; and leave those who deviate concerning His names — they will be recompensed for what they used to do."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:180 · سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف

إِنَّ الَّذِينَ يُلْحِدُونَ فِي آيَاتِنَا لَا يَخْفَوْنَ عَلَيْنَا

"Indeed those who deviate concerning Our signs are not hidden from Us."

Sūrat Fuṣṣilat 41:40 · سُورَةُ فُصِّلَت

Note that the first āyah gives the duty and the warning together: call upon Him by them — and leave those who bend them. The names are not there to be catalogued. They are there to be used in duʿāʾ.

لَا سَمِيَّ لَهُ وَلَا كُفْوَ لَهُ وَلَا نِدَّ لَهُ No Namesake, No Equal, No Rival

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

لِأَنَّهُ سُبْحَانَهُ لَا سَمِيَّ لَهُ، وَلَا كُفْوَ لَهُ، وَلَا نِدَّ لَهُ، وَلَا يُقَاسُ بِخَلْقِهِ سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three near-synonyms, and al-Fawzān keeps them apart — each with its own āyah:

TermMeaningIts proof
سَمِيّa namesake — one who deserves a name like Hisهَلْ تَعْلَمُ لَهُ سَمِيًّا (Maryam 19:65) — a question whose force is denial
كُفْوa match, a counterpartوَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ (al-Ikhlāṣ 112:4)
نِدّa likeness set up as a rivalفَلَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَنْدَادًا (al-Baqarah 2:22)

وَلَا يُقَاسُ بِخَلْقِهِ — and القِيَاس in the language is likening. So He is not compared to His creation: not in His Essence, nor in His names and attributes, nor in His acts. al-Fawzān ends the point with a question rather than an argument, and it is the better for it: “and how is the perfect Creator to be compared with the deficient creature?”

فَلَا تَضْرِبُوا لِلَّهِ الْأَمْثَالَ

"So do not set up comparisons for Allah."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:74 · سُورَةُ النَّحْل

«أَعْلَمُ بِنَفْسِهِ وَبِغَيْرِهِ» He Knows Himself Best

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَإِنَّهُ سُبْحَانَهُ أَعْلَمُ بِنَفْسِهِ وَبِغَيْرِهِ، وَأَصْدَقُ قِيلًا، وَأَحْسَنُ حَدِيثًا مِنْ خَلْقِهِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the author's reason — and al-Fawzān marks it as such. It is the justification both for the obligation to affirm what Allah affirmed of Himself, and for the prohibition on comparing Him with His creation.

The argument is simple and very hard to answer. If He knows Himself better than we do — and better than we know anything — then the attributes He affirmed for Himself, and that His Messenger ﷺ affirmed for Him, must be affirmed. To deny one of them is to say we know His Self better than He does.

And the creation, al-Fawzān adds, cannot encompass Him in knowledge. He is described with attributes of perfection that the intellects of creatures cannot reach. Which leaves us with one duty, and it is a restful one:

We must be content with what He was content with for Himself — for He knows best what befits Him.

That sentence is the temperament of this whole book. Everything that follows — the face, the hands, the rising, the descending, the pleasure and the anger — is received on exactly this basis: He said it of Himself, and He knows.

مُشَارَكَةُ الاسْمِ لَا تَعْنِي مُشَارَكَةَ الحَقِيقَةِ. Sharing a name does not mean sharing the reality.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ البَصِيرُ

Learn it as two halves that must be held together. Take the first alone and you end a muʿaṭṭil; take the second alone and you end a mumaththil. Said whole, it is the entire creed of Ahl al-Sunnah on the attributes, in six words.
