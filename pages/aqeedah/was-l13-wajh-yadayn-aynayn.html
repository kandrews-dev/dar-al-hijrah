الوَجْهُ وَاليَدَانِ وَالعَيْنَان

The Face, the Two Hands, and the Eyes

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٥١–٥٦
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 51–56.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the two reasons the interpretations of الوَجْه as “the Essence” or “the reward” are void.
  • Give al-Fawzān’s three arguments that اليَد does not mean power or favour.
  • Explain why the plural بِأَعْيُنِنَا does not imply many eyes — from Arabic usage.
  • Define الصَّبْر in the language, in its three parts.
١٠ · إِثْبَاتُ الوَجْهِ لِلَّهِ سُبْحَانَه Section 10 — The Face

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَيَبْقَىٰ وَجْهُ رَبِّكَ ذُو الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿كُلُّ شَيْءٍ هَالِكٌ إِلَّا وَجْهَهُ﴾.

كُلُّ مَنْ عَلَيْهَا فَانٍ وَيَبْقَىٰ وَجْهُ رَبِّكَ ذُو الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ

"Everyone upon it is passing away, and there remains the Face of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honour."

Sūrat al-Raḥmān 55:26–27 · سُورَةُ الرَّحْمَن

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān reads it in context: the āyah comes after كُلُّ مَنْ عَلَيْهَا فَانٍ — Allah informs us that all the people of the earth will go and die, and none will remain but His noble Face; for the Lord does not die, rather He is the Living who never dies.

ذُو الجَلَالِ is greatness and grandeur. وَالإِكْرَام — the One who honours His prophets and His righteous servants; and it is also said: the One who deserves to be held above everything that does not befit Him.

كُلُّ شَيْءٍ هَالِكٌ إِلَّا وَجْهَهُ

"Everything will perish except His Face."

Sūrat al-Qaṣaṣ 28:88 · سُورَةُ القَصَص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The shāhid: both āyāt affirm the Face for Allah, and it is of صِفَاتِهِ الذَّاتِيَّة — His attributes of Essence. It is a real face, befitting His majesty, لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ — not as the deniers of the attributes claim, that the Face is not real and that what is meant is the Essence, or the reward, or a direction.

And al-Fawzān says those interpretations are void on more than one ground. Two he states:

  1. The Face is mentioned alongside the Essence — and a thing is not joined to itself. If “Face” simply meant “Essence,” the wording would be saying nothing twice.
  2. No nation’s language works that way. It is not known in the language of any people on earth that “the face of a thing” means its essence, or its reward. The interpretation is not a reading of Arabic; it is a replacement for it.

And he gives the word's actual meaning: الوَجْه in the language is the front of anything — because it is the first part of it that one faces — and in each thing it is according to what it is annexed to. A man's face, a house's face, a garment's face: in every case the front, in the manner suited to that thing.

١١ · إِثْبَاتُ اليَدَيْنِ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى Section 11 — The Two Hands

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿مَا مَنَعَكَ أَنْ تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَقَالَتِ الْيَهُودُ يَدُ اللَّهِ مَغْلُولَةٌ غُلَّتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَلُعِنُوا بِمَا قَالُوا بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ يُنْفِقُ كَيْفَ يَشَاءُ﴾.

مَا مَنَعَكَ أَنْ تَسْجُدَ لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ

"What prevented you from prostrating to what I created with My two Hands?"

Sūrat Ṣād 38:75 · سُورَة ص

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The address is to Iblīs, when he refused to prostrate to Ādam عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ: what thing turned you away and held you back from prostrating? And لِمَا خَلَقْتُ بِيَدَيَّ means I undertook his creation with My Hands, without any intermediary — and in this is an ennobling and honouring of Ādam.

وَقَالَتِ الْيَهُودُ يَدُ اللَّهِ مَغْلُولَةٌ غُلَّتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَلُعِنُوا بِمَا قَالُوا بَلْ يَدَاهُ مَبْسُوطَتَانِ يُنْفِقُ كَيْفَ يَشَاءُ

"And the Jews said: Allah's hand is tied. Their hands are tied, and they are cursed for what they said. Rather, His two Hands are outstretched; He gives as He wills."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:64 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān explains what they meant and what they did not: by يَدُ اللهِ مَغْلُولَةٌ they described Him as miserly — as they also described Him as poor while they were rich — not that they meant His hand was literally bound. And غُلَّتْ أَيْدِيهِمْ is Allah's reply, meeting them with the very thing they said, matching them for what they fabricated. And so it came to pass for them — for among them is a great deal of miserliness and envy.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The shāhid: the āyāt affirm two real Hands, befitting His majesty and greatness, not like the hands of the creature — لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ. And in this is the refutation of whoever negated the two real Hands and claimed that what is meant by “hand” is power or favour. al-Fawzān calls that a void interpretation and a distortion of the Noble Qurʾān — and then gives three arguments. The first is the one to remember:

1. It would destroy the honour being given to Ādam. If “Hand” meant power, then singling Ādam out as created by them is pointless — because every creature, including Iblīs himself, was created by His power. What distinction over Iblīs would Ādam then have?

— and Iblīs could have answered back: “and You created me with Your hands too!” The rebuke in the āyah only works because the Hands are real and particular.

  1. If “hand” meant power, Allah would have to have two powers — and the Muslims are agreed that this is false.
  2. If it meant favour, the meaning would be that He created Ādam with two favours — also false, because Allah’s favours are beyond counting, not two.

Three arguments, and none of them appeals to anything but the text and plain reasoning. That is how this book handles the attributes throughout.

١٢ · إِثْبَاتُ العَيْنَيْنِ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى Section 12 — The Eyes

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَاصْبِرْ لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ فَإِنَّكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿تَجْرِي بِأَعْيُنِنَا جَزَاءً لِمَنْ كَانَ كُفِرَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَأَلْقَيْتُ عَلَيْكَ مَحَبَّةً مِنِّي وَلِتُصْنَعَ عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي﴾.

وَاصْبِرْ لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ فَإِنَّكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا

"And be patient for the decree of your Lord — for you are before Our Eyes."

Sūrat al-Ṭūr 52:48 · سُورَةُ الطُّور

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Before the attribute, al-Fawzān defines the command. الصَّبْر in the language is restraining and withholding. And he divides it into three, which is worth carrying well beyond this lesson:

  • restraining the soul from anguish;
  • restraining the tongue from complaining and from discontent;
  • restraining the limbs from striking the cheeks and tearing the garments.

So patience is not a feeling one waits to arrive. It is three refusals, and a man can perform all three while his heart is breaking.

لِحُكْمِ رَبِّكَ means His decree, both universal and legislated. And فَإِنَّكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا means in Our sight, seen by Us — which is precisely why the patience is commanded. The one being wronged is being watched.

تَجْرِي بِأَعْيُنِنَا جَزَاءً لِمَنْ كَانَ كُفِرَ

"…sailing before Our Eyes — a reward for the one who had been denied."

Sūrat al-Qamar 54:14 · سُورَةُ القَمَر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Of Nūḥ's ship, carried عَلَىٰ ذَاتِ أَلْوَاحٍ وَدُسُرٍ — a vessel of broad planks, and nails by which those planks were bound (the singular of دُسُر being دِسَار). It sailed in Our sight and view, and under Our protection. And جَزَاءً لِمَنْ كَانَ كُفِرَ means: what We did with Nūḥ and his people, of saving him and drowning them, was a recompense for the one who had been denied — and that is Nūḥ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَامُ.

وَأَلْقَيْتُ عَلَيْكَ مَحَبَّةً مِنِّي وَلِتُصْنَعَ عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي

"And I cast upon you love from Me, and so that you would be brought up before My Eye."

Sūrat Ṭā Hā 20:39 · سُورَةُ طَه

«بِأَعْيُنِنَا» وَ«عَلَى عَيْنِي»: مَسْأَلَةٌ فِي اللُّغَة A Question of Arabic Usage

الشَّرْح · the explanation

A student reading these three āyāt side by side will notice something: one says بِأَعْيُنِنَا (plural), another says عَلَىٰ عَيْنِي (singular). Does the Qurʾān affirm many eyes? One eye?

al-Fawzān settles it from Arabic usage, and begins by ruling out the wrong answer: what is meant is not the affirming of a single eye, for that would be an evident defect — Allah is far above it.

Then the rule: the Arabs make an annexed noun singular, dual or plural according to the state of what it is annexed to.

Annexed to…The noun becomesExample
a singularsingularعَلَىٰ عَيْنِي
a plural (stated or as a pronoun)better made plural, to match the wordingفَإِنَّكَ بِأَعْيُنِنَا
a dualmost eloquently made pluralفَقَدْ صَغَتْ قُلُوبُكُمَا — and there are only two hearts

That third row is the proof, and it is decisive. Allah says to two people “your hearts have inclined” — plural — when between two people there are exactly two hearts. Nobody reading it imagines a crowd of hearts. The plural is the eloquent form when annexed to a dual, and that is all it is.

So, al-Fawzān concludes: the hearer is not confused by a speaker saying “We see you with Our eyes” or “We seize you with Our hands” — and not one human being on the face of the earth understands from it many eyes on a single face.

Which is the method of this whole book applied to a grammar question: the text is left standing, the Arabic is read as Arabic, and the imagined difficulty turns out to have been imported rather than found.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

كُلُّ مَنْ عَلَيْهَا فَانٍ ۝ وَيَبْقَىٰ وَجْهُ رَبِّكَ ذُو الْجَلَالِ وَالْإِكْرَامِ

Two āyahs that put every attachment in its place. Everyone on the earth is passing — and the only thing that remains is the One whose Face this is. Recite it when the world feels permanent.
