القُرْآنُ كَلَامُ اللهِ وَلَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِه

The Qurʾān Is the Speech of Allah, and None Can Change His Words

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the āyah that proves the recited Qurʾān is itself كَلَامُ اللهِ.
  • Define التَّحْرِيف as al-Fawzān defines it, and say what the Jews did with the Torah.
  • State the madhhab of Ahl al-Sunnah on speech — both of the two kinds of attribute it is.
  • Give the two reasons al-Fawzān offers for why Allah has never ceased to be a Speaker.
٥ · القُرْآنُ كَلَامُ الله Fifth — The Qurʾān Is the Speech of Allah

Lesson 18 established that Allah speaks. This part of the section establishes what His speech is — that the Book being recited is itself His speech, not a created report about it — and then that no one may alter it, with the Jews and the Bedouin who stayed behind as the two worked examples.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ﴾، ﴿وَقَدْ كَانَ فَرِيقٌ مِنْهُمْ يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ﴾، ﴿يُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يُبَدِّلُوا كَلَامَ اللَّهِ قُلْ لَنْ تَتَّبِعُونَا كَذَلِكُمْ قَالَ اللَّهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ﴾، ﴿وَاتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِنْ كِتَابِ رَبِّكَ لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ﴾، ﴿إِنَّ هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ يَقُصُّ عَلَى بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَكْثَرَ الَّذِي هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ﴾.

وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُ

"And if any one of the mushrikūn seeks your protection, then grant him protection until he hears the speech of Allah; then convey him to his place of safety."

Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:6 · سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ — those whom you were commanded to fight — اسْتَجَارَكَ — O Muḥammad, that is: he sought your protection, your shelter and your security — فَأَجِرْهُ — be for him a protector and a guarantor of safety — حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ — from you, and ponders it, and comes to stand upon the reality of what you are calling to.

And the shāhid from the āyah: that in it is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that what is recited is the speech of Allah.

That is the decisive point, and the āyah carries it almost incidentally. The enemy combatant is given safe passage for the purpose of hearing — and what he is to hear is named: كَلَامَ اللَّهِ. He will hear it from a human reciter, in Arabic, with his ears. And the Qurʾān calls that the speech of Allah.

There is something else in the ruling worth noticing. The protection is not withdrawn once he has heard and refused; ثُمَّ أَبْلِغْهُ مَأْمَنَهُ — he is escorted back to safety. Hearing is not made a trap.

وَقَدْ كَانَ فَرِيقٌ مِنْهُمْ يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ

"And a group of them used to hear the speech of Allah, then distort it after they had understood it — and they knew."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:75 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

فَرِيقٌ مِنْهُمْ — that is: the Jews. And a point of language: الفَرِيق is a collective noun which has no singular from its own root.

يَسْمَعُونَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ — that is: the Torah. ثُمَّ يُحَرِّفُونَهُ — they interpret it upon other than its interpretation — which is al-Fawzān's working definition of taḥrīf and worth having exactly: not the deleting of a text, but the reading of it against its meaning.

مِنْ بَعْدِ مَا عَقَلُوهُ — that is: after they had understood it; and with this they oppose it with open eyes. وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ — that they are in error in what they went to, of distorting it and reinterpreting it.

Read the sequence: they heard, they understood, then they distorted, and they knew they were doing it. Taḥrīf in this āyah is not a mistake made by the ignorant. It is comprehension followed by evasion — which is exactly why the author quotes it in a book written against people who reinterpret texts they understand perfectly well.

And the shāhid: in it is the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that the Torah is from His speech تَعَالَى, and that the Jews distorted it, and altered and substituted in it.

يُرِيدُونَ أَنْ يُبَدِّلُوا كَلَامَ اللَّهِ قُلْ لَنْ تَتَّبِعُونَا كَذَلِكُمْ قَالَ اللَّهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ

"They wish to change the speech of Allah. Say: you shall not follow us. Thus did Allah say beforehand."

Sūrat al-Fatḥ 48:15 · سُورَةُ الفَتْح

الشَّرْح · the explanation

يُرِيدُونَ — the ones left behind, from among the Bedouin, who chose to remain with their families and their occupations and abandoned setting out with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ when he went out in the year of al-Ḥudaybiyah.

أَنْ يُبَدِّلُوا كَلَامَ اللَّهِ — they would alter the speech of Allah by which Allah had promised the people of al-Ḥudaybiyah specifically the spoils of Khaybar. So the «changing» at issue is concrete: a promise was made to a named body of people, and those who had stayed behind wanted its terms rewritten in their favour.

قُلْ لَنْ تَتَّبِعُونَا — and the grammar matters: this is a negation carrying the meaning of a prohibition — that is, do not follow us. كَذَلِكُمْ قَالَ اللَّهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ — Allah promised the people of al-Ḥudaybiyah that the spoils of Khaybar were theirs specifically.

And the shāhid: in it is the affirming of speech for Allah and the affirming of saying for Him; and that Allah سُبْحَانَهُ speaks and says when He wills, if He wills; and that it is not permitted to alter His speech سُبْحَانَهُ — rather it is obligatory to act by it and to follow it.

وَاتْلُ مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيْكَ مِنْ كِتَابِ رَبِّكَ لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ

"And recite what has been revealed to you of the Book of your Lord. There is no changer of His words."

Sūrat al-Kahf 18:27 · سُورَةُ الكَهْف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Allah commanded His Prophet to keep constant upon the recitation of the Book revealed to him. And on the word itself: الوَحْي is informing with speed and concealment — and it has modes which are mentioned in the books of the principles of tafsīr.

مِنْ كِتَابِ رَبِّكَ is a clarification of that which was revealed to him. لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ — no alterer of them, and no distorter, and no remover.

And the shāhid from the āyah: the affirming of the words for Allah تَعَالَى. Set it beside the previous āyah and the pair is complete: men wish to change His speech (al-Fatḥ 48:15), and there is no changer of it (here). The wish is real; the capacity is not.

إِنَّ هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ يَقُصُّ عَلَى بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ أَكْثَرَ الَّذِي هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ

"Indeed this Qurʾān relates to the Children of Israel most of that over which they differ."

Sūrat al-Naml 27:76 · سُورَةُ النَّمْل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ — and they are the bearers of the Torah and the Gospel. أَكْثَرَ الَّذِي هُمْ فِيهِ يَخْتَلِفُونَ — as their differing over ʿĪsā: the Jews fabricated against him, and the Christians went to excess regarding him.

So the Qurʾān came with القَوْلِ الوَسَطِ الحَقِّ — the middle, true statement: that he is the servant of Allah, and His messenger, and His word which He cast to Maryam, and a spirit from Him.

There is the pattern of the opening lessons again, arriving from a new direction. Two parties erred in opposite directions on one man, and the truth was neither of their positions — it was the middle. The wasaṭ of this book's title is not a temperament; it is a method.

And the shāhid: that in it is the affirming that the Qurʾān is the speech of Allah تَعَالَى, because of what it contains of encompassing the previous books and of judging with equity in the disputes between the sects of the People of the Book — and this could not be from anyone except Allah.

That is an argument from the content, not from a claim. A book that settles, justly, the internal disputes of two older scriptural communities — disputes its reciter had no access to — is evidence about its own origin.

مَذْهَبُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ فِي الكَلَام The Madhhab of Ahl al-Sunnah on the Attribute of Speech

al-Fawzān now gathers the whole section. And what is derived from the totality of the āyāt the author brought is: the affirming of speech for Allah. And the madhhab of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah is the affirming of what the Book and the Sunnah have indicated — that Allah is described with speech.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the classification, and this is the part to learn precisely, because it is where most people go wrong by choosing one half:

مِنْ صِفَاتِهِ الذَّاتِيَّة— because it subsists with Him and He is described by it. Speech belongs to Him as He is, not as something added to Him.
وَمِنْ صِفَاتِهِ الفِعْلِيَّة— occurring by His will and His power. So He speaks if He wills, how He wills, with what He wills.

Both, not one. This is the resolution of a dispute that split the schools. Make speech purely dhātī and you must say Allah speaks eternally and necessarily, with no choice in it — and then the āyāt in which He speaks to Mūsā at an appointed time, or speaks to ʿĪsā on the Day of Rising, have to be explained away. Make it purely fiʿlī and you have said there was a time when He was not a Speaker, which is a deficiency. Ahl al-Sunnah affirm both and need explain nothing away.

Hence the formula: وَلَمْ يَزَلْ مُتَكَلِّمًا وَلَا يَزَالُ مُتَكَلِّمًا — and He has never ceased to be Speaking and will never cease to be Speaking. And al-Fawzān gives two reasons for it:

الأَوَّل · Because He has never ceased and will never cease being Perfect — and speech is of the attributes of perfection. The argument from Lesson 16 again: an attribute of perfection cannot have been absent from the Perfect.

الثَّانِي · And because Allah described Himself with it, and His Messenger described Him with it. The tawqīfī principle: the subject is received, not reasoned out.

Two reasons, and notice they are of two different kinds — one rational, one transmitted. That is the signature of this book's method, and the same pairing you met in Lesson 16 on al-ʿuluww.

And the mention of the madhhab of the opponents in this question, along with the refutation of it, is to come, إِنْ شَاءَ اللهُ. The author keeps his polemic for later and his texts for now — which is worth imitating.

التَّحْرِيفُ فِي آيَةِ البَقَرَةِ وَقَعَ مِنْ جَهْلٍ بِالمَعْنَى. The taḥrīf in the āyah of al-Baqarah occurred out of ignorance of the meaning.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لَا مُبَدِّلَ لِكَلِمَاتِهِ

Four words to set against al-Fatḥ 48:15, where men wish to change His speech. The wish has never been in doubt. The āyah answers the capacity.

al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah · sharḥ of Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
