الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ السَّادِسَ عَشَرYear 3

الاسْتِوَاءُ عَلَى العَرْشِ وَالعُلُوُّ عَلَى المَخْلُوقَات

The Istiwāʾ upon the Throne, and the Highness above the Creation

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the seven sūrahs in which الاسْتِوَاء is affirmed, and the one expression they share.
  • Give the four senses of اسْتَوَى in the speech of the Arabs.
  • Reproduce all four of al-Fawzān’s refutations of reading اسْتَوَى as اسْتَوْلَى.
  • State the two differences between العُلُوّ and الاسْتِوَاء.
١٨ · إِثْبَاتُ اسْتِوَاءِ اللهِ عَلَى عَرْشِه Section 18 — The Istiwāʾ of Allah upon His Throne

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ فِي سُورَةِ الأَعْرَافِ: ﴿إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ يُونُسَ: ﴿إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ الرَّعْدِ: ﴿اللَّهُ الَّذِي رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍ تَرَوْنَهَا ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ طَه: ﴿الرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى الْعَرْشِ اسْتَوَى﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ الفُرْقَانِ: ﴿ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ الرَّحْمَنُ﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ الم السَّجْدَةِ: ﴿اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ وَمَا بَيْنَهُمَا فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ﴾، وَقَالَ فِي سُورَةِ الحَدِيدِ: ﴿هُوَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān opens with what the matn has just demonstrated by sheer accumulation: the affirmation of Allah's istiwāʾ upon His Throne has come in seven āyāt of the Book of Allah — al-Aʿrāf, Yūnus, al-Raʿd, Ṭā-Hā, al-Furqān, al-Sajdah, al-Ḥadīd — and in all of them the affirmation came with one single expression: اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ.

And from that uniformity he draws the conclusion: فَهُوَ نَصٌّ فِي مَعْنَاهُ الحَقِيقِيِّ لَا يَحْتَمِلُ التَّأْوِيلَ بِمَعْنًى آخَر — it is explicit in its real meaning; it does not admit of being reinterpreted to some other meaning. Seven places, one wording, no variant. There is nothing here for a taʾwīl to get a grip on.

Then the classification: الاسْتِوَاءُ صِفَةٌ فِعْلِيَّةٌ ثَابِتَةٌ لِلَّهِ سُبْحَانَهُ عَلَى مَا يَلِيقُ بِجَلَالِه — a verbal attribute, established for Allah, in the manner that befits His majesty, like the rest of His attributes. Keep the phrase عَلَى مَا يَلِيقُ بِجَلَالِه in view; the whole of Lesson 6 was spent earning the right to say it here without hesitation.

إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ الَّذِي خَلَقَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ

"Indeed your Lord is Allah, who created the heavens and the earth in six days, then rose over the Throne."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:54 · سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف — and the same wording in Yūnus 10:3

الشَّرْح · the explanation

إِنَّ رَبَّكُمُ اللَّهُ — He is your Creator and the One who nurtures you with His favours, and the One whom it is obligatory upon you to worship alone. The āyah does not begin with the Throne; it begins with the claim on you.

فِي سِتَّةِ أَيَّامٍ — the six are named: al-aḥad, al-ithnayn, al-thulāthāʾ, al-arbiʿāʾ, al-khamīs and al-jumuʿah. And on the day of Jumuʿah the whole of the creation came together, and on it Ādam عليه السلام was created.

ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ — He rose and ascended over the Throne, as befits His majesty; and this is the place of the shāhid from the āyah.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And العَرْش? al-Fawzān gives both the lexical sense and what the texts establish: in the language it is the bed/couch of the king. And what is intended by it here — as the totality of the texts indicates — is a couch having legs, borne by the angels, and it is like a dome over the world, and it is the roof of the created things.

Note the discipline in that sentence: he does not reason from the lexical meaning to a picture, and he does not refuse to say anything at all. He says what the totality of the texts indicates, and stops there.

اللَّهُ الَّذِي رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ بِغَيْرِ عَمَدٍ تَرَوْنَهَا ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ وَسَخَّرَ الشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ

"Allah is He who raised the heavens without pillars that you see, then rose over the Throne, and made the sun and the moon subservient."

Sūrat al-Raʿd 13:2 · سُورَةُ الرَّعْد

الشَّرْح · the explanation

رَفَعَ السَّمَاوَاتِ — He raised them above the earth with a far raising, whose extent is not attained and not grasped. العَمَد are the columns, the plural of عِمَاد: standing without pillars that they rest upon — rather, by His power.

And تَرَوْنَهَا — al-Fawzān reports the two readings fairly and then chooses: it is an emphasis of the negation of pillars; and it has been said: they have pillars but we do not see them — and the first is more correct.

ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ — this is the place of the shāhid from the noble āyah for the affirming of al-istiwāʾ; and the discussion on the remaining āyāt is like the discussion on this one.

مَعَانِي «اسْتَوَى» فِي لُغَةِ العَرَب The Senses of istawā in the Speech of the Arabs

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Before the refutation, the lexical ground. al-Fawzān: and it has four meanings in the language of the Arabs —

عَلَاhe was high over
ارْتَفَعَhe rose
صَعِدَhe ascended
اسْتَقَرَّhe settled

And these four meanings are what the explanations of the Salaf for the istiwāʾ mentioned in these noble āyāt revolve around.

That is the entire controversy in one observation. The Salaf had four words to hand, and all four of them are words of height. Not one of them is اسْتَوْلَى.

أَرْبَعَةُ أَوْجُهٍ فِي رَدِّ تَأْوِيلِ «اسْتَوْلَى» Four Grounds for Rejecting the Reading "He Seized Power"

al-Fawzān states what is derived from all seven āyāt together: the affirming of Allah's istiwāʾ upon His Throne in the manner that befits His majesty — and in them is the refutation of whoever reinterpreted al-istiwāʾ as meaning seizing control and overpowering, and explained the Throne as meaning the dominion, so he said: "He rose over the Throne" means "He seized the dominion and overpowered everyone else." And this is invalid from many angles, among them:

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَوَّلًا · It is a newly invented interpretation. It contradicts the explanation of the Salaf — of the Ṣaḥābah, the Tābiʿīn and those who followed them. And the first to say it were the Jahmiyyah and the Muʿtazilah — so it is rejected.

ثَانِيًا · It would make mentioning the Throne pointless. Were seizing the dominion what is meant, there would be no difference between the Throne and the lowest seventh earth and the beasts and all the created things — because He is in control of all of them and the Owner of all of them. So there would be no benefit in the mention of the Throne at all.

ثَالِثًا · The wording never varies. This expression اسْتَوَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ has run consistently through the Book and the Sunnah, and not in a single wording has اسْتَوْلَى عَلَى الْعَرْشِ come — so that the remaining texts could be explained by it.

رَابِعًا · The ثُمَّ destroys it. He brought ثُمَّ, which conveys sequence and an interval. Were the meaning of al-istiwāʾ the seizing of the Throne and power over it, that would not have been delayed until after the creation of the heavens and the earth — for the Throne existed before the creation of the heavens and the earth by fifty thousand years, as is established in the two Ṣaḥīḥs. So how could it be permissible that He was not able, nor in control of it, until He created the heavens and the earth?! هَذَا مِنْ أَبْطَلِ البَاطِلِ — this is of the most false of the false. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

The fourth is the one worth sitting with. It does not argue from the lexicon or from the Salaf; it argues from a single particle. ثُمَّ puts the istiwāʾ after the creating. If istiwāʾ meant gaining control, then before that moment Allah was not in control of a Throne that had already existed for fifty thousand years. The taʾwīl, followed out honestly, makes Allah deficient — which is the very thing its authors said they were protecting Him from.

١٩ · إِثْبَاتُ عُلُوِّ اللهِ عَلَى مَخْلُوقَاتِه Section 19 — The ʿUluww of Allah above His Creation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿يَا عِيسَى إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ﴾، ﴿بَلْ رَفَعَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ﴾، ﴿إِلَيْهِ يَصْعَدُ الْكَلِمُ الطَّيِّبُ وَالْعَمَلُ الصَّالِحُ يَرْفَعُهُ﴾، ﴿يَا هَامَانُ ابْنِ لِي صَرْحًا لَعَلِّي أَبْلُغُ الْأَسْبَابَ ۝ أَسْبَابَ السَّمَاوَاتِ فَأَطَّلِعَ إِلَى إِلَهِ مُوسَى وَإِنِّي لَأَظُنُّهُ كَاذِبًا﴾، ﴿أَأَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يَخْسِفَ بِكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فَإِذَا هِيَ تَمُورُ ۝ أَمْ أَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يُرْسِلَ عَلَيْكُمْ حَاصِبًا فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ كَيْفَ نَذِيرِ﴾.

إِذْ قَالَ اللَّهُ يَا عِيسَى إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ

"When Allah said: O ʿĪsā, I am going to take you and raise you to Myself."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:55 · سُورَةُ آلُ عِمْرَان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

إِنِّي مُتَوَفِّيكَ — al-Fawzān reports the majority position: what is intended by al-wafāh here is sleep, as in وَهُوَ الَّذِي يَتَوَفَّاكُمْ بِاللَّيْلِ — «and He is the One who takes you by night» (al-Anʿām 6:60) — and اللَّهُ يَتَوَفَّى الْأَنْفُسَ حِينَ مَوْتِهَا وَالَّتِي لَمْ تَمُتْ فِي مَنَامِهَا — «Allah takes the souls at the time of their death, and those that do not die, in their sleep» (al-Zumar 39:42).

وَرَافِعُكَ إِلَيَّ — Allah raised him to Himself in the heaven while he is alive; and this is the place of the shāhid from the āyah, which is the affirming of al-ʿuluww for Allah — because the raising is to what is above.

بَلْ رَفَعَهُ اللَّهُ إِلَيْهِ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ عَزِيزًا حَكِيمًا

"Rather, Allah raised him to Himself — and Allah is ever Mighty, Wise."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:158 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

This is a refutation of the Jews, who claim that they killed the Messiah ʿĪsā the son of Maryam — against which Allah said: «and they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him; rather it was made to appear so to them» — وَمَا قَتَلُوهُ وَمَا صَلَبُوهُ وَلَكِنْ شُبِّهَ لَهُمْ (al-Nisāʾ 4:157). He raised the Messiah to Himself while he is alive and was not killed; and this is the place of the shāhid, because in it is the affirming of Allah's highness above His creation — for the raising is to what is above.

إِلَيْهِ يَصْعَدُ الْكَلِمُ الطَّيِّبُ وَالْعَمَلُ الصَّالِحُ يَرْفَعُهُ

"To Him ascends the goodly word, and righteous action raises it."

Sūrat Fāṭir 35:10 · سُورَةُ فَاطِر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الكَلِمُ الطَّيِّب is the remembrance, the recitation and the supplication. And وَالْعَمَلُ الصَّالِحُ يَرْفَعُهُ — righteous action raises the goodly word, for the goodly word is not accepted except together with righteous action.

And then the sentence that should make a student put the book down for a moment: فَمَنْ ذَكَرَ اللهَ تَعَالَى وَلَمْ يُؤَدِّ فَرَائِضَهُ رُدَّ كَلَامُه — so whoever makes remembrance of Allah and does not discharge His obligations, his speech is returned to him.

al-Fawzān brings two of the early generation on it. إِيَاسُ بْنُ مُعَاوِيَة: were it not for righteous action, speech would not be raised. And الحَسَنُ and قَتَادَةُ: no statement is accepted except with action.

And the shāhid from the āyah: that in it is the affirming of Allah's highness above His creation — because ascending and raising are both to what is above.

وَقَالَ فِرْعَوْنُ يَا هَامَانُ ابْنِ لِي صَرْحًا لَعَلِّي أَبْلُغُ الْأَسْبَابَ أَسْبَابَ السَّمَاوَاتِ فَأَطَّلِعَ إِلَى إِلَهِ مُوسَى وَإِنِّي لَأَظُنُّهُ كَاذِبًا

"And Pharaoh said: O Hāmān, build for me a tower, that I may reach the ways — the ways of the heavens — and look upon the God of Mūsā; and indeed I think him a liar."

Sūrat Ghāfir 40:36–37 · سُورَةُ غَافِر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is from the statement of Pharaoh to his minister Hāmān, commanding him to build him a lofty, high castle. أَسْبَابَ السَّمَاوَاتِ — the roads of the heavens, or their gates. And a grammatical note, since the matn quotes it: فَأَطَّلِعَ is manṣūb by an implied أَنْ after the فَاءِ السَّبَبِيَّة.

And the meaning of this statement of his is the belying of Mūsā عليه السلام in that Allah sent him, or in that he has a God in the heaven — and that is why he said وَإِنِّي لَأَظُنُّهُ كَاذِبًا.

Now follow the logic of the proof, because it is an unusual one. The shāhid is that in it is the affirming of Allah's highness above His creation — since Mūsā عليه السلام informed of that, and Pharaoh set about trying to reach the heavens to look into it. Pharaoh is not a witness for the truth. He is a witness for what Mūsā had taught: his mockery takes the form of building upward, which only makes sense if upward is where he had been told to look. The enemy's own ridicule preserves the doctrine he was ridiculing.

أَأَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يَخْسِفَ بِكُمُ الْأَرْضَ فَإِذَا هِيَ تَمُورُ أَمْ أَمِنْتُمْ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ أَنْ يُرْسِلَ عَلَيْكُمْ حَاصِبًا فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ كَيْفَ نَذِيرِ

"Do you feel secure from Him who is above the heaven, that He should cause the earth to swallow you up — and then it is heaving? Or do you feel secure from Him who is above the heaven, that He should send upon you a storm of stones? Then you will know how My warning was."

Sūrat al-Mulk 67:16–17 · سُورَةُ المُلْك

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الأَمْن — security is the opposite of fear. مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ — a punishment from Him who is above the heaven, and He is Allah سُبْحَانَهُ.

And then the point of Arabic that carries the whole āyah. The meaning of فِي السَّمَاءِ is عَلَى السَّمَاءِ — above the heaven — as in His saying وَلَأُصَلِّبَنَّكُمْ فِي جُذُوعِ النَّخْلِ, «and I will surely crucify you on the trunks of the palm trees» (Ṭā-Hā 20:71), where no one imagines being crucified inside a trunk. This is if what is intended by «the heaven» is the built heaven. And if what is intended by «the heaven» is absolute height, then the فِي is for the adverbial sense — that is: in the loftiness.

أَنْ يَخْسِفَ بِكُمُ الْأَرْضَ — He uproots it with you, as He did with Qārūn; تَمُورُ — it convulses and moves. حَاصِبًا — stones from the sky, as He sent them upon the people of Lūṭ and the Companions of the Elephant; and it has been said: a cloud with stones in it; and it has been said: a wind with stones in it. فَسَتَعْلَمُونَ كَيْفَ نَذِيرِ — My warning, when you behold the punishment.

And the shāhid from the two āyāt: that in them is the affirming of Allah's highness above His creation, since the two of them stated explicitly that He سُبْحَانَهُ is above the heaven.

الفَرْقُ بَيْنَ الاسْتِوَاءِ وَالعُلُوّ The Difference Between al-Istiwāʾ and al-ʿUluww

Having established both, al-Fawzān will not let the student run them together. These āyāt which the author رَحِمَهُ اللهُ mentioned indicate the affirming of al-ʿuluww, just as these āyāt before them indicated the affirming of Allah's istiwāʾ upon the Throne. They are two attributes, not one, and they differ in two ways.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

العُلُوّالاسْتِوَاء
١ kind of attributeof the attributes of the Essence — His highness above His creation is a description necessarily belonging to His Essenceof the attributes of the Actions — an act among His acts, which He does by His will and His power when He wills; and that is why He said ثُمَّ اسْتَوَى, and it was after the creation of the heavens and the earth
٢ how establishedestablished by reason and transmission bothestablished by transmission, not reason

The second row is the quiet methodological lesson. ʿUluww the intellect can reach — perfection is above deficiency, and the Creator is not contained by what He created. Istiwāʾ it cannot: no amount of reasoning would have told you that He rose over the Throne after creating the heavens and the earth. That is known only because He said it — which is exactly why the one who tries to reason his way to a meaning for it ends up at اسْتَوْلَى.
