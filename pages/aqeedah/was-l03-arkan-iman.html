أَرْكَانُ الإِيمَانِ السِّتَّة

The Six Pillars of Īmān

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define الإِيمَان in the language and in the Sharīʿah.
  • Name the six pillars in the matn’s order and state what belief in each means.
  • Give the difference between a نَبِيّ and a رَسُول.
  • Name the أُولُو العَزْم among the messengers.
أَرْكَانُ الإِيمَانِ السِّتَّة The Six Pillars of Īmān

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَهُوَ الإِيمَانُ بِاللهِ، وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ، وَكُتُبِهِ، وَرُسُلِهِ، وَالبَعْثِ بَعْدَ المَوْتِ، وَالإِيمَانُ بِالقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الإِيمَان in the language is التَّصْدِيق — affirming something as true. al-Fawzān proves it from the brothers of Yūsuf saying وَمَا أَنْتَ بِمُؤْمِنٍ لَنَا, “you would not be a believer of us” — meaning you would not credit us (Yūsuf 12:17).

And in the Sharīʿah it is three things together, and the definition is worth learning exactly: قَوْلٌ بِاللِّسَانِ، وَاعْتِقَادٌ بِالقَلْبِ، وَعَمَلٌ بِالجَوَارِحِ — a statement with the tongue, a conviction in the heart, and action with the limbs. Drop any one and what remains is not what the Sharīʿah calls īmān.

These six are the pillars without all of which no one's faith is sound — believed in together, in the correct manner the Book and the Sunnah indicate.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

1 · الإِيمَانُ بِاللهِ — that He is described with the attributes of perfection, free of every defect and deficiency, and that He alone deserves worship with no partner; and to establish that in knowledge and in action.

2 · الإِيمَانُ بِالمَلَائِكَةِ — affirming their existence and what Allah and His Messenger ﷺ reported of them. They are عِبَادٌ مُكْرَمُونَ لَا يَسْبِقُونَهُ بِالْقَوْلِ وَهُمْ بِأَمْرِهِ يَعْمَلُونَ — honoured servants who do not precede Him in speech and who act by His command (al-Anbiyāʾ 21:26–27).

3 · الإِيمَانُ بِالكُتُبِ — affirming the books Allah sent down upon His messengers, that they are His speech, and that they are truth, light and guidance. We believe in those He named, such as the Tawrāh, and in those He did not name.

4 · الإِيمَانُ بِالرُّسُلِ — affirming them all, that they were truthful in what they reported and that they conveyed the messages of their Lord; we do not distinguish between any of them, but believe in all — those Allah named in His Book and those He did not (al-Nisāʾ 4:164). The best of them are أُولُو العَزْمِ: Nūḥ, Ibrāhīm, Mūsā, ʿĪsā and Muḥammad عَلَيْهِمُ الصَّلَاةُ وَالسَّلَامُ; then the rest of the messengers, then the prophets — and the best of all is the seal of the messengers, our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ.

نَبِيّ or رَسُول? The soundest of what is said: a nabī is one to whom a law was revealed but who was not commanded to convey it; a rasūl is one to whom a law was revealed and who was commanded to convey it.

5 · الإِيمَانُ بِالبَعْثِ — affirming that the dead will be brought out of their graves alive on the Day of Resurrection, for judgement between them and recompense for their deeds, in the manner Allah set out in His Book and His Messenger ﷺ in his Sunnah.

6 · الإِيمَانُ بِالقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ — and note how al-Fawzān states it, because the four stages of qadar are already inside the sentence: that Allah knew the measures of things and their times before they existed, then wrote them in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ, then brought them into being by His power and His will at their appointed times. So everything that comes to pass, of good or evil, proceeds from His knowledge, His decree, His will and His intent: what He willed came to be, and what He did not will did not come to be.

al-Fawzān closes by noting that this is only a summary — “and the detailed explanation of these foundations will come, Allah willing.” The rest of the Wāsiṭiyyah is that detail, which is why this lesson is the map for everything after it.

الإِيمَانُ فِي الشَّرْعِ هُوَ التَّصْدِيقُ بِالقَلْبِ فَقَطْ. Īmān in the Sharīʿah is affirmation of the heart only.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

قَوْلٌ بِاللِّسَانِ، وَاعْتِقَادٌ بِالقَلْبِ، وَعَمَلٌ بِالجَوَارِحِ

The Sharīʿah definition of īmān, in three clauses. Learn it in this form — it is the line that separates ahl al-sunnah from both those who reduced faith to words and those who expelled from it anyone who sinned.
