الإِرَادَتَانِ وَإِثْبَاتُ المَحَبَّة

The Two Wills, and the Attribute of Love

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٤١–٤٦
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 41–46.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Distinguish الإِرَادَة الكَوْنِيَّة from الإِرَادَة الشَّرْعِيَّة on all three of al-Fawzān’s points.
  • Say which will the Jabriyyah affirmed and which the Qadariyyah, and why both went astray.
  • Explain what the āyāt of المَحَبَّة refute.
  • Give Ibn al-Qayyim on the two kinds of Allah’s تَوْبَة.
الإِرَادَتَانِ: كَوْنِيَّةٌ وَشَرْعِيَّة The Two Wills

Before leaving the will, al-Fawzān sets out a distinction without which a great deal of the Qurʾān reads as contradiction. Allah says He does not will hardship for you (al-Māʾidah 5:6) — and also that nothing happens in His dominion except what He intends (al-Baqarah 2:253). Both are true, because will is of two kinds.

مَا يُرِيدُ اللَّهُ لِيَجْعَلَ عَلَيْكُمْ مِنْ حَرَجٍ وَلَٰكِنْ يُرِيدُ لِيُطَهِّرَكُمْ

"Allah does not intend to place you in hardship, but He intends to purify you."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:6 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The three differences, as al-Fawzān lays them out:

الإِرَادَة الكَوْنِيَّةالإِرَادَة الشَّرْعِيَّة
1. LoveHe may love it, or may notHe must love and be pleased with it
2. Purposeintended for something else — as Iblīs and the evils were created, so that striving, repentance and seeking forgiveness would come about through themintended for itself
3. Occurrencemust come to passneed not — it may occur, or may not

Which yields the sentence that dissolves the apparent contradiction: أَرَادَ المَعْصِيَةَ كَوْنًا وَلَا يَرْضَاهَا شَرْعًا — Allah willed disobedience universally, and is not pleased with it legislatively. And obedience He willed both ways, and loved it, and was pleased with it.

al-Fawzān adds two تَنْبِيه. First: the two wills come together in the case of the sincere and obedient servant, and the universal will stands alone in the case of the disobedient.

And second — a diagnostic worth keeping, because it sorts two whole sects at once: whoever does not affirm both wills and distinguish between them has gone astray. The Jabriyyah affirmed the universal will alone, so nothing was left of the servant's responsibility. The Qadariyyah affirmed the legislated will alone, so nothing was left of Allah's dominion. Each took half the truth and was wrecked on the half it dropped.

٦ · إِثْبَاتُ المَحَبَّةِ وَالمَوَدَّة Section 6 — Love and Affection

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَأَحْسِنُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُحْسِنِينَ﴾، ﴿وَأَقْسِطُوا إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ﴾، ﴿إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿قُلْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَهُوَ الْغَفُورُ الْوَدُودُ﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Having brought the āyāt of will, the author now brings the āyāt of love — and al-Fawzān names the target: in this is the refutation of those who equated will with love. If the two were the same thing, then everything Allah willed He would love, and the whole of the preceding section collapses. The distinction between the two wills is exactly what makes love a separate attribute.

قُلْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ

"Say: if you love Allah, then follow me — and Allah will love you."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:31 · سُورَةُ آلُ عِمْرَان

The āyah of the test. Love of Allah is not measured by what a man feels but by whom he follows — and the reward for following is that the love becomes mutual. The scholars have called this āyat al-imtiḥān, the verse of the examination, for exactly that reason.

إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ التَّوَّابِينَ وَيُحِبُّ الْمُتَطَهِّرِينَ

"Indeed Allah loves those who turn to Him in repentance, and He loves those who purify themselves."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:222 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّوَّابِين is an intensive form from التَّوْبَة, which in the language is returning, and in the Sharīʿah is returning from sin. But al-Fawzān notes that this is its meaning with respect to the servant — because التَّوَّاب is also one of Allah's own names. And here Ibn al-Qayyim draws the distinction:

“The servant is tawwāb, and Allah is Tawwāb. The servant’s turning is his returning to his Master. And Allah’s turning is of two kinds: permission and enabling — and acceptance and reckoning it for him.”

Read that order carefully, because it is the whole doctrine of repentance in one line. Allah's turning comes first, as permission and enabling — the servant could not repent at all unless granted it. Then the servant turns. Then Allah's turning comes again, as acceptance. The servant's repentance is bracketed on both sides by Allah's.

And المُتَطَهِّرِين is from الطَّهَارَة — freedom and cleanliness from filth, whether physical or moral. Both kinds, in one word.

On إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُتَّقِينَ: taqwā, al-Fawzān defines, is guarding oneself by obedience to Allah against disobedience to Him, hoping for His reward and fearing His punishment. And the context in which it appears is striking — the command to keep a treaty with the mushrikīn so long as they keep it, with “Allah loves the muttaqīn” as the reason. Keeping your word to an enemy is named among the deeds of the God-fearing.

فَسَوْفَ يَأْتِي اللَّهُ بِقَوْمٍ يُحِبُّهُمْ وَيُحِبُّونَهُ

"…then Allah will bring a people whom He loves and who love Him."

Sūrat al-Māʾidah 5:54 · سُورَةُ المَائِدَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān identifies them: Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ and his army of Companions and Successors who fought the people of apostasy — and then everyone who came after them fighting the apostates, until the Day of Resurrection. A description left open at one end.

And of those described as صَفًّا — they rank themselves in rows at the fighting and do not budge from their places; كَأَنَّهُمْ بُنْيَانٌ مَرْصُوصٌ means packed and joined one part to another, with no gap in it and no flaw.

وَهُوَ الْغَفُورُ الْوَدُودُ

"And He is the Forgiving, the Loving."

Sūrat al-Burūj 85:14 · سُورَةُ البُرُوج

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الغَفُور is abundant in forgiveness; and الغَفْر is covering, so He covers the sins of whoever turns to Him and passes over his faults. الوَدُود is from الوُدّ — pure love — so He loves the people of His obedience.

And al-Fawzān points out that in mentioning these two names together there is سِرٌّ لَطِيفٌ, a subtle secret: He loves His servant after the forgiveness — He forgives him, and then loves him after that.

Sit with that. The order is not love, therefore forgive. It is forgive, and then love. The sin is not merely overlooked by a love that was already there; the forgiving is itself the doorway into being loved.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

قُلْ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ

The verse of the examination. Every claim to love Allah is tested by one question, and the answer is not a feeling: whom do you follow?
