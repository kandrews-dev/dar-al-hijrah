مَعْنَى الإِيمَان The Meaning of Īmān

In Units 1 and 2 we studied what we believe about Allah — His oneness (tawḥīd) and His beautiful names. Now we step back to the bigger framework: īmān itself, and its six pillars. The word īmān comes from the root أ-م-ن, connected to amān (security) and tasdīq (affirming as true). To have īmān is to affirm the truth with the heart in a way that brings certainty and security.

الإيمان Īmān — Ahl al-Sunnah definition

قَوْلٌ بِاللِّسَانِ، وَاعْتِقَادٌ بِالجَنَانِ، وَعَمَلٌ بِالأَرْكَانِ

Statement with the tongue, belief in the heart, and action with the limbs.

Faith is not belief in the heart alone — it is affirmed by the tongue and proven by action. This is why Ahl al-Sunnah say that īmān increases with obedience and decreases with disobedience.

حَدِيثُ جِبْرِيل The Ḥadīth of Jibrīl — "The Mother of the Sunnah"

One day, while the Prophet ﷺ sat with his Companions, a man with intensely white clothes and jet-black hair came and sat knee-to-knee with him. No one knew him, yet he showed no sign of travel. He asked the Prophet ﷺ about Islām, then Īmān, then Iḥsān, then the Hour. When he left, the Prophet ﷺ said: "That was Jibrīl, who came to teach you your religion." Scholars call this the foundational hadith of the entire religion.

«...قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِيمَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاللهِ، وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ، وَكُتُبِهِ، وَرُسُلِهِ، وَالْيَوْمِ الآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالْقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ»

"...He said: Tell me about īmān. He ﷺ said: It is to believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in al-qadar (divine decree) — its good and its bad."

Narrated by Muslim — Ḥadīth of Jibrīl · رواه مسلم

This single sentence lists all six pillars of īmān — and it is the syllabus of this entire unit. We met this same hadith in Unit 2 when it defined iḥsān ("to worship Allah as though you see Him").

الإِسْلَامُ وَالإِيمَانُ وَالإِحْسَان Three Levels of the Religion

The hadith presents the religion as three ascending levels. Each contains the one before it — every muḥsin is a muʾmin, and every muʾmin is a muslim, but not the reverse.

الإسلام

Islām — Submission

The 5 pillars — outward acts of worship (shahādah, ṣalāh, zakāh, ṣawm, ḥajj).

الإيمان

Īmān — Faith

The 6 pillars of belief — the inner convictions of the heart that this unit studies.

الإحسان

Iḥsān — Excellence

The highest level — "to worship Allah as though you see Him." (Unit 2, L.3)

أَرْكَانُ الإِيمَانِ السِّتَّة The Six Pillars of Faith

These are the six beliefs every Muslim must hold. The rest of Unit 3 takes them in turn.

1الإيمان باللهBelief in Allah
2الملائكةHis Angels
3الكُتُبHis Books
4الرُّسُلHis Messengers
5اليَوْمُ الآخِرThe Last Day
6القَدَرDivine Decree

Note on the count: The Quran sometimes lists five (e.g. al-Baqarah 2:177 mentions Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets). Belief in al-qadar is established explicitly in the Ḥadīth of Jibrīl and other verses (e.g. 54:49). Together they make the well-known six.

هَلْ يَزِيدُ الإِيمَانُ وَيَنْقُص؟ Does Īmān Increase and Decrease?

Yes. Because īmān includes action, it is not a fixed switch that is simply "on" or "off." It increases with obedience (prayer, dhikr, good deeds) and decreases with disobedience (sin and heedlessness). This is the position of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah, and it is full of hope: every good deed you do strengthens your faith.

إِنَّمَا الْمُؤْمِنُونَ الَّذِينَ إِذَا ذُكِرَ اللَّهُ وَجِلَتْ قُلُوبُهُمْ وَإِذَا تُلِيَتْ عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتُهُ زَادَتْهُمْ إِيمَانًا

"The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts tremble, and when His verses are recited to them, it increases them in faith."

Sūrat al-Anfāl 8:2 · سورة الأنفال

تَدْرِيبُ القَوَاعِدGrammar Drill — The Active Participle (اسم الفاعل)

From each pillar's verb we form the doer (اسم الفاعل, active participle) on the pattern مُفْعِل / فَاعِل. The one who has īmān is a مُؤْمِن (muʾmin), the one who does Islām is a مُسْلِم (muslim). Identify the root of each word.

الإيمان بالقَدَر خيره وشره من أركان الإيمان. Belief in qadar, its good and bad, is one of the pillars of īmān.
كل مسلم هو محسن بالضرورة. Every muslim is necessarily a muḥsin (one of iḥsān).
