أَقْسَامُ الصِّفَات

Categories of the Attributes

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Distinguish thubūtiyyah (affirmed) attributes from salbiyyah (negated) attributes.
  • Distinguish dhātiyyah (essential) attributes from fiʿliyyah (action-related) attributes.
  • Know the four rules Ahl al-Sunnah apply when negating something of Allah.

In Lesson 1 you learned the four forbidden doors — taḥrīf, taʿṭīl, takyīf, tamthīl. Now we sort the attributes themselves into the categories the imams used, so when you read al-Wāsiṭiyyah and the Qur'an you know exactly what you are looking at.

الصِّفَاتُ الثُّبُوتِيَّةُ وَالسَّلْبِيَّة 1. Affirmed vs. Negated Attributes

The first division: every attribute Allah has is either thubūtiyyah (affirmed — something Allah has) or salbiyyah (negated — something Allah is free of).

الصفات الثبوتيةaṣ-ṣifāt ath-thubūtiyyah · affirmed attributes

What Allah has affirmed of Himself, or what His Messenger ﷺ affirmed for Him — like life, knowledge, power, hearing, sight, mercy, wisdom, speech, and so on. These are most of the attributes mentioned in the Qur'an and Sunnah, because affirmation is more complete praise than mere negation.

الصفات السلبيةaṣ-ṣifāt as-salbiyyah · negated attributes

What Allah has denied of Himself — like negating tiredness, sleep, a son, a partner, any likeness. «لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ» ("there is nothing like Him," al-Shūrā 42:11) and «وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا» ("guarding them tires Him not," al-Baqarah 2:255) are negations.

A foundational point — قاعدة

The Qur'an is mostly affirmation, not negation. Why? Because affirming an attribute of perfection (like knowledge) implies the negation of its opposite (ignorance) and establishes a positive praise. Pure negation, by itself, is no praise — a stone "is not unjust," but that is not praise of the stone. So Ahl al-Sunnah affirm in detail what Allah affirmed, and negate in summary what Allah negated.

الصِّفَاتُ الذَّاتِيَّةُ وَالفِعْلِيَّة 2. Essential vs. Action-Related Attributes

The second division (within the affirmed attributes): every affirmed attribute is either dhātiyyah (tied to Allah's very essence, never separated from Him) or fiʿliyyah (tied to His will — He does it when He wills).

النوعDescriptionExamples
EssentialذاتيةNever separated from Allah — He always has themlife, knowledge, power, hearing, sight, face, hands
Action-relatedفعليةTied to His will — He acts when He wills, how He willsrising over the Throne (istiwāʾ), descending (nuzūl), speaking, coming, being pleased, becoming angry

Some attributes are both — like speech (kalām). The capacity to speak is essential (Allah is always a speaker); the actual speaking of a particular word happens when He wills. So speech is dhātiyyah in one sense and fiʿliyyah in another.

قَوَاعِدُ النَّفْي 3. Four Rules When Negating Something of Allah

Ahl al-Sunnah do not invent negations. When Allah or His Messenger ﷺ negate something of Him, four rules apply:

  1. The negation must come from the texts — Qur'an or Sunnah — never from our reasoning.
  2. What is negated is a deficiency — sleep, tiredness, ignorance, a partner, a likeness.
  3. The negation implies the opposite perfection — negating tiredness affirms perfect strength; negating sleep affirms perfect life and watching over creation.
  4. We do not negate in detail what Allah did not negate in detail — we negate in summary "any likeness" because Allah did; we do not list out one by one what He "does not" have.

Memorize & Reflect — احفظ وتدبّر

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like Him; and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing." (al-Shūrā 42:11) — this single āyah unites the two: negation in summary ("nothing like Him") and affirmation in detail ("All-Hearing, All-Seeing"). This is the way of Ahl al-Sunnah.
