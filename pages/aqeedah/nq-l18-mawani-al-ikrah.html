مَوَانِعُ التَّكْفِير · مَانِعُ الإِكْرَاه

The Barriers to Takfīr — Coercion

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Explain what مَوَانِعُ تَكْفِيرِ المُعَيَّن means, and the one distinction the whole section exists to draw — the ruling on the act versus the ruling on a particular person.
  • Give the matn’s own closing sentence and how its exception «إِلَّا المُكْرَه» opens the barriers.
  • State the daleel of the barrier of coercion (al-Naḥl 16:106) and the ijmāʿ on coercion to say kufr.
  • Give the ṣaḥīḥ position on coercion to do an act of kufr, and the reasoning «القَوْلُ وَالعَمَلُ شَيْءٌ وَاحِد».
  • State Ibn Bāz’s two conditions for the excuse of the coerced, and the standing of one who acts with no coercion or is content.
  • Give the three levels of الغَضَب and the ruling on one whose reason is overtaken, with the precedent the scholars cited.
  • Name what is not coercion (acting for a post or wealth), and the ijmāʿ that coercion never makes lawful the killing of a maʿṣūm.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ٢٣٦–٢٤٢
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 236–242, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَلَا فَرْقَ فِي جَمِيعِ هَذِهِ النَّوَاقِضِ بَيْنَ الهَازِلِ وَالجَادِّ وَالخَائِفِ إِلَّا المُكْرَه. نَعُوذُ بِاللهِ مِنْ مُوجِبَاتِ غَضَبِه، وَأَلِيمِ عِقَابِه، وَصَلَّى اللهُ عَلَى خَيْرِ خَلْقِهِ مُحَمَّد.

The author seals the ten with a single sentence: in none of these nullifiers is there a difference between the one who jests, the one in earnest, and the one afraid — except the one coerced. That lone exception is the door to the last section of the book. The jester is not excused by his jest, nor the frightened by his fear — those were refused, one by one, in the clauses before. Only al-mukrah, the coerced, is set apart. And naming one barrier opens the question of the rest.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is where the book turns from the act to the man.

Everything before this named what nullifies Islām — the النَّوْع, the type of deed. This closing section asks the harder question: granting that an act is a nullifier, when may you rule that this particular person — المُعَيَّن — has left the religion by it? The two are not the same ruling, and the whole section is built to keep them apart.

This is exactly the distinction the eighth nullifier ended pointing toward. There, the spy Ḥāṭib ؓ did an act that in its type is betrayal — and he was not declared a disbeliever, because a barrier stood in the way of applying the ruling to him. «الحُكْمُ عَلَى الفِعْلِ شَيْء، وَالحُكْمُ عَلَى المُعَيَّنِ شَيْءٌ آخَر.» A thing may be kufr, and the man who did it may yet not be a kāfir — because takfīr of a named person requires that its conditions be met and its barriers absent.

Imām Ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb named one barrier in the matn — coercion — and the compiler gathers Ibn Bāz’s words on the four the scholars count: الإِكْرَاه (coercion), الجَهْل (ignorance), الخَطَأ (honest error) and التَّأْوِيل (misinterpretation). This lesson takes the first; the two after it take the rest. Read them as the discipline the entire book has been teaching: the same scholars most willing to name a deed a nullifier are the most careful about pinning it on a man.

مَانِعُ الإِكْرَاه The Barrier of Coercion

The matn’s one stated exception. A man forced, under threat he cannot escape, to utter or do kufr — while his heart holds firm — is not touched by it. The daleel is the verse revealed about عَمَّارِ بْنِ يَاسِر ؓ (ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir), made to speak a word of disbelief under torture:

مَن كَفَرَ بِاللَّهِ مِن بَعْدِ إِيمَانِهِ إِلَّا مَنْ أُكْرِهَ وَقَلْبُهُ مُطْمَئِنٌّ بِالْإِيمَانِ

Whoever disbelieves in Allah after his belief — except one who is coerced while his heart is at rest upon faith…

سُورَةُ النَّحْل · 16:106

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz reads the coerced man as one driven, not one who chose. «المُكْرَهُ الَّذِي أُكْرِهَ بِالضَّرْبِ أَوْ بِالتَّهْدِيدِ بِالقَتْلِ مِمَّنْ يَظُنُّ إِيقَاعَهُ بِه... كَمَا أَنَّ مَنْ أُكْرِهَ عَلَى الكُفْرِ لَا يَكْفُرُ بِنُطْقِهِ بِالكُفْرِ بِسَبَبِ الإِكْرَاهِ عَلَيْه؛ لِأَنَّهُ مُلْجَأٌ إِلَى ذَلِك.» His tongue moved; his will did not. The word is spoken to push away the whip or the sword, and the heart behind it never left.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The footnote fixes the reach of the excuse. The scholars agreed that one coerced into saying kufr is excused. They differed over coercion into an act — and the ṣaḥīḥ view is that it too is excused: «لِعُمُومِ الآيَة ﴿إِلَّا مَنْ أُكْرِه﴾ فَلَمْ تُقَيَّدْ بِقَوْلٍ أَوْ عَمَل... وَلِأَنَّ القَوْلَ وَالعَمَلَ شَيْءٌ وَاحِدٌ مِنْ جِهَةِ التَّكْفِير؛ وَإِذَا عُذِرَ بِقَوْلِ كَلِمَةِ الكُفْرِ عُذِرَ بِالعَمَل». The verse says «one coerced» without limiting it to speech.

Then the two conditions, named plainly:

«المُكْرَهُ... لَهُ شَرْطَانِ: ١ ‑ الإِكْرَاه، وَهُوَ كَوْنُهُ يَعْجِزُ عَنِ التَّخَلُّص. ٢ ‑ وَالطُّمَأْنِينَةُ بِالإِيمَان. فَإِذَا أُكْرِهَ وَقَلْبُهُ مُطْمَئِنٌّ بِالإِيمَانِ فَلَا شَيْءَ عَلَيْه؛ وَالإِثْمُ عَلَى مَنْ أَكْرَهَه.»

And the limit on the other side, which keeps the excuse honest: «فَأَمَّا إِذَا خَرَجَ مِنْ غَيْرِ إِكْرَاه، أَوْ خَرَجَ مُكْرَهًا وَلَكِنِ اطْمَأَنَّ إِلَيْهِم — فَلَيْسَ بِمُكْرَه؛ بَاءَ بِإِثْمِ ذَلِك.» Compulsion he cannot escape, and a heart that did not go over: remove either, and the barrier is gone.

الغَضَبُ وَمَرَاتِبُه Overwhelming Anger, and Its Three Levels

Ibn Bāz places intense anger beside coercion, because both can take a man’s intent out of his own hands. He gives the people of knowledge’s three levels — and the ruling turns, as it did with coercion, on whether any choice remained.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — المَرْتَبَةُ الأُولَى — أَنْ يَشْتَدَّ غَضَبُهُ حَتَّى يَفْقِدَ عَقْلَه؟

Ibn Bāz — «حُكْمُهُ حُكْمُ المَجَانِينِ وَالمَعَاتِيه؛ لَا يَتَرَتَّبُ عَلَى كَلَامِهِ حُكْم — لَا طَلَاقُه، وَلَا سَبُّه، وَلَا غَيْرُ ذَلِك.» A man whose reason is gone carries no ruling at all.

Q — المَرْتَبَةُ الثَّانِيَة — دُونَ ذَلِك، يَغْلِبُ عَلَيْهِ الغَضَبُ حَتَّى يَصِيرَ كَالمَدْفُوعِ الَّذِي لَا يَسْتَطِيعُ التَّخَلُّص؟

Ibn Bāz — The scholars differed; and Ibn Bāz gives his own verdict: «هَذَا القَوْلُ أَرْجَحُ عِنْدِي، وَهُوَ الَّذِي أُفْتِي بِه» — he is «أَقْرَبُ إِلَى المَجْنُونِ... مِنْهُ إِلَى العَاقِلِ السَّلِيم», so no ṭalāq falls and no ridda is recorded, «لَكِنْ لَا مَانِعَ مِنْ كَوْنِهِ يُؤَدَّبُ بَعْضَ الأَدَب» by way of caution — but he is not judged with the ruling of the apostates.

Q — المَرْتَبَةُ الثَّالِثَة — الغَضَبُ العَادِيُّ الَّذِي لَا يَزُولُ مَعَهُ العَقْل؟

Ibn Bāz — «تَقَعُ تَصَرُّفَاتُه... لِأَنَّ غَضَبَهُ خَفِيفٌ لَا يُغَيِّرُ عَلَيْهِ قَصْدَهُ وَلَا قَلْبَه.» Ordinary anger changes nothing; the man is sound of mind and his words stand.

The precedent the scholars cited is Mūsā ﷺ. When he found his people at the calf, «اشْتَدَّ غَضَبُهُ عَلَيْهِم», he threw down the Tablets and seized his brother’s head, dragging Hārūn — a Prophet like himself — toward him. «فَلَمْ يُؤَاخِذْهُ اللهُ لَا بِإِلْقَاءِ الأَلْوَاحِ وَلَا بِجَرِّ أَخِيه.» Had he done either in contempt, while composed, it would have been grave; but overtaking anger for Allah’s sake lifted the reckoning. Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim, the sharḥ notes, chose this view and «بَسَطَا» its proofs.

مَا لَيْسَ بِإِكْرَاه What Is Not Coercion

The barrier is for the one truly compelled — not for the one who bargains. Ibn Bāz draws the line at the motive.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He divides the man faced with a command into two: «حَالٌ يُطِيعُهُ مُخْتَارًا — فَإِذَا قِيلَ لَه: اشْرَبِ الخَمْرَ شَرِبَ، وَإِذَا قِيلَ لَه: سُبَّ الدِّينَ سَبّ — فَهَذَا لَا عُذْرَ لَهُ فِي اخْتِيَارِه. وَالحَالُ الثَّانِي: يَكُونُ مُكْرَهًا» — threatened with a beating or death he believes will fall, or wine poured into his mouth by force. The first obeys; the second is overpowered.

And the motive that voids the excuse outright: «أَمَّا لِقَصْدِ الدُّنْيَا وَالطَّمَعِ وَالتَّوَسُّعِ فِي المَال، أَوْ لِأَجْلِ حَظِّ الرِّيَاسَة، أَوْ لِأَجْلِ الوَظِيفَة — فَهَذَا كُلُّهُ مُنْكَرٌ وَلَيْسَ بِعُذْر، نَعُوذُ بِاللهِ.» A post, a rank, an income — none of these is the sword at the throat the verse is speaking of.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the one place coercion stops even for the truly compelled: killing a maʿṣūm. The footnote gathers it — Ibn Taymiyyah: «لَوْ أُكْرِهَ رَجُلٌ رَجُلًا عَلَى قَتْلِ مُسْلِمٍ مَعْصُوم، فَإِنَّهُ لَا يَجُوزُ لَهُ قَتْلُهُ بِاتِّفَاقِ المُسْلِمِين... فَلَيْسَ حِفْظُ نَفْسِهِ بِقَتْلِ ذَلِكَ المَعْصُومِ أَوْلَى مِنَ العَكْس»; Ibn Rajab and al-Nawawī report the same ijmāʿ. You may speak a word to save your life; you may not take a protected life to save it.

So the first barrier, read whole, teaches the shape of all four. It does not say the act was no kufr — it says the ruling is withheld from this man because something stood between him and his own will. The act keeps its name; the person is spared it. That is māniʿ — a barrier — and it is the generosity the book builds into its own gravest rulings.

He is rewarded
His case is deferred to the Day of Judgement with no ruling

Ibn Bāz treats intense anger (الغَضَب) as having three levels. What is the ruling on one whose reason is entirely overtaken by rage so that he is like one driven and unable to restrain himself?

His words carry no ruling — «كَالمَجْنُونِ وَالمَعْتُوه» — no ṭalāq falls and no ridda is recorded; this is Ibn Bāz’s own fatwā
He is a disbeliever outright
He is flogged as a ḥadd
He must emigrate

Which precedent did the scholars cite for the one overcome by rage?

Mūsā ﷺ, who in intense anger for Allah threw down the Tablets and seized his brother’s head — and Allah did not take him to task
Yūnus ﷺ leaving his people
Ādam ﷺ eating from the tree
Yūsuf ﷺ in prison

Does one who does an act of kufr for the sake of a job, position or wealth fall under the excuse of coercion?

No — «فَهَذَا كُلُّهُ مُنْكَرٌ وَلَيْسَ بِعُذْر»; the excuse is for genuine compulsion, not for worldly gain
Yes, employment is a recognised coercion
Yes, if he keeps his heart content
Only if he is poor

The footnote carries Ibn Taymiyyah, Ibn Rajab and al-Nawawī on coercion to kill an inviolable Muslim. What is their ruling?

It is not made lawful by coercion — by the agreement of the Muslims — «فَلَيْسَ حِفْظُ نَفْسِهِ بِقَتْلِ ذَلِكَ المَعْصُومِ أَوْلَى مِنَ العَكْس»
It is lawful to save one’s own life
It is lawful only in war
There is no ruling on it

What does the whole barrier of coercion presuppose about the person — and why does that matter for takfīr al-muʿayyan?

That the act was in itself kufr; the barrier does not deny the act’s ruling, it withholds the ruling from this man because a cause preventing it is present
That the act was never kufr to begin with
That no act is ever kufr
That the person was never a Muslim

How does Ibn Bāz describe the one who is coerced in the daleel of al-Naḥl — «مَنْ كَفَرَ... إِلَّا مَنْ أُكْرِه»?

Compelled against his will — «مُلْجَأ» — as one forced into a divorce he did not intend; so his uttered word does not count against him
A hypocrite concealing disbelief
An apostate deserving death
One who loves the disbelievers
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
مَوَانِعُ تَكْفِيرِ المُعَيَّنِ مَعْنَاهَا: أَنَّ الفِعْلَ قَدْ يَكُونُ كُفْرًا وَلَا يُحْكَمُ عَلَى فَاعِلِهِ المُعَيَّنِ بِالكُفْرِ حَتَّى تَنْتَفِيَ المَوَانِع. The barriers to takfīr of a particular person mean: an act may be kufr while its named doer is not ruled a disbeliever until the barriers are absent.
مَنْ أُكْرِهَ عَلَى قَوْلِ الكُفْرِ لَا يُعْذَر، وَإِنَّمَا يُعْذَرُ المُكْرَهُ عَلَى الفِعْلِ فَقَط. One coerced into saying kufr is not excused; only coercion into an act is excused.
لِلْمُكْرَهِ شَرْطَانِ: العَجْزُ عَنِ التَّخَلُّص، وَالطُّمَأْنِينَةُ بِالإِيمَان. The coerced has two conditions: inability to escape, and the heart’s rest upon faith.
مَنْ فَعَلَ الكُفْرَ لِأَجْلِ الوَظِيفَةِ أَوِ المَالِ فَهُوَ مَعْذُورٌ كَالمُكْرَه. One who does kufr for the sake of a job or wealth is excused like the coerced.
مَنِ اشْتَدَّ بِهِ الغَضَبُ حَتَّى فَقَدَ عَقْلَهُ فَحُكْمُهُ حُكْمُ المَجْنُون، لَا يَتَرَتَّبُ عَلَى كَلَامِهِ حُكْم. One whose reason is lost through intense anger carries the ruling of the insane; no ruling attaches to his words.
الإِكْرَاهُ يُبِيحُ قَتْلَ المَعْصُومِ لِحِفْظِ النَّفْس. Coercion makes lawful the killing of an inviolable person to preserve one’s own life.
خَتَمَ المُؤَلِّفُ النَّوَاقِضَ بِقَوْلِهِ: لَا فَرْقَ بَيْنَ الهَازِلِ وَالجَادِّ وَالخَائِفِ إِلَّا المُكْرَه. The author sealed the nullifiers by saying: no difference between the jester, the earnest and the fearful — except the coerced.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

المُكْرَهُ لَهُ شَرْطَانِ: الإِكْرَاهُ وَهُوَ كَوْنُهُ يَعْجِزُ عَنِ التَّخَلُّص، وَالطُّمَأْنِينَةُ بِالإِيمَان؛ فَإِذَا أُكْرِهَ وَقَلْبُهُ مُطْمَئِنٌّ بِالإِيمَانِ فَلَا شَيْءَ عَلَيْه، وَالإِثْمُ عَلَى مَنْ أَكْرَهَه

Ibn Bāz on the first of the four barriers. This is the shape of every barrier in miniature. A deed can be kufr in its type and still not fall upon the man who did it — here because he was overpowered, his will overridden and his heart unmoved. The act keeps its name; the person is shielded by a māniʿ. It is the same care the eighth nullifier showed with the spy, now made a principle: the ruling on an act and the ruling on a particular man are two rulings.
