كَرَامَاتُ الأَوْلِيَاء

The Karāmāt of the Awliyāʾ

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٨٨–١٩٠
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 188–190.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define الكَرَامَة and الوَلِيّ, and say what root the second is derived from.
  • Give the three groups of people regarding the karāmāt, and the argument of the first group.
  • Answer the deniers’ objection in the two ways al-Fawzān answers it.
  • Distinguish a karāmah from a خَارِقٌ شَيْطَانِيّ, and give the test that separates the walī of Allah from the walī of Shayṭān.
  • Name the two classes the matn’s own wording divides the karāmāt into, with an example of each.
  • State the condition on which the karāmāt continue in this Ummah until the Hour.
مَذْهَبُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ فِي كَرَامَاتِ الأَوْلِيَاء The Position of Ahl al-Sunnah on the Karāmāt of the Awliyāʾ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ التَّصْدِيقُ بِكَرَامَاتِ الأَوْلِيَاءِ، وَمَا يَجْرِي اللهُ عَلَى أَيْدِيهِمْ مِنْ خَوَارِقِ العَادَاتِ فِي أَنْوَاعِ العُلُومِ وَالمُكَاشَفَاتِ، وَأَنْوَاعِ القُدْرَةِ وَالتَّأْثِيرَاتِ؛ وَالمَأْثُورُ عَنْ سَالِفِ الأُمَمِ فِي سُورَةِ الكَهْفِ وَغَيْرِهَا، وَعَنْ صَدْرِ هَذِهِ الأُمَّةِ مِنَ الصَّحَابَةِ وَالتَّابِعِينَ وَسَائِرِ فِرَقِ الأُمَّةِ — وَهِيَ مَوْجُودَةٌ فِيهَا إِلَى يَوْمِ القِيَامَةِ.

Part Seven opens, and with it the book turns from what Ahl al-Sunnah believe to how they are. This first section is the bridge: it is still a matter of creed — «and of the foundations of Ahl al-Sunnah» — but it is a creed about the people of faith rather than about Allah and the Last Day. And it is placed here for a reason. Having just finished defending the best generation of this Ummah, the author now states what Allah grants to the righteous of it in every generation after them.

حَدُّ الكَرَامَةِ وَحَدُّ الوَلِيّ Defining a Karāmah, and Defining a Walī

الشَّرْح · the explanation

His words وَمِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّة — that is: of the foundations of their creed. So this is not an optional refinement; affirming it is part of the madhhab.

الكَرَامَةplural كَرَامَات; and it is what Allah brings about at their hands of the breaking of customary order — so a karāmah is a matter that breaks the custom, that is: what is habitual for human beings.
الوَلِيّplural أَوْلِيَاء; and he is the believer who has taqwā — as Allah said in the two āyāt below.

أَلَا إِنَّ أَوْلِيَاءَ اللَّهِ لَا خَوْفٌ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا هُمْ يَحْزَنُونَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَكَانُوا يَتَّقُونَ

"Unquestionably, the awliyāʾ of Allah — no fear is upon them, nor will they grieve: those who believed and used to have taqwā."

Sūrat Yūnus 10:62–63 · سُورَةُ يُونُس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Note how the Qurʾān defines the word itself. It says «the awliyāʾ of Allah», and then the very next āyah tells you who they are: those who believed and used to have taqwā. That is a definition given by Allah, not one assembled by the scholars — which is why no claim of wilāyah can stand against it.

And then the etymology, which carries the whole meaning: he is called a وَلِيّ by derivation from الوَلَاء, and it is love and nearness. So the walī of Allah is: the one who allies himself with Allah by agreeing with Him in what He loves, and by drawing near to Him through what pleases Him.

Read that definition closely, because it decides every question in the section:

الوَلَاء  =  المَحَبَّةُ وَالقُرْب   →   وَلِيُّ اللهِ  =  مَنْ وَالَى اللهَ بِمُوَافَقَتِهِ فِي مَحْبُوبَاتِهِ وَالتَّقَرُّبِ إِلَيْهِ بِمَرْضَاتِهِ

Wilāyah is therefore earned by obedience and by nothing else. It is not a station conferred by a lineage, a robe, a chain of initiation or a tomb. A man is a walī of Allah exactly to the extent that he agrees with what Allah loves — so the most obedient are the greatest awliyāʾ, and a man who opposes the Sharīʿah is not one at all, whatever is reported at his hands.

That last sentence is the key the whole section turns on. Once wilāyah is defined by taqwā, a wonder worked by a man who abandons the prayer proves nothing in his favour — because the definition was never about wonders. Hold on to it, and the third group below becomes obvious rather than complicated.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the karāmāt of the awliyāʾ are true; the Book, the Sunnah, and the mutawātir reports from the Companions and the Tābiʿūn have all indicated them.

النَّاسُ فِيهَا ثَلَاثَةُ أَصْنَاف People Fall Into Three Groups

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān sets the question out as he usually does — the two extremes, then the position of the Sunnah between them. And as in Lesson 49, the middle position is not a compromise between the two: it is the only one that answers both.

١
النَّافُون		Those who deny them, from the people of innovation — like the المُعْتَزِلَة and the الجَهْمِيَّة and some of the Ashāʿirah.
٢
الغَالُون		Those who go to excess in affirming them, from the people of the Ṣūfī orders and the grave-venerators who practise deception upon the people and bring satanic wonders.
٣
أَهْلُ السُّنَّة		Those the Shaykh mentioned here — they believe in the karāmāt of the awliyāʾ and affirm them according to the measure of what came in the Book and the Sunnah.
شُبْهَةُ النَّافِينَ وَالجَوَابُ عَنْهَا The Deniers’ Objection, and the Answer to It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Their objection is worth stating at its strongest, because it is not stupid: that if it were permissible for the breaking of custom to appear at the hands of the awliyāʾ, then the prophet would be confused with other than him — since the difference between a prophet and another man is the muʿjizah, which is a breaking of custom.

In other words: if miracles are the credential of prophethood, and non-prophets can work them, the credential is worthless. That is a real argument, and al-Fawzān answers it in two moves rather than one.

١There are immense distinctions between the Prophets and others besides the breaking of customs — the revelation, the message, the law brought, the ʿiṣmah in conveying it. So the credential of prophethood was never the wonder alone, and nothing is confused by a wonder occurring elsewhere.
٢And the walī does not claim prophethood; and were he to claim it, he would leave wilāyah altogether and become a lying claimant and no walī. The confusion the objection fears requires a claim that a walī by definition does not make.

And it is of the Sunnah of Allah that He disgraces the liar — as happened to مُسَيْلِمَةَ الكَذَّاب and others.

That is the third guard, and it is the one that answers the objection from history rather than from argument. False claimants to prophethood are not left standing. The man who called himself a prophet in the Prophet’s ﷺ own lifetime is remembered by the title al-Kadhdhāb — the Liar — which is the disgrace itself, still attached to his name fourteen centuries later.

غُلُوُّ الغَالِينَ وَالجَوَابُ عَنْهُ The Excess of the Exaggerators, and the Answer to It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān is concrete about what he means, and the list is not historical: they bring satanic wonders — such as entering fire, and striking themselves with weapons, and handling serpents, and other than that; and what they claim for the occupants of the graves of dispositions over the creation, which they name karāmāt.

Then the answer, in three clauses:

١These are not the awliyāʾ of Allah; rather they are the awliyāʾ of Shayṭān.
٢And what occurs at their hands is either a lie and a deception — a trick, not a wonder at all.
٣or a trial for them and for others, and an istidrāj — a real event, granted in order to draw a man on by degrees until he is taken.

The third clause is the one that disarms the whole claim. Even granting that something genuinely happened, its happening is not a certificate. An extraordinary event can be a favour and it can equally be a rope — and the Qurʾān uses the word الاسْتِدْرَاج precisely for a good given in order to ruin. So the event never settles the question; the man’s adherence to the Sharīʿah does.

The test, stated as a question to put to any reported wonder:

Not «did it happen?» — but «is this man, by the Qurʾān’s own definition, a believer who has taqwā?» If he is, the wonder may be a karāmah from Allah. If he calls upon the dead, circles a tomb, or sets his way against the Sunnah, then whatever occurred is a deception or a snare — and the more impressive it is, the more dangerous.

al-Fawzān closes the question by naming where it is treated in full: and Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah has on this subject a magnificent book, named الفُرْقَانُ بَيْنَ أَوْلِيَاءِ الرَّحْمَنِ وَأَوْلِيَاءِ الشَّيْطَان — The Distinction Between the Allies of the Most Merciful and the Allies of Shayṭān. The title is the answer: the two are not told apart by their wonders, but by whose allies they are.

نَوْعَا الكَرَامَة The Two Classes of Karāmah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now al-Fawzān reads the matn’s own wording as a division. And in his words فِي أَنْوَاعِ العُلُومِ وَالمُكَاشَفَاتِ وَأَنْوَاعِ القُدْرَةِ وَالتَّأْثِيرَات there is an indication that of the karāmah there is what is of the category of knowledge and unveiling… and of it there is what is of the category of power and effect.

The author did not merely list two phrases; he classified. And the examples the sharḥ supplies for each are drawn from the Qurʾān first, then from the Companions.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

العِلْمُ وَالكَشْف
the first class — that the servant hears what others do not hear, or sees what others do not see, awake or in sleep, or knows what others do not know		— the saying of ʿUmar: «يَا سَارِيَةُ الجَبَل» while he was in Madīnah and Sāriyah was in the East
— Abū Bakr’s informing that what was in his wife’s womb was a female
— ʿUmar’s informing of which of his offspring would come forth and be just
— the story of the companion of Mūsā and his knowledge of the state of the boy
القُدْرَةُ وَالتَّأْثِير
the second class — a wonder in acting rather than in knowing		— the one who had knowledge from the Book, and his bringing the throne of Bilqīs to Sulaymān عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام
— the story of the People of the Cave
— the story of Maryam
— the story of Khālid ibn al-Walīd when he drank poison and no harm came to him from it

قَالَ الَّذِي عِنْدَهُ عِلْمٌ مِنَ الْكِتَابِ أَنَا آتِيكَ بِهِ قَبْلَ أَنْ يَرْتَدَّ إِلَيْكَ طَرْفُكَ

"Said the one who had knowledge from the Book: I will bring it to you before your glance returns to you."

Sūrat al-Naml 27:40 · سُورَةُ النَّمْل

And mark how that one reports it when the throne is before him: «هَذَا مِنْ فَضْلِ رَبِّي لِيَبْلُوَنِي أَأَشْكُرُ أَمْ أَكْفُرُ» — this is from the favour of my Lord, to test me: will I be grateful or ungrateful. The man at whose hand the wonder occurred did not read it as a rank; he read it as a trial — which is exactly the opposite of what the exaggerators do with it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And his words وَالمَأْثُورُ عَنْ سَالِفِ الأُمَمِ فِي سُورَةِ الكَهْفِ وَغَيْرِهَا point to the karāmāt that occurred and were mentioned in the Noble Qurʾān and in other sound transmissions. So of what Allah mentioned in the Qurʾān about the bygone nations: Maryam’s conceiving without a husband, and what is mentioned in Sūrat al-Kahf of the story of the People of the Cave, and the story of the companion of Mūsā, and the story of Dhū al-Qarnayn.

And المَأْثُور — that is: what is transmitted by a sound chain — about صَدْرِ هَذِهِ الأُمَّة, that is: its first part, of the Companions and the Tābiʿūn.

قِصَّةُ سَارِيَةَ — al-Fawzān tells it twice, so it is worth having in full.

ʿUmar’s seeing the army of Sāriyah while he was upon the pulpit of Madīnah — and the army of Sāriyah was at نَهَاوَنْد, in the East — and his calling out to him: «يَا سَارِيَةُ الجَبَل!» · So Sāriyah heard him, and benefited by this direction, and was saved from the stratagem of the enemy.

Two things in it are instructive. It is a karāmah of both classes at once — a seeing (knowledge) and a being heard across a continent (effect). And it was for a purpose outside itself: it saved a Muslim army. It was not a display, it was not sought, and ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ built no claim upon it afterwards — which is how the karāmāt of the awliyāʾ characteristically come.

وَهِيَ مَوْجُودَةٌ إِلَى يَوْمِ القِيَامَة And They Continue Until the Day of Standing

الشَّرْح · the explanation

His words وَهِيَ مَوْجُودَةٌ فِيهَا إِلَى يَوْمِ القِيَامَة — that is: the karāmāt do not cease to be present in this Ummah until the Day of Standing — so long as wilāyah is found in them, with its conditions. And Allah knows best.

Do not read past the final clause. The matn’s promise is open — they continue to the end of time — but al-Fawzān attaches the condition the definition already contained: مَا وُجِدَتْ فِيهِمُ الوَلَايَةُ بِشُرُوطِهَا. The karāmāt continue wherever the taqwā continues, and not elsewhere. Which means the section ends exactly where it began, with the two āyāt of Yūnus — and the claim of a wonder has still never been allowed to stand in the place of faith and taqwā.

That the reports are not authentic

What are al-Fawzān’s two answers to that objection?

That the reports are mutawātir, and that the Ummah has agreed on them
That there are immense distinctions between prophets and others besides wonders; and that a walī makes no claim to prophethood — were he to claim it he would leave wilāyah and be a lying claimant
That muʿjizāt are greater in degree than karāmāt
That karāmāt occur only in sleep

What third consideration answers the deniers from history?

That no one has claimed prophethood since
That it is of the Sunnah of Allah that He disgraces the liar — as happened to Musaylimah, remembered by the title al-Kadhdhāb
That the Caliphs executed every false claimant
That wonders ceased after the Companions

What does al-Fawzān say of the wonders of the grave-venerators?

That they are lesser karāmāt
That they are satanic wonders — either a lie and a deception, or a trial and an istidrāj; and these are awliyāʾ of Shayṭān, not of Allah
That they are permissible if the man prays
That they must be judged case by case with no ruling

Why does istidrāj mean that a wonder can never settle the question?

Because no event can be verified
Because a real event may be granted precisely in order to draw a man on by degrees until he is seized — so the man’s adherence to the Sharīʿah decides, not the event
Because only prophets receive genuine wonders
Because the Sharīʿah forbids investigating such reports

What two classes does the matn’s own wording divide the karāmāt into?

The witnessed and the reported
العِلْمُ وَالمُكَاشَفَة — knowledge and unveiling; and القُدْرَةُ وَالتَّأْثِير — power and effect
Those in this world and those in the next
Those of the Companions and those of later generations

How did the one who brought the throne of Bilqīs read what happened at his hand?

As proof of his rank over Sulaymān’s court
As a favour from his Lord that was a TEST — «will I be grateful or ungrateful» — which is the opposite of what the exaggerators do with wonders
As a sign that prophethood had passed to him
As something he had earned by his knowledge

Why is the story of Sāriyah instructive twice over?

Because it is narrated by al-Bukhārī and Muslim
Because it joins both classes at once — a seeing and a being heard across a continent — and it served a purpose outside itself, saving a Muslim army, with no claim built upon it afterwards
Because ʿUmar رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ claimed prophetic knowledge by it
Because it occurred in a dream

What condition does al-Fawzān attach to the karāmāt continuing until the Hour?

That a living chain of shaykhs is preserved
So long as wilāyah is found in them WITH ITS CONDITIONS — so they continue wherever the taqwā continues, and not elsewhere
That the Ummah remains united
That they occur only in the two Ḥarams
True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
التَّصْدِيقُ بِكَرَامَاتِ الأَوْلِيَاءِ مِنْ أُصُولِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ. Affirming the karāmāt of the awliyāʾ is of the foundations of Ahl al-Sunnah.
الوَلِيُّ مَنْ ظَهَرَتْ عَلَى يَدِهِ الخَوَارِقُ وَلَوْ خَالَفَ الشَّرِيعَةَ. A walī is one at whose hand wonders appear, even if he opposes the Sharīʿah.
لَوِ ادَّعَى الوَلِيُّ النُّبُوَّةَ خَرَجَ عَنِ الوَلَايَةِ وَصَارَ مُدَّعِيًا كَذَّابًا. Were a walī to claim prophethood he would leave wilāyah and become a lying claimant.
كُلُّ خَارِقٍ يَجْرِي عَلَى يَدِ رَجُلٍ فَهُوَ كَرَامَةٌ لَهُ. Every wonder occurring at a man’s hand is a karāmah in his favour.
الكَرَامَاتُ بَاقِيَةٌ فِي الأُمَّةِ إِلَى يَوْمِ القِيَامَةِ مَا وُجِدَتْ فِيهِمُ الوَلَايَةُ بِشُرُوطِهَا. The karāmāt remain in the Ummah until the Day of Standing so long as wilāyah with its conditions is found in them.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَكَانُوا يَتَّقُونَ

Four words, and they settle the whole section. Allah named the awliyāʾ and then defined them Himself, in the very next āyah — by faith and taqwā, and by nothing else. So no robe, no lineage, no tomb and no wonder can make a man one, and none of them can keep him one. Memorize the definition and you will never need to adjudicate a reported marvel again: you will ask about the man, not about the marvel.
