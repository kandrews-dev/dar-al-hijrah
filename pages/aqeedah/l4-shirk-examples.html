أمثلة الشرك وتطبيقها

Examples of Shirk — Classification in Practice

Year 2+

After this lesson you will be able to · بعد هذا الدرس ستتمكن من

  • Correctly classify real-life scenarios as major, minor, or hidden shirk — or not shirk at all
  • Explain the reasoning behind each classification using the correct dalīl
  • Recognise nuance — acts that look like shirk but are not, and acts that look innocent but are
  • Understand the importance of not making takfīr (declaring someone a kāfir) hastily
كيف نصنّف؟ How to Classify — The Method

When you encounter an act and ask "is this shirk?" — follow these steps:

1

Is there a dalīl (text) that calls this act "shirk"?

If yes → it is shirk. If no → do not call it shirk based on your own reasoning alone.

2

Does it involve directing a right of Allah to other than Allah?

Worship (ʿibādah), ultimate fear (khawf), complete hope (rajāʾ), ultimate love (maḥabbah) — these belong only to Allah.

3

If it is shirk — is it major, minor, or hidden?

Major → exits Islam. Minor → named shirk in text but does not exit Islam. Hidden → in the heart.

!

Be careful: classifying an act vs. declaring a person

Saying "this act is major shirk" is different from saying "this person is a kāfir." The second requires additional conditions — knowledge, no coercion, no misunderstanding — and is left to scholars.

أمثلة واضحة Clear Examples — Before the Scenarios
Example — المثال Type Why
Bowing before an idol and asking it to heal you Major Directing ʿibādah (bowing/supplication) to other than Allah
Visiting a fortune-teller and believing their information Major Claiming knowledge of the unseen (ʿilm al-ghayb) belongs only to Allah
Reciting Quran louder when the teacher enters the room Minor Riyāʾ — performing worship to be noticed (unless genuinely motivated for correction)
Saying "I swear by the Prophet ﷺ this is true" Minor Prophet ﷺ forbade swearing by other than Allah — though we love the Prophet ﷺ deeply
Praying because you fear Allah might punish you and because your parents are watching Hidden Deed mixed with wanting others' approval alongside Allah's — corrupts sincerity

تدريب على التصنيف Scenario Classification Drill — صنّف كل موقف

Read each scenario carefully. Select the correct category, then read the explanation. Some scenarios are deliberately subtle — think before you click.

Scenario 1

Umm Khālid keeps a blue bead hanging in her car. She believes it protects her from the evil eye and harm while driving. She does not pray to the bead, but she feels genuinely unsafe without it.
Minor Shirk (الأصغر) — wearing a charm or amulet believing it independently protects from harm is a form of minor shirk (tamīmah). The Prophet ﷺ said: «مَنْ تَعَلَّقَ شَيْئًا وُكِّلَ إِلَيْهِ» "Whoever hangs himself on something will be entrusted to it." (Tirmidhī) Harm and benefit belong only to Allah. A bead has no power to protect. Note: if she believed the bead was a deity or had divine power in itself, it would move toward major shirk. The key distinction: she doesn't worship it, but she relies on it — that is the minor category.

Scenario 2

Bilāl is a student who donates to charity every week sincerely. One Friday, his teacher publicly praises him in class for his charity. The following week, Bilāl gives a larger donation than usual — and notices that his motivation is partly to be praised again.
Hidden Shirk (الخفي) — with elements of minor shirk — The sincere donations are rewarded. But the extra donation made partly for praise is tainted by riyāʾ (showing off) — a form of hidden shirk that poisons the specific deed it enters.

The scholars explain: if the deed started sincerely and riyāʾ entered later, the original deed is not cancelled — but the additional part done for others loses its reward. This scenario is a good reminder to constantly check intentions (محاسبة النفس).

Scenario 3

Yūsuf visits a local "healer" who claims to know the future and who places written charms under a pillow to bring good luck. Yūsuf pays the healer and believes in his power. He returns regularly for guidance.
Major Shirk (الأكبر) — visiting fortune-tellers and believing them is major shirk. The Prophet ﷺ said: «مَنْ أَتَى كَاهِنًا فَصَدَّقَهُ بِمَا يَقُولُ فَقَدْ كَفَرَ بِمَا أُنْزِلَ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ» "Whoever goes to a fortune-teller and believes what he says has disbelieved in what was revealed to Muḥammad ﷺ." (Abū Dāwūd, ṣaḥīḥ) Knowledge of the unseen belongs only to Allah. Believing a human can access it is major shirk.

Scenario 4

Maryam is unwell. Her mother takes her to a doctor. The doctor prescribes medicine. Maryam takes the medicine, makes duʿāʾ to Allah for cure, and says "all healing comes from Allah."
Not shirk — this is correct tawakkul (reliance on Allah). Using means (asbāb) while the heart relies on Allah is not shirk — it is how Muslims are meant to live. The Prophet ﷺ himself used medicine, wore armour in battle, and ate food.

Tawhid does not mean abandoning means — it means not placing your ultimate reliance in the means. Maryam takes medicine and her heart is anchored in Allah — this is tawhid in action.

Scenario 5

Ḥamzah is in a difficult situation. He says to his friend: "If it were not for Allah and you, I would have been ruined." He means this as a genuine expression of gratitude to both Allah and his friend.
Minor Shirk (الأصغر) — The Prophet ﷺ corrected exactly this wording. The word "and (وَ)" joins two things at equal level. Placing a person's help alongside Allah's with "and" gives the person a level of causation that belongs only to Allah.

The correct expression: «مَا شَاءَ اللهُ ثُمَّ شَاءَ فُلَانٌ» "As Allah willed, then as so-and-so willed." (thumma — "then" — shows proper sequence and priority.)
Ḥamzah's intention is good — but the wording itself falls under minor shirk. He should be corrected gently.

Scenario 6

Zaynab visits the grave of a beloved scholar. At the grave, she makes duʿāʾ — she faces the qiblah, raises her hands, and says "O Allah, forgive me and have mercy on me." She also says "May Allah have mercy on this scholar and grant him a high rank."
Not shirk — Zaynab is making duʿāʾ to Allah, not to the deceased. Visiting graves is a recommended sunnah. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Visit graves, for they remind you of the hereafter." (Muslim)

The shirk would be if she said: "O scholar, intercede for me!" or "O scholar, cure my illness!" — that is: directing duʿāʾ, hope, or fear to the deceased as if they can act independently. What Zaynab did is fully correct — her duʿāʾ is directed to Allah alone.

Scenario 7 Y3+

Tariq's boss at work is known for firing people who speak against him. Tariq is asked about a matter of Islamic law at a gathering. He knows the correct answer, but stays silent — fearing his boss will hear and fire him, even though stating the truth would not harm anyone except financially.
Hidden Shirk (الخفي) — fear of creation suppressing truth — This is a subtle but serious form of hidden shirk. Fearing people to the point where it prevents fulfilling a religious duty shows that the fear of creation has taken precedence over the fear of Allah.

The Quran commands: «فَلَا تَخْشَوُا النَّاسَ وَاخْشَوْنِ» "Do not fear the people — fear Me." (5:44)
Note: fearing losing a job is natural and not shirk by itself. It becomes hidden shirk when that fear actively prevents a religious obligation and the heart prioritises the creation's displeasure over Allah's pleasure.
الدقة والحذر Nuance and Caution — Things That May Seem Like Shirk But Are Not

These acts are sometimes wrongly called shirk — but are not:

Loving the Prophet ﷺ intensely

Loving the Prophet ﷺ above all creation is commanded. It only becomes problematic if that love leads to directing ʿibādah to him — which is not the case for a Muslim who prays to Allah alone.

Tawassul through righteous people (alive)

Asking a living righteous person to make duʿāʾ for you is not shirk — it is a sunnah practice. The key: duʿāʾ is still made to Allah; the person is asked to ask Allah on your behalf.

Respect for scholars and parents

Standing for a scholar, kissing a parent's hand, obeying parents in lawful matters — all permitted. Shirk requires the acts of worship directed elsewhere, not ordinary respect.

Pride or sadness during worship

Feeling naturally pleased that your prayer was good, or sad about a sin — this is not riyāʾ. Riyāʾ requires performing the deed because of people's eyes on you.

Important principle: Being too quick to call something shirk is itself a mistake. The Prophet ﷺ warned against hastily declaring a Muslim outside of Islam (التكفير — takfīr). Our job as students of knowledge is to understand what shirk is, avoid it ourselves, and teach it correctly — not to label people.

تدريب القواعد Grammar Drill — Prohibitions in Arabic (لا + مضارع مجزوم)

In Arabic, prohibitions are formed with لَا (lā al-nāhiyah — the prohibiting lā) + the verb in the jussive (مجزوم) form. This appears many times in our Quranic evidence. Look at the pattern, then complete the table:

Pattern:

لَا + فعل مضارع مجزوم = نهي

e.g. لَا تُشْرِكْ (lā tushrik) — "Do not associate partners!"

Quranic prohibition Transliteration Translation Root
لَا تُشْرِكْ بِاللَّهِ lā tushrik billāh ش-ر-ك
لَا تَجْعَلُوا لِلَّهِ أَنْدَادًا lā tajʿalū lillāhi andādan ج-ع-ل / ن-د-د
وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِ أَحَدًا wa lā yushrik biʿibādati rabbihī aḥadan ش-ر-ك / ع-ب-د
