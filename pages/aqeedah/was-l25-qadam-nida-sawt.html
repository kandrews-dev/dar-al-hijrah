الرِّجْلُ وَالقَدَمُ · وَالنِّدَاءُ بِصَوْت

The Foot, and the Calling Out with a Voice

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Explain al-Fawzān’s account of why the Fire is filled in this way.
  • Give both refutations of the muʿaṭṭilah’s reading of رِجْلَه and قَدَمَه.
  • Say why بِصَوْتٍ is added after فَيُنَادِي, and what it parallels in the Qurʾān.
  • State what the ḥadīth of the تُرْجُمَان establishes about every believer.
٤ · إِثْبَاتُ الرِّجْلِ وَالقَدَمِ لِلَّهِ سُبْحَانَه Section 4 — The Affirmation of the Foot

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «لَا تَزَالُ جَهَنَّمُ يُلْقَى فِيهَا وَهِيَ تَقُولُ: هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ؟ حَتَّى يَضَعَ رَبُّ العِزَّةِ فِيهَا رِجْلَهُ» وَفِي رِوَايَةٍ: «عَلَيْهَا قَدَمَهُ، فَيَنْزَوِي بَعْضُهَا إِلَى بَعْضٍ، فَتَقُولُ: قَطْ قَطْ» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ.

لَا تَزَالُ جَهَنَّمُ يُلْقَى فِيهَا وَهِيَ تَقُولُ: هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ؟ حَتَّى يَضَعَ رَبُّ العِزَّةِ فِيهَا رِجْلَهُ — فَيَنْزَوِي بَعْضُهَا إِلَى بَعْضٍ، فَتَقُولُ: قَطْ قَطْ

"Hellfire will not cease — people being cast into it while it says: is there any more? — until the Lord of Might places His Foot in it. Then parts of it will draw together, and it will say: enough, enough."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

جَهَنَّم is one of the names of the Fire. On the name itself al-Fawzān reports two views: it has been said it was named so because of the remoteness of its floor; and it has been said because of its darkness, from الجُهُومَة, which is darkness.

يُلْقَى فِيهَا — that is: its people are cast into it. Keep that verb in mind; the refutation below turns on it.

وَهِيَ تَقُولُ: هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ — it asks for more, because of its vastness; and Allah had promised it that He would fill it. The question is the one the Qurʾān records: هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ — «is there any more?» (Qāf 50:30).

يَوْمَ نَقُولُ لِجَهَنَّمَ هَلِ امْتَلَأْتِ وَتَقُولُ هَلْ مِنْ مَزِيدٍ

"On the Day We will say to Hell: are you filled? And it will say: is there any more?"

Sūrat Qāf 50:30 · سُورَة ق

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now al-Fawzān explains why it happens this way, and the reasoning is the finest thing in the section. Three facts stand together:

— Since the Fire was at the utmost of largeness and vastness, and Allah had promised it its filling, and the requirement of His mercy was that He would not punish anyone without a crime — He fulfilled His promise and placed His Foot upon it.

Read that again, because it answers a question most people never think to ask. Why is the Fire not simply filled with people? Because filling a vastness that keeps asking for more would mean condemning beyond the measure of justice — and His mercy will not punish anyone without a crime. So there is a promise to be kept and a mercy that will not be crossed, and the Foot is how both hold at once. The ḥadīth is a text of mercy as much as of attributes.

فَيَنْزَوِي بَعْضُهَا إِلَى بَعْضٍ — parts of it draw in to each other, and its two edges meet, and no surplus remains in it beyond its people. فَتَقُولُ: قَطْ قَطْ — that is: enough for me, it suffices me.

And the shāhid: the affirming of الرِّجْل and القَدَم for Allah تَعَالَى in the manner befitting Him سُبْحَانَهُ — and it is of the attributes of the Essence, like the Face and the Hand. So it belongs with Lesson 13, not with the descent and the laughter.

غَلَطُ المُعَطِّلَةِ فِي هَذَا الحَدِيث The Muʿaṭṭilah’s Error in This Ḥadīth

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the muʿaṭṭilah erred in the explanation of this ḥadīth, and al-Fawzān states their two readings exactly:

قَدَمَهُthey said: a kind of created thing — i.e. a group He sends ahead into it
رِجْلَهُthey said: a crowd of people — as one says رِجْلُ جَرَادٍ, a swarm of locusts

The reply is to say:

الأَوَّل · that the Prophet ﷺ said يَضَعَ — He places — and he did not say يُلْقِي, He casts, as he did say at the beginning of the ḥadīth: يُلْقَى فِيهَا.

الثَّانِي · and also, القَدَم cannot validly be explained as “a people” — neither really nor figuratively.

The first reply is the kind of argument that only comes from reading the whole text. The ḥadīth uses both verbs, a few words apart. People are cast into the Fire — يُلْقَى — and this is placed — يَضَعَ. Had the Foot meant another batch of people, the Prophet ﷺ had the verb for that already in his mouth and would have used it again. The change of verb is deliberate, and it is the muʿaṭṭilah who must explain it away.

And the second reply simply tests their lexicography. They had an argument of a sort for رِجْل, since رِجْلُ جَرَادٍ is a known idiom for a swarm. But the other narration says قَدَمَه — and there is no usage in Arabic, literal or figurative, in which a foot means a crowd. A taʾwīl that works for one narration and collapses on the other has not explained the ḥadīth.

٥ · إِثْبَاتُ النِّدَاءِ وَالصَّوْتِ وَالكَلَامِ لِلَّهِ تَعَالَى Section 5 — Calling Out, the Voice, and Speech

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «يَقُولُ اللهُ تَعَالَى: يَا آدَمُ! فَيَقُولُ: لَبَّيْكَ وَسَعْدَيْكَ. فَيُنَادِي بِصَوْتٍ: إِنَّ اللهَ يَأْمُرُكَ أَنْ تُخْرِجَ مِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِكَ بَعْثًا إِلَى النَّارِ» مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ. وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا سَيُكَلِّمُهُ رَبُّهُ، لَيْسَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهُ تُرْجُمَانٌ».

يَقُولُ اللهُ تَعَالَى: يَا آدَمُ! فَيَقُولُ: لَبَّيْكَ وَسَعْدَيْكَ. فَيُنَادِي بِصَوْتٍ: إِنَّ اللهَ يَأْمُرُكَ أَنْ تُخْرِجَ مِنْ ذُرِّيَّتِكَ بَعْثًا إِلَى النَّارِ

"Allah will say: O Ādam! And he will say: at Your service, and in Your pleasure. Then He will call out with a voice: Allah commands you to bring out from your offspring a contingent for the Fire."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ādam's answer is two words, and both are built the same way. لَبَّيْكَ — the sense is standing firm upon obedience, and it is manṣūb as a maṣdar, and it was put in the dual for emphasis — that is, an answering after an answering. And وَسَعْدَيْكَ — from المُسَاعَدَة, which is compliance: that is, a helping in Your obedience after a helping.

The dual is not a quirk of idiom. Both words are doubled to say again and again, without end — which is how the first man answers when he is called by name.

فَيُنَادِي — with a kasrah on the دال; and the One calling out is Allah تَعَالَى.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the word the section is named for. بِصَوْتٍ — is an emphasis of his saying يُنَادِي, because a calling-out does not occur except with a voice.

al-Fawzān then gives the parallel, and it is exactly the construction from Lesson 18: and this is like His saying تَعَالَى وَكَلَّمَ اللَّهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًا — «and Allah spoke to Mūsā with a direct speaking» (al-Nisāʾ 4:164).

The logic is the same in both places. تَكْلِيمًا after كَلَّمَ forecloses reading the speaking loosely; بِصَوْتٍ after يُنَادِي forecloses reading the calling as a mere conveying of meaning. In each case a word is added that a figurative reading cannot absorb.

بَعْثًا إِلَى النَّارِ — al-baʿth here carries the meaning of the contingent dispatched to it; and the meaning of that is: separate out the people of the Fire.

And the shāhid: the affirming of saying from Allah, and of calling out with a voice that is heard; and that this will occur on the Day of Rising — so in it is that Allah says and calls out whenever He wills and however He wills.

مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا سَيُكَلِّمُهُ رَبُّهُ، لَيْسَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهُ تُرْجُمَانٌ

"There is not one of you but his Lord will speak to him, with no interpreter between him and Him."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ وَمُسْلِمٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ — the address is to the Ṣaḥābah, and it is general to all of the believers. إِلَّا سَيُكَلِّمُهُ رَبُّهُ — that is: without an intermediary. And لَيْسَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهُ تُرْجُمَانٌ — al-turjumān is one who renders into another language.

And the shāhid: the affirming of Allah's سُبْحَانَهُ addressing of His servants; and that He speaks when He wills — so His speech is of His attributes of the Actions — and that He will speak to every believer on the Day of Rising.

Set this beside Lesson 18, where التَّكْلِيم was the honour that earned Mūsā the title الكَلِيم, and the messengers spoken to without an intermediary were counted on the fingers of one hand. Here the same thing is promised to every believer in the room. The attribute that the deniers worked hardest to explain away turns out to be the one each of them is personally promised.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا سَيُكَلِّمُهُ رَبُّهُ، لَيْسَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَهُ تُرْجُمَانٌ

In Lesson 18, being spoken to without an intermediary was the distinction of two or three messengers. Here it is promised to you. Learn it as a creed and let it work on you as a warning: the conversation is coming, and nobody will be translating.
