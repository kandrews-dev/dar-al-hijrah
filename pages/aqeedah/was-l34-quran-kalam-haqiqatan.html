الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ الرَّابِعُ وَالثَّلَاثُونYear 3

القُرْآنُ كَلَامُ اللهِ حَقِيقَةً · حُرُوفُهُ وَمَعَانِيه

The Qurʾān Is Allah’s Speech in Reality — Its Letters and Its Meanings

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Explain the distinction between نَوْعُ الكَلَام and مُفْرَدَاتُه in Allah’s speech.
  • Give both meanings al-Fawzān offers for وَإِلَيْهِ يَعُودُ.
  • State the position of the Jahmiyyah, the Kullābiyyah, the Ashāʿirah and the Muʿtazilah, and what each one costs.
  • Give the author’s own proof that the Qurʾān remains Allah’s speech when a man recites it.
وُجُوبُ الإِيمَانِ بِأَنَّ القُرْآنَ كَلَامُ اللهِ حَقِيقَة The Obligation of Believing That the Qurʾān Is the Speech of Allah in Reality

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَصْلٌ — وَمِنَ الإِيمَانِ بِاللهِ وَكُتُبِهِ: الإِيمَانُ بِأَنَّ القُرْآنَ كَلَامُ اللهِ مُنَزَّلٌ غَيْرُ مَخْلُوقٍ، مِنْهُ بَدَأَ وَإِلَيْهِ يَعُودُ، وَأَنَّ اللهَ تَكَلَّمَ بِهِ حَقِيقَةً، وَأَنَّ هَذَا القُرْآنَ الَّذِي أَنْزَلَهُ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ هُوَ كَلَامُ اللهِ حَقِيقَةً لَا كَلَامُ غَيْرِهِ، وَلَا يَجُوزُ إِطْلَاقُ القَوْلِ بِأَنَّهُ حِكَايَةٌ عَنْ كَلَامِ اللهِ أَوْ عِبَارَةٌ، بَلْ إِذَا قَرَأَهُ النَّاسُ أَوْ كَتَبُوهُ فِي المَصَاحِفِ لَمْ يَخْرُجْ بِذَلِكَ عَنْ أَنْ يَكُونَ كَلَامَ اللهِ تَعَالَى حَقِيقَةً؛ فَإِنَّ الكَلَامَ إِنَّمَا يُضَافُ حَقِيقَةً إِلَى مَنْ قَالَهُ مُبْتَدِئًا، لَا إِلَى مَنْ قَالَهُ مُبَلِّغًا مُؤَدِّيًا. وَهُوَ كَلَامُ اللهِ حُرُوفُهُ وَمَعَانِيهِ، لَيْسَ كَلَامُ اللهِ الحُرُوفَ دُونَ المَعَانِي، وَلَا المَعَانِي دُونَ الحُرُوفِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first shows why this belongs under two of the six pillars at once: of the foundations of faith are belief in Allah and belief in His Books — and belief that the Qurʾān is the speech of Allah enters into both of these foundations. For belief in Allah عَزَّ وَجَلَّ comprises belief in His attributes, and His speech is one of His attributes; and of His speech is the Mighty Qurʾān, which is the greatest of His Books — so it enters into belief in His Books by the foremost kind of entry.

Then the formula for the attribute, which gathers Lesson 19 into one line: Allah تَعَالَى is described as speaking with what He wills, when He wills; He has never ceased and will never cease speaking; and His speech does not run out.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And now a distinction that is the most precise thing in the section, and which resolves an old difficulty in one stroke:

نَوْعُ الكَلَام
the KIND		أَزَلِيٌّ أَبَدِيٌّ — beginningless and endless. He was never not a Speaker.
مُفْرَدَاتُه
the individual instances		do not cease occurring one after another, شَيْئًا فَشَيْئًا, according to His wisdom تَعَالَى.

This is how Ahl al-Sunnah can say both halves of Lesson 19 without strain. Speech is an attribute of the Essence — because the kind never began. And it is an attribute of the Actions — because each instance occurs by His will, at a time. So He addressed Mūsā at an appointed hour, and will address ʿĪsā on the Day of Rising, without His having become a Speaker at some point. The two parties of Lesson 19 each had to deny one of these; the distinction between the kind and its instances lets you keep both.

And from this the conclusion the whole section is built on: and His attributes are not created, so His speech is not created. Not a separate claim, but a consequence — if the speech is an attribute, then whoever says it is created has said that an attribute of Allah is created.

١ · مَقَالَةُ الجَهْمِيَّة First — the Position of the Jahmiyyah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And parties opposed this, and the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ mentioned the statement of some of them here. The first: the Jahmiyyah, who say that Allah does not speak at all; rather He created a speech in something other than Himself and made it express Him. So the ascribing of the speech to Allah with them is figurative, not real — because He created the speech, so He is «a speaker» in the sense of being the creator of speech in another.

And this statement is false, contradicting the transmitted and the rational proofs, and contradicting the statement of the Salaf and the imāms of the Muslims. And then al-Fawzān's refutation, which is two questions and needs no apparatus at all:

For it is not intelligible that anyone be called a speaker except one in whom the speech really subsists.

— So how is it said: «Allah said», while the sayer is someone else?! And how is it said: «the speech of Allah», while it is the speech of someone else?!

That is the whole matter. A created speech placed in a creature is that creature's speech, however it got there — and calling its maker the speaker is like calling a carpenter the author of the book printed on his paper.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And this is what the author's clause مِنْهُ بَدَأَ وَإِلَيْهِ يَعُودُ was written against. al-Fawzān: his intent by this is the refutation of the Jahmiyyah, who say that the Qurʾān began from someone other than Him, and that Allah did not speak it in reality but figuratively, and that it is the speech of another ascribed to Him because He created it.

مِنْهُ بَدَأَ — that the Qurʾān began and came forth from Allah تَعَالَى, and He spoke it; and the مِنْ is for the starting-point (لِابْتِدَاءِ الغَايَة). The preposition is the argument: from Him, not through Him.

وَإِلَيْهِ يَعُودُ — and here al-Fawzān gives two possible meanings, and the first is striking: that the Qurʾān returns to Allah تَعَالَى, because it will be raised up at the end of time, so that nothing of it remains — neither in the breasts nor in the copies — and that is among the signs of the Hour; or the meaning is that it is ascribed to Him.

٢ وَ٣ · الكُلَّابِيَّةُ وَالأَشَاعِرَة Second and Third — the Kullābiyyah and the Ashāʿirah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الكُلَّابِيَّة
عَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ سَعِيدِ بْنِ كُلَّابٍ		the Qurʾān is حِكَايَةٌ — a RECOUNTING of Allah’s speech. For Allah’s speech with them is a meaning subsisting in Himself, necessary to His Essence as life and knowledge are, not connected to His will and volition. That meaning is uncreated; but these utterances, made of letters and sounds, are created, and are a recounting of His speech — not His speech.
الأَشَاعِرَة
أَبُو الحَسَنِ الأَشْعَرِيّ		the Qurʾān is عِبَارَةٌ — an EXPRESSION of Allah’s speech. For His speech with them is a meaning subsisting in Himself, and that meaning is uncreated; but these recited utterances are an expression of that inner meaning, and they are created — and it is not said that they are a recounting of it.

And some of the scholars say that the difference between the Kullābiyyah and the Ashāʿirah is a verbal difference with nothing under it (خِلَافٌ لَفْظِيٌّ لَا طَائِلَ تَحْتَه). For both say: the Qurʾān is of two kinds — utterances and meanings; the utterances are created, and these are the ones in existence; and the meanings are pre-eternal, subsisting in the Self, a single meaning admitting of no division and no multiplicity.

Follow what that last clause costs. If Allah's speech is one single undivided meaning, then the Torah and the Qurʾān are the same thing; the command and the prohibition are the same thing; «do not approach prayer while intoxicated» and «and Allah created you and what you do» are the same thing. And nothing you have ever recited is any of it.

And the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ pointed to the invalidity of both of these statements by saying وَلَا يَجُوزُ إِطْلَاقُ القَوْلِ بِأَنَّهُ حِكَايَةٌ عَنْ كَلَامِ اللهِ — that is, as the Kullābiyyah say — أَوْ عِبَارَةٌ عَنْه, as the Ashāʿirah say.

And بَلْ إِذَا قَرَأَهُ النَّاسُ أَوْ كَتَبُوهُ فِي المَصَاحِفِ لَمْ يَخْرُجْ بِذَلِكَ عَنْ أَنْ يَكُونَ كَلَامَ اللهِ حَقِيقَةً — that is: the Mighty Qurʾān is the speech of Allah, its utterances and its meanings, wherever it is found: whether it is preserved in the breasts, or recited by the tongues, or written in the copies — it does not thereby cease to be the speech of Allah تَعَالَى in reality.

دَلِيلُ المُصَنِّف · المُبْتَدِئُ لَا المُبَلِّغ The Author’s Proof — the Originator, Not the Conveyer

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ mentioned the proof of that, saying: فَإِنَّ الكَلَامَ إِنَّمَا يُضَافُ حَقِيقَةً إِلَى مَنْ قَالَهُ مُبْتَدِئًا، لَا إِلَى مَنْ قَالَهُ مُبَلِّغًا مُؤَدِّيًا — for the conveying transmitter is only called وَاسِطَة — a means, and nothing more.

This is a rule everyone already uses. When a messenger reads out a king's letter, the words are the king's — nobody says the herald wrote them, and nobody says that the letter stopped being the king's when it was read aloud. Speech is ascribed to whoever first said it.

And then the proof from the Qurʾān, which the student has already met once:

وَإِنْ أَحَدٌ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ اسْتَجَارَكَ فَأَجِرْهُ حَتَّى يَسْمَعَ كَلَامَ اللَّهِ

"And if any one of the mushrikūn seeks your protection, then grant him protection until he hears the speech of Allah."

Sūrat al-Tawbah 9:6 · سُورَةُ التَّوْبَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the hearing mentioned in this āyah can only be by means of a conveying transmitter — and what is heard is called «the speech of Allah». So it indicates that speech is ascribed only to the one who said it as the originator.

Notice how exactly this āyah is being used, and that it is a second job for it. In Lesson 19 it proved that the recited Qurʾān is Allah's speech. Here it proves why that stays true through a human reciter — because the mushrik in the āyah hears it from the Prophet's ﷺ mouth or a Companion's, with his ears, in Arabic, and the Qurʾān still calls what reached his ears «the speech of Allah».

Which is precisely what the Kullābiyyah and the Ashāʿirah must deny. On their account, what that man heard was a created recounting or expression, and the speech of Allah never reached him at all. The āyah says it did.

٤ · مَقَالَةُ المُعْتَزِلَة · وَالمَذْهَبُ الحَقّ Fourth — the Muʿtazilah; and the True Position

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the Shaykh mentioned the statement of the Muʿtazilah, who say: the speech of Allah is the letters without the meanings. So they say: what is named by «saying» and «speech», unrestrictedly, is a name for the utterance alone; and the meaning is not a part of what it names, but rather what the name points to.

Then he mentioned the opposing madhhab, saying وَلَا المَعَانِي دُونَ الحُرُوفِ — as is the madhhab of the Kullābiyyah and the Ashāʿirah, as was explained above.

So the author closes the fasl by placing two errors against each other, and the symmetry is worth seeing laid out:

المُعْتَزِلَةthe letters without the meanings
الكُلَّابِيَّةُ وَالأَشَاعِرَةthe meanings without the letters
أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةthe Qurʾān is the speech of Allah, its letters AND its meanings

And المَذْهَبُ الحَقّ — the true madhhab is that the Qurʾān is the speech of Allah, its letters and its meanings, as is the statement of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah, and it is that upon which the proofs from the Book and the Sunnah have stood. وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ العَالَمِينَ.

And there is the pattern of Lesson 30 again, in a fifth place. Two parties each hold one half of a thing that was never two things. The Muʿtazilah kept the words you can hear and emptied them of a Speaker's meaning; the Ashāʿirah kept an inner meaning and cut it off from every word you can hear. And Ahl al-Sunnah, holding both, are the only ones for whom the Qurʾān in your hands is what the Qurʾān says it is.

كَلَامُ اللهِ غَيْرُ مَخْلُوقٍ لِأَنَّ صِفَاتِهِ غَيْرُ مَخْلُوقَةٍ. Allah’s speech is uncreated because His attributes are uncreated.
إِذَا كَتَبَ النَّاسُ القُرْآنَ فِي المَصَاحِفِ خَرَجَ عَنْ كَوْنِهِ كَلَامَ اللهِ. When people write the Qurʾān in copies it ceases to be the speech of Allah.
الكَلَامُ يُضَافُ حَقِيقَةً إِلَى المُبَلِّغِ المُؤَدِّي. Speech is ascribed in reality to the conveying transmitter.
المَذْهَبُ الحَقُّ أَنَّ القُرْآنَ كَلَامُ اللهِ حُرُوفُهُ وَمَعَانِيهِ. The true position is that the Qurʾān is Allah’s speech, its letters and its meanings.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَهُوَ كَلَامُ اللهِ حُرُوفُهُ وَمَعَانِيهِ، لَيْسَ كَلَامُ اللهِ الحُرُوفَ دُونَ المَعَانِي، وَلَا المَعَانِي دُونَ الحُرُوفِ

The author states the creed and both errors in one sentence, which is why it is worth having word for word. And notice what it secures: that the Book in your hands, in the letters you can see, is the speech of Allah — not a report of it.
