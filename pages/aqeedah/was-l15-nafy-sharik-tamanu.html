تَمَامُ نَفْيِ الشَّرِيكِ وَبُرْهَانُ التَّمَانُع

Completing the Negation of a Partner, and the Proof of al-Tamānuʿ

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the four attributes al-Fawzān draws out of al-Furqān 25:2 and which party each answers.
  • Lay out the proof of التَّمَانُع from al-Muʾminūn 23:91 in your own words.
  • Explain what ضَرْبُ الأَمْثَالِ لِلَّهِ means and why the mushrikūn fell into it.
  • Give the shāhid al-Fawzān states for this whole group of āyāt.
تَمَامُ القَوْلِ فِي نَفْيِ الشَّرِيك Completing the Negation of a Partner

Lesson 14 closed on al-Isrāʾ 17:111 — no son, no partner in dominion, no ally taken out of weakness. The author does not leave it at one āyah. He brings four more, and al-Fawzān treats them as one connected argument: first the declaration that creation itself declares Him free of a partner, then the attributes that rule a partner out, then a rational proof that two gods is incoherent, and finally the prohibition of the very move that produced the error.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿يُسَبِّحُ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي نَزَّلَ الْفُرْقَانَ عَلَى عَبْدِهِ لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَالَمِينَ نَذِيرًا ۝ الَّذِي لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿مَا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ مِنْ وَلَدٍ وَمَا كَانَ مَعَهُ مِنْ إِلَهٍ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿فَلَا تَضْرِبُوا لِلَّهِ الْأَمْثَالَ﴾، وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿قُلْ إِنَّمَا حَرَّمَ رَبِّيَ الْفَوَاحِشَ﴾ الآيَة.

١ · تَسْبِيحُ الخَلْقِ كُلِّه First — All of Creation Declares Him Free

يُسَبِّحُ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ

"Whatever is in the heavens and whatever is in the earth declares Allah free of all imperfection. His is the dominion and His is the praise, and He is Able over everything."

Sūrat al-Taghābun 64:1 · سورة التغابن

الشَّرْح · the explanation

يُسَبِّحُ لِلَّهِ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ — al-Fawzān: all of His creation, in His heavens and His earth, declares Him free of every defect and fault. The tasbīḥ is not a human opinion about Allah that He then confirms; it is the standing testimony of everything He made.

لَهُ الْمُلْكُ وَلَهُ الْحَمْدُ — the two are His exclusively; nothing of either belongs to anyone else. And then the qualification that answers the obvious question — but people own things: whatever ownership His servants have, it is from His giving. A creature's dominion is loaned, not held.

وَهُوَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ — nothing renders Him unable.

٢ · أَرْبَعُ صِفَاتٍ تَنْفِي الشَّرِيك Second — Four Attributes That Rule a Partner Out

تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي نَزَّلَ الْفُرْقَانَ عَلَى عَبْدِهِ لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَالَمِينَ نَذِيرًا الَّذِي لَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ وَلَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًا وَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِ وَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا

"Blessed is He who sent down the Criterion upon His servant, that he might be a warner to the worlds — He to whom belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth, who has not taken a son, and who has no partner in dominion, and who created everything and measured it out with exact measure."

Sūrat al-Furqān 25:1–2 · سورة الفرقان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Before the four attributes, three notes on the opening. تَبَارَكَ is a past-tense verb from البَرَكَة — growth and increase, settled, firm, lasting; and al-Fawzān states two restrictions on it: this word is not used of anyone but Allah, and it is not used except in the past-tense form.

الفُرْقَان is the Qurʾān; it is called the Criterion because it separates truth from falsehood. And عَلَى عَبْدِهِ means Muḥammad ﷺ — and this is a description of praise and commendation, because He ascribed him to Himself by an ascription of honour and ennobling, in the very context of sending the Qurʾān down upon him. Being named His servant is the station, not a demotion from one.

لِيَكُونَ لِلْعَالَمِينَ نَذِيرًا — a warner to mankind and the jinn, and this is from his particular distinctions ﷺ: the general messengership. نَذِيرًا is from الإِنْذَار, which is informing of the causes of fear.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the four attributes al-Fawzān numbers in the second āyah:

الأُولَىلَهُ مُلْكُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِHis, and no one else’s — so He alone disposes of them
الثَّانِيَةوَلَمْ يَتَّخِذْ وَلَدًاagainst what the Christians and Jews allege — because of the perfection of His freedom from need, and every creature’s need of Him
الثَّالِثَةوَلَمْ يَكُنْ لَهُ شَرِيكٌ فِي الْمُلْكِa refutation of the parties of the mushrikūn — the idolaters, the dualists and others
الرَّابِعَةوَخَلَقَ كُلَّ شَيْءٍof the created things — and the deeds of the servants enter into this: they are Allah’s creating and the servant’s doing

That last line is small and load-bearing: it is the Sunnī position on the servant's acts, dropped in here in passing and taken up in full in the section on al-qadar. The act is created by Allah and done by the servant — not one to the exclusion of the other.

فَقَدَّرَهُ تَقْدِيرًا — He measured out everything He created: the appointed terms, the provisions, the happiness and the wretchedness; and He prepared each thing for what it is suited to.

al-Fawzān closes the āyah with Ibn Kathīr: He declared Himself free of a son and of a partner; then He informed that He created everything and measured it out with exact measure — that is, everything besides Him is created and governed, and He is the Creator of everything, its Lord, its Owner and its God; and everything is beneath His overpowering, His governing, His subjecting and His measuring.

٣ · بُرْهَانُ التَّمَانُع Third — The Proof of Mutual Obstruction

مَا اتَّخَذَ اللَّهُ مِنْ وَلَدٍ وَمَا كَانَ مَعَهُ مِنْ إِلَهٍ إِذًا لَذَهَبَ كُلُّ إِلَهٍ بِمَا خَلَقَ وَلَعَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَى بَعْضٍ سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ

"Allah has not taken any son, nor was there ever any god alongside Him. Had there been, each god would have gone off with what he created, and some of them would have risen over others. Glory be to Allah above what they describe."

Sūrat al-Muʾminūn 23:91 · سورة المؤمنون

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first notes the grammar: مِنْ in both places is to emphasise the negation — not merely "He has no son," but the negation driven home. Then he identifies what the rest of the āyah is doing: إِذًا لَذَهَبَ كُلُّ إِلَهٍ بِمَا خَلَقَ is an evidentiary argument for what preceded it at the start of the āyah — the denial of a son and a partner in divinity. The Qurʾān states the claim, then reasons for it.

The argument runs in two steps, and it is worth following exactly:

1 · الانْقِسَام. Were a plurality of gods supposed, each one of them would go off separately from the other with what he created — and at that point the universe would not hold together, because of the existence of division. And the observed reality is that the universe is ordered with the most complete order; no plurality and no division has occurred in it.

2 · المُغَالَبَة. And were there another god with Him, each of them would seek to overpower the other and to oppose him, so some of them would rise over others — as is the state of the kings of this world. And then that defeated weak one does not deserve to be a god at all.

Note what the second step does. It does not merely say a second god is unlikely; it says the concept collapses. Two gods must contend; contention produces a winner and a loser; and a loser is not a god. So the supposition destroys itself.

And therefore: when the invalidity of a co-sharer is established, it is necessarily the case that the God is one — He is Allah alone. Which is why the āyah ends سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ عَمَّا يَصِفُونَ — free of the partner and the son.

٤ · النَّهْيُ عَنْ ضَرْبِ الأَمْثَالِ لِلَّه Fourth — The Prohibition of Striking Likenesses

فَلَا تَضْرِبُوا لِلَّهِ الْأَمْثَالَ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"So do not strike likenesses for Allah. Indeed Allah knows, and you do not know."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:74 · سورة النحل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

ضَرْبُ المَثَلِ is comparing one state to another state — and here al-Fawzān explains precisely how the mushrikūn reasoned themselves into their shirk, which is the most useful paragraph in the section:

The mushrikūn used to say: Allah is too exalted for one of us to worship Him directly, so there must be the taking of an intermediary between us and Him. So they would seek nearness to Him through the idols and other things — likening Him to the kings of this world. And Allah forbade that, because He has no like, so He is not likened to His creation nor compared with them.

That is the whole mechanism: a courtly analogy. You cannot approach a king without a chamberlain, so — they reasoned — you cannot approach the King of kings without one either. The reasoning is not stupid; it is simply an analogy applied where no likeness exists. Hence: إِنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ — He informs that He has no like. And وَأَنْتُمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَ: this act of yours issued from a corrupt supposition and a false notion; and you also do not know what evil outcome lies in the worship of the idols.

This is the same disease met in al-Qawāʿid al-Arbaʿ and in Lesson 4 of this track, now traced to its root. Shirk is built on ignorance and imagination — not on a proof that came out wrong, but on no proof at all.

قُلْ إِنَّمَا حَرَّمَ رَبِّيَ الْفَوَاحِشَ مَا ظَهَرَ مِنْهَا وَمَا بَطَنَ وَالْإِثْمَ وَالْبَغْيَ بِغَيْرِ الْحَقِّ وَأَنْ تُشْرِكُوا بِاللَّهِ مَا لَمْ يُنَزِّلْ بِهِ سُلْطَانًا وَأَنْ تَقُولُوا عَلَى اللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ

"Say: my Lord has only forbidden the gross indecencies — what is open of them and what is hidden — and sin, and aggression without right, and that you associate with Allah that for which He has sent down no authority, and that you say about Allah what you do not know."

Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:33 · سورة الأعراف

الشَّرْح · the explanation

قُلْ — the address is to the Prophet ﷺ, and in that is a proof that the Qurʾān is the speech of Allah, and that the Prophet ﷺ is a conveyer from Allah. A single imperative carries both: the words are not his, and his office is to deliver them.

مَا is a particle of restriction — only these. الفَوَاحِش is the plural of فَاحِشَة: whatever has reached the furthest point of ugliness among the acts of disobedience; مَا ظَهَرَ مِنْهَا وَمَا بَطَنَ — what is made public of them and what is kept secret. الإِثْم is every act of disobedience that occasions sin, and it has been said it is wine specifically. البَغْيُ بِغَيْرِ الحَقِّ is wrongdoing that crosses the limit, and transgression against people.

And then وَأَنْ تُشْرِكُوا بِاللَّهِ — that you make for Him a partner in worship — and this is the place of the shāhid from the āyah. سُلْطَانًا: a proof and an evidence. Shirk is forbidden and unsupported: no authority was ever sent down for it.

The last clause, وَأَنْ تَقُولُوا عَلَى اللَّهِ مَا لَا تَعْلَمُونَ, covers the fabricating and lying involved in claiming that He has a son, and the like of that of which you have no knowledge; and such as what they used to attribute to Him of permissions and prohibitions which He never authorised. Speaking about Allah without knowledge is listed alongside the gross sins, not beneath them.

الشَّاهِدُ مِنْ هَذِهِ الآيَات The Point of Evidence in These Āyāt

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān gathers the whole group into one statement, and it is the summary to memorise:

In them is the negation of a partner to Allah, and the affirming of His being alone in perfection; and the negation of a son and of a like from Him; and that all His creation declares Him free and holy of that. As there is in them also the establishing of the proof for the invalidity of shirk, and that it is built on ignorance and imagination, and that He has no like and no resemblance. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

Four moves, in order: creation testifies · the attributes exclude a partner · reason shows two gods is incoherent · and the analogy that produced the error is forbidden by name. The negation is never left as a bare denial — each time it is attached to a perfection that makes the denial necessary.

لَفْظُ «تَبَارَكَ» لَا يُسْتَعْمَلُ إِلَّا بِلَفْظِ المَاضِي. The word tabāraka is not used except in the past-tense form.
سُمِّيَ القُرْآنُ فُرْقَانًا لِأَنَّهُ يَفْرِقُ بَيْنَ الحَقِّ وَالبَاطِلِ. The Qurʾān is called al-Furqān because it separates truth from falsehood.
وَصْفُ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ بِأَنَّهُ «عَبْدُهُ» وَصْفُ نَقْصٍ. Describing the Prophet ﷺ as "His servant" is a description of deficiency.
الشِّرْكُ مَبْنِيٌّ عَلَى جَهْلٍ وَخَيَالٍ. Shirk is built on ignorance and imagination.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

إِذًا لَذَهَبَ كُلُّ إِلَهٍ بِمَا خَلَقَ وَلَعَلَا بَعْضُهُمْ عَلَى بَعْضٍ

The whole proof of al-tamānuʿ is in one line. Two gods means division, and division means contention, and contention means a loser — and a loser is not a god. Look at the order of the universe and ask whether it looks like the work of rivals.
