الحَوْضُ وَالصِّرَاطُ وَالقَنْطَرَة

The Ḥawḍ, the Ṣirāṭ, and the Qanṭarah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٣٧–١٤٠
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 137–140.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say how many Companions narrated the aḥādīth of the الحَوْض, and on whose authority.
  • Define الصِّرَاط linguistically and in the Sharīʿah, and say where it is set.
  • Explain al-Fawzān’s link between the two ṣirāṭs — the moral and the physical.
  • Say what happens at the القَنْطَرَة and why it is needed before the Garden.
٦ · حَوْضُ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ Sixth — The Ḥawḍ of the Prophet ﷺ

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَفِي عَرَصَاتِ القِيَامَةِ الحَوْضُ المَوْرُودُ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ: مَاؤُهُ أَشَدُّ بَيَاضًا مِنَ اللَّبَنِ، وَأَحْلَى مِنَ العَسَلِ، آنِيَتُهُ كَنُجُومِ السَّمَاءِ، طُولُهُ شَهْرٌ وَعَرْضُهُ شَهْرٌ، مَنْ شَرِبَ مِنْهُ شَرْبَةً لَا يَظْمَأُ بَعْدَهَا أَبَدًا.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first establishes how firmly this is transmitted, and quotes الإِمَامُ ابْنُ القَيِّم for it: and the aḥādīth of the Ḥawḍ were narrated by forty Companions, and many of them — or most of them — are in the authentic collections.

Forty Companions. That is worth pausing on in a chapter where the Muʿtazilah will shortly deny the thing: this is not a doctrine resting on one chain.

الحَوْض — in the language is a gathering-place of water. And Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah have reached consensus on affirming the Ḥawḍ; the Muʿtazilah opposed that and did not affirm it, and reinterpreted the texts that came concerning it and turned them away from their apparent sense.

And the location, which the author gives and which ties back to Lesson 35: فِي عَرَصَاتِ القِيَامَةِ — the standing-places of the Reckoning, the wide places in which there is no building. The Ḥawḍ is reached before the Garden, in the heat of the waiting.

حَوْضِي مَسِيرَةُ شَهْرٍ، مَاؤُهُ أَبْيَضُ مِنَ اللَّبَنِ، وَرِيحُهُ أَطْيَبُ مِنَ المِسْكِ، وَكِيزَانُهُ كَنُجُومِ السَّمَاءِ، مَنْ شَرِبَ مِنْهُ لَا يَظْمَأُ أَبَدًا

"My Ḥawḍ is a month's journey. Its water is whiter than milk, its scent sweeter than musk, and its drinking-vessels are like the stars of the sky. Whoever drinks from it will never thirst again."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim, from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿAmr رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا

Set the description against the scene Lesson 37 described — the sun a mile overhead, the sweat rising to the mouth like a bridle — and the mercy of the detail becomes plain. The one promise made about the water is that the thirst does not come back. And the vessels are as many as the stars: nobody is waiting for a cup.

٧ · الصِّرَاطُ المَنْصُوبُ عَلَى مَتْنِ جَهَنَّم Seventh — The Ṣirāṭ, Set Over the Back of Hellfire

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَالصِّرَاطُ مَنْصُوبٌ عَلَى مَتْنِ جَهَنَّمَ، وَهُوَ الجِسْرُ الَّذِي بَيْنَ الجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ. يَمُرُّ النَّاسُ عَلَيْهِ عَلَى قَدْرِ أَعْمَالِهِمْ: فَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمُرُّ كَلَمْحِ البَصَرِ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمُرُّ كَالبَرْقِ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمُرُّ كَالرِّيحِ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمُرُّ كَالفَرَسِ الجَوَادِ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمُرُّ كَرِكَابِ الإِبِلِ، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَعْدُو عَدْوًا، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَمْشِي مَشْيًا، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يَزْحَفُ زَحْفًا، وَمِنْهُمْ مَنْ يُخْطَفُ وَيُلْقَى فِي جَهَنَّمَ؛ فَإِنَّ الجِسْرَ عَلَيْهِ كَلَالِيبُ تَخْطَفُ النَّاسَ بِأَعْمَالِهِمْ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الصِّرَاط — in the language is the clear road. And in the Sharīʿah it is what the Shaykh explained: the bridge which is between the Garden and the Fire. And its place: عَلَى مَتْنِ جَهَنَّمَ — that is, upon the back of the Fire.

And the timing, which the author does not state but al-Fawzān supplies: the time of passing over it is after the people's departure from the standing, the gathering and the Reckoning. For upon the Ṣirāṭ the believers are saved from the Fire into the Garden, and the people of the Fire fall from it into it, as is established in the aḥādīth.

يَمُرُّ النَّاسُ عَلَيْهِ عَلَى قَدْرِ أَعْمَالِهِمْ — that is: they are, in the speed of the passage and its slowness, according to their faith and the righteous deeds they sent ahead in this world.

And then al-Fawzān states the principle that organises the whole list, which is the finest thing in the section:

— So according to a person’s uprightness upon the religion of Islam, and his firmness upon it, will be his firmness and his passage upon the Ṣirāṭ. Whoever stood firm upon the MORAL ṣirāṭ — and it is Islam — stands firm upon the PHYSICAL ṣirāṭ set over the back of Hellfire; and whoever slipped from the moral ṣirāṭ slips from the physical one.

Two ṣirāṭs, and the second is the shape of the first. That is why the list of nine speeds is not arbitrary and not a curiosity: it is the measure of how steadily each man walked the straight path when nobody was making him. And it gives the ṣirāṭ mustaqīm of al-Fātiḥah — asked for seventeen times a day — a second and very literal meaning.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The vocabulary of the matn, as al-Fawzān glosses it: يَعْدُو عَدْوًا — that is: he runs at a run. يَزْحَفُ زَحْفًا — that is: he moves along on his backside instead of his legs.

كَلَالِيب — the plural of كَلُّوب — and they are an iron hook with a bent head. تَخْطَفُ — from الخَطْف, which is the taking of a thing swiftly.

And the last phrase is where the two ṣirāṭs meet again, and it is exact: بِأَعْمَالِهِمْ — that is: by reason of their evil deeds. So the hooks' snatching of them on the ṣirāṭ of Hellfire will be according to the snatching of them away from the straight path, in this world, by the doubts and the desires.

الشُّبُهَات and الشَّهَوَات — doubts and desires. Those are the two things that pull a man off the road here, and the hooks are their image there. What snatched at you then, snatches at you then.

مُخَالَفَةُ المُعْتَزِلَةِ فِي الصِّرَاط The Muʿtazilah’s Opposition over the Ṣirāṭ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah believe in the Ṣirāṭ set over the back of Hellfire, and the people's passing over it, according to what the authentic aḥādīth from the Prophet ﷺ came with. And القَاضِي عَبْدُ الجَبَّارِ المُعْتَزِلِيّ opposed that, and many of his followers, and they said: what is meant by the ṣirāṭ mentioned is the road of the Garden, pointed to in His saying سَيَهْدِيهِمْ وَيُصْلِحُ بَالَهُمْ — and the road of the Fire, pointed to in His saying فَاهْدُوهُمْ إِلَى صِرَاطِ الْجَحِيمِ.

سَيَهْدِيهِمْ وَيُصْلِحُ بَالَهُمْ

"He will guide them and set right their state."

Sūrat Muḥammad 47:5 · سُورَةُ مُحَمَّد

فَاهْدُوهُمْ إِلَى صِرَاطِ الْجَحِيمِ

"Then lead them to the path of the Blaze."

Sūrat al-Ṣāffāt 37:23 · سُورَةُ الصَّافَّات

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And this is a false statement, and a rejection of the authentic texts without any evidence. And what is obligatory is to carry the texts upon their apparent meaning.

Notice the form of the error, because it is a new variety. The muʿaṭṭilah of Parts 1 and 2 reinterpreted a word; here the move is to replace an event with a metaphor by citing two āyāt that are about something else. Neither āyah denies a bridge — they speak of guidance and of being driven — and so neither is evidence against the aḥādīth. Producing a text that is merely compatible with your view is not producing a text against the other.

٨ · القَنْطَرَةُ بَيْنَ الجَنَّةِ وَالنَّار Eighth — The Qanṭarah between the Garden and the Fire

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَمَنْ مَرَّ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ؛ فَإِذَا عَبَرُوا عَلَيْهِ وَقَفُوا عَلَى قَنْطَرَةٍ بَيْنَ الجَنَّةِ وَالنَّارِ، فَيُقْتَصُّ لِبَعْضِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ؛ فَإِذَا هُذِّبُوا وَنُقُّوا أُذِنَ لَهُمْ فِي دُخُولِ الجَنَّةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

فَمَنْ مَرَّ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ دَخَلَ الجَنَّةَ — and the one who passes over the Ṣirāṭ has succeeded, as Allah said:

فَمَنْ زُحْزِحَ عَنِ النَّارِ وَأُدْخِلَ الْجَنَّةَ فَقَدْ فَازَ

"So whoever is drawn away from the Fire and admitted to the Garden has indeed succeeded."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:185 · سُورَةُ آلُ عِمْرَان

فَرِيقٌ فِي الْجَنَّةِ وَفَرِيقٌ فِي السَّعِيرِ

"A party in the Garden, and a party in the Blaze."

Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:7 · سُورَةُ الشُّورَى

الشَّرْح · the explanation

فَإِذَا عَبَرُوا عَلَيْهِ — that is: they crossed the Ṣirāṭ and were saved from falling into the Fire — وَقَفُوا عَلَى قَنْطَرَةٍ: and it is the bridge, and what is raised up of a building.

And on what exactly it is, al-Fawzān reports two views without choosing: and this qanṭarah, it has been said, is the end of the Ṣirāṭ on the side nearest the Garden; and it has been said it is another ṣirāṭ particular to the believers.

فَيُقْتَصُّ لِبَعْضِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ — that is: the retaliation is carried out among them in the matters of wrongdoing: the wronged one is given his right from the one who wronged him.

فَإِذَا هُذِّبُوا وَنُقُّوا — that is: they were freed from the liabilities and the claims — أُذِنَ لَهُمْ فِي دُخُولِ الجَنَّةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Now the reason the Qanṭarah is needed at all, which al-Fawzān states and which is the point of the section: so that they enter the Garden while they are in the most complete state, having been cleared of the wrongs.

These are people who have already crossed. The Fire is behind them and the Garden is in front, and they are halted — not to be judged again, but to settle with each other. Everything owed between them is paid before anyone goes in. Nothing unresolved is carried through the gate.

And what was in the hearts of some of them against others, of rancour, has gone — as Allah said:

وَنَزَعْنَا مَا فِي صُدُورِهِمْ مِنْ غِلٍّ إِخْوَانًا عَلَى سُرُرٍ مُتَقَابِلِينَ

"And We shall remove whatever was in their breasts of rancour — brothers, upon couches, facing one another."

Sūrat al-Ḥijr 15:47 · سُورَةُ الحِجْر

Read the āyah as the completion of the Qanṭarah and it stops being a decorative promise. The retaliation settles what was owed; the removing of the rancour settles what was felt. And the word the āyah then uses for them is إِخْوَانًا — brothers, sitting facing one another: the posture of people with nothing left between them. Paid in full, and then able to look each other in the eye.

رَوَى أَحَادِيثَ الحَوْضِ أَرْبَعُونَ صَحَابِيًّا. Forty Companions narrated the aḥādīth of the Ḥawḍ.
الصِّرَاطُ مَنْصُوبٌ عَلَى مَتْنِ جَهَنَّمَ. The Ṣirāṭ is set over the back of Hellfire.
المُرُورُ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ سَوَاءٌ لِجَمِيعِ النَّاسِ. The passage over the Ṣirāṭ is the same for all people.
يَدْخُلُ المُؤْمِنُونَ الجَنَّةَ قَبْلَ أَنْ يُقْتَصَّ لِبَعْضِهِمْ مِنْ بَعْضٍ. The believers enter the Garden before the retaliation among them is settled.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

يَمُرُّ النَّاسُ عَلَيْهِ عَلَى قَدْرِ أَعْمَالِهِمْ

Learn it with al-Fawzān's principle, because it turns the whole list of nine speeds into a single question about today: whoever stood firm on the moral ṣirāṭ stands firm on the physical one. The صِرَاط مُسْتَقِيم you ask for seventeen times a day is the same road, met twice.
