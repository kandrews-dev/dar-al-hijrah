النَّاقِضُ السَّابِع · حَقِيقَةُ السِّحْر

The Seventh Nullifier, Part 1 — What Magic Is

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define السِّحْر from the root, and give the two other uses of that root Ibn Bāz brings as proof.
  • Show from the wording of al-Baqarah 2:102 that teaching and learning it are disbelief.
  • Distinguish the kawnī from the sharʿī permission in «إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللهِ», and say which one the verse means.
  • State Ibn Bāz’s corollary — «نُعَالِجَ قَدَرَ اللهِ بِقَدَرِه» — and what it licenses.
  • Explain why siḥr is shirk and not merely a grave sin, and name the three acts of worship by which the magician reaches it.
  • Name the two kinds of siḥr, say which is the more common, and give an example of each.
  • State the ruling on one who says siḥr is a superstition with no reality, and al-Qurṭubī’s distinction within it.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ١٥٩–١٦٥
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 159–165, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

السَّابِع: السِّحْر، وَمِنْهُ الصَّرْفُ وَالعَطْف؛ فَمَنْ فَعَلَهُ أَوْ رَضِيَ بِهِ كَفَر؛ وَالدَّلِيلُ قَوْلُهُ تَعَالَى: ﴿وَمَا يُعَلِّمَانِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ حَتَّى يَقُولَا إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ فِتْنَةٌ فَلَا تَكْفُرْ﴾.

Note the matn’s two additions: it names الصَّرْف and العَطْف as kinds of it, and it makes the clause fall on «فَعَلَهُ أَوْ رَضِيَ بِهِ» — the one who does it, or is pleased with it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the longest clause in the book — thirty-five folios, printed ١٥٩–١٩٣. It takes three lessons, and they divide where the material divides:

Part 1 (here) — what siḥr is: the word, the proof that it is disbelief, the qadar that governs it, and why it is shirk rather than merely a grave sin.
Part 2 — the ruling on the magician, the athar of ʿUmar, and the grading of the ḥadīth everybody quotes for it.
Part 3 — the treatment, النُّشْرَة, whether the Prophet ﷺ was bewitched, and Hārūt and Mārūt.

مَا هُوَ السِّحْر The Word, and What It Tells You

He begins where the book’s habit is to begin — with the word — and the derivation is not an aside. It is the first piece of evidence about the thing.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«السِّحْر — with a kasrah on the sīn — is: what the magicians practise of knots, and of potions, and of blowing into the knots, and other than that of what the masters of this craft practise. And it was named siḥr because they practise it by hidden means — and for this it was called siḥr. And siḥr is: what bewitches people and alters their perceptions, by whatever kind — but in most cases they are hidden, and for this it was called siḥr.»

And then the two proofs from the language itself: «and the last part of the night is called سَحَر, because it occurs at the end of the night when people are asleep; and the lung is called سَحْر, because it is inside the belly, within the cavity — hidden.»

Three words off one root, and all three mean concealed. So the hiddenness is not incidental to magic; it is what the Arabs named it for. Which also tells you why the subject attracts so much nonsense, in both directions — the thing is by definition not on display.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He then gives the working description: «so siḥr is knots and incantations which the magicians perform, and they blow into their knots; and things, some of them potions which they gather; and things they receive from the jinn and the devils, until he directs it into whomever they wish.»

Hold the last clause. It is the one that makes this a nullifier and not a chapter of fiqh: the operative part is received from the devils, and nothing is received from the devils for nothing.

كُفْرٌ صَرِيحٌ بِنَصِّ القُرْآن Disbelief by the Text — Including Teaching It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And it is plain disbelief by the text of the Qurʾān» — and he proves it twice over from one passage. First from what the verse says of the two who taught it:

«…where He said: «وَيُعَلِّمُونَ النَّاسَ السِّحْرَ» — so He informed of their disbelief by their teaching people magic; so He made teaching magic one of the deeds of disbelief.»

وَمَا يُعَلِّمَانِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ حَتَّىٰ يَقُولَا إِنَّمَا نَحْنُ فِتْنَةٌ فَلَا تَكْفُرْ

And they did not teach anyone until they had said, “We are only a trial, so do not disbelieve.”

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:102

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read the grammar of the daleel, because that is the argument. The two do not teach a man until they warn him — and what they warn him of is فَلَا تَكْفُرْ, “do not disbelieve.” The warning names what the learning amounts to. Ibn Bāz: «so that indicates that learning it is disbelief.»

So the one verse the matn cites covers both ends of the transaction — the teacher by «وَيُعَلِّمُونَ النَّاسَ السِّحْر», and the student by «فَلَا تَكْفُر».

He then walks the rest of the passage, and each clause adds something. «وَيَتَعَلَّمُونَ مَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنْفَعُهُمْ» — they learn what harms them and does not benefit them: «so the harm is immense.» «وَلَقَدْ عَلِمُوا لَمَنِ اشْتَرَاهُ مَا لَهُ فِي الآخِرَةِ مِنْ خَلَاقٍ» — and they certainly knew that whoever purchased it would have no share in the Hereafter: «meaning — no portion and no share.»

وَلَوْ أَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا وَاتَّقَوْا لَمَثُوبَةٌ مِّنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ خَيْرٌ لَّوْ كَانُوا يَعْلَمُونَ

And if they had believed and been mindful of Allah, a reward from Allah would have been better — if only they knew.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:103

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And that verse is the one he builds the structural point on: «so it indicates that it is the opposite of faith and the opposite of taqwā.»

Notice the form of the inference. The verse sets «if they had believed and been mindful» against what they were in fact doing. A thing set in opposition to īmān in the Qurʾān’s own construction is not a lesser sin inside īmān. That is a cleaner argument than any assertion, and it is the reason this clause sits among the ten.

إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللهِ The Qadar Frame — Which Permission?

This is the part of the clause that is actually ʿaqīdah, and the sharḥ gives it more room than anything else on these folios. The verse says the magicians harm no one «except by Allah’s permission» — and the whole question is which permission that is.

وَمَا هُم بِضَارِّينَ بِهِ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِ اللَّهِ

And they could not harm anyone by it except by the permission of Allah.

سُورَةُ البَقَرَة · 2:102

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«Meaning: by His kawnī, decreed permission — not by His legislative permission. For the Sharīʿah forbids them that and makes it unlawful upon them. But by the decreed permission, by which Allah’s knowledge and His prior decree have passed — that magic occurs from so-and-so, and occurs from such a woman, and befalls so-and-so and such a woman; just as His decree passed that so-and-so is afflicted with a killing, or afflicted with such an illness, and dies in such a land, and is provided such, and becomes rich or becomes poor — and all of it is by the will of Allah and His decree.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الإِذْنُ الكَوْنِيُّ القَدَرِيّالإِذْنُ الشَّرْعِيّ
what it isThat Allah allowed it to occur — it fell within His knowledge and decreeThat Allah permitted it in His law
does siḥr have it?Yes — nothing occurs without itNo — «the Sharīʿah forbids them that and makes it unlawful upon them»
what followsNo harm reaches anyone outside Allah’s decree — «so whoever Allah wills be harmed by that, the harm reaches him»The magician is a criminal, and the ḥudūd are established against him

Confusing those two is the single commonest error on this subject, in both directions. One man reasons: if it only happens by Allah’s permission, who am I to resist it — and becomes passive. Another reasons: it is forbidden, so it cannot really touch me — and denies the decree. The distinction answers both at once.

إِنَّا كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَاهُ بِقَدَرٍ

Indeed, We have created everything by measure.

سُورَةُ القَمَر · 54:49

مَا أَصَابَ مِن مُّصِيبَةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي أَنفُسِكُمْ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مِّن قَبْلِ أَن نَّبْرَأَهَا إِنَّ ذَٰلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

No affliction befalls in the earth or in yourselves but that it is in a Book before We bring it into being. Indeed, that is easy for Allah.

سُورَةُ الحَدِيد · 57:22

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the conclusion he draws from those two verses is aimed at the heart, not the intellect:

«So these evils that may occur from the magicians and from others do not occur out of ignorance on the part of our Lord — for He is the Knower of all things; nothing hidden is hidden from Him.»

«For our Lord is not overcome, and there does not occur in His dominion what He does not will — rather nothing occurs in this world nor in the Hereafter except by a prior decree, for a far-reaching wisdom which He has willed. So these may be tried with magic, and these tried with illness, and these tried with killing, and other than that — and to Allah belongs the far-reaching wisdom in what He decrees and ordains.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the corollary, which is the practical half and which people drop.

«And even if these things occur by the will of Allah and His decree, it is obligatory that we treat Allah’s decree with His decree; and it is obligatory that we war against all shirk and all acts of disobedience — while knowing that none of it occurs except by His will. But He has legislated for us that we war against them, and that we refrain from them, and that the legislated ḥudūd be established concerning them.»

«نُعَالِجَ قَدَرَ اللهِ بِقَدَرِه» is a rule far beyond this clause — it is the answer to fatalism about illness, about poverty, about sin and about harm. The decree is not an argument against acting; acting is part of the decree.

لِمَاذَا كَانَ السِّحْرُ شِرْكًا Why It Is Shirk, Not Merely a Grave Sin

Here is the hinge of the whole clause. Magic is not ranked with the gravest sins because of the harm it does — plenty of sins do harm. It is ranked there because of what it is reached by.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«And that is only because they attain their magic by worship of the devils, and drawing near to them by what they desire of slaughter and vows and prostration and other than that; so the magicians draw near to the devils by worshipping them besides Allah, and they assist them in what they want of harming people, for the gain of this world.»

So the shirk is not a by-product. It is the price. The three acts he names — ذَبْح, نَذْر, سُجُود — are three acts of worship, and the first two are the subject of the second nullifier and the third of the first. This clause is built on top of those.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And he states the exchange in both directions, which is the clearest description of the trade on these folios:

«So the jinn serve mankind, and mankind serve the jinn.»

«The jinn serve mankind by informing them of some events in near and far lands, and aiding them in wronging people. And mankind serve the jinn by worshipping them besides Allah, and supplicating them, and vowing to them, and slaughtering for them, and the like of that — and this is the mutual enjoyment of one another.»

The last phrase is a Qurʾānic one, and he uses it deliberately. Note what the human side of the bargain costs and what it buys: he gives away worship and receives information and the power to wrong people. Which is the worst exchange described anywhere in the ten clauses.

اجْتَنِبُوا السَّبْعَ المُوبِقَات. قُلْنَا: وَمَا هُنَّ يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ؟ قَال: الشِّرْكُ بِاللهِ، وَالسِّحْر، وَقَتْلُ النَّفْسِ الَّتِي حَرَّمَ اللهُ إِلَّا بِالحَقّ، وَأَكْلُ الرِّبَا، وَأَكْلُ مَالِ اليَتِيم، وَالتَّوَلِّي يَوْمَ الزَّحْف، وَقَذْفُ المُحْصَنَاتِ الغَافِلَاتِ المُؤْمِنَات.

“Avoid the seven destructive sins.” We said: and what are they, Messenger of Allah? He said: associating partners with Allah; magic; killing the soul Allah has made inviolable, except by right; consuming ribā; consuming the property of the orphan; turning away on the day of battle; and slander of chaste, unsuspecting believing women.

يُورِدُهُ الشَّيْخُ بِقَوْلِهِ «أَخْبَرَ النَّبِيُّ ﷺ» · the sharḥ gives neither an attribution nor a grading at this place, so none is supplied here

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And look at where siḥr sits in that list. Ibn Bāz says it himself: «so the greatest of them is shirk with Allah, then magic — and magic is of shirk, because it is not attained except by worship of the devils and drawing near to them by whom they are pleased with and by what they desire of slaughter and vows and prostration and other than that.»

Second in the list of seven, and immediately after shirk itself — and then he says the second is a species of the first.

The footnote adds a ḥadīth that states the equation outright: «And al-Nasāʾī has from Abū Hurayrah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْه from the Prophet ﷺ that he said: «مَنْ عَقَدَ عُقْدَةً ثُمَّ نَفَثَ فِيهَا فَقَدْ سَحَر، وَمَنْ سَحَرَ فَقَدْ أَشْرَك» — whoever ties a knot and then blows into it has performed magic, and whoever has performed magic has committed shirk. And this indicates that magic is shirk with Allah; and that is because it is not attained except by worship of the jinn and drawing near to them.» Ibn Bāz names al-Nasāʾī and Abū Hurayrah but gives no grading at this place, so this page reports the attribution and supplies no grading.

سِحْرُ التَّخْيِيلِ وَسِحْرُ التَّأْثِير Two Kinds — and the Common One

The footnote names them: سِحْرُ التَّخْيِيل and سِحْرُ التَّأْثِير. And Ibn Bāz is careful about the difference, because the first is an effect on the eye of the beholder and the second is a real effect in the world.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

سِحْرُ التَّخْيِيلسِحْرُ التَّأْثِير
what happensThe thing is seen other than as it is — «they alter the appearances in people’s eyes»; the rope is seen as a snake, a man as a donkey or an ape, a dog as a goat and a goat as a dogA real effect — «this is other than mere imagining; rather it is something that has an effect»: «فَيَحْصُلُ بِهِ تَأْثِيرٌ عَلَى الزَّوْجِ فِي المَحَبَّةِ وَالبَغْضَاء»
its daleel«يُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ مِنْ سِحْرِهِمْ أَنَّهَا تَسْعَى» — it is made to SEEM to him, Ṭā Hā 20:66; and «سَحَرُوا أَعْيُنَ النَّاس», al-Aʿrāf 7:116«مَا يُفَرِّقُونَ بِهِ بَيْنَ المَرْءِ وَزَوْجِهِ» — what they separate a man and his wife with, al-Baqarah 2:102
how common«وَغَالِبُ السِّحْرِ هُوَ التَّخْيِيل» — most of itReal, and he says plainly that among its kinds are «أَنْوَاعٌ مِنَ السِّحْرِ لَهَا أَثَرٌ وَلَهَا وُجُود»

فَإِذَا حِبَالُهُمْ وَعِصِيُّهُمْ يُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ مِن سِحْرِهِمْ أَنَّهَا تَسْعَىٰ

And at once their ropes and staffs were made to seem to him, by their magic, to be moving.

سُورَةُ طَه · 20:66

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He gives the examples at length, and they are worth having because they are the shapes the thing actually takes: a rope or a staff seen as a snake walking; birds or snakes or scorpions apparently brought out of the mouth or the belly — «وَلَيْسَ لَهُ حَقِيقَة، كُلُّهُ تَخْيِيل»; and a man shown his wife’s form as ugly until he hates her, or she shown his form as ugly when he comes to her until she hates him — and he adds the detail that it is her husband seen black after having been white, or seen ill. «فَعِنْدَ ذَلِكَ تَقَعُ البَغْضَاءُ وَالعَدَاوَةُ وَالفُرْقَة.»

And he gives the mechanism of the second kind as the first kind’s consequence: «وَلَعَلَّ أَسْبَابَ التَّأْثِيرِ أَنَّ السِّحْرَ يُخَيِّلُ إِلَى الرَّجُلِ قَبَاحَةَ الزَّوْجَة» — perhaps the causes of the effect are that the magic makes the wife’s ugliness seem to the man. Note the «perhaps». He is explaining a mechanism, not asserting one, and he marks it as such.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He also explains the onlookers, which is where stage conjuring and real magic get confused: «and the magician bewitches the onlookers who witness his work; and there may be there one who assists him whom the onlookers do not see, of the devils who help him — for they see us and we do not see them… and it may be the magic of the eye by what he has done of sleight, like one who brings out of his pocket or his mouth a bird or an egg or other than that in the eyes of people — and the matter is otherwise.»

And the heavy loads dragged «by a hair or two» are explained the same way: «مِمَّا سَاعَدَهُ فِيهِ الشَّيَاطِين، وَهُمْ لَا يُرَوْن» — the devils are pulling, unseen. Which is the point: what looks like a power in the man is a service rendered by something he has paid for in worship.

فَلَمَّا أَلْقَوْا سَحَرُوا أَعْيُنَ النَّاسِ وَاسْتَرْهَبُوهُمْ وَجَاءُوا بِسِحْرٍ عَظِيمٍ

And when they threw, they bewitched the eyes of the people and overawed them, and they produced a great magic.

سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف · 7:116

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The footnote asks a question worth the asking: does Allah’s prophet Mūsā عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام fall under the general «they bewitched the eyes of the people and overawed them»?

«As for “and overawed them” — yes, by the proof of His saying «فَأَوْجَسَ فِي نَفْسِهِ خِيفَة» [he conceived fear within himself]. And as for “they bewitched the eyes of the people” — it was made to seem to Mūsā, by reason of their magic, that they were great snakes; and had it not been for that he would not have feared, and His saying «يُخَيَّلُ إِلَيْهِ» — i.e. to Mūsā — indicates it; and that is of the magic of the eye.»

This is also the compiler’s proof for Ibn Bāz’s sentence «the magician bewitches the onlookers».

And the sharḥ does not leave Pharaoh’s magicians where it found them. It follows them to the end of the story, and the end of the story is the only place in this entire clause where magicians come out well. When Mūsā threw his staff, «it swallowed up these ropes and staffs; and when the magicians saw it they believed, and fell down prostrate, believing in what Mūsā brought — and when Pharaoh threatened them they paid him no heed.»

قَالُوا لَن نُّؤْثِرَكَ عَلَىٰ مَا جَاءَنَا مِنَ الْبَيِّنَاتِ وَالَّذِي فَطَرَنَا فَاقْضِ مَا أَنتَ قَاضٍ

They said: we will never prefer you over what has come to us of clear proofs, and over Him who created us — so decree whatever you will decree.

سُورَةُ طَه · 20:72

إِنَّا آمَنَّا بِرَبِّنَا لِيَغْفِرَ لَنَا خَطَايَانَا وَمَا أَكْرَهْتَنَا عَلَيْهِ مِنَ السِّحْرِ وَاللَّهُ خَيْرٌ وَأَبْقَىٰ

Indeed, we have believed in our Lord, that He may forgive us our sins and the magic to which you compelled us. And Allah is better and more enduring.

سُورَةُ طَه · 20:73

الشَّرْح · the explanation

They were magicians in the morning and martyrs by the evening. And the thing they ask forgiveness for by name is «the magic to which you compelled us» — so the clause that makes magic a nullifier of Islām also preserves, in the Qurʾān’s own words, the case of magicians who repented and were accepted.

Keep that for part 2, where the question of the magician’s repentance is answered at length — and where the answer turns out to depend entirely on whether he came of his own accord or was seized.

الصَّرْفُ وَالعَطْف What the Matn Named Alongside It

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The matn singled out two kinds, and the sharḥ places them among the magician’s trade:

«And of their wicked deeds: الصَّرْف [turning a man away from what he desires — as from his wife], and العَطْف [bending him toward what he does not], and separating between the two spouses and between relatives by what they do of the deeds of magic and its kinds, which harms everyone; and it makes this one hateful to that one and that one to this — from what they receive from the jinn and the devils and serve them by.»

Which is why the matn named them: they are the forms of the thing that reach ordinary households. And both are instances of «مَا يُفَرِّقُونَ بِهِ بَيْنَ المَرْءِ وَزَوْجِهِ» — the verse’s own example.

مَنْ قَالَ إِنَّ السِّحْرَ خُرَافَة Denying It Is Itself Disbelief

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — A man says: magic is a superstition and has no reality.

Ibn Bāz — «Whoever says it is a disbeliever — because Allah mentioned magic in the Qurʾān and the Messenger ﷺ explained it. So magic exists, and it has a reality: some of it exists in reality — it sickens and it kills — and some of it is imagining; and all of it occurs. Whoever denies it is a disbeliever, denying the Qurʾān.»

So the clause cuts in two directions at once, and that is unusual. The magician is a disbeliever for practising it. The modern man is a disbeliever for saying it is not real. Both rulings rest on the same thing — the text of the Qurʾān — and that is why both are in this clause.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the compiler adds al-Qurṭubī, who draws a distinction inside the denial:

«The Qurʾān has indicated, in more than one verse, and the Sunnah in more than one ḥadīth, that magic exists and has an effect on the one bewitched; so whoever denies that is a disbeliever, denying Allah and His Messenger ﷺ, and a denier of what is known by direct observation. Then: the denier of magic — if he denies it secretly, he is a zindīq; and if he denies it openly, he is an apostate.»

Reported by the compiler by way of al-Ubbī’s commentary on Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim.

Part 2 takes printed folios ١٦٦–١٧٥ — the ruling on the magician himself: the killing and who carries it out, whether his repentance is accepted and when, the athar of ʿUmar and of Ḥafṣah and of Jundub, and the grading of the one ḥadīth everyone quotes for the ḥadd — which the compiler works through at length and does not leave standing. With it, Ibn Bāz’s refutation of al-Rāzī on whether a magician can turn a man into a donkey.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

يَجِبُ أَنْ نُعَالِجَ قَدَرَ اللهِ بِقَدَرِه

Ibn Bāz, in the middle of the qadar passage on this clause — and it is the biggest sentence on the page. He has just said that nothing reaches anyone except by Allah’s decree, including the magician’s harm. The obvious wrong conclusion is passivity, and he closes it off in the same breath: «وَلَكِنَّهُ — سُبْحَانَهُ — شَرَعَ لَنَا أَنْ نُحَارِبَهَا، وَأَنْ نَمْتَنِعَ مِنْهَا، وَأَنْ تُقَامَ فِيهَا الحُدُودُ الشَّرْعِيَّة.» The decree is not an argument against acting — acting is part of the decree. Which answers fatalism about illness, poverty and sin, and not only about magic. And keep beside it the sentence that makes the clause a nullifier at all: «وَالسِّحْرُ مِنَ الشِّرْك».
