Unit 5 · al-Qawā‘id al-Arba‘ · Lesson 4Year 2

القَاعِدَةُ الرَّابِعَة

The Shirk That Does Not Break

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the fourth foundation and the reason the author gives for it.
  • Contrast the shirk of the earlier mushrikīn with that of the later ones.
  • Give the proof from Sūrat al-‘Ankabūt and explain what it shows.
  • Draw the conclusion the whole treatise was written for.
  • Answer the objection “but they pray and fast” with العُمْدَةُ عَلَى أَصْلِ الدِّينِ.
  • Explain, from al-Shuʿarāʾ 26:98, in what sense they equated others with the Lord of the Worlds.
  • Reason from the ḥadīth on riyāʾ to the gravity of directing worship itself to another.
القَاعِدَةُ الرَّابِعَة The Fourth Foundation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَنَّ مُشْرِكِي زَمَانِنَا أَغْلَظُ شِرْكًا مِنَ الأَوَّلِينَ؛ لِأَنَّ الأَوَّلِينَ يُشْرِكُونَ فِي الرَّخَاءِ وَيُخْلِصُونَ فِي الشِّدَّةِ، وَمُشْرِكُو زَمَانِنَا شِرْكُهُمْ دَائِمٌ فِي الرَّخَاءِ وَالشِّدَّةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the foundation that startles students, so it is worth stating the claim precisely. The author is not saying the later mushrikīn worship more idols, or worship them more often. He is saying their shirk is more severe in one specific respect: it does not break.

The earlier mushrikīn had a limit. When the ship began to sink, the intermediaries were forgotten and they called on Allah alone, with complete sincerity. Their shirk was real, but it was fair-weather shirk — it collapsed under genuine terror, and in collapsing it admitted the truth.

The later ones keep calling on the created thing in the very moment of drowning. They call on it in ease, and they call on it in the extremity where even the mushrikīn of Quraysh had turned to Allah. That constancy is the whole of the charge.

فَإِذَا رَكِبُوا فِي الْفُلْكِ دَعَوُا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ فَلَمَّا نَجَّاهُمْ إِلَى الْبَرِّ إِذَا هُمْ يُشْرِكُونَ

"And when they board a ship they call upon Allah, sincere to Him in religion. But when He delivers them to the land, at once they associate others with Him."

Sūrat al-‘Ankabūt 29:65 · سورة العنكبوت

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The āyah is a portrait in two halves. On the water, facing death: مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ — pure, undivided calling upon Allah. The idols are not mentioned, because at that moment even they knew the idols were nothing.

Back on dry land: إِذَا هُمْ يُشْرِكُونَ — immediately, no interval. The sincerity was never belief; it was terror. And the author’s point lands here: Allah recorded this of them as a criticism — and yet they were still better in this one respect than those who do not even have that moment of clarity.

وَإِذَا مَسَّكُمُ الضُّرُّ فِي الْبَحْرِ ضَلَّ مَنْ تَدْعُونَ إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ

"And when harm touches you at sea, those you call upon vanish — except Him."

Sūrat al-Isrā’ 17:67 · سورة الإسراء

The word ضَلَّ is exact: the false objects of worship do not merely fail — they disappear from the mind. Real distress strips away what was never believed in.

وَإِذَا غَشِيَهُمْ مَوْجٌ كَالظُّلَلِ دَعَوُا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ

"And when waves cover them like canopies, they call upon Allah, sincere to Him in religion."

Sūrat Luqmān 31:32 · سورة لقمان

الخُلَاصَة What the Four Foundations Come To

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Set the four side by side and the treatise closes like an argument:

  1. Affirming that Allah alone creates and governs is not Islam — the people fought by the Prophet ﷺ affirmed it.
  2. "We only want nearness and intercession" is not a defence — it was their exact defence.
  3. The rank of the thing worshipped changes nothing — angel, prophet, saint, stone, sun: all were fought alike.
  4. And shirk that holds firm in hardship is graver, not lighter, than shirk that breaks.

Which returns the student to the first page: worship is not worship without tawḥīd, as the prayer is not a prayer without purity. Everything in between has been the author showing you how a man can be certain he is worshipping — and be wrong.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَصَلَّى اللهُ عَلَى مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَلَى آلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ وَسَلَّمَ.

With this the author closes the treatise.

«لَكِنَّهُمْ يُصَلُّونَ وَيَصُومُونَ» "But They Pray, and They Fast"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ anticipates the objection that this foundation always provokes, and it is a fair one. These people ascribe to Islam; they have prayers and acts of devotion; they pray, give zakāh and fast. How can they be graver in shirk than the mushrikīn of the Jāhiliyyah, who had none of that?

The answer is one line, and it sends you back to the first lesson of this unit: العُمْدَةُ عَلَى أَصْلِ الدِّينِ — the reliance is on the foundation of the religion. These acts of worship without tawḥīd do not benefit, just as the prayer does not benefit without purity. If great worship joined to shirk is not accepted, what of one who associates others with Allah in ease and in hardship both?

ثُمَّ إِذَا كَشَفَ الضُّرَّ عَنْكُمْ إِذَا فَرِيقٌ مِنْكُمْ بِرَبِّهِمْ يُشْرِكُونَ

"Then when He removes the harm from you, at once a party of you associates others with their Lord."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:54 · سورة النحل

وَإِذَا غَشِيَهُمْ مَوْجٌ كَالظُّلَلِ دَعَوُا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ

"And when waves cover them like canopies, they call upon Allah, sincere to Him in religion."

Sūrat Luqmān 31:32 · سورة لقمان

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Three sūrahs, one scene, told again and again: the sea, the terror, the sudden perfect sincerity — and then the land, and the forgetting. Allah records it as a criticism of them. And the fourth foundation observes that even so, they had that moment. The later ones do not.

The sharḥ is unsparingly concrete about what replaces it. When harm touches the mushrikūn of these times, they flee to — and then it names the shrines and the dead by name, in Egypt, the Sudan, Africa, Pakistan, India, Iraq, Syria: people turning to tombs and to the burial places of awliyāʾ, and of those who are not even awliyāʾ, holding beliefs about them, “assigning them a share of divinity.”

And one exchange it records, which says more than an argument could. A scholar met a man from al-Ṭāʾif, before the daʿwah had reached there, and remarked: these people of al-Ṭāʾif — when hardship comes to them they flee to Ibn ʿAbbās, and they do not know Allah. The man replied: “Knowing Ibn ʿAbbās is enough.” (al-Durar al-Saniyyah.)

أَعْظَمُ مَا يَسْتَحِقُّهُ اللهُ The Greatest of What Allah Deserves

الشَّرْح · the explanation

“The greatest of what He deserves is that the heart worship Him, and that there be no worship at all except for Him alone.” The sharḥ puts the whole treatise into that sentence, and brings the āyah the Prophet ﷺ was commanded with:

فَمَنْ كَانَ يَرْجُو لِقَاءَ رَبِّهِ فَلْيَعْمَلْ عَمَلًا صَالِحًا وَلَا يُشْرِكْ بِعِبَادَةِ رَبِّهِ أَحَدًا

"So whoever hopes for the meeting with his Lord — let him do righteous work, and not associate anyone in the worship of his Lord."

Sūrat al-Kahf 18:110 · سورة الكهف

أَنَا أَغْنَى الشُّرَكَاءِ عَنِ الشِّرْكِ، مَنْ عَمِلَ عَمَلًا أَشْرَكَ فِيهِ مَعِي غَيْرِي تَرَكْتُهُ وَشِرْكَهُ

"I am the most free of partners from needing a partner. Whoever does a deed in which he associates another with Me, I leave him and his association."

حَدِيثٌ قُدْسِيٌّ · رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ · Muslim, from Abū Hurayrah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ then reasons from the lesser to the greater, and the argument is hard to escape. If this is Allah's judgement upon riyāʾ — where a man performs the deed for Allah but wants some person to see it — then what of directing the act of worship itself to another than Allah?

And it lists them, so there is no vagueness about what “worship” covers: to supplicate (الدُّعَاء) another than Allah; to call for rescue (الاسْتِغَاثَة); to vow (النَّذْر); to sacrifice (الذَّبْح); to seek refuge (الاسْتِعَاذَة) in another than Allah, in what none but Allah is able to do.

«إِذْ نُسَوِّيكُمْ بِرَبِّ العَالَمِينَ» The Confession in the Fire

تَاللَّهِ إِنْ كُنَّا لَفِي ضَلَالٍ مُبِينٍ إِذْ نُسَوِّيكُمْ بِرَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ

"By Allah, we were surely in clear error, when we equated you with the Lord of the Worlds."

Sūrat al-Shu‘arā’ 26:97–98 · سورة الشعراء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the capstone, and it closes the circle back to the first foundation. In what sense did they equate their gods with the Lord of the Worlds?

Ibn Taymiyyah and Ibn al-Qayyim both answer, and the answer is precise: not in creating, providing, giving life or giving death. No one ever said that — not one people among the disbelievers ever claimed the world had two equal makers. They equated them with Allah in worship, in love and devotion, by directing some portion of worship to them.

So they equated the created with the Creator — and the sharḥ calls this “the most hideous of injustice, and the ugliest transgression against the right of Allah.” Which is the first foundation arriving at its destination: they never disputed that He alone is the Lord. They simply gave away a share of what belongs to Him alone — and the share is what the Fire makes them confess.

And the sharḥ ends exactly where it began. Its last words are a du‘ā’ that Allah make us among those who, when given, are grateful; when tried, are patient; and when they sin, seek forgiveness — the three signs of happiness from the first page of the book. Having walked through the four foundations, you can now see why those three were the right place to start.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَلَمَّا نَجَّاهُمْ إِلَى الْبَرِّ إِذَا هُمْ يُشْرِكُونَ

"But when He delivers them to the land, at once they associate others with Him." The test of what a man truly believes is what he turns to when he is safe. Everyone is a muwaḥḥid in the sinking ship. Ask: when the fear passed and the need was met, who did I thank, and who did I go back to?
