تَنْزِيلُ القُرْآنِ مِنَ اللهِ تَعَالَى

The Sending Down of the Qurʾān from Allah

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٨٥–٨٧
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 85–87.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say why the author moves from إِثْبَاتِ الكَلَامِ to إِثْبَاتِ التَّنْزِيلِ, and what the second adds.
  • Define النَّسْخ and explain al-Fawzān’s account of the wisdom in it.
  • Answer the claim that a man was teaching the Prophet ﷺ, in the Qurʾān’s own terms.
  • Show how the fact of الإِنْزَال is itself a proof of العُلُوّ.
٢٢ · إِثْبَاتُ تَنْزِيلِ القُرْآنِ مِنَ اللهِ تَعَالَى Section 22 — The Qurʾān Is Sent Down from Allah

al-Fawzān marks the transition himself: when the author رَحِمَهُ اللهُ had brought the āyāt indicating the affirming of speech for Allah تَعَالَى, and that the Mighty Qurʾān is of His speech سُبْحَانَهُ, he then proceeded to set out the āyāt indicating that the Qurʾān is sent down from Allah. Lesson 19 settled whose speech it is; this section settles how it reached us — and, as it turns out, where it came from.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وَهَذَا كِتَابٌ أَنْزَلْنَاهُ مُبَارَكٌ﴾، ﴿لَوْ أَنْزَلْنَا هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَى جَبَلٍ لَرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُتَصَدِّعًا مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ﴾، ﴿وَإِذَا بَدَّلْنَا آيَةً مَكَانَ آيَةٍ وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يُنَزِّلُ قَالُوا إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ مُفْتَرٍ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ ۝ قُلْ نَزَّلَهُ رُوحُ الْقُدُسِ مِنْ رَبِّكَ بِالْحَقِّ لِيُثَبِّتَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَهُدًى وَبُشْرَى لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ ۝ وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ إِنَّمَا يُعَلِّمُهُ بَشَرٌ لِسَانُ الَّذِي يُلْحِدُونَ إِلَيْهِ أَعْجَمِيٌّ وَهَذَا لِسَانٌ عَرَبِيٌّ مُبِينٌ﴾.

وَهَذَا كِتَابٌ أَنْزَلْنَاهُ مُبَارَكٌ مُصَدِّقُ الَّذِي بَيْنَ يَدَيْهِ

"And this is a Book We have sent down, blessed, confirming what came before it."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:92 · سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān parses it before explaining it. هَذَا is a demonstrative pointing to the Noble Qurʾān, and it is the mubtadaʾ; its khabar is كِتَابٌ; and أَنْزَلْنَاهُ مُبَارَكٌ are two descriptions of كِتَاب.

And then an observation about word order that is worth more than it first looks: وَقَدَّمَ صِفَةَ الإِنْزَالِ — and He put the description of the sending-down first, because the disbelievers were denying it.

The Qurʾān does not arrange its descriptions decoratively. Of the two things said about the Book — that it was sent down, and that it is blessed — the contested one is placed first. The order of the words answers the objection before the objection is stated.

المُبَارَك — abundant in blessing, for what it comprises of religious and worldly benefits.

لَوْ أَنْزَلْنَا هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَى جَبَلٍ لَرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُتَصَدِّعًا مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ

"Had We sent down this Qurʾān upon a mountain, you would have seen it humbled, split apart from fear of Allah."

Sūrat al-Ḥashr 59:21 · سُورَةُ الحَشْر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is an informing about the immensity of the Qurʾān, and that it is deserving that hearts should be humbled before it. And al-Fawzān lays out the reasoning as a comparison from the greater to the lesser:

For if it were sent down upon a mountain — along with its being at the utmost of hardness and the extreme of solidity — were it to understand this Qurʾān, it would be humbled and would split apart out of fear of Allah, in wariness of His punishment. So how is it fitting for you, O mankind, that your hearts should not soften and be humbled — and you have understood from Allah His command, and pondered His Book?!

The force of it is in the aside about the mountain. A mountain is the hardest thing the listener can picture, and the āyah says even that would break. The comparison is not between a man and a mountain in strength, but in hardness — and it is the man who comes out harder.

الشُّبْهَةُ الأُولَى · النَّسْخُ افْتِرَاء The First Objection — That Abrogation Is Fabrication

وَإِذَا بَدَّلْنَا آيَةً مَكَانَ آيَةٍ وَاللَّهُ أَعْلَمُ بِمَا يُنَزِّلُ قَالُوا إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ مُفْتَرٍ بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ

"And when We substitute an āyah in place of an āyah — and Allah knows best what He sends down — they say: you are only a fabricator. Rather, most of them do not know."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:101 · سُورَةُ النَّحْل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is a proceeding from Him سُبْحَانَهُ into mentioning a disbelieving objection surrounding the Noble Qurʾān, together with the refutation of it.

بَدَّلْنَا — and the definition is exact: the meaning of التَّبْدِيل is the lifting of a thing together with the placing of something else in its place; and the substituting of an āyah is the lifting of it by another āyah besides it — and that is its abrogation by an āyah other than it.

قَالُوا — that is: the disbelievers of Quraysh, who were ignorant of the wisdom in abrogation. إِنَّمَا أَنْتَ — O Muḥammad — مُفْتَرٍ — a liar, an inventor, speaking falsely against Allah, since you claim that He commanded you with a thing, and then you claim that He commanded you with its opposite.

State the objection plainly and it is not a foolish one: a message that changes its own rulings looks like a message its bearer is making up as he goes. That is the charge, and the āyah does not dodge it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So Allah refuted them with what conveys their ignorance, saying بَلْ أَكْثَرُهُمْ لَا يَعْلَمُونَ — which al-Fawzān reads in two possible ways: they do not know anything of knowledge at all, or they do not know the wisdom in abrogation.

And then the wisdom itself: for it is built upon the benefits which Allah سُبْحَانَهُ knows. So there may be in the legislating of this thing a benefit limited to a time; then, after that time, the benefit lies in the legislating of something else.

That is the whole answer, and it is an answer about knowledge, not about consistency. A ruling fitted to a situation is not falsified when the situation changes — unless the one who gave it could not see the change coming. The objection assumes a legislator who does not know the future.

al-Fawzān closes it with a sentence worth keeping: and had the covering been lifted for these disbelievers, they would have known that this is the face of correctness, and the path of justice, and of gentleness, and of kindness. Not merely permissible — kind. The staged legislation was a mercy to the people it was staged for.

قُلْ نَزَّلَهُ رُوحُ الْقُدُسِ مِنْ رَبِّكَ بِالْحَقِّ لِيُثَبِّتَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَهُدًى وَبُشْرَى لِلْمُسْلِمِينَ

"Say: the Spirit of Holiness brought it down from your Lord in truth, to make firm those who believe — and as guidance and glad tidings for the Muslims."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:102 · سُورَةُ النَّحْل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then He refuted them regarding their claim that this substituting was from Muḥammad, and that he was thereby a fabricator against Allah. The answer names the chain of transmission: نَزَّلَهُ — that is, the Qurʾān — رُوحُ الْقُدُسِ — that is, Jibrīl.

And a point of Arabic on the title: القُدْس is purity, and the meaning is the purified Spirit brought it down — so it is of the kind where the thing described is annexed to its own description (إِضَافَةُ المَوْصُوفِ إِلَى صِفَتِه).

مِنْ رَبِّكَ — that is: the beginning of its sending down is from Allah سُبْحَانَهُ. بِالْحَقِّ — in the position of naṣb as a ḥāl, that is: characterised by being true.

So the āyah answers the charge by giving the route in full and in order: from your Lord → by Jibrīl → in truth. The Prophet ﷺ is not in the list of authors.

لِيُثَبِّتَ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا — upon faith; so they say: each of the abrogating and the abrogated is from our Lord. And because, when they come to know the benefits in abrogation, they become firm upon faith. And وَهُدًى وَبُشْرَى are conjoined to the position of لِيُثَبِّتَ — that is: as a making firm for them, and a guidance, and glad tidings.

Notice the inversion. The same feature of the revelation that the disbelievers took as proof of fraud is named here as what makes the believers firm. Naskh is not a difficulty the Qurʾān survives; it is listed among its mercies.

الشُّبْهَةُ الثَّانِيَة · بَشَرٌ يُعَلِّمُه The Second Objection — That a Man Was Teaching Him

وَلَقَدْ نَعْلَمُ أَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ إِنَّمَا يُعَلِّمُهُ بَشَرٌ لِسَانُ الَّذِي يُلْحِدُونَ إِلَيْهِ أَعْجَمِيٌّ وَهَذَا لِسَانٌ عَرَبِيٌّ مُبِينٌ

"And We certainly know that they say: a man is only teaching him. The tongue of the one they incline towards is foreign, while this is a clear Arabic tongue."

Sūrat al-Naḥl 16:103 · سُورَةُ النَّحْل

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān spells out the objection in full, including the part of it that is an argument and not merely an insult: these disbelievers say: a human being from the children of Ādam is teaching Muḥammad the Qurʾān, and not an angel from the angels. And this human who teaches him had studied the Torah, the Gospel and the foreign books — because Muḥammad is an unlettered man, and it is not possible that he should come with what is mentioned in the Qurʾān of the reports of the earliest generations.

Grant them their premise and the inference is reasonable. An unlettered man in Makkah should not know the histories of Israel. So they posited a source. The Qurʾān's reply does not deny the premise — it examines the source they named.

لِسَانُ الَّذِي يُلْحِدُونَ إِلَيْهِ أَعْجَمِيٌّ — the tongue of the one they incline towards, and claim is teaching you, O Muḥammad, is foreign — that is, non-Arab: he does not speak Arabic. وَهَذَا لِسَانٌ عَرَبِيٌّ مُبِينٌ — and this Qurʾān possesses Arabic eloquence and clear expression.

And then the question the āyah leaves them with, which al-Fawzān puts at full force: so how do you claim that a human being from the non-Arabs teaches the Prophet ﷺ — when you yourselves were unable to oppose it, or to oppose a single sūrah or some sūrahs of it, and you are the people of the Arabic tongue, the men of eloquence, and the leaders of rhetoric?!

The refutation has two blades and both are factual. Their candidate cannot speak the language the Book is in; and the acknowledged masters of that language could not produce the like of one sūrah of it. A foreigner is being credited with an Arabic the Arabs could not match.

مَا يُسْتَفَادُ مِنَ الآيَات What the Āyāt Yield

الشَّرْح · the explanation

— the affirming that the Qurʾān is sent down from Allah تَعَالَى, and that it is His speech جَلَّ وَعَلَا — not the speech of anyone else, of the angels or of mankind; and the refutation of whoever claimed that it is created speech. And in the āyāt also is the affirming of al-ʿuluww for Allah سُبْحَانَهُ — because the sending DOWN cannot be except from what is ABOVE. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

That last line is the quietest argument in the section and perhaps the most far-reaching. Nobody disputes that the Qurʾān was sent down; the word أَنْزَلَ is on every page and in every creed, including those of the people who deny that Allah is above His creation. But a thing is only sent down from somewhere higher. So the ʿuluww of Lesson 16 is not resting only on the āyāt gathered for it — it is built into the vocabulary that everyone already uses about revelation.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

لَوْ أَنْزَلْنَا هَذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَى جَبَلٍ لَرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُتَصَدِّعًا مِنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ

Read it as the argument it is, not as an image. The mountain is named because it is the hardest thing you can picture — and the āyah says even that would break. Then ask which of the two of you has actually understood the Book.
