القَاعِدَةُ الثَّانِيَة

Nearness and Intercession

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the second foundation and the two excuses the mushrikīn gave.
  • Give the proof for القُرْبَة and the proof for الشَّفَاعَة separately.
  • Define الشَّفَاعَةُ المَنْفِيَّة and الشَّفَاعَةُ المُثْبَتَة, and give the proof of each.
  • Name the three conditions of the intercession that is affirmed.
  • Use الوَسَاطَة to state what the mushrikīn actually claimed.
  • Name the two roots of the world’s shirk — the people of Ibrāhīm and the people of Nūḥ.
  • Identify what al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā and Manāt actually were.
  • Give the two conditions of the affirmed intercession, and the words with which it is asked.
القَاعِدَةُ الثَّانِيَة The Second Foundation

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

أَنَّهُمْ يَقُولُونَ: مَا دَعَوْنَاهُمْ وَتَوَجَّهْنَا إِلَيْهِمْ إِلَّا لِطَلَبِ القُرْبَةِ وَالشَّفَاعَةِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here the author anticipates the defence. No one who prays to a dead saint says "I believe he is my Lord." He says something far more reasonable-sounding: I know Allah is the Lord — I am only using this righteous man as a means of drawing near, and asking him to speak for me.

The author’s reply is not to argue about the sincerity of the motive. It is to show that the mushrikīn of old gave exactly this defence, word for word — and Allah still called them mushrikīn. He proves it with two āyahs, one for each half of their excuse.

وَالَّذِينَ اتَّخَذُوا مِنْ دُونِهِ أَوْلِيَاءَ مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا إِلَى اللَّهِ زُلْفَىٰ

"And those who take protectors besides Him [say]: We worship them only so that they may bring us nearer to Allah."

Sūrat al-Zumar 39:3 · سورة الزمر — the proof of القُرْبَة

وَيَعْبُدُونَ مِنْ دُونِ اللَّهِ مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنْفَعُهُمْ وَيَقُولُونَ هَٰؤُلَاءِ شُفَعَاؤُنَا عِنْدَ اللَّهِ

"And they worship besides Allah that which neither harms them nor benefits them, and they say: These are our intercessors with Allah."

Sūrat Yūnus 10:18 · سورة يونس — the proof of الشَّفَاعَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two things deserve attention. First, in both āyahs Allah calls the act عِبَادَة — worship — even though they themselves called it only "seeking nearness." A man does not get to rename an act of worship and thereby exempt it.

Second, the description in Yūnus is devastating: مَا لَا يَضُرُّهُمْ وَلَا يَنْفَعُهُمْ — "that which neither harms them nor benefits them." The whole reason a person turns to an intermediary is that he imagines some benefit there. Allah denies the premise outright.

الشَّفَاعَةُ شَفَاعَتَانِ Intercession Is of Two Kinds

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَالشَّفَاعَةُ شَفَاعَتَانِ: شَفَاعَةٌ مَنْفِيَّةٌ وَشَفَاعَةٌ مُثْبَتَةٌ. فَالشَّفَاعَةُ المَنْفِيَّةُ: مَا كَانَتْ تُطْلَبُ مِنْ غَيْرِ اللهِ فِيمَا لَا يَقْدِرُ عَلَيْهِ إِلَّا اللهُ. وَالشَّفَاعَةُ المُثْبَتَةُ: هِيَ الَّتِي تُطْلَبُ مِنَ اللهِ، وَالشَّافِعُ مُكَرَّمٌ بِالشَّفَاعَةِ، وَالمَشْفُوعُ لَهُ مَنْ رَضِيَ اللهُ قَوْلَهُ وَعَمَلَهُ بَعْدَ الإِذْنِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the distinction that saves the student from two opposite errors: denying intercession altogether, or using it as a licence for calling on the dead. Intercession is real — but it is asked of Allah, never of the intercessor.

المَنْفِيَّة — negatedالمُثْبَتَة — affirmed
Asked ofsomeone other than AllahAllah alone
Concerningwhat none but Allah can dowhat Allah has permitted
Verdictshirktrue, and to be hoped for

And the author gives the affirmed kind three conditions, all of them in his one sentence: (1) it is sought from Allah; (2) the intercessor is honoured by being permitted to intercede — مُكَرَّمٌ بِالشَّفَاعَةِ; (3) the one interceded for is someone whose speech and deed Allah was pleased with, and only after permission is granted. Take away any one of the three and nothing remains.

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَنْفِقُوا مِمَّا رَزَقْنَاكُمْ مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ يَأْتِيَ يَوْمٌ لَا بَيْعٌ فِيهِ وَلَا خُلَّةٌ وَلَا شَفَاعَةٌ

"O you who believe, spend from what We have provided you before a Day comes in which there is no trade, no friendship and no intercession."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:254 · سورة البقرة — the proof of the negated intercession

مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ

"Who is there that can intercede with Him except by His permission?"

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:255 · سورة البقرة — آيةُ الكُرْسِيّ, the proof of the affirmed intercession

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Read the two together and the rule writes itself. On that Day there is no خُلَّةٌ وَلَا شَفَاعَةٌ — no bargaining, no pulling of connections, no intercession of the kind people imagine. And yet intercession does occur — إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ. The exception names the condition: by His permission. Intercession is not influence over Allah; it is a mercy He grants, to whom He wills, when He wills.

So the believer does hope for the intercession of the Prophet ﷺ — and he asks Allah for it, which is why we answer the adhān and ask Allah to grant him the praised station. He does not address the request to the Prophet ﷺ himself.

الوَسَاطَةُ: مِفْتَاحُ القَاعِدَةِ Mediation — the Key to This Foundation

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ gives the one word that organises the whole foundation: الوَسَاطَة, mediation. The mushrikīn of the Arabs did not ask of their gods independently — they asked through them, as go-betweens. They held that the gods brought them near to Allah, or carried their needs up to Allah, or spoke for them with Allah.

So the request was always aimed, in their own minds, at Allah. That is what makes this foundation necessary: the thing being refuted is not crude idolatry but a sophisticated and pious-sounding theology.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

There is a point of Arabic here that the sharḥ does not let pass. In مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا the construction is الحَصْر — restriction, what the rhetoricians call a qaṣr. They are not merely giving a reason; they are saying this and nothing else is our reason: we worship them for no motive whatsoever except to be brought near.

Which means — and this is the uncomfortable part — they wanted what is with Allah. Their aim was His nearness, His reward. The motive was not the problem. The act was.

And the mechanics of intercession, so the word is precise: الشَّفَاعَة is to ask. The intercessor joins his request to the petitioner's and raises it to the one who holds the authority. That is all it is — which is why who you address it to decides everything.

أَصْلُ شِرْكِ العَالَمِ Where the Shirk of the World Began

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ steps back to show that all the shirk in the world runs back to two roots — and both are forms of mediation.

The first: the shirk of Ibrāhīm's people — belief in the spirits of the heavenly bodies. They held that particular stars had an effect in the dominion, carved idols to represent them, and believed those spirits came to dwell in the idols and spoke to them. (In reality, the sharḥ notes, it was shayāṭīn toying with them.) Hence Allah showed Ibrāhīm مَلَكُوتَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ — the dominion of the heavens and the earth (al-Anʿām 6:75).

A question the scholars raise on the āyah that follows — when Ibrāhīm said of the star هَٰذَا رَبِّي, “this is my lord” — was he نَاظِرًا, reasoning his own way to the truth, or مُنَاظِرًا, arguing against his people? The sharḥ says the sound view, and the one Ibn Taymiyyah establishes, is that he was debating them, not discovering. The proof is that Allah had already said: وَلَقَدْ آتَيْنَا إِبْرَاهِيمَ رُشْدَهُ مِنْ قَبْلُ — “and We had given Ibrāhīm his guidance before” (al-Anbiyāʾ 21:51). He was not working it out; he was dismantling their reasoning from the inside.

وَقَالُوا لَا تَذَرُنَّ آلِهَتَكُمْ وَلَا تَذَرُنَّ وَدًّا وَلَا سُوَاعًا وَلَا يَغُوثَ وَيَعُوقَ وَنَسْرًا

"And they said: do not abandon your gods — do not abandon Wadd, nor Suwāʿ, nor Yaghūth and Yaʿūq and Nasr."

Sūrat Nūḥ 71:23 · سورة نوح

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The second root: the shirk of Nūḥ's people — veneration of the spirits of the righteous. And the identity of those five names is not left to guesswork; it is in al-Bukhārī:

هَذِهِ أَسْمَاءُ رِجَالٍ صَالِحِينَ كَانَتْ فِي قَوْمِ نُوحٍ

"These are the names of righteous men among the people of Nūḥ."

عَبْدُ اللهِ بْنُ عَبَّاسٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُمَا · Ibn ʿAbbās, رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That single report reframes the whole subject. The first idolatry on earth did not begin with people worshipping stones they thought were gods. It began with people attached to men they knew to be righteous.

And the Arabs inherited precisely that. The sharḥ identifies the three great idols of al-Najm 53:19–20 — اللَّات was a grave, in which they believed that man's spirit dwelt, and they set an idol over it; العُزَّى was a tree; and مَنَاة was a rock — and at the tree, and at Manāt, there had been righteous men who used to worship there. So “stones and trees” in the third foundation are not a separate, cruder category after all. They are graves and shrines of the righteous, in their first form.

الإِذْنُ وَالرِّضَا Permission, and Good Pleasure

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The affirmed intercession has conditions, and the sharḥ reduces them to two that the Qur’ān repeats: الإِذْن — that the intercessor be permitted to intercede — and الرِّضَا — that Allah be pleased with the intercessor and with the one interceded for.

  • إِلَّا مِنْ بَعْدِ أَنْ يَأْذَنَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَرْضَى (al-Najm 53:26) — even the angels' intercession avails nothing until He permits.
  • وَلَا يَشْفَعُونَ إِلَّا لِمَنِ ارْتَضَى (al-Anbiyāʾ 21:28) — and only for one with whom He is pleased.
  • إِلَّا مَنْ شَهِدَ بِالْحَقِّ وَهُمْ يَعْلَمُونَ (al-Zukhruf 43:86) — the one who bore witness to the truth, knowingly.

There is also a distinction worth holding onto. Asking intercession of someone living and present — in this world, or on the plains of the Resurrection — the texts permit; this is the whole subject of the famous ḥadīth of the intercession, where the people go to Ādam, then to Nūḥ, and onward. But the dead is not in a place of action or of petition. So the request is turned to Allah.

أَسْعَدُ النَّاسِ بِشَفَاعَتِي يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ خَالِصًا مِنْ قَلْبِهِ

"The most fortunate of people with my intercession on the Day of Resurrection is the one who said lā ilāha illā Allāh sincerely from his heart."

رَوَاهُ البُخَارِيُّ · al-Bukhārī, from Abū Hurayrah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, who asked him ﷺ who that would be

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Note the condition inside the answer: خَالِصًا مِنْ قَلْبِهِ — sincerely, from his heart. The Prophet's own intercession is for the people of tawḥīd. So the very thing people commit shirk hoping to obtain is the thing shirk disqualifies them from.

(A fine point the commentators add, such as al-ʿAynī in ʿUmdat al-Qārī: أَسْعَد here carries the sense of سَعِيد — the fortunate one — rather than a comparison, since there is no fortune at all on the other side of the line.

فَكَيْفَ تَطْلُبُهَا؟ So How Do You Ask for It?

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is where the lesson becomes practice. The believer does hope for the intercession of the Prophet ﷺ, of the angels, of the righteous, of the scholars. And he asks Allah to grant it. The sharḥ even gives the words:

اللَّهُمَّ شَفِّعْ فِيَّ رَسُولَكَ ﷺ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ، اللَّهُمَّ شَفِّعْ فِيَّ مَلَائِكَتَكَ، اللَّهُمَّ شَفِّعْ فِيَّ عِبَادَكَ الصَّالِحِينَ

“O Allah, grant that Your Messenger ﷺ intercede for me on the Day of Resurrection; O Allah, grant that Your angels intercede for me; O Allah, grant that Your righteous servants intercede for me.”

Read who is being addressed in every one of those sentences. The intercessor is named — and Allah is the One asked.

And now the reasoning that settles the matter. Intercession is a request. When you say “I seek your intercession,” you are saying: I ask you to supplicate, I ask you to raise my need. But a request of that kind, directed at one who cannot be asked, is supplication — and supplication is worship. So:

intercession = a request · a request of this kind = duʿāʾ · duʿāʾ directed to other than Allah = major shirk

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
الشَّفَاعَةُ كُلُّهَا شِرْكٌ، فَلَا شَفَاعَةَ أَصْلًا. All intercession is shirk; there is no intercession at all.
الشَّفَاعَةُ المُثْبَتَةُ تُطْلَبُ مِنَ اللهِ لَا مِنَ الشَّافِعِ. The affirmed intercession is asked of Allah, not of the intercessor.
قَوْلُهُمْ «مَا نَعْبُدُهُمْ إِلَّا لِيُقَرِّبُونَا» عُذْرٌ مَقْبُولٌ. Their saying "we worship them only to bring us nearer" is an accepted excuse.
الشَّافِعُ مُكَرَّمٌ بِالشَّفَاعَةِ، وَلَيْسَ لَهُ سُلْطَانٌ عَلَى اللهِ. The intercessor is honoured with permission to intercede; he holds no power over Allah.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ

"Who is there that can intercede with Him except by His permission?" Memorise the exception, not only the denial. Intercession is real — and it belongs to Allah to grant. So ask Him for it, and never ask it of anyone else.
