القِيَامَةُ الكُبْرَى وَنَصْبُ المَوَازِين

The Greater Resurrection, and the Setting Up of the Scales

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Name the three abodes and the two Resurrections.
  • Give the five things by which the البَعْث is established.
  • Reproduce the rational argument for the recompense that al-Fawzān draws from the Qurʾān.
  • Say what is weighed, and what the heaviness is reckoned by.
٢ · القِيَامَةُ الكُبْرَى وَمَا يَجْرِي فِيهَا Second — The Greater Resurrection, and What Occurs in It

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

إِلَى أَنْ تَقُومَ القِيَامَةُ الكُبْرَى، فَتُعَادَ الأَرْوَاحُ إِلَى الأَجْسَادِ، وَتَقُومَ القِيَامَةُ الَّتِي أَخْبَرَ اللهُ بِهَا فِي كِتَابِهِ وَعَلَى لِسَانِ رَسُولِهِ وَأَجْمَعَ عَلَيْهَا المُسْلِمُونَ؛ فَيَقُومُ النَّاسُ مِنْ قُبُورِهِمْ لِرَبِّ العَالَمِينَ حُفَاةً عُرَاةً غُرْلًا.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first sets the frame, and it is the map for everything that follows: the abodes are three — دَارُ الدُّنْيَا, دَارُ البَرْزَخ, and الدَّارُ الآخِرَة — and each of these three abodes has rulings particular to it, and events that occur in it. And the Shaykh has spoken on what occurs in the abode of al-barzakh (Lesson 36); and here he begins to speak on what occurs in the Last Abode.

And then a distinction the student will not have met: the Resurrection is of two kinds —

قِيَامَةٌ صُغْرَىand it is death. This Resurrection comes upon every human being individually — by the going out of his soul and the cutting off of his striving
قِيَامَةٌ كُبْرَىthis one comes upon all the people together and takes them in a single seizing

And it was named a قِيَامَة — a standing — because of the people's standing up from their graves for the Lord of the worlds. The name is not about the end of the world; it is about what the dead do.

And this is why he said فَتُعَادَ الأَرْوَاحُ إِلَى الأَجْسَادِ — and that is when Isrāfīl blows in the Horn:

وَنُفِخَ فِي الصُّورِ فَإِذَا هُمْ مِنَ الْأَجْدَاثِ إِلَى رَبِّهِمْ يَنْسِلُونَ قَالُوا يَا وَيْلَنَا مَنْ بَعَثَنَا مِنْ مَرْقَدِنَا

"And the Horn will be blown, and at once they are hastening from the graves to their Lord. They will say: woe to us — who has raised us from our resting-place?"

Sūrat Yā-Sīn 36:51–52 · سُورَةُ يَس

وَنُفِخَ فِي الصُّورِ فَصَعِقَ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَنْ فِي الْأَرْضِ إِلَّا مَنْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ ثُمَّ نُفِخَ فِيهِ أُخْرَى فَإِذَا هُمْ قِيَامٌ يَنْظُرُونَ

"And the Horn will be blown, and all in the heavens and all in the earth will fall senseless — except whom Allah wills. Then it will be blown again, and at once they are standing, looking on."

Sūrat al-Zumar 39:68 · سُورَةُ الزُّمَر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الأَرْوَاح is the plural of رُوح — and it is that by which man and other possessors of souls live; and none knows its reality but Allah:

وَيَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ الرُّوحِ قُلِ الرُّوحُ مِنْ أَمْرِ رَبِّي وَمَا أُوتِيتُمْ مِنَ الْعِلْمِ إِلَّا قَلِيلًا

"And they ask you about the soul. Say: the soul is of the affair of my Lord — and you have not been given of knowledge but a little."

Sūrat al-Isrāʾ 17:85 · سُورَةُ الإِسْرَاء

There is a lesson of method in that pairing. The creed requires you to believe that the souls are returned to the bodies — and tells you in the same breath that you do not know what a soul is. Which is the discipline of the whole book in a new place: affirm what was reported, and do not pretend to the knowledge that would answer «how».

أَدِلَّةُ البَعْث The Proofs of the Resurrection

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author's words وَتَقُومَ القِيَامَةُ الَّتِي أَخْبَرَ اللهُ بِهَا فِي كِتَابِهِ وَعَلَى لِسَانِ رَسُولِهِ وَأَجْمَعَ عَلَيْهَا المُسْلِمُونَ are, al-Fawzān says, an indication of the proofs of the resurrection, and that it is established by five things:

the Book · the Sunnah · the consensus of the Muslims · reason · and the sound innate natures

For Allah informed of it in His Book, established the proof for it, and refuted those who deny the resurrection — in most of the sūrahs of the Qurʾān. And since our Prophet Muḥammad ﷺ is the seal of the prophets, he set out the details of the Hereafter with a clarity not found in many of the books of the prophets.

Note what that last observation implies about the role of this part of the book. The detail is itself a mark of the finality of his prophethood — the last messenger describes the end most fully, because there will be no further messenger to add to it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the rational argument, which al-Fawzān lays out as the Qurʾān does — for Allah alerted the intellects to it in many places of the Qurʾān, where He reminded them:

— that it does not befit His wisdom and His praiseworthiness that He leave people aimless, or create them in vain, neither commanded nor forbidden, neither rewarded nor punished; and that the doer of good should be like the doer of evil, or that He make the Muslims like the criminals.

— For some of those who do good die before being recompensed for their good, and some of the criminals die before being recompensed for their crime. So there must necessarily be an abode in which each of the two is recompensed.

That is a complete argument, and it is worth seeing that it does not start from scripture. It starts from an observation anyone can make — that the ledger of this world does not balance — and reasons from Allah's wisdom and justice to the necessity of another. The recompense is established by reason and occurs in the Sharīʿah.

And the ruling: and the denier of the resurrection is a disbeliever — as He said:

زَعَمَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا أَنْ لَنْ يُبْعَثُوا قُلْ بَلَى وَرَبِّي لَتُبْعَثُنَّ ثُمَّ لَتُنَبَّؤُنَّ بِمَا عَمِلْتُمْ وَذَلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

"Those who disbelieve claim that they will not be raised. Say: yes, by my Lord, you will surely be raised, and then you will surely be informed of what you did — and that is easy for Allah."

Sūrat al-Taghābun 64:7 · سُورَةُ التَّغَابُن

حُفَاةً عُرَاةً غُرْلًا "Barefoot, Naked, Uncircumcised"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

حُفَاةً — the plural of حَافٍ, and he is the one upon whose foot there is neither sandal nor shoe. عُرَاةً — the plural of عَارٍ, and he is the one upon whom there is no clothing. غُرْلًا — the plural of أَغْرَل, and he is the uncircumcised one.

And these three descriptions are what they will be in when they stand up from their graves; and this is established in the authentic from the Prophet ﷺ.

إِنَّكُمْ تُحْشَرُونَ إِلَى اللهِ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ حُفَاةً عُرَاةً غُرْلًا

"You will be gathered to Allah on the Day of Rising barefoot, naked, uncircumcised."

فِي الصَّحِيحَيْنِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim, from ʿĀʾishah رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهَا

The third word is the one that makes the point. Barefoot and naked a man can imagine; uncircumcised returns the body to the state it was in on the day it was born — undoing even the one mark a person did not choose and could not remove. Nothing acquired comes with you.

٣ · دُنُوُّ الشَّمْسِ · وَنَصْبُ المَوَازِين Third — The Sun Draws Near, and the Scales Are Set Up

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَتَدْنُو مِنْهُمُ الشَّمْسُ، وَيُلْجِمُهُمُ العَرَقُ. وَتُنْصَبُ المَوَازِينُ، فَتُوزَنُ بِهَا أَعْمَالُ العِبَادِ: ﴿فَمَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ ۝ وَمَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ خَسِرُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ فِي جَهَنَّمَ خَالِدُونَ﴾.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَتَدْنُو مِنْهُمُ الشَّمْسُ — that is: it comes close to their heads; as Muslim reported from المِقْدَادِ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, who said: I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: «when it is the Day of Rising the sun will be brought near to the servants, until it is at the measure of a mile or two».

وَيُلْجِمُهُمُ العَرَقُ — that is: it reaches their mouths, so that it becomes like a bridle, preventing them from speech; and that is the result of the sun's drawing near to them. And the qualification: and that is with respect to most of the creation; and there are excepted from it the prophets, and whomever Allah wills.

فَمَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ وَمَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَئِكَ الَّذِينَ خَسِرُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ فِي جَهَنَّمَ خَالِدُونَ

"So whoever's scales are heavy — those are the successful. And whoever's scales are light — those are the ones who have lost themselves; in Hell they abide."

Sūrat al-Muʾminūn 23:102–103 · سُورَةُ المُؤْمِنُون

الشَّرْح · the explanation

المَوَازِين is the plural of مِيزَان — and it is that by which the good deeds and the evil deeds are weighed; and it is a real scale, having a tongue and two pans. And it is of the matters of the Hereafter: we believe in it as it came, and we do not enquire into its manner except in the light of what has come in the texts.

And the wisdom in the weighing of the deeds is the making apparent of their measures, so that the recompense may be according to them.

فَمَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ — that is: his good deeds outweighed his evil deeds — فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ, the winners, and those saved from the Fire, deserving of entering the Garden. وَمَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ — that is: his evil deeds outweighed his good deeds — خَسِرُوا أَنْفُسَهُمْ, they were ruined and came to the Fire.

And then a point that gathers the apparently conflicting texts, which is the most useful thing in the section: the totality of the texts has conveyed that the doer is weighed, and the deed is weighed, and the pages are weighed — and there is no conflict between them: all of it is weighed. But the reckoning of heaviness and lightness is by the deed itself, not by the person of the doer, nor by the page. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

Three sets of texts, three things weighed, and one of them is what the verdict turns on. That is the same move as al-ḥaqqu lā yatanāqaḍ in Lesson 33: the texts were never in competition, and the resolution was to find what each was reporting.

تَأْوِيلُ المُعْتَزِلَةِ لِلْمِيزَان The Muʿtazilah’s Reinterpretation of the Scales

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Muʿtazilah reinterpreted the texts concerning that, claiming that what is meant by the weighing and the Scales is justice — and that there is no real weighing and no real scale. And this is a corrupt reinterpretation, contradicting the texts and the consensus of the predecessors of the Ummah and its imāms.

And al-Fawzān answers with al-Shawkānī, whose words are worth having in full because they name the method rather than only the error:

— and the utmost of what they clung to is mere rational improbabilities, and there is no proof in that against anyone. So if their intellects did not accept this, then it was accepted by the intellects of a people stronger than their intellects — of the Ṣaḥābah, the Tābiʿūn and those who followed them — until the innovations came like a dark night, and each said whatever he wished, and they left the Sharīʿah behind their backs.

الاسْتِبْعَادَاتُ العَقْلِيَّة — rational improbabilities. That is the name for the whole class of argument, and the student has met it four times now: the sky cannot carry Him, so reinterpret; mercy cannot speak, so reinterpret; a foot cannot be placed, so reinterpret; a deed cannot be weighed, so reinterpret. In each case the premise is that what the speaker cannot picture cannot have happened.

And al-Shawkānī's reply is not that reason is worthless, but that this particular reason is not strong enough to overturn that testimony — the Companions found no difficulty here, and they were not the weaker minds. And the matters of the Hereafter are not of what the intellects grasp. وَاللهُ أَعْلَم.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
القِيَامَةُ الكُبْرَى تَأْخُذُ النَّاسَ جَمِيعًا أَخْذَةً وَاحِدَةً. The Greater Resurrection takes all people in a single seizing.
يُعْلَمُ حَقِيقَةُ الرُّوحِ بِالعَقْلِ. The reality of the soul is known by the intellect.
المِيزَانُ مِيزَانٌ حَقِيقِيٌّ لَهُ لِسَانٌ وَكَفَّتَانِ. The Scale is a real scale with a tongue and two pans.
مُنْكِرُ البَعْثِ كَافِرٌ. The denier of the resurrection is a disbeliever.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَمَنْ ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ

Learn it with al-Fawzān's clause: the doer is weighed, and the pages, and the deeds — but the heaviness is reckoned by the deed. Nothing about who you were will tip the pan; only what you actually did.

al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah · sharḥ of Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān · in our own words
