الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ الثَّالِثُ وَالأَرْبَعُونYear 5

المَشِيئَةُ وَالخَلْقُ · وَلَا تَعَارُضَ بَيْنَ القَدَرِ وَالشَّرْع

The Volition and the Creating — and No Conflict with the Law

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say what النَّافِذَة and الشَّامِلَة mean in the second degree.
  • Name Ibn Taymiyyah’s three sects of those who go astray in qadar, and the error of each.
  • State the rule on the relation between الإِرَادَة and المَحَبَّة, with an example each way.
  • Say which of the two irādahs the rule concerns.
الدَّرَجَةُ الثَّانِيَة · المَشِيئَةُ النَّافِذَةُ وَالقُدْرَةُ الشَّامِلَة The Second Degree — the Effective Volition and the All-Embracing Power

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَأَمَّا الدَّرَجَةُ الثَّانِيَةُ: فَهِيَ مَشِيئَةُ اللهِ النَّافِذَةُ وَقُدْرَتُهُ الشَّامِلَةُ؛ وَهُوَ الإِيمَانُ بِأَنَّ مَا شَاءَ اللهُ كَانَ، وَمَا لَمْ يَشَأْ لَمْ يَكُنْ؛ وَأَنَّهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الأَرْضِ مِنْ حَرَكَةٍ وَلَا سُكُونٍ إِلَّا بِمَشِيئَةِ اللهِ سُبْحَانَهُ، لَا يَكُونُ فِي مُلْكِهِ مَا لَا يُرِيدُ؛ وَأَنَّهُ سُبْحَانَهُ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ مِنَ المَوْجُودَاتِ وَالمَعْدُومَاتِ؛ فَمَا مِنْ مَخْلُوقٍ فِي الأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ إِلَّا اللهُ خَالِقُهُ سُبْحَانَهُ، لَا خَالِقَ غَيْرُهُ، وَلَا رَبَّ سِوَاهُ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān first notes the numbering, with a footnote that saves the reader confusion: this is a statement of the third level and the fourth level of the levels of the decree — and the author reckoned it «the second» because he made the knowledge and the writing a single degree.

النَّافِذَة — is the effective one, which nothing turns back; الشَّامِلَة — is the general one, over every thing — of the existent and the non-existent.

مَا شَاءَ اللهُ كَانَ — that is: it came to be; وَمَا لَمْ يَشَأْ لَمْ يَكُنْ — that is: it did not come to be. مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ… مِنْ حَرَكَةٍ وَلَا سُكُونٍ إِلَّا بِمَشِيئَةِ اللهِ — that is: nothing of that occurs but that Allah willed it. لَا يَكُونُ فِي مُلْكِهِ مَا لَا يُرِيدُ — its occurring, by way of the universal decree.

Note the parenthesis al-Fawzān adds there — كَوْنًا وَقَدَرًا, by way of the universal decree. He is marking which of the two irādahs the clause is about, because the whole of the rest of this lesson turns on that distinction. «Nothing occurs in His dominion that He does not will» is about the decreeing will, not about His approving of it.

فَمَا مِنْ مَخْلُوقٍ فِي الأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي السَّمَاءِ إِلَّا اللهُ خَالِقُهُ — and in this is an indication of the fourth level, which is the level of the creating and the bringing into being: so everything besides Allah is created, and all the actions — their good and their evil — issue from His creating them and His originating them — لَا خَالِقَ غَيْرُهُ وَلَا رَبَّ سِوَاهُ.

لَا تَعَارُضَ بَيْنَ القَدَرِ وَالشَّرْع No Conflict between the Decree and the Law

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمَعَ ذَلِكَ فَقَدْ أَمَرَ العِبَادَ بِطَاعَتِهِ وَطَاعَةِ رُسُلِهِ، وَنَهَاهُمْ عَنْ مَعْصِيَتِهِ. وَهُوَ سُبْحَانَهُ يُحِبُّ المُتَّقِينَ وَالمُحْسِنِينَ وَالمُقْسِطِينَ، وَيَرْضَى عَنِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ، وَلَا يُحِبُّ الكَافِرِينَ، وَلَا يَرْضَى عَنِ القَوْمِ الفَاسِقِينَ، وَلَا يَأْمُرُ بِالفَحْشَاءِ، وَلَا يَرْضَى لِعِبَادِهِ الكُفْرَ، وَلَا يُحِبُّ الفَسَادَ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

When the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ had established the decree with its four levels — the knowledge, the writing, the volition and will, and the creating and bringing into being — and that nothing happens but Allah knew it, wrote it, willed it, intended it and brought it into being — he made clear here that there is no conflict between that and His having commanded the servants with His obedience and forbidden them His disobedience; nor between His decreeing the occurrence of the sin and His hating it.

And there is in that no conflict between His Law and His decree — as some of the misguided suppose, who set the Law and the decree against each other.

ثَلَاثُ فِرَقٍ خَاضَتْ فِي القَدَر Three Sects That Plunged into the Question of Qadar

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And here al-Fawzān quotes the author's own words from another of his works — the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ says on this subject in his treatise «التَّدْمُرِيَّة»: and the people of misguidance who plunged into the question of qadar divided into three sects: مَجُوسِيَّة, مُشْرِكِيَّة, and إِبْلِيسِيَّة.

١ المَجُوسِيَّةwho belied the decree of Allah, even while they believed in His command and His prohibition. Their extremists denied the knowledge and the Book; and their moderates denied the generality of His volition, His creating and His power. And these are the Muʿtazilah and whoever agreed with them.
٢ المُشْرِكِيَّةwho acknowledged the decree and the measuring, and denied the command and the prohibition. — So whoever argues for the nullifying of the command and the prohibition is of these. And this has become widespread among those of the ṣūfiyyah who lay claim to «the Reality».
٣ الإِبْلِيسِيَّةwho acknowledged both matters — but made this a contradiction on the part of the Lord سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى, and impugned His wisdom and His justice — as is related of Iblīs, the foremost of them, as the authors of the doctrinal works transmitted, and as is transmitted of the People of the Book.

That classification is worth more than a list of names, because it covers every available move. There are only three things to do with two premises that look incompatible — deny the first, deny the second, or accept both and call the author of them inconsistent. The Majūsiyyah took the first, the Mushrikiyyah the second, and the Iblīsiyyah the third — which is why they are named after Iblīs rather than after a scholar.

And the second row is the one with a living application. «Whoever argues for the nullifying of the command and the prohibition is of these» — that is, anyone who answers a rebuke with it was decreed. And the Qurʾān records the words:

سَيَقُولُ الَّذِينَ أَشْرَكُوا لَوْ شَاءَ اللَّهُ مَا أَشْرَكْنَا وَلَا آبَاؤُنَا وَلَا حَرَّمْنَا مِنْ شَيْءٍ

"Those who associated others with Allah will say: had Allah willed, we would not have associated, nor our fathers, nor would we have forbidden anything."

Sūrat al-Anʿām 6:148 · سُورَةُ الأَنْعَام

The argument is impeccable as far as it goes, and that is what makes it instructive: everything in it is true. Had Allah willed, they would not have associated. The error is not in the premise but in what it is being used for — a true statement about the decree, produced in order to be excused from the Law. And al-Fawzān notes where the move survives: among those of the ṣūfiyyah who claim to have passed beyond the commands to «the Reality».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then the author's own summary, which al-Fawzān quotes to the end: and the point is: that this is what the people of misguidance say falsely. As for the people of guidance and success, they believe in THIS and in THAT — and they believe that Allah is the Creator of everything, and its Lord and its Owner; and that what He willed came to be and what He did not will did not come to be; and that He is Able over everything; and that He has encompassed everything in knowledge; and that He has counted everything in a clear register.

يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهَذَا وَهَذَا — they believe in this and in that. That is the whole of the answer, and it is the method of Lesson 30 in its last application: each sect kept one half of the revelation and had to discard the other, and Ahl al-Sunnah hold both.

لَا تَلَازُمَ بَيْنَ الإِرَادَةِ وَالمَحَبَّة No Necessary Link between the Will and the Love

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَهُوَ سُبْحَانَهُ يُحِبُّ المُتَّقِينَ وَالمُحْسِنِينَ وَالمُقْسِطِينَ — that is: He loves whoever is characterised by the praiseworthy qualities, such as taqwā, iḥsān and justice; وَيَرْضَى عَنِ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ, as He informed in many āyāt, for what they were characterised by of faith and righteous action.

وَلَا يُحِبُّ الكَافِرِينَ، وَلَا يَرْضَى عَنِ القَوْمِ الفَاسِقِينَ — that is: He is not pleased with whoever is characterised by the qualities He hates, such as disbelief and depravity and the rest of the blameworthy qualities. وَلَا يَأْمُرُ بِالفَحْشَاءِ — and it is what has reached the furthest point of ugliness, of statements and of actions. وَلَا يَرْضَى لِعِبَادِهِ الكُفْرَ وَلَا يُحِبُّ الفَسَادَ — for He hates them both, and for what is in them both of harm to the servants and to the lands.

And the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ intends by this speech the refutation of whoever claimed that between the will and the love there is a necessary link — so that if Allah willed a thing He has loved it, and if He decreed a thing He has loved it.

And this is a false statement. And the true statement is: that there is no necessary link between the will and the love, or between the volition and the love — I mean the universal will and volition.

That qualification is the key to the whole question, and it is why al-Fawzān flagged كَوْنًا وَقَدَرًا earlier. The student met the two irādahs in Lesson 11: the الإِرَادَةُ الكَوْنِيَّة, which is the decreeing will and is not tied to love; and the الإِرَادَةُ الشَّرْعِيَّة, which is the legislative will and is. Collapse the two into one word and the question becomes unanswerable; keep them apart and it dissolves.

قَدْ يَشَاءُ مَا لَا يُحِبُّهHe may will what He does not love — example: His volition for the existence of Iblīs, and his continuing
وَقَدْ يُحِبُّ مَا لَا يَشَاءُ وُجُودَهand He may love what He does not will the existence of — example: the acts of obedience of the disbelievers, which He did not will to come from them — and had He willed it, it would have existed.

Two examples, one in each direction, and together they settle it. Iblīs exists and is not loved; the disbeliever's obedience is loved and does not exist. So the two things come apart in both directions, which is as complete a refutation of a claimed necessary link as can be given.

And notice what the second example quietly affirms: had He willed the disbeliever's obedience, it would have existed. The all-embracing power of the second degree is not softened in order to make room for the love — both are stated at full strength, which is what «they believe in this and in that» means in practice.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

يُؤْمِنُونَ بِهَذَا وَهَذَا

Ibn Taymiyyah's own four words for the people of guidance, after naming the three sects who each kept one half. Learn them as the test you apply to your own reasoning: if holding two established things together feels impossible, the fault is in the holding — not in one of the two.

al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah of Shaykh al-Islām Ibn Taymiyyah · sharḥ of Shaykh Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
