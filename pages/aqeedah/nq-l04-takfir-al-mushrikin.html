النَّاقِضُ الثَّالِث · عَدَمُ تَكْفِيرِ المُشْرِكِين

The Third Nullifier — Not Declaring the Mushrikīn Disbelievers

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the three acts this clause of the matn names, and in what order they rise.
  • Explain why it is kufr in terms of تَكْذِيب — belying a report — and not in terms of severity.
  • Show from Muslim’s ḥadīth of Ṭāriq ibn Ashyam that this is the نَفْي half of the kalimah, not an addition to it.
  • Give Ibn Bāz’s argument from سُورَةُ الفَاتِحَة — the creed a Muslim asks for in every rakʿah.
  • State precisely where doubt is kufr and where doubt is اجْتِهَاد, using Ibn Bāz’s own worked example.
  • Say who may carry out a sentence, and what the ʿāmmī does with a matter that is obscure to him.

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ٩٨–١٠٧
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 98–107, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الثَّالِث: مَنْ لَمْ يُكَفِّرِ المُشْرِكِينَ، أَوْ شَكَّ فِي كُفْرِهِمْ، أَوْ صَحَّحَ مَذْهَبَهُمْ — كَفَرَ إِجْمَاعًا.

Three acts, and they rise in subtlety: withholding the ruling, then wavering over it, then declaring their way sound.

Of the ten, this is the clause most often quoted with its restraints stripped off — and the sharḥ does the opposite. Of its ten folios, the greater part is spent on إِقَامَةُ الحُجَّة, on what a layman may and may not say, on the difference between doubting what Allah has settled and doubting what the scholars dispute, and on whose hand carries out a sentence. This lesson is weighted the same way the source is.

هَذِهِ الدَّارُ جَامِعَةٌ Where Ibn Bāz Begins

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He does not open with a ruling. He opens with a fact about the world:

«This abode gathers the wholesome and the foul, the disbeliever and the Muslim — and most of its people are upon disbelief and misguidance.»

And he lays four proofs under it: يُوسُف ١٢:١٠٣ — and most of mankind, however much you should crave it, will not believe; الأَنْعَام ٦:١١٦ — and if you obey most of those upon the earth they will lead you away from the path of Allah; سَبَأ ٣٤:١٣ — and few of My slaves are grateful; and the refrain that closes story after story in سُورَةُ الشُّعَرَاء — «وَمَا كَانَ أَكْثَرُهُمْ مُؤْمِنِينَ».

Why open there? Because this nullifier only has teeth if the category it speaks about is populated. A man who has quietly concluded that there are no mushrikūn left in the world never has to break this clause — he has dissolved it. So the sharḥ settles first that the category is not empty, and is not even small.

وَمَا أَكْثَرُ النَّاسِ وَلَوْ حَرَصْتَ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ

And most of mankind, however much you crave it, will not believe.

سُورَةُ يُوسُف · 12:103

مَا يَدْخُلُ فِي التَّوْحِيد The Clause Is Inside the Definition

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The objection that writes itself is: why should my view of other people be a condition of my own tawḥīd? Ibn Bāz answers it by defining tawḥīd, and the answer is in the definition:

«Tawḥīd of Allah is singling Him out for worship — out of belief, out of truthfulness, and out of action, not mere speech — together with holding that the worship of other than Him is false, and that the worshippers of other than Him are mushrikūn, and together with barāʾah from them.»

So it is not a sixth item bolted onto five. The ruling on العِبَادَة and the ruling on العَابِد are two faces of one belief, and a man who affirms the first while refusing the second has not finished a sentence.

His proof is Ibrāhīm, and the wording repays attention — the people come before the objects, in both verses.

قَدْ كَانَتْ لَكُمْ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ فِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَالَّذِينَ مَعَهُ إِذْ قَالُوا لِقَوْمِهِمْ إِنَّا بُرَآءُ مِنكُمْ وَمِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ

There has already been for you an excellent example in Ibrāhīm and those with him, when they said to their people: indeed we are free of you, and of whatever you worship besides Allah.

سُورَةُ المُمْتَحَنَة · 60:4

إِنَّا بُرَآءُ مِنكُمْ — free of you, and only then of what you worship. The same order in Ibrāhīm’s words to his father and his people: إِنَّنِي بَرَاءٌ مِّمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ * إِلَّا الَّذِي فَطَرَنِي — الزُّخْرُف ٤٣:٢٦–٢٧. The disavowal that the Qurʾān holds up as the excellent example is addressed to persons first.

لِمَاذَا كَانَ كُفْرًا Because It Is Takdhīb, Not Harshness

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Here is the sentence the whole clause turns on:

«And the one who knows the disbeliever and the falsehood he is upon, and then does not declare him a disbeliever, or doubts his disbelief — the meaning of that is that he is belying Allah and His Messenger, not believing in what Allah has ruled upon them of disbelief… so they are disbelievers by the text of the Qurʾān and the text of the Sunnah. So it is obligatory upon the accountable from among the Muslims to hold as a belief their disbelief and their misguidance.»

Read what that does to the question. The clause is not asking you to issue a ruling; the ruling has been issued, and it was not issued by you. What is asked of you is الاعْتِقَاد — to hold as true a report that has already come. Refusing is therefore not mercy. It is disbelieving a report, and the softness of the motive does not change what the act is.

Which is also why this lesson is not about naming people. It is about believing Allah about a category He has already described. Everything in the restraints section below follows from keeping those two things apart.

حَقِيقَةُ الشَّكّ What Doubt Is

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The sharḥ quotes the ʿallāmah سُلَيْمَانُ بْنُ سَحْمَان defining it, because the word is used loosely:

«The Qurʾān has indicated that doubt in the foundations of the religion is disbelief, and doubt is wavering between two things — like one who is decisive about neither the truthfulness of the Messenger nor his lying; who is decisive about neither the occurrence of the Resurrection nor its non-occurrence; who holds neither the obligation of the prayer nor its non-obligation; who holds neither the prohibition of zinā nor its non-prohibition.»

«And know that not every error and ijtihād and ignorance is forgiven its possessor — for Allah has informed us of the ignorance of many of the disbelievers while expressly declaring them disbelievers, and described the Christians as ignorant, though no Muslim doubts their disbelief.»

So doubt here is not the ordinary uncertainty of a student who has not yet studied a question. It is suspension where the texts have not left the matter open — and he is making the point that ignorance, as such, is not automatically a covering.

Ibn Saḥmān also answers the category objection, and it is worth having: declaring the disbeliever a disbeliever is from the أُصُول, not the فُرُوع which may be hidden from many of the creation — so ignorance of it is not accommodated. And in the same place he explains why the matn lists ten: some of the people of knowledge counted the nullifiers at close to four hundred — but these ten are «of what the scholars have agreed upon».

كَلِمَةُ الإِخْلَاص This Is the لَا of لَا إِلَهَ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz grounds the clause in a ḥadīth that makes the point structurally, and he quotes the narration that interprets the kalimah with it:

مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَكَفَرَ بِمَا يُعْبَدُ مِنْ دُونِ اللهِ، حَرُمَ مَالُهُ وَدَمُهُ، وَحِسَابُهُ عَلَى اللهِ

Whoever says “there is no deity except Allah” and disbelieves in what is worshipped besides Allah — his property and his blood become inviolable, and his reckoning is with Allah.

رَوَاهُ مُسْلِمٌ فِي صَحِيحِهِ · from طَارِقُ بْنُ أَشْيَمَ الأَشْجَعِيّ — and in another narration of Muslim: مَنْ وَحَّدَ اللهَ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two conditions, not one. The Prophet ﷺ made a man’s blood and property inviolable upon an affirmation and a disbelief — and the second narration, مَنْ وَحَّدَ اللهَ, shows that the second clause is the Prophet’s own gloss on the first. Ibn Bāz draws it out: «whoever singled Allah out, devoted worship to Him alone and disbelieved in the ṭāghūt — his property and blood are inviolable, because he is a Muslim in truth; but whoever did not disbelieve in the ṭāghūt and did not single Allah out, this one is not a Muslim.»

And Ibn Saḥmān notes the grammar of it: «وَكَفَرَ بِمَا يُعْبَدُ مِنْ دُونِ اللهِ» is تَأْكِيدٌ لِلنَّفْي — a reinforcement of the negation half of the kalimah. Which gives the sentence the whole lesson can be folded into:

Declaring the mushrikīn to be disbelievers is not an addition to tawḥīd. It is the لَا of لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ. «So its meaning is not completed except by declaring a disbeliever whoever made for Allah a partner in his worship.»

فِي كُلِّ رَكْعَة The Creed You Ask For Seventeen Times a Day

الشَّرْح · the explanation

This is the most striking argument on the ten folios, and it is not a ruling at all — it is a reading of سُورَةُ الفَاتِحَة:

«Allah commanded the Muslim to ask Him every day, in every prayer, and in every rakʿah, that He guide him to the straight path — and that He keep him from the path of المَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ, and they are the Jews and those like them, who know that they are upon falsehood and persist upon it; and keep him from the path of الضَّالِّينَ, who worship without knowledge and claim that they are upon a guidance while they are upon a misguidance… and all of that so that the Muslim may know with certain knowledge that every religion other than Islam is false.»

اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ المُسْتَقِيمَ * صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ المَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ

Guide us to the straight path — the path of those upon whom You have bestowed favour, not of those who earned anger, nor of those who went astray.

سُورَةُ الفَاتِحَة · 1:6–7

Notice what that does to the argument that this clause is a hard extra. It is not an extra; it is in the opening sūrah, and a Muslim is made to ask for it before every single thing he asks for. A man cannot recite al-Fātiḥah and hold at the same time that all paths arrive — in that very rakʿah he asks to be kept off two of them by name. The two are distinguished, too, and the distinction is instructive: one earned anger because it knew; the other went astray because it worshipped without knowledge.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Two consequences Ibn Bāz draws in the same place:

One — the disbelief is not for want of monotheism alone. «As for the Jews and the Christians, when they did not affirm the truthfulness of Muḥammad ﷺ they became by that disbelievers, astray — and if we were to suppose that some of them singled Allah out, they are still astray, disbelievers, by the consensus of the Muslims, for their not believing in Muḥammad ﷺ.»

Two — the taṣdīq is not divisible. His own example: a man who says «I worship Allah alone, and I affirm the truthfulness of Muḥammad in every single thing — except in the prohibition of zinā», making it lawful — «he becomes by this a disbeliever by the consensus of the Muslims»; and likewise one who excepts the prohibition of اللِّوَاط. One exception voids the affirmation, because the affirmation was of a messenger, not of a list.

الضَّوَابِط The Restraints — the Longer Half of the Sharḥ

Everything to this point establishes a belief about a category. None of it licenses a man to go about assigning people to it. The sharḥ draws that line in five places, and they are the reason the clause is safe to teach.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

١ — The clause concerns what Allah has settled, not what the scholars dispute. Ibn Bāz works this out on a live question: the man who abandons the prayer without denying that it is obligatory. He reports the ikhtilāf, states his own position, and then — this is the part that is always dropped — rules on the one who doubts:

«As for one who leaves the prayer and does not deny its obligation, in this there is disagreement among the scholars. Among them are those who declared him a disbeliever — and that is correct… and others of the people of knowledge said he does not disbelieve by that… And whoever doubts the disbelief of this one is not a disbeliever, because it is a place of ijtihād among the people of knowledge; so whoever saw by the legislated evidences that he is a disbeliever, it is obligatory upon him to declare him so; and whoever doubted in that, and the evidences did not become clear to him, and he held that he does not disbelieve with major disbelief but with lesser disbelief — this one is excused in his ijtihād, and does not become a disbeliever by that.»

And the contrast, immediately after: «As for one who denies its obligation and says: the prayer is not obligatory — this one is a disbeliever in the view of all, and whoever doubts his disbelief is a disbeliever.» Likewise one who denies the obligation of the zakāh, or of the fast of Ramaḍān: «because he is belying Allah and His Messenger, and belying the consensus of the Muslims.»

the casethe rulingone who doubts this case
denies the obligation of الصَّلَاة, الزَّكَاة or الصَّوْمa disbeliever in the view of all — it is تَكْذِيب of Allah, His Messenger and the ijmāʿhimself a disbeliever, after the evidence is made clear to him
abandons the prayer without denying its obligationa place of ijtihād — Ibn Bāz: a disbeliever, and that is correct; others: كُفْرٌ دُونَ كُفْرnot a disbeliever — he is مَعْذُورٌ فِي اجْتِهَادِه

That one table is the whole difference between this nullifier and recklessness. «Doubt is disbelief» was never said of doubt as such; it was said of doubt about what Allah has already settled. Where the scholars still differ, the man who suspends is not breaking the clause — he is doing what a student is supposed to do.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

٢ — إِقَامَةُ الحُجَّة means establishing the evidence, each according to his state. He was asked about those who stipulate that the proof be established only by a scholar, so that when a layman falls into something he is told «no, you are not a scholar»:

«Establishing the ḥujjah means: establishing the evidence. This is establishing the ḥujjah — each according to his state.»

«It suffices that the ḥujjah be established by clarifying the truth with its evidences… by the people of knowledge even if they are not mujtahids; rather it suffices that they know the legislated evidences from the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger ﷺ in what they are calling him to.»

So the bar is not a credential. It is that the evidence actually reached the man, in a form he could receive — and that it reached him, which is what «each according to his state» is protecting.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

٣ — What the ʿāmmī may say, and what he does with the rest. Asked whether the layman is kept from this altogether, the answer divides the ground in two:

«The ʿāmmī does not declare anyone a disbeliever except by evidence. The ʿāmmī has no knowledge — poor fellow.»

«But if he has knowledge of a specific thing — like one who denies the prohibition of zinā — then this one disbelieves in the view of the layman and the specialist alike; there is no ambiguity in it. If someone said: zinā is lawful — he disbelieves in the view of everyone. This is something that does not need evidences… or he said: shirk is permitted, it is allowed for people to worship other than Allah — does anyone doubt the disbelief of this one?… or he says: the prayer is not obligatory, whoever wishes prays and whoever wishes does not pray.»

«التَّوَقُّف — suspending judgement — is in the problematic matters, which may be hidden from the layman.»

Note the tone of «مِسْكِين». The restraint is not contempt for the layman; it is protection of him. And note that the division is not by rank but by clarity — the matters named are ones where no evidence-gathering is required, because the denial is of something the religion is known by.

He also names the cases we do affirm, and the list is pointed: أَبُو جَهْل, أَبُو طَالِب, عُتْبَةُ بْنُ رَبِيعَة and شَيْبَةُ بْنُ رَبِيعَة — «with us is the evidence that they died upon disbelief.» Abū Ṭālib is in that list, and he is the Prophet’s own uncle who sheltered him. The ruling does not bend for kinship, and it does not bend for gratitude either.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

٤ — The ḥadīth people reach for, and a forgery people quote. The objection is usually made with a ṣaḥīḥ text:

مَنْ قَالَ لِأَخِيهِ المُسْلِمِ: يَا كَافِرُ، فَقَدْ بَاءَ بِهَا أَحَدُهُمَا

Whoever says to his Muslim brother “O disbeliever” — it returns upon one of the two of them.

Ibn Bāz’s answer turns on a condition already inside the wording: «the meaning is: if he said to him “O disbeliever” and he was not deserving of it. But if he was deserving of it, then it returns upon him — the one it was said to.» The ḥadīth is a warning against a false accusation, not a prohibition of a true description.

And the sharḥ’s footnote stops a forgery that circulates on this exact subject: the wording «مَنْ كَفَّرَ مُسْلِمًا فَقَدْ كَفَرَ» — «whoever declares a Muslim a disbeliever has himself disbelieved» — is graded there as كَذِبٌ لَا أَصْلَ لَه, a lie with no basis for it. It is quoted constantly, and it is not a ḥadīth.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

٥ — Holding a ruling is not carrying out a sentence. This is the restraint that closes the gap everything else leaves open, and the sharḥ puts it in a footnote:

«But the killing of apostates and the like of them is particular to the guardians of the affairs of the Muslims.»

And ابْنُ تَيْمِيَّة: «المُحْتَسِب — the one who enjoins the right — has no right to killing or amputation.»

So the two things this clause is forever confused with are formally separated: a sharʿī description, which a student may be obliged to hold, and the execution of a ḥadd, which belongs to the one in authority and to nobody else. Not even the muḥtasib — a man whose office is correcting wrongs — may take it upon himself.

The sharḥ also records the objection raised against the clause — «this is a matter that chains endlessly; if we carried it through we would declare the Muslims disbelievers» — and Ibn Saḥmān’s answer is two facts rather than a counter-argument: the objector brought no legislated evidence at all, only his own reasoning; and he did not name a single scholar who holds that the disbeliever is not declared a disbeliever. The sharḥ’s own reply to it is considerably sharper than that, and the substance is what is reproduced here.

فَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِن A Verse Read Backwards

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz names a confusion that arrives from the opposite direction — «and among what some of the ignorant fall into is that he says: man is given a choice — if he wishes he disbelieves and if he wishes he submits, because Allah says «فَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِن وَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ»». His answer:

«And this is from the great affliction — we ask Allah for safety — because this matter is a threat, not a giving of choice; and for this reason He said after it: إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ نَارًا أَحَاطَ بِهِمْ سُرَادِقُهَا.»

«Meaning: if he wishes let him believe, and he has the Garden; and if he wishes let him disbelieve, and he has the Fire.» The sentence does not end where the quotation ends — and the half that is cut off is the half that tells you which of the two it was.

وَقُلِ الحَقُّ مِن رَّبِّكُمْ فَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيُؤْمِن وَمَن شَاءَ فَلْيَكْفُرْ إِنَّا أَعْتَدْنَا لِلظَّالِمِينَ نَارًا أَحَاطَ بِهِمْ سُرَادِقُهَا

And say: the truth is from your Lord. So whoever wills — let him believe; and whoever wills — let him disbelieve. Indeed We have prepared for the wrongdoers a Fire whose canopy encloses them.

سُورَةُ الكَهْف · 18:29

الشَّرْح · the explanation

He treats «لَا إِكْرَاهَ فِي الدِّينِ» the same way, and gives both scholarly positions rather than the one that suits him:

the first viewit was before the legislating of jihād; and the person is still obliged to enter Islam
the second viewit concerns أَهْلُ الكِتَاب and the Majūs — they are not compelled once they render the jizyah — and it is therefore not abrogated

«And the intent in every case is that what is meant by it is not the giving of a choice, and it is not meant that there is no harm in his remaining upon his disbelief — this is not said by one who knows what he is saying» — he is still obliged to enter Islam, «so that he may rescue himself from the Fire.»

And there is something in that verse the objection never notices. Read the rest of it: «فَمَن يَكْفُرْ بِالطَّاغُوتِ وَيُؤْمِن بِاللَّهِ فَقَدِ اسْتَمْسَكَ بِالعُرْوَةِ الوُثْقَى» البَقَرَة ٢:٢٥٦ — disbelieve in the ṭāghūt first, then believe in Allah. The verse produced against this clause carries the same two halves, in the same order, as Muslim’s ḥadīth.

مَسْأَلَتَانِ Two Questions He Was Asked

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The munāfiq. Asked whether it may be said to the hypocrite you are a disbeliever: «If you know that he is a disbeliever, then he is a disbeliever — the hypocrites are disbelievers», and he cites المُنَافِقُون ٦٣:٣. «So if you know that he belies Allah and His Messenger, and that he reviles Allah and His Messenger — if you know this of him, you say to him: you are a disbeliever by this, and you denounce it upon him.» The condition is repeated three times in four lines: «إِذَا عَرَفْتَ». Knowledge of the man, not suspicion of him.

ذَلِكَ بِأَنَّهُمْ آمَنُوا ثُمَّ كَفَرُوا فَطُبِعَ عَلَى قُلُوبِهِمْ فَهُمْ لَا يَفْقَهُونَ

That is because they believed, then disbelieved; so their hearts were sealed, and so they do not understand.

سُورَةُ المُنَافِقُون · 63:3

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Does the shahādah shield a man from a nullifier? He was asked about one who does not pray the obligatory prayers, or who mocks the Qurʾān, while he testifies that there is no deity but Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah:

«Yes. If one is found who testifies that there is no deity except Allah and that Muḥammad is the Messenger of Allah, and he comes with an act that necessitates disbelief, it is obligatory that he be declared a disbeliever — because the Muslim disbelieves if he comes with something from the nullifiers of Islam. So the one who testifies… is not protected from a nullifier occurring from him; rather whenever a nullifier is found from him, he disbelieves by it.»

The shahādah is what makes a man a Muslim. It was never issued as an immunity — which is, after all, the premise of the entire matn: a treatise about the ten things that nullify an Islam that was really there.

وَالمَقْصُودُ بِكُلِّ حَال Where the Sharḥ Chooses to End

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Having spent ten folios establishing that most of mankind is not upon guidance, the last thing he does is refuse the sweep:

«And there is no doubt that most of the creation are to the Fire, and the fewer of them to the Garden» — يُوسُف ١٢:١٠٣, الأَنْعَام ٦:١١٦.

«But as for the statement that all the people of the earth are in the Fire — this is false speech. Rather, whoever believed in Allah and the Last Day is from the people of the Garden; and only the one who disbelieved in Allah and opposed His command is from the people of the Fire.»

So the clause closes where it opened — on proportion rather than a verdict about the world. The ruling is about a category Allah described, applied by evidence, with doubt in a disputed question left as ijtihād, with the layman suspending where the matter is obscure to him, and with the sword in nobody’s hand but the ruler’s. Take any one of those away and you no longer have the clause the author wrote.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

مَنْ قَالَ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللهُ وَكَفَرَ بِمَا يُعْبَدُ مِنْ دُونِ اللهِ

Hold the two halves and most of the confusion around this clause falls away. An affirmation and a disbelief — and the second is the Prophet’s own gloss on the first, in the narration «مَنْ وَحَّدَ اللهَ». So this nullifier is not a severity added onto tawḥīd; it is the لَا that tawḥīd begins with. And hold the restraint beside it: مَعْذُورٌ فِي اجْتِهَادِه — where the scholars differ, the one who suspends is not breaking the clause.
