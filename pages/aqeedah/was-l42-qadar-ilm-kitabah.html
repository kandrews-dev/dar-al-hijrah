الإِيمَانُ بِالقَدَرِ · العِلْمُ وَالكِتَابَة

Belief in the Decree — the Knowledge and the Writing

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٤٧–١٥٢
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 147–152.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Define القَدَر and name its four مَرَاتِب.
  • Say why the author treats the knowledge and the writing as one degree.
  • Give both readings of أَوَّلُ مَا خَلَقَ اللهُ القَلَم and the meaning each yields.
  • Name the three kinds of particular decree, each with its proof.
القِسْمُ الخَامِس · الإِيمَانُ بِالقَدَر Part Five — Belief in the Decree

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَتُؤْمِنُ الفِرْقَةُ النَّاجِيَةُ — أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ — بِالقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ. وَالإِيمَانُ بِالقَدَرِ عَلَى دَرَجَتَيْنِ، كُلُّ دَرَجَةٍ تَتَضَمَّنُ شَيْئَيْنِ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

القَدَر — is a maṣdar of قَدَرْتَ الشَّيْءَ, when you encompass its measure; and what is intended by it here is: the attachment of Allah's knowledge to the things that come to be, and His will for them, in eternity before their existence. So there is no event but Allah has decreed it — that is, His knowledge of it preceded, and His will attached to it.

And the obligation: belief in al-qadar is one of the six pillars of faith; and it is belief in the decree, its good and its evil.

al-Fawzān then notices what the author's phrasing implies, and it is a sharp reading: in the Shaykh's saying «and the saved sect — Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah — believe in al-qadar» is an indication that whoever does not believe in al-qadar is not of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah. And this is what the texts require, as in the ḥadīth of Jibrīl, when he asked the Prophet ﷺ about faith, and he said: «faith is that you believe in Allah, His angels, His Books, His Messengers and the Last Day, and that you believe in the decree, its good and its evil» — so he ﷺ made belief in al-qadar the sixth of the pillars of faith; so whoever denies it is not a believer, just as if he had not believed in another of the pillars.

The student met that ḥadīth in full on the Islam 101 track. Here it does the work of establishing that this part of the book is not an advanced topic but one sixth of the minimum.

مَرَاتِبُ القَدَرِ الأَرْبَع The Four Levels of the Decree

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Shaykh mentioned here that belief in al-qadar comprises four levels, which in summary are as follows:

الأُولَى · العِلْمAllah’s eternal knowledge of everything — and of that is His knowledge of the deeds of the servants before they do them
الثَّانِيَة · الكِتَابَةthe writing of that in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ
الثَّالِثَة · المَشِيئَةHis all-embracing volition and His complete power over every event
الرَّابِعَة · الخَلْقAllah’s bringing all created things into being — and that He is the Creator and everything besides Him is created

And a note on the counting, which otherwise confuses the reader: the author speaks of two degrees, each comprising two things — so the four levels are grouped into two pairs. al-Fawzān remarks on this where the author calls the volition «the second»: he reckoned it so because he made the knowledge and the writing a single degree.

Which is not arbitrary. The first pair is about what Allah knew and recorded; the second is about what He willed and brought about. Knowing and writing belong together because neither of them makes anything happen.

الدَّرَجَةُ الأُولَى · العِلْمُ وَالكِتَابَة The First Degree — the Knowledge and the Writing

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

فَالدَّرَجَةُ الأُولَى: الإِيمَانُ بِأَنَّ اللهَ تَعَالَى عَلِيمٌ بِمَا الخَلْقُ عَامِلُونَ، بِعِلْمِهِ القَدِيمِ الَّذِي هُوَ مَوْصُوفٌ بِهِ أَزَلًا وَأَبَدًا، وَعَلِمَ جَمِيعَ أَحْوَالِهِمْ مِنَ الطَّاعَاتِ وَالمَعَاصِي وَالأَرْزَاقِ وَالآجَالِ. ثُمَّ كَتَبَ اللهُ فِي اللَّوْحِ المَحْفُوظِ مَقَادِيرَ الخَلْقِ: فَأَوَّلُ مَا خَلَقَ اللهُ القَلَمَ قَالَ لَهُ: اكْتُبْ. قَالَ: مَا أَكْتُبُ؟ قَالَ: اكْتُبْ مَا هُوَ كَائِنٌ إِلَى يَوْمِ القِيَامَةِ. فَمَا أَصَابَ الإِنْسَانَ لَمْ يَكُنْ لِيُخْطِئَهُ، وَمَا أَخْطَأَهُ لَمْ يَكُنْ لِيُصِيبَهُ. جَفَّتِ الأَقْلَامُ وَطُوِيَتِ الصُّحُفُ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The vocabulary first, as al-Fawzān gives it: أَزَلًا — is the pre-eternity which has no beginning; أَبَدًا — is the perpetuity in the future which has no end.

الطَّاعَات — the plural of طَاعَة, and it is agreement with the command. المَعَاصِي — the plural of مَعْصِيَة, and it is contravening the command. الأَرْزَاق — the plural of رِزْق, and it is what benefits. الآجَال — the plural of أَجَل, and it is the duration of a thing; and a man's ajal is the end of his time in this world, by death.

اللَّوْحُ المَحْفُوظ — and it is أُمُّ الكِتَاب — the Mother of the Book; مَحْفُوظ — guarded from any addition to it or subtraction from it.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

المَرْتَبَةُ الأُولَى — belief in Allah's knowledge, encompassing everything of the existent and the non-existent; this knowledge which is an attribute of His attributes of the Essence, with which He never ceases to be described, in eternity and perpetually. And of that is His knowledge of the deeds of the creation — of the acts of obedience and of disobedience — and His knowledge of their states, of the provisions and the appointed terms and other things.

Note the phrase المَوْجُودَاتِ وَالمَعْدُومَات — the existent and the non-existent. That is the reach of it: not only what is and was, but what never happened. The knowledge is not a record of events; it is complete independently of whether they occur.

المَرْتَبَةُ الثَّانِيَة — the level of the writing: that Allah wrote in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ the measures of the creation; so nothing occurs in the universe but Allah knew it and wrote it before it occurred.

أَوَّلُ مَا خَلَقَ اللهُ القَلَمَ، فَقَالَ لَهُ: اكْتُبْ. قَالَ: وَمَا أَكْتُبُ؟ قَالَ: اكْتُبْ مَقَادِيرَ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ حَتَّى تَقُومَ السَّاعَةُ

"The first thing Allah created was the Pen. He said to it: write. It said: and what shall I write? He said: write the measures of everything until the Hour comes."

رَوَاهُ أَبُو دَاوُدَ فِي سُنَنِهِ — from ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So this ḥadīth indicates the level of the writing, and that the measures are all of them written.

And then a point of vowelling with two whole meanings hanging on it. أَوَّلُ مَا خَلَقَ اللهُ القَلَمَ is narrated with أَوَّل and القَلَم in the accusative, on the basis that the speech is a single sentence; and its meaning is: that at the first of His creating the Pen, He said to it: write.

And it is narrated with أَوَّل and القَلَم in the nominative, on the basis that the speech is two sentences: the first «the first thing Allah created was the Pen», and «He said to it: write» a second sentence. So the meaning would be: that the first of the created things of this world is the Pen.

بِالنَّصْبone sentence — when He first created the Pen, He commanded it. Says nothing about what was created before
بِالرَّفْعtwo sentences — the Pen is the first created thing of this world, and then the command

al-Fawzān reports both without choosing, and that is worth imitating: the level of the writing is established on either reading, and the question of what was created first is a separate matter the ḥadīth may or may not be settling.

And a point of attribution the student should not miss: and his saying فَمَا أَصَابَ الإِنْسَانَ لَمْ يَكُنْ لِيُخْطِئَهُ is from the speech of ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit, the narrator of the ḥadīth — not from the Prophet ﷺ. That is: whatever reaches a man of what benefits him or harms him is decreed for him; it must befall him, and its opposite will not befall him. The matn carries it, and the sharḥ tells you whose words they are.

جَفَّتِ الأَقْلَامُ وَطُوِيَتِ الصُّحُفُ — is a figure for the priorness of the writing of the measures and the finishing of it; and it is the meaning of what came in the ḥadīth of Ibn ʿAbbās: «the pens have been lifted and the pages have dried» — reported by al-Tirmidhī.

أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ إِنَّ ذَلِكَ فِي كِتَابٍ إِنَّ ذَلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

"Do you not know that Allah knows what is in the heaven and the earth? Indeed that is in a Book. Indeed that is easy for Allah."

Sūrat al-Ḥajj 22:70 · سُورَةُ الحَجّ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَلَمْ تَعْلَمْ — the interrogative is لِلتَّقْرِير — for affirmation; that is: you have come to know, O Muḥammad, and are certain.

And now see how neatly the āyah contains both levels of the first degree: أَنَّ اللَّهَ يَعْلَمُ مَا فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالْأَرْضِ — in it is the encompassing of His knowledge of the upper world and the lower world, and this is the level of the KNOWLEDGE. إِنَّ ذَلِكَ فِي كِتَابٍ — that is: written with Him in the Mother of the Book, and this is the level of the WRITING.

إِنَّ ذَلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ — that is: the encompassing of His knowledge of what is in the heaven and the earth, and His writing of it, is easy for Him. And that is the shāhid: it affirms Allah's knowledge of the things and His writing of them in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ — which is exactly what the first degree comprises.

مَا أَصَابَ مِنْ مُصِيبَةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ وَلَا فِي أَنْفُسِكُمْ إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ نَبْرَأَهَا إِنَّ ذَلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ

"No calamity befalls in the earth, nor in yourselves, but it is in a Book before We bring it into being. Indeed that is easy for Allah."

Sūrat al-Ḥadīd 57:22 · سُورَةُ الحَدِيد

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān glosses the two kinds of calamity the āyah names, and the detail is worth having: مَا أَصَابَ مِنْ مُصِيبَةٍ فِي الْأَرْضِ — of drought of rain, weakness of plants, and shortfall of fruits; وَلَا فِي أَنْفُسِكُمْ — of pains and sicknesses and straitness of living.

إِلَّا فِي كِتَابٍ — that is: but that it is written in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ; مِنْ قَبْلِ أَنْ نَبْرَأَهَا — that is: before We create it and bring it into existence; إِنَّ ذَلِكَ عَلَى اللَّهِ يَسِيرٌ — that is: its recording in the Book, for all its multitude, is easy for Allah.

And the shāhid: it is a proof for the writing of the events in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ before their occurrence; and that comprises His knowledge of them before the writing — so it is a proof for the two levels of knowledge and writing together.

التَّقْدِيرُ العَامُّ وَالخَاصّ The General Decree and the Particular

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then after that the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ pointed out that the decreeing is of two kinds:

تَقْدِيرٌ عَامٌّ — comprehensive of every thing that comes to be; and it is the one whose discussion with its proofs has just come, and it is what is written in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ.

وَتَقْدِيرٌ خَاصّ — and it is a detailing of the general decree; and it is three kinds. This is al-Fawzān's reading of the author's phrase يَكُونُ فِي مَوَاضِعَ جُمْلَةً وَتَفْصِيلًا — «in places, in summary and in detail».

١ عُمْرِيّ
lifetime		as in the ḥadīth of Ibn Masʿūd concerning what is written upon the foetus in its mother’s belly, of four words: his provision, his appointed term, his deed, and whether he is wretched or happy
٢ حَوْلِيّ
yearly		what is decreed on Laylat al-Qadr of the events of the year — as in فِيهَا يُفْرَقُ كُلُّ أَمْرٍ حَكِيمٍ (al-Dukhān 44:4)
٣ يَوْمِيّ
daily		what is decreed of the day’s events — of life and death, honour and humiliation and the like — as in كُلَّ يَوْمٍ هُوَ فِي شَأْنٍ (al-Raḥmān 55:29)

So the structure is one decree and three windows onto it. Nothing new is decided at any of the three — they are, in al-Fawzān's word, a detailing of what is already in the Preserved Tablet. Which is why Laylat al-Qadr is a night to be sought rather than a night on which the outcome is still open.

فِيهَا يُفْرَقُ كُلُّ أَمْرٍ حَكِيمٍ

"In it every decided affair is apportioned."

Sūrat al-Dukhān 44:4 · سُورَةُ الدُّخَان

يَسْأَلُهُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ كُلَّ يَوْمٍ هُوَ فِي شَأْنٍ

"All who are in the heavens and the earth ask of Him. Every day He is upon some affair."

Sūrat al-Raḥmān 55:29 · سُورَةُ الرَّحْمَن

غُلَاةُ القَدَرِيَّة The Extremists of the Qadariyyah

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And his saying فَهَذَا القَدَر — that is, the one just set out in its two kinds, the general and the particular — قَدْ كَانَ يُنْكِرُهُ غُلَاةُ القَدَرِيَّةِ, that is: those who went to excess in denying the decree.

So they deny Allah's knowledge of things before their existence, and His writing of them in al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ and elsewhere; and they say: Allah commanded and forbade while not knowing who will obey Him and who will disobey Him — so the affair is أُنُف, that is: newly begun, not having preceded in Allah's knowledge and His decree.

أُنُف is the word they used, and it is worth knowing because it names the position exactly: the affair is fresh — made up as it goes, unknown in advance even to Allah. That is not a view about human freedom; it is a denial of an attribute.

And these the imāms declared disbelievers; but they have died out — and this is why the Shaykh said وَمُنْكِرُوهُ اليَوْمَ قَلِيل.

And then al-Fawzān names who is left, which is the bridge to the next lesson: and there has remained the sect which acknowledges the knowledge, but denies that the deeds of the servants enter into the decree, and claims that they are created by them independently — that Allah did not create them and did not will them, as its explanation is to come.

So the dispute has moved. The old Qadariyyah denied the first degree — the knowledge and the writing. The later ones concede it and deny the second — the volition and the creating. Which is exactly what Lesson 43 takes up.

Check Your Understanding · MCQ أَسْئِلَة

What does the author’s wording imply about one who denies al-qadar?

That he is sinful but still of Ahl al-Sunnah
That he is not of Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah — and per the ḥadīth of Jibrīl, which made it the sixth pillar, he is not a believer
That his case is left to Allah
That he must be taught before being judged

Why does the author treat the knowledge and the writing as one degree?

Because the texts always mention them together
Because the first pair is about what Allah knew and recorded, and neither of them makes anything happen — the second pair is what He willed and brought about
Because the writing is a kind of knowledge
To make the count come to two

What does المَوْجُودَاتِ وَالمَعْدُومَاتِ establish about the reach of His knowledge?

That He knows only what will occur
That it includes what never happened — so the knowledge is complete independently of whether events occur
That He knows the present but not the future
That His knowledge increases with events

What are the two readings of أَوَّلُ مَا خَلَقَ اللهُ القَلَمَ?

One is authentic and the other weak
In the accusative it is one sentence — when He first created the Pen He commanded it; in the nominative it is two — the Pen is the first created thing of this world
They differ over whether the Pen or the Throne was first
They differ over who narrated it

Whose words are فَمَا أَصَابَ الإِنْسَانَ لَمْ يَكُنْ لِيُخْطِئَهُ?

The Prophet’s ﷺ, in the same ḥadīth
ʿUbādah ibn al-Ṣāmit’s, the narrator of the ḥadīth
Ibn ʿAbbās’s
Ibn Taymiyyah’s own

How does al-Ḥajj 22:70 contain both levels of the first degree?

By mentioning the Pen and the Tablet
"Allah knows what is in the heaven and the earth" is the level of knowledge; "indeed that is in a Book" is the level of writing
By mentioning the heaven and the earth
By affirming that it is easy for Allah

What are the three kinds of particular decree?

Of the angels, of men and of the jinn
ʿUmrī — the four words on the foetus; ḥawlī — on Laylat al-Qadr; and yawmī — the day’s events
Of provision, of lifespan and of deeds
Of the heavens, the earth and what is between

What did the ghulāt al-qadariyyah mean by saying the affair is أُنُف?

That it is decided by the servant alone
That it is newly begun — not having preceded in Allah’s knowledge or decree, so He commanded without knowing who would obey
That it changes each year on Laylat al-Qadr
That it is written but may be altered

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

اكْتُبْ مَقَادِيرَ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ حَتَّى تَقُومَ السَّاعَةُ

The second level of the decree in one command. And keep ʿUbādah's own comment beside it, which the sharḥ is careful to attribute to him rather than to the Prophet ﷺ: what reaches a man was never going to miss him, and what missed him was never going to reach him.
