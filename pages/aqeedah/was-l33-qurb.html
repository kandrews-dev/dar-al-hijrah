وُجُوبُ الإِيمَانِ بِقُرْبِهِ مِنْ خَلْقِه

The Obligation of Believing in His Nearness to His Creation

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١١٩–١٢٠
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 119–120.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the question that occasioned al-Baqarah 2:186, and what was striking in the answer.
  • State the principle الحَقُّ لَا يَتَنَاقَض, and use it on this question.
  • Say what faulty step produces the question “if He is above them, how is He with them?”
  • Explain each half of عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ، قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّه.
وُجُوبُ الإِيمَانِ بِقُرْبِهِ مِنْ خَلْقِهِ وَأَنَّ ذَلِكَ لَا يُنَافِي عُلُوَّهُ وَفَوْقِيَّتَه The Obligation of Believing in His Nearness — and That It Does Not Contradict His Highness

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَدْ دَخَلَ فِي ذَلِكَ الإِيمَانُ بِأَنَّهُ قَرِيبٌ مُجِيبٌ، كَمَا جَمَعَ بَيْنَ ذَلِكَ فِي قَوْلِهِ: ﴿وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ﴾، وَقَوْلِهِ ﷺ: «إِنَّ الَّذِي تَدْعُونَهُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَى أَحَدِكُمْ مِنْ عُنُقِ رَاحِلَتِهِ». وَمَا ذُكِرَ فِي الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ مِنْ قُرْبِهِ وَمَعِيَّتِهِ لَا يُنَافِي مَا ذُكِرَ مِنْ عُلُوِّهِ وَفَوْقِيَّتِهِ، فَإِنَّهُ سُبْحَانَهُ لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ فِي جَمِيعِ نُعُوتِهِ، وَهُوَ عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ، قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِ.

al-Fawzān marks the turn: when the author had established the obligation of believing in Allah's highness above His creation and His istiwāʾ upon His Throne, he gave warning in this section that it is obligatory, along with that, to believe that He is near to His creation. The order matters — and so does the fact that both are stated as obligations, not as two permitted opinions.

وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي عَنِّي فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ فَلْيَسْتَجِيبُوا لِي وَلْيُؤْمِنُوا بِي لَعَلَّهُمْ يَرْشُدُونَ

"And when My servants ask you about Me — indeed I am near. I answer the call of the caller when he calls Me. So let them respond to Me and believe in Me, that they may be rightly guided."

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:186 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And there has come, regarding the occasion of this āyah's revelation, that a man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: O Messenger of Allah —

أَقَرِيبٌ رَبُّنَا فَنُنَاجِيهِ، أَمْ بَعِيدٌ فَنُنَادِيهِ؟

— is our Lord near, so that we may confer with Him quietly — or far, so that we may call out to Him?

— so the Prophet ﷺ was silent, and this āyah was sent down.

تَنْبِيهٌ — al-Fawzān attributes this report to ابْنُ جَرِيرٍ and ابْنُ أَبِي حَاتِمٍ and others, without stating a grading for it. Per the rule in Lesson 22 this page reports the attribution and supplies no grading of its own. The doctrine does not rest on it: the āyah and the ḥadīth that follow are the proof, and the report only supplies the occasion.

Still, notice what the question assumes and what the answer refuses. The man offered two options and asked which. Near, or far — and in each case he had worked out the correct etiquette of duʿāʾ to match. The āyah takes the first and drops the alternative entirely.

فَإِنِّي قَرِيبٌ — near to the caller; أُجِيبُ دَعْوَةَ الدَّاعِ إِذَا دَعَانِ. And al-Fawzān draws out the practical consequence the questioner was after: and this indicates the directing toward المُنَاجَاة in supplication — conferring quietly, without raising the voice, as in the ḥadīth:

إِنَّ الَّذِي تَدْعُونَهُ أَقْرَبُ إِلَى أَحَدِكُمْ مِنْ عُنُقِ رَاحِلَتِهِ

"The One you are calling upon is nearer to each of you than the neck of his riding-beast."

مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ — al-Bukhārī and Muslim

Its explanation came in Lesson 28, where the Companions were told to go easy on themselves on the march to Khaybar. Here the author brings it for a different purpose: there it answered «why lower your voices», and here it answers «is He near or far». And in this āyah and this ḥadīth is an indication of Allah's nearness to the caller, by His answering him.

الحَقُّ لَا يَتَنَاقَض The Truth Does Not Contradict Itself

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And this nearness does not contradict His highness; and this is why the author said: وَمَا ذُكِرَ فِي الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ مِنْ قُرْبِهِ وَمَعِيَّتِهِ لَا يُنَافِي مَا ذُكِرَ مِنْ عُلُوِّهِ وَفَوْقِيَّتِهِ.

And al-Fawzān gives two reasons, the first of which is a principle that reaches far beyond this question:

لِأَنَّ الكُلَّ حَقٌّ، وَالحَقُّ لَا يَتَنَاقَضُ

— because all of it is true, and the truth does not contradict itself.

That is the sentence to carry out of Part 3. It is an argument about where to look when two revealed texts seem to clash. Not at the texts — at oneself. If both are established, and truth cannot contradict truth, then the contradiction is in my reading, and the work is to find which step of it was mine rather than the text's.

And the second reason: and because Allah تَعَالَى is لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ فِي جَمِيعِ نُعُوتِهِ — there is nothing like Him in the totality of His descriptions, that is, His attributes.

Note the author's addition of فِي جَمِيعِ نُعُوتِه. al-Shūrā 42:11 governed each attribute one at a time through Parts 1 and 2; here it governs the relations between them. Not only is His hearing unlike ours and His height unlike height we know — the way His nearness sits with His height is unlike anything we could model.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

So it is not said: if He is above His creation, how is He with them? — because this question arises from a mistaken conception, which is قِيَاسُهُ سُبْحَانَهُ بِخَلْقِهِ — his measuring Him سُبْحَانَهُ against His creation; and this is an invalid analogy, because Allah سُبْحَانَهُ is لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ.

There is the faulty step, named. The question is not neutral. It smuggles in a premise — that whatever is true of being above and being near for us must hold for Him — and then presents the consequence of that premise as a problem in the texts. It is the same move as ضَرْبُ الأَمْثَالِ لِلَّه in Lesson 15: an analogy applied where no likeness exists.

So the nearness and the highness come together in His case — because of His greatness, His supreme majesty, and His encompassing; and because السَّمَاوَاتُ السَّبْعُ فِي يَدِهِ كَخَرْدَلَةٍ فِي يَدِ العَبْدِ — the seven heavens are in His Hand like a mustard seed in the hand of a servant.

And then the question turned back on the questioner: so how should it be impossible, in the case of One some of whose greatness is this, that He be above His Throne and draw near to His creation however He wills, while He is upon the Throne?!

Which is the right way round. The objection assumed that greatness makes nearness difficult. al-Fawzān answers that greatness is exactly what makes it easy — a being who holds the seven heavens like a seed in his palm is not strained by attending to one man calling in the dark.

عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ، قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّه "High in His Nearness, Near in His Highness"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The author's closing formula, and al-Fawzān explains each half separately so that neither is read as a softening of the other:

عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِthat is: in the state of His nearness to His creation — He is high while near
قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِthat is: near to His creation in the state of His highness above His Throne — He is near while high

— as the texts of the Book and the Sunnah have indicated, and the scholars of the religion were agreed upon; and it is of His particular characteristics سُبْحَانَهُ (مِنْ خَصَائِصِهِ سُبْحَانَهُ).

That last clause is the quiet resolution of the whole difficulty, and it is worth stating plainly. Being high while near is not a paradox He resolves; it is something that belongs to Him alone. No creature is both, which is exactly why the human mind reaches for a contradiction — it has no instance to reason from. And a description that belongs to Allah alone is not refuted by the observation that nothing else has it.

You met this formula first in Lesson 28, in the Prophetic duʿāʾ of al-Ẓāhir and al-Bāṭin. There it was the conclusion drawn from two names the Prophet ﷺ placed side by side. Here it is the author's own statement of the creed, and the two arrive at the same words — which is itself a small demonstration of why Part 2 was not a second argument but the same one.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وَهُوَ عَلِيٌّ فِي دُنُوِّهِ، قَرِيبٌ فِي عُلُوِّهِ

The author's own words, and the end of a road that began in Lesson 16. Keep al-Fawzān's reason with them — لِأَنَّ الكُلَّ حَقٌّ، وَالحَقُّ لَا يَتَنَاقَضُ — because that is the tool, and this is only its first use.
