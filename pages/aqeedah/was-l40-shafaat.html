الشَّفَاعَاتُ وَشَرْطَاهَا

The Intercessions, and Their Two Conditions

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the derivation of الشَّفَاعَة and what the image behind it is.
  • Name the three intercessions particular to the Prophet ﷺ.
  • State the two conditions of a valid intercession, with their proofs.
  • Name the three parties into which people divided over intercession, and the error of each.
٩ · أَوَّلُ مَنْ يَسْتَفْتِحُ بَابَ الجَنَّة Ninth — The First to Seek the Opening of the Garden

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَأَوَّلُ مَنْ يَسْتَفْتِحُ بَابَ الجَنَّةِ مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ، وَأَوَّلُ مَنْ يَدْخُلُهَا مِنَ الأُمَمِ أُمَّتُهُ. وَلَهُ ﷺ فِي القِيَامَةِ ثَلَاثُ شَفَاعَاتٍ: أَمَّا الشَّفَاعَةُ الأُولَى فَيَشْفَعُ فِي أَهْلِ المَوْقِفِ حَتَّى يُقْضَى بَيْنَهُمْ، بَعْدَ أَنْ يَتَرَادَّ الأَنْبِيَاءُ: آدَمُ وَنُوحٌ وَإِبْرَاهِيمُ وَمُوسَى وَعِيسَى ابْنُ مَرْيَمَ عَنِ الشَّفَاعَةِ حَتَّى تَنْتَهِيَ إِلَيْهِ. وَأَمَّا الشَّفَاعَةُ الثَّانِيَةُ فَيَشْفَعُ فِي أَهْلِ الجَنَّةِ أَنْ يَدْخُلُوا الجَنَّةَ؛ وَهَاتَانِ الشَّفَاعَتَانِ خَاصَّتَانِ لَهُ. وَأَمَّا الشَّفَاعَةُ الثَّالِثَةُ فَيَشْفَعُ فِيمَنِ اسْتَحَقَّ النَّارَ — وَهَذِهِ الشَّفَاعَةُ لَهُ وَلِسَائِرِ النَّبِيِّينَ وَالصِّدِّيقِينَ وَغَيْرِهِمْ.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān marks where the narrative has reached: the Shaykh explains what the affair of the believers comes to on the Day of Rising, after they have passed through those states — Lessons 36 to 39. So they do not enter the Garden except after a permission from Allah تَعَالَى and a request for its gates to be opened.

That is the hinge of this lesson, and it is easy to read past. The believers are cleared, settled and standing at the gate — and the gate is still shut. Everything that follows is about who asks for it to be opened.

آتِي بَابَ الجَنَّةِ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ فَأَسْتَفْتِحُ، فَيَقُولُ الخَازِنُ: مَنْ أَنْتَ؟ فَأَقُولُ: مُحَمَّدٌ

"I shall come to the gate of the Garden on the Day of Rising and seek its opening. The keeper will say: who are you? And I shall say: Muḥammad."

فِي الصَّحِيح — from Anas رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الاسْتِفْتَاح is the seeking of the opening; and in this is an ennobling of him ﷺ and a showing forth of his excellence.

وَأَوَّلُ مَنْ يَدْخُلُهَا مِنَ الأُمَمِ أُمَّتُهُ — and that is because of its excellence over the rest of the nations. Its proof is in the ḥadīth of أَبِي هُرَيْرَةَ which Muslim reported, of his saying ﷺ: «and we are the first to enter the Garden».

الشَّفَاعَةُ · لُغَةً وَعُرْفًا Intercession — in the Language, and in Usage

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشَّفَاعَة — in the language is الوَسِيلَة — the means; and in usage it is سُؤَالُ الخَيْرِ لِلْغَيْرِ — asking good for another.

And then the derivation, which carries the whole picture: it is derived from الشَّفْع, which is the opposite of الوَتْر — the odd; as though the intercessor joined his request to the request of the one interceded for, after it had been alone.

One request becomes two. The petitioner was an odd number and the intercessor makes him even — which means the petitioner must already be petitioning. Intercession is a second voice added to a request that exists, not a request made on behalf of someone who is making none.

The student met this derivation in Lesson 8, on Āyat al-Kursī. Here it does doctrinal work: it is already, by itself, the answer to the first of the three errors below.

أَنْوَاعُ الشَّفَاعَةِ الثَّمَانِيَة The Eight Kinds of Intercession

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Thus the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ mentioned the kinds of intercession here in brief; and by way of a full enumeration they are eight kinds: of them what is particular to the Prophet ﷺ, and of them what is shared between him and others.

خَاصَّةٌ بِالنَّبِيِّ ﷺ — particular to him
١الشَّفَاعَةُ العُظْمَى — and it is المَقَامُ المَحْمُود: that he ﷺ intercede that Allah judge between His servants, after the standing has grown long upon them, and after their going from prophet to prophet to undertake it — and our Prophet ﷺ undertakes it after the permission of his Lord
٢his intercession for the people of the Garden to enter it, after the finishing of the Reckoning
٣his intercession for his uncle أَبِي طَالِبٍ, that the punishment be lightened for him — and this is particular to him, because Allah informed that the disbelievers are not benefited by the intercession of the intercessors, and our Prophet informed that his intercession is particular to the people of tawḥīd. So his intercession for his uncle is particular to him and particular to Abū Ṭālib
يُشَارِكُهُ فِيهَا غَيْرُهُ — shared with the prophets, the angels, the ṣiddīqūn and the martyrs
٤for one of the disobedient monotheists who deserved the Fire, that he not enter it
٥for one of the disobedient monotheists who entered the Fire, that he come out of it
٦for the raising of the ranks of some of the people of the Garden
٧for those whose good and evil deeds were equal, that they enter the Garden — and they are أَهْلُ الأَعْرَاف, upon one statement
٨for the entry of some believers into the Garden without reckoning or punishment — as his intercession for عُكَاشَةَ بْنِ مِحْصَنٍ رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ, when the Prophet ﷺ supplicated that he be of the seventy thousand who enter the Garden without reckoning or punishment

Two observations the table makes visible. First: the one intercession everybody has heard of — for sinners to be spared the Fire or taken out of it — is not among the three that belong to him alone. The prophets, the angels, the ṣiddīqūn and the martyrs share it. Second: the greatest of them is not about rescuing anyone. al-Shafāʿah al-ʿUẓmā is a request that the judging begin — made when the standing has become unbearable and every earlier prophet has declined.

شَرْطَا الشَّفَاعَة The Two Conditions of Intercession

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And Ahl al-Sunnah wa-l-Jamāʿah believe in all of these intercessions, because of the establishment of their proofs — and that they do not occur except by two conditions:

الشَّرْطُ الأَوَّل · إِذْنُ اللهِ لِلشَّافِعِ أَنْ يَشْفَعَ — Allah's permission to the intercessor that he intercede:

مَنْ ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِنْدَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ

"Who is there that can intercede with Him except by His permission?"

Sūrat al-Baqarah 2:255 · سُورَةُ البَقَرَة

مَا مِنْ شَفِيعٍ إِلَّا مِنْ بَعْدِ إِذْنِهِ

"There is no intercessor except after His permission."

Sūrat Yūnus 10:3 · سُورَةُ يُونُس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الشَّرْطُ الثَّانِي · رِضَا اللهِ عَنِ المَشْفُوعِ لَهُ — Allah's being pleased with the one interceded for:

وَلَا يَشْفَعُونَ إِلَّا لِمَنِ ارْتَضَى

"And they do not intercede except for one with whom He is pleased."

Sūrat al-Anbiyāʾ 21:28 · سُورَةُ الأَنْبِيَاء

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the two conditions are gathered together in His saying:

وَكَمْ مِنْ مَلَكٍ فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ لَا تُغْنِي شَفَاعَتُهُمْ شَيْئًا إِلَّا مِنْ بَعْدِ أَنْ يَأْذَنَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَرْضَى

"And how many an angel there is in the heavens whose intercession avails nothing at all — except after Allah permits, for whom He wills and is pleased with."

Sūrat al-Najm 53:26 · سُورَةُ النَّجْم

Both conditions are about Allah, and neither is about the intercessor's standing. The angels are named in the third āyah precisely because they are the most exalted creatures anyone could appeal to — and their intercession avails nothing at all until He permits it and is pleased with its object. So intercession is not influence brought to bear on Allah; it is something He grants twice over before it happens.

مُخَالَفَةُ المُعْتَزِلَة The Muʿtazilah’s Opposition

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Muʿtazilah opposed over the intercession for the people of the major sins among the believers — for the one of them who deserved the Fire, that he not enter it; and the one who entered it, that he come out. And they argue with His saying:

فَمَا تَنْفَعُهُمْ شَفَاعَةُ الشَّافِعِينَ

"So the intercession of the intercessors will not avail them."

Sūrat al-Muddaththir 74:48 · سُورَةُ المُدَّثِّر

الشَّرْح · the explanation

The answer: that it came concerning the disbelievers — they are the ones whom the intercession of the intercessors does not benefit. As for the believers, the intercession benefits them, with its conditions.

And the answer is simply a matter of reading the preceding āyāt, which name who «them» is. The Muʿtazilī argument requires the pronoun to mean sinners when the passage has been describing deniers — and the point of difference between the two parties is exactly the question of whether a major sin makes a man one of the latter, which Lesson 30 showed it does not.

ثَلَاثَةُ أَصْنَافٍ فِي أَمْرِ الشَّفَاعَة Three Parties in the Matter of Intercession

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān closes Part 4 by dividing people three ways, and the pattern is the one from Lesson 30 — two excesses and a middle:

الصِّنْفُ الأَوَّلthey went to excess in AFFIRMING it — and they are the Christians, the mushrikūn, the extremists of the ṣūfiyyah, and the grave-worshippers: in that they made the intercession of the one they venerate, with Allah, like the intercession known in this world before kings — so they sought it from other than Allah, as Allah mentioned that of the mushrikūn
الصِّنْفُ الثَّانِيthey went to excess in DENYING it — and they are the Muʿtazilah and the Khawārij: they denied the intercession of the Prophet ﷺ and of others for the people of the major sins
الصِّنْفُ الثَّالِثأَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَة — they affirmed the intercession in accordance with what the Qurʾānic texts and the Prophetic aḥādīth came with — so they affirmed the intercession WITH ITS CONDITIONS.

Stop on the first party, because it is where this part of the book joins the beginning of the track. Their error is named as the courtly analogy — like the intercession known in this world before kings — which is word for word the mechanism of Lesson 15, where the Qurʾān forbade ضَرْبَ الأَمْثَالِ لِلَّهِ by name, and the whole subject of al-Qawāʿid al-Arbaʿ.

And now see what the derivation of the word has been doing all along. الشَّفَاعَة means the intercessor makes the petitioner's request even — so the petitioner must be the one petitioning. The grave-worshipper reverses it: he asks the intercessor instead of asking Allah, which is not intercession at all but worship directed elsewhere. The error is visible in the word's own etymology.

And the middle position is characteristically the one that needs no text explained away. The first party kept the intercession and lost the tawḥīd; the second kept the tawḥīd by throwing out the aḥādīth. Ahl al-Sunnah affirm every text — and the two conditions are what make that possible, because an intercession that requires His permission and His pleasure takes nothing away from Him.

أَثْبَتَ أَهْلُ السُّنَّةِ الشَّفَاعَةَ بِلَا شُرُوطٍ. Ahl al-Sunnah affirmed the intercession without conditions.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

إِلَّا مِنْ بَعْدِ أَنْ يَأْذَنَ اللَّهُ لِمَنْ يَشَاءُ وَيَرْضَى

Both conditions in one clause, said of the angels — the most exalted creatures anyone could appeal to. Learn it and the whole business of seeking intercession from the dead collapses: what is granted twice over by Allah cannot be obtained by asking someone else for it.
