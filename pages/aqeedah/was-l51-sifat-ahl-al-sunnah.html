صِفَاتُ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَلِمَ سُمُّوا بِذَلِك

The Qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah, and Why They Are So Named

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ١٩١–١٩٥
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 191–195.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Say what آثَارُ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ means here — and what it deliberately does NOT mean.
  • Give the three grounds on which the Companions’ way is followed, and al-Fawzān’s answer to «the way of the salaf is safer, the way of the khalaf is more learned».
  • Define الرَّاشِد and الغَاوِي as a pair.
  • Define البِدْعَة linguistically and in the Sharīʿah.
  • Explain why Ahl al-Sunnah carry BOTH names — أَهْلُ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّة and أَهْلُ الجَمَاعَة.
  • Say which ijmāʿ is the one that can be pinned down, and give the two reasons a later ijmāʿ cannot be.
  • Say which fourth uṣūl the author left out, and why.
فَصْلٌ · فِي صِفَاتِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ وَلِمَ سُمُّوا بِذَلِك A Section on the Qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah, and Why They Are So Named

When the Shaykh had set out the way of Ahl al-Sunnah in the matters of creed, he mentioned in this section and the one after it their way in the religion at large — its foundations and its branches — and the descriptions by which they are distinguished from the people of innovation and opposition. So the book turns, in its last two sections, from what this group holds to what this group is like; and L52 will close with their manners.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

ثُمَّ مِنْ طَرِيقَةِ أَهْلِ السُّنَّةِ وَالجَمَاعَةِ: اتِّبَاعُ آثَارِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ بَاطِنًا وَظَاهِرًا، وَاتِّبَاعُ سَبِيلِ السَّابِقِينَ الأَوَّلِينَ مِنَ المُهَاجِرِينَ وَالأَنْصَارِ، وَاتِّبَاعُ وَصِيَّةِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ حَيْثُ قَالَ: «عَلَيْكُمْ بِسُنَّتِي وَسُنَّةِ الخُلَفَاءِ الرَّاشِدِينَ المَهْدِيِّينَ مِنْ بَعْدِي، تَمَسَّكُوا بِهَا وَعَضُّوا عَلَيْهَا بِالنَّوَاجِذِ، وَإِيَّاكُمْ وَمُحْدَثَاتِ الأُمُورِ، فَإِنَّ كُلَّ بِدْعَةٍ ضَلَالَةٌ». وَيَعْلَمُونَ أَنَّ أَصْدَقَ الكَلَامِ كَلَامُ اللهِ، وَخَيْرَ الهَدْيِ هَدْيُ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ؛ وَيُؤْثِرُونَ كَلَامَ اللهِ عَلَى غَيْرِهِ مِنْ كَلَامِ أَصْنَافِ النَّاسِ، وَيُقَدِّمُونَ هَدْيَ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ عَلَى هَدْيِ كُلِّ أَحَدٍ. وَلِهَذَا سُمُّوا أَهْلَ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ، وَسُمُّوا أَهْلَ الجَمَاعَةِ؛ لِأَنَّ الجَمَاعَةَ هِيَ الاجْتِمَاعُ وَضِدُّهَا الفُرْقَةُ. وَالإِجْمَاعُ هُوَ الأَصْلُ الثَّالِثُ الَّذِي يُعْتَمَدُ عَلَيْهِ فِي العِلْمِ وَالدِّينِ؛ وَهُمْ يَزِنُونَ بِهَذِهِ الأُصُولِ الثَّلَاثَةِ جَمِيعَ مَا عَلَيْهِ النَّاسُ مِنْ أَقْوَالٍ وَأَعْمَالٍ بَاطِنَةٍ أَوْ ظَاهِرَةٍ مِمَّا لَهُ تَعَلُّقٌ بِالدِّينِ. وَالإِجْمَاعُ الَّذِي يَنْضَبِطُ هُوَ مَا كَانَ عَلَيْهِ السَّلَفُ الصَّالِحُ، فَإِنَّ بَعْدَهُمْ كَثُرَ الاخْتِلَافُ وَانْتَشَرَتِ الأُمَّةُ.

١ · اتِّبَاعُ آثَارِ النَّبِيِّ ﷺ بَاطِنًا وَظَاهِرًا Following His ﷺ Traces, Inwardly and Outwardly

الشَّرْح · the explanation

That is: treading his road and travelling upon his method — بَاطِنًا وَظَاهِرًا, in contrast to the hypocrites, who follow him outwardly and not inwardly.

Then the definition that does the real work in this clause:

آثَارُهُ ﷺ
what it IS		his Sunnah — and it is what is narrated from him and transmitted from him of a saying, a deed, or an approval.
لَا آثَارُهُ الحِسِّيَّة
what it is NOT		not his physical traces, such as the places where he sat and slept and the like — because pursuing that is a cause of falling into shirk, as happened in the nations before.

That second row is one of the sharpest things in the sharḥ, and it turns on a pun the Arabic makes available and English does not. أَثَر can mean a transmitted report or a physical relic — and al-Fawzān insists the matn means the first and warns against the second in the same breath.

So following his traces means doing what he said, did and approved. It does not mean venerating the ground he stood on. And the reason given is not aesthetic distaste: it is that this exact practice is how the earlier nations arrived at shirk — which the whole first half of this creed was spent dismantling.

٢ · اتِّبَاعُ سَبِيلِ السَّابِقِينَ الأَوَّلِين Following the Way of the First Forerunners

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Because of what Allah singled them out with of knowledge and fiqh — and al-Fawzān gives three grounds, each of which is a structural advantage that cannot be reproduced later:

١they witnessed the sending down of the revelation
٢they heard its interpretation
٣and they received from the Messenger ﷺ without an intermediary

So they are nearer to what is correct, and more deserving of being followed after the Messenger ﷺ. Their being followed comes in the second degree, after the following of the Messenger ﷺ.

And then the ruling that follows from it: the sayings of the Companions are a proof that must be followed when no text from the Prophet ﷺ is found — because their way is أَسْلَمُ وَأَعْلَمُ وَأَحْكَم.

Those three words are chosen to answer a specific saying, and al-Fawzān quotes it in order to refuse it:

— not as some of the later ones say: that the way of the salaf is safer (أَسْلَم), while the way of the khalaf is more learned and more judicious (أَعْلَمُ وَأَحْكَم) — so they follow the way of the khalaf and abandon the way of the salaf.

Notice the shape of the refusal. The saying concedes safety to the salaf and then takes knowledge away from them — which is a way of honouring them out of the argument. al-Fawzān does not meet it with indignation; he meets it by claiming all three words at once: أَسْلَمُ وَأَعْلَمُ وَأَحْكَم. The men who watched the revelation descend and asked the Messenger ﷺ what it meant were not merely more cautious than those who came later. They knew more. A safety that is bought by ignorance would be no safety at all — so if the salaf were safer, it is because they were better informed, not in spite of being less so.

٣ · اتِّبَاعُ وَصِيَّةِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ Following the Bequest of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ

عَلَيْكُمْ بِسُنَّتِي وَسُنَّةِ الخُلَفَاءِ الرَّاشِدِينَ المَهْدِيِّينَ مِنْ بَعْدِي، تَمَسَّكُوا بِهَا وَعَضُّوا عَلَيْهَا بِالنَّوَاجِذِ، وَإِيَّاكُمْ وَمُحْدَثَاتِ الأُمُورِ، فَإِنَّ كُلَّ بِدْعَةٍ ضَلَالَةٌ

"Hold to my Sunnah and the sunnah of the rightly-guided, guided Caliphs after me. Cling to it and bite upon it with the molars. And beware of newly-invented matters, for every innovation is a misguidance."

رَوَاهُ الإِمَامُ أَحْمَدُ وَأَبُو دَاوُدَ وَالتِّرْمِذِيُّ وَابْنُ مَاجَهْ — وَقَالَ التِّرْمِذِيُّ: حَسَنٌ صَحِيحٌ

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the Shaykh’s purpose is to make clear that Ahl al-Sunnah follow the way of the rightly-guided Caliphs in particular, after their following of the first forerunners of the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār in general — because the Prophet ﷺ enjoined following the way of the rightly-guided Caliphs with a bequest specific to them in this ḥadīth.

So in it he joined the sunnah of the rightly-guided Caliphs to his own sunnah — which indicates that what the rightly-guided Caliphs, or one of them, instituted, it is not permissible to turn away from.

Then the wording, word by word:

الخُلَفَاءُ الرَّاشِدُونthe four Caliphs: أَبُو بَكْرٍ · عُمَرُ · عُثْمَانُ · عَلِيٌّ. And they were described as الرَّاشِدِين because they knew the truth and followed it.
المَهْدِيِّينthat is: those whom Allah guided to the truth
تَمَسَّكُوا بِهَاthat is: cleave to it
وَعَضُّوا عَلَيْهَا بِالنَّوَاجِذa figure for the intensity of clinging to it; and النَّوَاجِذ are the furthest back of the molars
مُحْدَثَاتُ الأُمُورthey are the innovations

الرَّاشِد and its opposite — al-Fawzān defines the word by giving its contrary, which is how he teaches a term whenever the contrast carries the meaning:

الرَّاشِدone who knew the truth AND acted by it
الغَاوِيone who knew the truth and did NOT act by it

So the two differ by one thing only, and it is not knowledge. Both men know. The title of the four Caliphs is therefore a statement about their obedience and not about their learning — and the pair quietly warns the student that knowing the truth is the cheaper half of the matter.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And then البِدْعَة, defined in the two registers al-Fawzān always gives:

لُغَةًwhat has no prior precedent
شَرْعًاwhat no Sharīʿah evidence has indicated

So everyone who introduces something and ascribes it to the religion while it has no evidence — it is an innovation and a misguidance, whether in creed, or in sayings, or in deeds.

Mark the two conditions in that sentence, because together they are the whole definition. A thing is a bidʿah when it is introduced and ascribed to the religion without evidence. That is why the Sharīʿah definition is narrower than the linguistic one: a new tool, a new road, a new way of recording a text — these have no prior precedent and are not innovations in the religion, because nobody ascribes them to it. The ascription is what makes a novelty a bidʿah, and al-Fawzān returns to this on the next page.

٤ · تَقْدِيمُ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةِ عَلَى كَلَامِ النَّاس Putting the Book and the Sunnah Before the Speech of Men

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And of the qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah: that they magnify the Book of Allah and the Sunnah of His Messenger and hold them in awe, and put them first — in drawing evidence from them and in taking them as a pattern — over the sayings of men and their deeds. Because they know two things:

اللَّهُ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا هُوَ لَيَجْمَعَنَّكُمْ إِلَى يَوْمِ الْقِيَامَةِ لَا رَيْبَ فِيهِ وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ حَدِيثًا

"Allah — there is no deity but Him. He will surely gather you to the Day of Standing, about which there is no doubt. And who is more truthful than Allah in speech?"

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:87 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

وَعْدَ اللَّهِ حَقًّا وَمَنْ أَصْدَقُ مِنَ اللَّهِ قِيلًا

"The promise of Allah, in truth. And who is more truthful than Allah in word?"

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:122 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

Both are rhetorical questions, which is why al-Fawzān cites the pair. The matn says the most truthful speech is the speech of Allah, and the Qurʾān states that not as a claim to be proved but as a question nobody will answer: who is more truthful than Allah? Once that question has been asked, preferring any man’s word over His has already been answered.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And the second: that the best guidance is the guidance of Muḥammad ﷺ. al-Fawzān pauses on the vowelling, because the word carries two readings:

الهَدْي
fatḥah on the hāʾ, sukūn on the dāl		السَّمْتُ وَالطَّرِيقَةُ وَالسِّيرَة — his bearing, his way, his conduct
الهُدَى
ḍammah on the hāʾ, fatḥah on the dāl		and it is also read thus — that is: الدَّلَالَةُ وَالإِرْشَاد, the pointing-out and the directing

The two readings are not in competition; they name the two things a student takes from him ﷺ. On the first, what is best is how he lived; on the second, what is best is what he taught. And a single vowel decides which — the kind of observation this track has met before, in Lesson 4, where a vowel turned Mūsā into the speaker.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَيُؤْثِرُونَ كَلَامَ اللهِ — that is: they put it first and take it, and they leave whatever opposes it of the speech of the creation, whoever they may be — leaders, or scholars, or devotees.

That list of three is doing something. It names the three kinds of authority a man might actually be tempted to put above the text — power, learning, and piety — and rules out all three. The last is the hardest and so it is placed last: a reputation for worship is the credential that most readily overrides a text in people’s hearts.

And وَيُقَدِّمُونَ هَدْيَ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ — that is: his Sunnah, his conduct, his teaching and his directing — over the guidance of anyone else of the creation, however great his standing, if his guidance opposes the guidance of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. And that is in acting upon His saying:

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَطِيعُوا اللَّهَ وَأَطِيعُوا الرَّسُولَ وَأُولِي الْأَمْرِ مِنْكُمْ فَإِنْ تَنَازَعْتُمْ فِي شَيْءٍ فَرُدُّوهُ إِلَى اللَّهِ وَالرَّسُولِ إِنْ كُنْتُمْ تُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ

"O you who believe, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. Then if you differ in anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you believe in Allah and the Last Day."

Sūrat al-Nisāʾ 4:59 · سُورَةُ النِّسَاء

And see what the āyah does with the three obediences. It repeats the verb for Allah and for the Messenger, and it does not repeat it for those in authority — they are joined to the Messenger’s obedience rather than given one of their own. Then, when there is a dispute, the referral is to Allah and the Messenger only. The āyah establishes the obedience of rulers and subordinates it in the same breath — which is exactly the clause the matn is resting on.

لِمَاذَا حَمَلُوا الاسْمَيْن Why They Carry Both Names

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَهْلُ الكِتَابِ وَالسُّنَّةfor the sake of their clinging to the Book of Allah and preferring His speech to the speech of everyone, and their clinging to the guidance of the Messenger of Allah and putting it before the guidance of everyone — they were given this noble title, which conveys that these two belong to them in particular, as against those who deviated from the Book and the Sunnah among the sects of misguidance, such as the Muʿtazilah, the Khawārij and the Rawāfiḍ.
أَهْلُ الجَمَاعَةand al-jamāʿah is the opposite of al-furqah — because clinging to the Book and the Sunnah yields gathering and harmony

وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا

"And hold fast, all of you together, to the rope of Allah, and do not become divided."

Sūrat Āl ʿImrān 3:103 · سُورَةُ آلِ عِمْرَان

So al-jamāʿah here is: those gathered upon the truth.

That is the definition the whole name depends on, and it rules out the obvious misreading. The jamāʿah is not «the majority», and it is not «whoever happens to be assembled». The āyah commands holding to the rope of Allah and forbids division in the same sentence — so the gathering is a consequence of holding to the revelation, not an alternative to it. Which is why Ibn Masʿūd رَضِيَ اللهُ عَنْهُ could say, as Lesson 2 recorded, that the jamāʿah is whatever agrees with the truth even if you are alone.

الأُصُولُ الثَّلَاثَةُ وَالمِيزَان The Three Foundations, and the Scale

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And of the qualities of Ahl al-Sunnah: gathering upon taking the Book and the Sunnah, agreement upon the truth, and cooperation upon righteousness and taqwā — and this has borne fruit in the existence of ijmāʿ.

And the ijmāʿ is the third foundation relied upon in knowledge and in religion. The scholars of uṣūl have defined ijmāʿ as: the agreement of the scholars of an age upon a matter of religion; and it is a decisive proof that must be acted upon. And his words وَهُوَ الأَصْلُ الثَّالِث — that is: after the first two foundations, which are the Book and the Sunnah.

And of their qualities, that they يَزِنُونَ بِهَذِهِ الأُصُولِ الثَّلَاثَة — the Book, the Sunnah and the ijmāʿ — all that people are upon of sayings and deeds, inward or outward, of what has a connection to the religion. So they make these three foundations a scale for distinguishing truth from falsehood, and guidance from misguidance, in what issues from people of verbal or practical dealings, whether of belief or of action.

Then the clause that limits the scale — and it completes the definition of bidʿah above. al-Fawzān stresses the matn’s own restriction, مِمَّا لَهُ تَعَلُّقٌ بِالدِّين:

— of the deeds of people, such as the prayer, the fast, the Ḥajj and the zakāh… as for worldly matters, the default ruling in them is permissibility.

So the three foundations are the scale for what is offered as religion, and they are not a licence to demand a text for every ordinary act. Worship is restricted to what is legislated; the world is open except where a prohibition came. A student who misses this turns the creed into a burden it was never meant to be — and it is the same distinction that keeps the linguistic sense of bidʿah from swallowing the Sharīʿah sense.

الإِجْمَاعُ الَّذِي يَنْضَبِط The Ijmāʿ That Can Be Pinned Down

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Then the Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ clarified the reality of the ijmāʿ that is made a foundation in drawing evidence, and said: وَالإِجْمَاعُ الَّذِي يَنْضَبِط — that is: whose occurrence can be affirmed with certainty — it is what the righteous salaf were upon, since they were few, gathered in the Ḥijāz, so that recording them and knowing their view on a question was possible.

And وَبَعْدَهُمْ كَثُرَ الاخْتِلَافُ وَانْتَشَرَتِ الأُمَّة — that is: after the salaf:

أَوَّلًاthe abundance of disagreement, such that encompassing their sayings is not possible
ثَانِيًاthe spreading of the Ummah into the regions of the earth after the conquests, such that it is not customarily possible for the case to reach every one of them and for him to come to know of it — and then it is not possible to affirm with certainty that they concurred upon a single saying in it.

So this is a statement about what can be known, not about what is binding. Ijmāʿ remains a decisive proof; the point is that a claim of ijmāʿ is only as good as the possibility of having checked — and that possibility shrank as the Ummah grew. Which is why «the scholars have agreed» is a claim to be examined rather than a formula to be accepted.

تَنْبِيهٌ — why only three?

The Shaykh رَحِمَهُ اللهُ restricted himself to mentioning the three foundations and did not mention the fourth foundation, which is القِيَاس — because al-qiyās is disputed, just as they disputed over other foundations whose reference is the books of uṣūl.

Which is a characteristic move of this creed, and worth naming as a method. A book of ʿaqīdah states what the Ummah is bound by, so it carries only what is agreed — the disputed is left to the discipline that handles dispute. That is why the Wāsiṭiyyah is short and why nothing in it has needed to be withdrawn.

What does آثَارُ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ mean here — and what does it NOT mean?

His physical relics, which are to be honoured
His Sunnah — what is narrated of a saying, deed or approval; NOT his physical traces like where he sat or slept, since pursuing those caused shirk in earlier nations
Only his sayings, excluding his deeds
His lineage and household

What does «inwardly and outwardly» exclude?

Following him in matters of worldly custom
The way of the hypocrites, who follow him outwardly and not inwardly
Following the Companions
Acting on a single-chain narration

What three grounds does al-Fawzān give for following the forerunners?

Their numbers, their lineage and their wealth
They witnessed the sending down, they heard its interpretation, and they received from the Messenger ﷺ without an intermediary
Their consensus, their piety and their jihād
Their proximity to Makkah and Madīnah

How does al-Fawzān answer «the way of the salaf is safer, the way of the khalaf is more learned»?

By conceding it and adding that safety is more important
By claiming all three words for the salaf — أَسْلَمُ وَأَعْلَمُ وَأَحْكَم; a safety bought by ignorance would be no safety
By denying that the khalaf had any knowledge at all
By referring the question to the books of uṣūl

What is the difference between الرَّاشِد and الغَاوِي?

One has knowledge, the other does not
Both know the truth — the rāshid acts by it and the ghāwī does not; so they differ by obedience, not knowledge
One is a Companion, the other a later Muslim
One is a caliph, the other a subject

What are the two definitions of البِدْعَة?

A sin, and a major sin
لُغَةً what has no prior precedent; شَرْعًا what no Sharīʿah evidence indicated — introduced AND ascribed to the religion
An error in creed, and an error in action
What the Companions rejected, and what later scholars rejected

What do the two readings الهَدْي and الهُدَى give?

A weak reading and a strong one
الهَدْي = his bearing, way and conduct — HOW he lived; الهُدَى = the pointing-out and directing — WHAT he taught
A Madinan reading and a Makkan one
The same meaning in two dialects

Only that of the innovators
Anyone’s — leaders, or scholars, or devotees; a reputation for worship is no exception

What does al-Nisāʾ 4:59 do with the three obediences?

It makes all three equal and independent
It repeats the verb for Allah and the Messenger but not for those in authority, and refers disputes to Allah and the Messenger only — establishing the rulers’ obedience and subordinating it at once
It abrogates obedience to rulers
It restricts obedience to the Caliphs

What exactly is الجَمَاعَة, per al-Fawzān here?

The majority of the Muslims in any age

Those assembled behind one imām
The scholars of an age

What is the limit on the three foundations as a scale?

They apply only to creed, not to action
They weigh what has a connection to the RELIGION — worship is restricted to what is legislated, while in worldly matters the default is permissibility
They apply only to the salaf
They apply only where there is no dispute

Which ijmāʿ can be pinned down, and why?

Any ijmāʿ reported by a trustworthy scholar
That of the righteous salaf — they were few and gathered in the Ḥijāz, so recording them and knowing their view was possible
The ijmāʿ of the four schools
The ijmāʿ of the scholars of the Ḥaramayn

Why does the author mention only three foundations?

Because the fourth was not yet known
Because the fourth — القِيَاس — is disputed, and a book of creed carries only what is agreed
Because qiyās is a branch of the Sunnah

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

أَسْلَمُ وَأَعْلَمُ وَأَحْكَم

Three words, and they are an answer rather than a slogan. The saying they refute grants the salaf safety and takes knowledge away from them — which is how a man honours his predecessors out of the argument. Ahl al-Sunnah claim all three: the men who watched the revelation descend and asked the Messenger ﷺ what it meant were not merely more cautious than those who came after. They knew more.
