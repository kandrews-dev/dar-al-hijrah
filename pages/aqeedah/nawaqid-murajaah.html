نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام · المُرَاجَعَةYear 6

مُرَاجَعَةُ المَتْن

What You Should Now Know — a review of the ten nullifiers and the barriers

Read the question, answer it from memory and out loud, and only then open the answer. An item you cannot answer is not a mark lost — it is a lesson to go back to, and each one carries its link.

This is not a test and nothing is recorded. It is the check a student makes on himself before he says he has read a matn: هَلْ تَحْفَظُ المَعَانِيَ أَمْ قَرَأْتَ فَحَسْب؟ — do you hold the meanings, or did you merely read?

الجُزْءُ الأَوَّل · الشِّرْكُ وَأُصُولُ التَّوْحِيد

Part 1 — Shirk and the Roots of Tawḥīd · Nullifiers 1–3 · Lessons 1–3

1 What is the first nullifier, and what single word in the matn gives it its reach?

الشِّرْكُ فِي عِبَادَةِ الله — associating a partner with Allah in worship. The reach is in the matn’s own clause وَمِنْهُ الذَّبْحُ لِغَيْرِ الله: whoever directs an act of worship to other than Allah has nullified Islām — and «وَمِنْه» means it needs no idol and no denial, only the misdirected act.

Lesson 1 →

2 Why is leaving the prayer placed in a book of nullifiers, and what is the precise point of disagreement Ibn Bāz marks?

Because its abandonment is treated by weighty evidence as crossing out of Islām, not merely a major sin. But Ibn Bāz draws the line with care: one who denies the obligation of prayer is a disbeliever «فِي قَوْلِ الجَمِيع»; one who merely doubts about the abandoner’s ruling is maʿdhūr fī ijtihādihi, because that is a place of ijtihād.

Lesson 2 →

3 What makes taking an intermediary between oneself and Allah — asking them, relying on them — a nullifier?

It is the very structure of the first mushrikīn: they did not deny Allah, they placed وَسَائِط between themselves and Him «لِيُقَرِّبُونَا إِلَى اللهِ زُلْفَى». To call on them, to seek their intercession, is to repeat that act — and it is kufr «بِالإِجْمَاع», by the consensus the author names.

Lesson 3 →

الجُزْءُ الثَّانِي · المُشْرِكُونَ وَهُدَى النَّبِيّ ﷺ

Part 2 — The Mushrikīn, and the Guidance of the Prophet ﷺ · Nullifiers 3–5 · Lessons 4–8

4 The fourth nullifier is not declaring the mushrikīn disbelievers — but the sharḥ spends most of its folios on restraint. Why, and what is the governing rule?

Because the clause is about the establishing of the proof «each according to his state», and a lesson that carried the ruling and dropped that would misteach it. The ʿāmmī may say of plain shirk that it is shirk, and must make التَّوَقُّف where a matter is obscure to him.

And a warning the clause carries about itself: «مَنْ كَفَّرَ مُسْلِمًا فَقَدْ كَفَر» is not a ḥadīth at all — the sharḥ grades it kaḏib lā aṣla lah.

Lesson 4 →

5 The fifth nullifier — believing some guidance more complete than the Prophet’s ﷺ — turns on four words. Which, and what do they force?

وَلَوْ عَمِلَ بِه — «even if he acts by it». They force the footnote’s distinction: بُغْضُ التَّشْرِيع (hating the legislation) nullifies; بُغْضٌ طَبِيعِيّ — a merely natural dislike of a ruling while submitting to it — «فَهَذَا لَيْسَ مِنَ الكُفْرِ فِي شَيْء», and Allah describes the believers that way.

Lesson 8 →

6 When the third nullifier’s material on legislation is taught, what three categories must be kept apart?

The one who judges by other than what Allah revealed deeming it permitted or better — that is the kufr that nullifies; the one who does so out of bribe or desire while knowing it is wrong — a major sin; and the compelled (al-muḍṭarr). The lesson weights these exactly as the sharḥ does — the ruling, then the تَفْصِيل that governs it.

Lesson 7 →

الجُزْءُ الثَّالِث · الاسْتِهْزَاءُ وَالسِّحْر

Part 3 — Mockery, and Magic · Nullifiers 6–7 · Lessons 9–13

7 What is the argument that makes the sixth nullifier — mockery — teachable without a premise about intending insult?

Ibn Bāz does not say mockery is punished as contempt — he says it is contempt (اسْتِهْزَاء = تَنَقُّصٌ وَاحْتِقَار). The objects are perfection itself, so «إِنَّمَا كُنَّا نَخُوضُ وَنَلْعَب» fails in the daleel, and «even in jest» follows. But the ruling and the sentence are two things: «أَمَّا القَتْلُ فَشَيْءٌ آخَر، يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى اسْتِتَابَة».

Lesson 9 →

8 The seventh nullifier is the longest clause in the book. On the ruling «حَدُّ السَّاحِرِ ضَرْبُهُ بِالسَّيْف», what is the sharḥ’s verdict, and what carries the ruling instead?

That ḥadīth is graded weak by seven named scholars — al-Bukhārī («هَذَا لَا شَيْء»), al-Tirmidhī, al-Dhahabī, Ibn Ḥajar, Ibn Ḥazm, Ibn Rajab, and Ibn Bāz («الرَّاجِحُ وَقْفُهُ عَلَى جُنْدُب»). What carries the ruling is أَثَرُ عُمَر — ṣaḥīḥ in isnād, and Ibn Qudāmah: «اشْتُهِرَ وَلَمْ يُنْكَرْ فَكَانَ إِجْمَاعًا».

Lesson 12 →

9 How is the objection answered that the Prophet ﷺ was bewitched, given «وَاللهُ يَعْصِمُكَ مِنَ النَّاس»?

That āyah is among the last revealed, and the ʿiṣmah promised is «مِمَّا يَمْنَعُ مِنْ تَبْلِيغِ الرِّسَالَة» — protection in conveying the message, not from every bodily harm (Uḥud is listed). The siḥr touched his body, not the revelation; and ʿĀʾishah’s report stands, the claim that Hishām b. ʿUrwah was mukhtaliṭ answered by al-Dhahabī: «لَمْ يَخْتَلِطْ أَبَدًا».

Lesson 13 →

الجُزْءُ الرَّابِع · المُوَالَاةُ وَالخُرُوجُ وَالإِعْرَاض

Part 4 — Alliance, Standing Outside, and Turning Away · Nullifiers 8–10 · Lessons 14–17

10 The eighth nullifier turns on six words distinguishing two things that look alike. Give them.

المُوَالَاةُ غَيْرُ التَّوَلِّي — «المُوَالَاةُ مَعْصِيَة، وَالتَّوَلِّي كُفْر». To ally with the disbelievers in sin (cheering, imitation) is disobedience; to side with them against the Muslims, «مُسَاعَدَتُهُمْ عَلَى المُسْلِمِين», is the kufr. A page that collapses the two manufactures a takfīr the source refuses.

Lesson 14 →

11 The spy Ḥāṭib ؓ did an act of betrayal and was not declared a disbeliever. Where exactly was his taʾwīl located — and what principle does his case open?

Not in the concealment — a sin he knew he was committing — but in his belief the disclosure would do no harm; so a man can be sinning and interpreting at once, and fourteen imāms named him مُتَأَوِّل.

His case opens the book’s closing principle: the ruling on an act and the ruling on a particular man are two rulings — which is what مَوَانِعُ تَكْفِيرِ المُعَيَّن is for.

Lesson 15 →

12 The ninth nullifier is claiming to stand outside the sharīʿah. How does the deviant’s own proof-text — al-Khiḍr — refute him?

Al-Khiḍr was a نَبِيٌّ مُسْتَقِلّ with his own waḥy, never under Mūsā’s sharīʿah — so he is no precedent for a man who claims to rise above the Prophet’s ﷺ law on a feeling «حَدَّثَنِي قَلْبِي عَنْ رَبِّي». Al-Khiḍr had revelation; the claimant has a feeling. And the heart is not maʿṣūm: «المَعْصُومُ مَا ثَبَتَ عَنِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ أَوْ جَاءَ بِهِ الكِتَاب».

Lesson 16 →

13 The tenth and last nullifier is turning away (الإِعْرَاض). Where does Ibn Bāz locate the real deficiency of our age, and what sign does he give?

Not in access to knowledge — «أَمَّا العِلْمُ فَمُتَيَسِّر» — but «ضَعْفُ الطَّالِبِ وَإِعْرَاضُه», in action and will. The sign of good willed for a person is الفِقْهُ فِي الدِّين — «مَنْ يُرِدِ اللهُ بِهِ خَيْرًا يُفَقِّهْهُ فِي الدِّين» (muttafaq, from Muʿāwiyah ؓ): people are between مَحْرُوم and مَرْحُوم. And there is a fitness in it — the student who reached and studied the tenth nullifier has, by that act, done the opposite of it.

Lesson 17 →

الجُزْءُ الخَامِس · مَوَانِعُ تَكْفِيرِ المُعَيَّن

Part 5 — The Barriers to Takfīr of a Particular Person · The capstone · Lessons 18–20

14 What is the one distinction the whole capstone exists to draw — and which earlier nullifier already pointed to it?

Between the ruling on the act (the النَّوْع — that it is kufr) and the ruling on a particular person (the المُعَيَّن — that this man is a disbeliever). They are not the same ruling: takfīr of a named person requires its conditions met and its barriers absent. The eighth nullifier, with the spy Ḥāṭib ؓ, already pointed here.

Lesson 18 →

15 The matn names only one barrier — coercion. State its daleel and Ibn Bāz’s two conditions.

Daleel: إِلَّا مَنْ أُكْرِهَ وَقَلْبُهُ مُطْمَئِنٌّ بِالإِيمَان (al-Naḥl 16:106), revealed about ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir ؓ. The two conditions: الإِكْرَاه — that he genuinely cannot escape — and الطُّمَأْنِينَةُ بِالإِيمَان, the heart’s rest upon faith. Remove either — no coercion, or a heart gone over — and «بَاءَ بِإِثْمِ ذَلِك».

Lesson 18 →

16 Does coercion excuse everything? Name its two limits.

No. Acting for a job, a rank or wealth is not coercion — «فَهَذَا كُلُّهُ مُنْكَرٌ وَلَيْسَ بِعُذْر». And coercion never makes lawful the killing of a maʿṣūm, by the agreement of the Muslims: «فَلَيْسَ حِفْظُ نَفْسِهِ بِقَتْلِ ذَلِكَ المَعْصُومِ أَوْلَى مِنَ العَكْس». You may speak a word to save your life; you may not take a protected life to save it.

Lesson 18 →

17 The barrier of ignorance turns on one distinction. State it, and where ignorance does not excuse.

Between الأُمُورُ الظَّاهِرَة — the plain matters known from the religion by necessity (that grave-worship is shirk) — where ignorance is no excuse for one among Muslims reached by the Qurʾān; and الأُمُورُ الدَّقِيقَةُ الخَفِيَّة, the subtle hidden matters, where it can. The man who called on other than Allah «يَكُونُ مُشْرِكًا... لِأَنَّ عَلَيْهِ أَنْ يَتَعَلَّم».

Lesson 19 →

18 Distinguish البَيَان from التَّبَيُّن — and say why making the wrong one the condition would wreck the whole matter.

On al-Tawbah 9:115: what is obligatory is the bayān — that the proof be conveyed and made clear — «الوَاجِبُ قِيَامُ الحُجَّةِ فَقَط», even if tabayyun (the person’s actually grasping it) never follows. Make tabayyun the condition and no stubborn man is ever bound by any proof — he need only refuse to be convinced.

Lesson 19 →

19 The man who told his family to burn him was excused. The grave-worshipper is not. What is the difference, exactly?

The burned man denied only a تَفْصِيلًا خَفِيًّا مِنْ تَفَاصِيلِ القُدْرَة — a buried detail of how far Allah’s power reaches — not Allah’s power itself nor the resurrection, and «مِثْلُهُ يُجْهَل». The grave-worshipper denies the plain root. «الَّذِي يُعْذَرُ بِالجَهْلِ هُوَ الَّذِي يَجْهَلُ أَمْرًا دَقِيقًا خَفِيًّا».

Lesson 19 →

20 The report that Muʿādh ؓ prostrated to the Prophet ﷺ is used to stretch the excuse of ignorance over grave-worship. What is the ḥadīth-discipline verdict on it?

The prostration report is weak, even bāṭil — al-Qāsim al-Shaybānī alone narrates it, with great iḍṭirāb (five conflicting chains), and «مِثْلُهُ لَا يَحْتَمِلُ التَّفَرُّد». What is ṣaḥīḥ thābit is the wording «لَوْ كُنْتُ آمِرًا أَحَدًا أَنْ يَسْجُدَ لِأَحَد» — without the prostration. And even granting Muʿādh’s question, it belonged to the earliest Islam before the dīn settled.

Lesson 19 →

21 What is the barrier of honest error (الخَطَأ), with the sharḥ’s example?

On al-Baqarah 2:286: a word of kufr may leave a Muslim’s tongue with no will behind it — «فَلَا يَكْفُرُ بِهَا؛ لِعَدَمِ القَصْد». The man who, from the intensity of joy at his found mount, meant to say «You are my Lord and I am Your servant» and said instead «اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ عَبْدِي وَأَنَا رَبُّك» — the gravest inversion, and no kufr, because the heart never moved toward it.

Lesson 20 →

22 Interpretation (التَّأْوِيل) is the barrier most easily abused, so it is cut both ways. Give both edges.

A corrupt taʾwīl of a clear text is no excuse — «التَّأْوِيلُ الفَاسِدُ... لَيْسَ عُذْرًا؛ بَلْ زِيَادَةٌ فِي الكُفْر»; Iblīs reasoned against the plain command and was not excused but sank deeper, and the Khawārij interpreted sincerely, «بِاجْتِهَادٍ وَتَقَرُّبٍ إِلَى الله», yet مَرَقُوا from Islām.

But a taʾwīl in a genuine place of ikhtilāf and shubhah does excuse — the woman who uncovers her face thinking it allowed is «عَاصِيَة», not a disbeliever; one who denies the siḥr ḥadīth is «مُخْطِئ», not cast out — while denying a plain matter after bayān and persisting is kufr.

Lesson 20 →

23 Read over all four barriers, state in one line what the capstone — and the whole book — was built to establish.

The ruling on an act and the ruling on a man are two rulings. A deed may be a nullifier in its very type, and the person who did it may yet not be a disbeliever — compelled, ignorant of a hidden matter, slipping, or holding a doubt a doubt can honestly hold. The barriers are real; they are also only barriers — lift them, with a cause of takfīr established, and the ruling falls. «إِذَا وُجِدَ مُكَفِّرٌ كَفَر».

Lesson 20 →

دَارُ الهِجْرَة — المَدْرَسَةُ السَّلَفِيَّةُ التَّأْسِيسِيَّةُ لِلْعُلُومِ الشَّرْعِيَّة

نَوَاقِضُ الإِسْلَام of Imām Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb · explained from the words of Shaykh Ibn Bāz · in our own words

لِأَغْرَاضِ التَّعْلِيمِ فَقَطْ — هَذَا المَوْقِعُ لَا يُفْتِي. لِلْفَتَاوَى، يُرْجَعُ إِلَى العُلَمَاءِ المَوْثُوقِين.
