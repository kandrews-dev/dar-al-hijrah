النَّاقِضُ التَّاسِع · الخُرُوجُ عَنِ الشَّرِيعَة

The Ninth Nullifier — Claiming to Stand Outside the Sharīʿah

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • State the ninth nullifier in the matn’s own words, and the ruling on one who falls into it.
  • Explain why the al-Khiḍr story is no precedent for standing outside the sharīʿah — what al-Khiḍr was, and what he was not.
  • Give the two verses that establish the Prophet’s ﷺ message is universal, and why that closes the door this clause guards.
  • State the rule on the awliyāʾ and the anbiyāʾ — who is above whom, and in what walāyah actually consists.
  • Refute إِسْقَاطُ التَّكْلِيف — the claim that gnosis lifts worship — from al-Ḥijr 15:99 and from the state of the Messengers themselves.
  • Answer «حَدَّثَنِي قَلْبِي عَنْ رَبِّي»: is the heart an authority, and what is?
  • Give the footnote’s contemporary application — who holds that the People of the Book need not follow Muḥammad ﷺ, and why it is «ضَلَالٌ مُبِين».

مِنْ كَلَامِ الشَّيْخِ ابْنِ بَاز · ص ٢٢٣–٢٢٧
Subul al-Salām Sharḥ Nawāqiḍ al-Islām — pp. 223–227, gathering Ibn Bāz’s words on this clause.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

التَّاسِع: مَنِ اعْتَقَدَ أَنَّ بَعْضَ النَّاسِ يَسَعُهُ الخُرُوجُ عَنْ شَرِيعَةِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ كَمَا وَسِعَ الخَضِرَ الخُرُوجُ عَنْ شَرِيعَةِ مُوسَى عَلَيْهِ السَّلَام.

Read what the clause actually says before the explanation: it is not about disobeying the sharīʿah — it is about believing one is entitled to stand outside it, and it reaches for the one story in the Qurʾān that looks, to a careless eye, like a precedent.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«Whoever holds that it is permissible for him to exit the sharīʿah of Muḥammad ﷺ, as al-Khiḍr was permitted to exit the sharīʿah of Mūsā — فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ بِإِجْمَاعِ أَهْلِ العِلْم: he is a disbeliever by the consensus of the people of knowledge. He is asked to repent and the evidences are made clear to him; if he repents, else he is killed.»

The clause names a claim, not a lapse. A man who sins against the sharīʿah while holding it binding over him is a sinner. A man who holds that the sharīʿah does not reach him at all — that he has climbed above it — has stepped outside the religion, because he has denied the one thing that makes him a Muslim: that Muḥammad ﷺ was sent to him.

قِصَّةُ الخَضِرِ لَا تَنْفَعُهُ The al-Khiḍr Story Is No Refuge for Him

This is the heart of the clause, and it is the kind of answer a teacher savours: the deviant’s own proof-text is the refutation of him. The claim — made, Ibn Bāz says, by «كَثِيرٌ مِنَ الصُّوفِيَّةِ الضَّالِّينَ الزَّنَادِقَة», the deviant zanādiqah of the Sufis — is that a man may receive knowledge directly from Allah «مِنْ طَرِيقِ التَّحْدِيثِ الَّذِي يَقَعُ فِي القَلْب», be told and commanded and forbidden by his Lord in his heart, and so dispense with following the Messenger ﷺ, just as al-Khiḍr acted on a knowledge Mūsā did not have.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Ibn Bāz dismantles the analogy at its root: «فَإِنَّ الخَضِرَ لَيْسَ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيل، وَلَيْسَ مُوسَى مَبْعُوثًا إِلَيْه؛ فَالخَضِرُ نَبِيٌّ مُسْتَقِلٌّ لَيْسَ لَهُ تَعَلُّقٌ بِمُوسَى، وَمُوسَى إِنَّمَا بُعِثَ إِلَى بَنِي إِسْرَائِيل.»

So al-Khiḍr never was «outside Mūsā’s sharīʿah» in the sense the Sufi needs. He was never under it to begin with. He was an independent prophet with his own revelation from Allah — «وَلَهُ نُبُوَّة، وَلَهُ وَحْيٌ مِنْ جِهَةِ اللهِ غَيْرُ مَا جَاءَ بِهِ مُوسَى». The story is not a man bypassing a sharīʿah by way of his heart; it is two prophets, each carrying revelation.

إِنَّكَ عَلَى عِلْمٍ مِنْ عِلْمِ اللهِ عَلَّمَكَهُ اللهُ لَا أَعْلَمُهُ أَنَا، وَأَنَا عَلَى عِلْمٍ مِنْ عِلْمِ اللهِ عَلَّمَنِيهِ لَا تَعْلَمُهُ أَنْتَ

You are upon a knowledge from Allah’s knowledge which He taught you that I do not know; and I am upon a knowledge from Allah’s knowledge which He taught me that you do not know.

قَوْلُ الخَضِرِ لِمُوسَى، مِنْ حَدِيثِهِمَا المَشْهُورِ — مُتَّفَقٌ عَلَيْهِ؛ the sharḥ quotes it from the well-known story and grades it no further, so the page names only its home in the Ṣaḥīḥayn.

Notice what al-Khiḍr’s own sentence does to the argument. Each of the two had a knowledge from Allah — by revelation, by prophethood. Neither was leaning on a whisper in the breast. The man who cites this story to justify «حَدَّثَنِي قَلْبِي عَنْ رَبِّي» is quoting the very proof that sinks him: al-Khiḍr had waḥy; he has a feeling.

رِسَالَةٌ عَامَّة A Message Sent to All

Behind the correction sits the doctrine the whole clause guards. Every prophet before our Prophet ﷺ was sent to his own people specifically — «وَكُلُّ نَبِيٍّ قَبْلَ نَبِيِّنَا يُبْعَثُ إِلَى قَوْمِهِ خَاصَّة». Muḥammad ﷺ was not.

قُلْ يَا أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ إِنِّي رَسُولُ اللَّهِ إِلَيْكُمْ جَمِيعًا

Say: O mankind, I am the Messenger of Allah to you all.

سُورَةُ الأَعْرَاف · 7:158

وَمَا أَرْسَلْنَاكَ إِلَّا كَافَّةً لِّلنَّاسِ بَشِيرًا وَنَذِيرًا

And We have not sent you except to all of mankind, as a bringer of good tidings and a warner.

سُورَةُ سَبَأ · 34:28

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«أَمَّا مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ فَقَدْ بَعَثَهُ اللهُ لِلنَّاسِ عَامَّة: مِنَ الجِنِّ وَالإِنْس، وَالعَرَبِ وَالعَجَم، وَالذُّكُورِ وَالإِنَاث، وَالأَغْنِيَاءِ وَالفُقَرَاء، وَالحُكَّامِ وَالمَحْكُومِين؛ كُلُّهُمْ مَأْمُورُونَ بِاتِّبَاعِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ، وَكُلُّهُمْ مَأْمُورُونَ بِإِخْلَاصِ العِبَادَةِ لِلهِ وَحْدَه.»

So there is no one left over the edge of this message to whom a private path could run. The universality is exactly what forecloses the claim: if the Messenger ﷺ was sent to «all mankind», then «some people are outside his sharīʿah» is not a lesser error — it is a denial of the verse. «فَمَنْ خَرَجَ عَنْ طَرِيقَةِ مُحَمَّدٍ وَعَنْ شَرِيعَتِهِ فَقَدْ كَفَرَ بِاللهِ وَضَلَّ عَنْ سَوَاءِ السَّبِيل.»

الأَوْلِيَاءُ دُونَ الأَنْبِيَاء The Saints Are Below the Prophets, Never Above

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«وَالأَوْلِيَاءُ لَيْسُوا أَفْضَلَ مِنَ الأَنْبِيَاء، بَلِ الأَنْبِيَاءُ هُمْ أَفْضَلُ النَّاسِ وَهُمْ خَيْرُ النَّاس، ثُمَّ بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ طَبَقَاتُ المُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَى تَفَاوُتِهِم؛ فَمَنْ زَعَمَ أَنَّ الوَلِيَّ يَكُونُ أَفْضَلَ مِنَ النَّبِيِّ فَقَدْ ضَلَّ وَزَعَمَ قَوْلًا بَاطِلًا.»

And here is the sentence that turns the whole thing right-side up: the saints «إِنَّمَا كَانُوا مَحْمُودِينَ وَلَهُمُ الثَّوَابُ العَظِيمُ إِذَا كَانُوا مِنْ أَتْبَاعِ الأَنْبِيَاء؛ فَكَيْفَ يَكُونُونَ فَوْقَ الأَنْبِيَاء؟!»

Read the logic, because it inverts the Sufi’s picture exactly. Walāyah is not a ladder that rises past prophethood and out the top. It is constituted by following — «فَإِنَّ فَضْلَهُمْ وَكَمَالَ إِيمَانِهِمْ أَنْ يَكُونُوا مُتَّبِعِينَ لِلْأَنْبِيَاء، سَائِرِينَ خَلْفَ الأَنْبِيَاء، لَيْسُوا خَارِجِينَ عَنِ الأَنْبِيَاء». The closer a believer’s following, the stronger his walāyah — so a «saint» who steps outside the sharīʿah has not risen above walāyah; he has destroyed the only thing it was ever made of.

لَا يَسْقُطُ التَّكْلِيف No Station Lifts the Obligation

From the same root grows the claim the sharḥ spends its last folios on: that when a man reaches «المَعْرِفَةُ الكَامِلَة», complete gnosis, taklīf drops from him — he no longer prays, nor fasts, «وَلَا وَلَا وَلَا». Ibn Bāz: «هَذَا مِنَ الضَّلَالِ البَعِيد، وَالكُفْرِ الوَاضِح».

الشَّرْح · the explanation

His refutation is one clean line of reasoning, and it needs no ijmāʿ to carry it: «بَلْ هُوَ لَا يَزَالُ مُكَلَّفًا مَأْمُورًا مَنْهِيًّا وَإِنْ كَانَ أَعْلَمَ النَّاس؛ فَالرُّسُلُ أَعْلَمُ النَّاسِ وَهُمْ مُكَلَّفُون، فَمَنْ دُونَهُمْ مِنْ بَابِ أَوْلَى.»

If the highest knowledge there has ever been — the knowledge of the Messengers — did not lift the prayer from them, then no lesser knowledge lifts it from anyone. The ʿabd «مَأْمُورٌ بِالاسْتِمْرَارِ فِي طَاعَةِ اللهِ حَتَّى يَجِيئَهُ المَوْتُ أَوْ يَذْهَبَ عَقْلُه» — there is no threshold of knowledge at which obligation ends.

وَاعْبُدْ رَبَّكَ حَتَّىٰ يَأْتِيَكَ الْيَقِينُ

And worship your Lord until there comes to you the certainty [i.e. death].

سُورَةُ الحِجْر · 15:99

«الْيَقِين» here is death — «يَعْنِي: حَتَّى يَأْتِيَ المَوْت». Allah said it to His Prophet ﷺ himself, the most knowing and most honoured of creation. If worship ran «until certainty» for him, no servant reaches a certainty that excuses him from it.

القَلْبُ غَيْرُ مَعْصُوم The Heart Is Not Infallible

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — قَالَ بَعْضُهُم: حَدَّثَنِي قَلْبِي عَنْ رَبِّي، وَالمُتَّبِعُ يَقُول: عَنْ رَسُولِ اللهِ — فَمَا الفَرْق؟

Ibn Bāz — «هَذَا هُوَ الهَلَاك، وَهَذَا هُوَ الإِلْحَادُ وَالفَسَاد! وَهَلْ قَلْبُهُ مَعْصُوم؟! أَذْوَاقُهُ وَآرَاؤُهُ وَخَوَاطِرُه — يَخْطُرُ فِي قَلْبِهِ الشَّرُّ وَالخَيْر، وَيَخْطُرُ فِيهِ الفَسَادُ وَغَيْرُ الفَسَاد؛ فَالقَلْبُ غَيْرُ مَعْصُوم، وَإِنَّمَا المَعْصُومُ مَا ثَبَتَ عَنِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ أَوْ جَاءَ بِهِ الكِتَاب.»

That is the exact line the whole nullifier defends, and it is worth memorising as the answer to every «my heart told me» there will ever be. The breast is not a protected source. Good and evil both pass through it. What is protected — maʿṣūm — is the Book and what is authentically established from the Messenger ﷺ, and nothing else. «فَمَنْ جَعَلَ الطَّرِيقَ هَذَا: حَدَّثَنِي قَلْبِي عَنْ رَبِّي، أَوْ قَالَ شَيْخِي كَذَا — فَهَذَا قَدْ سَلَكَ طَرِيقَ الشَّيْطَان، وَلَمْ يَسْلُكْ طَرِيقَ الرَّحْمَن.»

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Q — هَلْ يُصَلَّى خَلْفَهُم؟

Ibn Bāz — «مَنْ كَانَ بِهَذِهِ المَثَابَةِ لَا يُصَلَّى خَلْفَه؛ لَيْسَ بِمُسْلِم. مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى عَنِ الرَّسُولِ لَيْسَ بِمُسْلِم؛ مَنْ قَالَ: إِنَّهُ يَسْتَغْنِي عَنِ الرَّسُولِ بِأَذْوَاقِهِ وَآرَائِهِ وَمَا يَقَعُ فِي قَلْبِهِ فَهُوَ خَارِجٌ مِنَ الشَّرِيعَة، غَيْرُ مُسْلِم.»

تَطْبِيقٌ مُعَاصِر A Living Application

The compiler closes the clause by pointing it at his own day — and not at the Sufis this time.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

«This nullifier is fallen into by some writers who claim the People of the Book are not obliged to follow the sharīʿah of Muḥammad ﷺ, taking as their pretext that their acknowledgement of paying the jizyah and their submission exempts them from the obligation of entering Islām — وَهَذَا ضَلَالٌ مُبِين; their being left upon the jizyah is no evidence that they are upon the truth.»

See how the clause has turned. It opened on a man claiming to stand above the sharīʿah by a secret knowledge; it closes on men leaving a whole community outside it by a legal excuse. Both are the same nullifier, because both deny the one thing it exists to protect: that the sharīʿah of Muḥammad ﷺ reaches everyone, with no one standing over it and no one left out from under it.

True or False صَحٌ أَمْ خَطَأٌ
مَنِ اعْتَقَدَ أَنَّهُ يَسَعُهُ الخُرُوجُ عَنْ شَرِيعَةِ مُحَمَّدٍ ﷺ فَهُوَ كَافِرٌ بِإِجْمَاعِ أَهْلِ العِلْم. Whoever believes he may stand outside the sharīʿah of Muḥammad ﷺ is a disbeliever by the consensus of the people of knowledge.
كَانَ الخَضِرُ مِنْ بَنِي إِسْرَائِيلَ وَتَحْتَ شَرِيعَةِ مُوسَى. al-Khiḍr was of Banū Isrāʾīl and under the sharīʿah of Mūsā.
بُعِثَ مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ إِلَى النَّاسِ كَافَّة: جِنِّهِمْ وَإِنْسِهِمْ، عَرَبِهِمْ وَعَجَمِهِم. Muḥammad ﷺ was sent to all people — their jinn and mankind, Arab and non-Arab.
قَدْ يَكُونُ الوَلِيُّ أَفْضَلَ مِنَ النَّبِيِّ إِذَا كَمُلَتْ مَعْرِفَتُه. A saint may be superior to a prophet once his gnosis is complete.
إِذَا بَلَغَ العَبْدُ المَعْرِفَةَ الكَامِلَةَ سَقَطَ عَنْهُ التَّكْلِيف. When a servant attains complete gnosis, obligation drops from him.
القَلْبُ غَيْرُ مَعْصُوم، وَإِنَّمَا المَعْصُومُ الكِتَابُ وَمَا ثَبَتَ عَنِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ. The heart is not infallible; only the Book and what is established from the Messenger ﷺ are protected.
مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى عَنِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ بِأَذْوَاقِهِ وَآرَائِهِ يُصَلَّى خَلْفَه. Prayer is prayed behind one who dispenses with the Messenger ﷺ by his tastes and opinions.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

فَالقَلْبُ غَيْرُ مَعْصُوم، وَإِنَّمَا المَعْصُومُ مَا ثَبَتَ عَنِ الرَّسُولِ ﷺ أَوْ جَاءَ بِهِ الكِتَاب

Ibn Bāz. This one sentence is the answer to every «my heart told me» and every «my shaykh said» there will ever be. The breast is not a protected source — «يَخْطُرُ فِي قَلْبِهِ الشَّرُّ وَالخَيْر». What is protected is the Book and the authentic Sunnah, and nothing besides. And keep beside it why the whole clause stands: the Messenger ﷺ was sent «كَافَّةً لِلنَّاس» — so no one rises above his sharīʿah, and no one is left outside it. The very story the deviant cites, al-Khiḍr’s, refutes him: al-Khiḍr had waḥy; the claimant has a feeling.
