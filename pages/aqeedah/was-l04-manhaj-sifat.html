الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ الرَّابِعYear 3

مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍ وَلَا تَعْطِيل

The Four Doors That Are Closed

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Recite the matn’s four exclusions, and define each one exactly.
  • Give al-Fawzān’s two kinds of تَحْرِيف, with an example of each.
  • State the difference between تَحْرِيف and تَعْطِيل — and which implies the other.
  • Give Imām Mālik’s answer on الاسْتِوَاء and say why it settles the matter.
مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍ وَلَا تَعْطِيل The Four Doors That Are Closed

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَمِنَ الإِيمَانِ بِاللهِ: الإِيمَانُ بِمَا وَصَفَ بِهِ نَفْسَهُ فِي كِتَابِهِ، وَوَصَفَهُ بِهِ رَسُولُهُ مُحَمَّدٌ ﷺ، مِنْ غَيْرِ تَحْرِيفٍ وَلَا تَعْطِيلٍ، وَمِنْ غَيْرِ تَكْيِيفٍ وَلَا تَمْثِيلٍ.

Having listed the six foundations in summary, the author now begins the detail — and he begins with the first of them, belief in Allah. This single sentence is the methodology of the entire book.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

What the sentence requires, in al-Fawzān's words: that we affirm the attributes for Him as they came in the Book and the Sunnah, in their wordings and their meanings — without distorting the wordings, without emptying the meanings, and without likening them to the attributes of created things. And that in affirming them we rely on the Book and the Sunnah only, going beyond neither the Qurʾān nor the ḥadīth — because this subject is تَوْقِيفِيَّة: it is received, not reasoned out.

Then he takes the four exclusions one at a time.

١ · التَّحْرِيف Distortion

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّحْرِيف is to turn a thing away from its proper course to something else. And it comes in two kinds.

(a) تَحْرِيفُ اللَّفْظِ — distorting the wording. Turning the word aside by adding a word or a letter, removing one, or changing a vowel. al-Fawzān's three examples are exact, and each is a different mechanism:

The revealed wordWhat they made itThe move
الرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى العَرْشِ اسْتَوَىاسْتَوْلَى — “took control”a letter added
وَجَاءَ رَبُّكَأَمْرُ رَبِّكَ — “your Lord’s command”a word added
وَكَلَّمَ اللهُ مُوسَى تَكْلِيمًاوَكَلَّمَ اللهَ مُوسَى — making Mūsā the speakera single vowel changed: rafʿ to naṣb

Look at the third. One ḍammah turned into a fatḥah, and the one who spoke and the one spoken to have swapped places. If you ever wondered why this school insists on full ḥarakāt and on iʿrāb, here is the answer: the creed can turn on a vowel.

(b) تَحْرِيفُ المَعْنَى — distorting the meaning. Turning the word from its face and its reality, and giving it another word's meaning. As the people of innovation say: that the meaning of mercy is “the will to bestow favour,” and the meaning of anger is “the will to take revenge.” The word is left standing; its content has been swapped out underneath it.

٢ · التَّعْطِيل Divestment

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّعْطِيل in the language is emptying — you say عَطَّلَهُ, meaning you left it empty. Here it means negating the attributes of Allah.

And al-Fawzān draws the distinction from taḥrīf precisely, which is the part students most often blur:

تَحْرِيف — negates the correct meaning the texts indicate and substitutes another for it.

تَعْطِيل — negates the correct meaning without substituting anything, as the مُفَوِّضَة do.

So: every muḥarrif is a muʿaṭṭil, but not every muʿaṭṭil is a muḥarrif.

That one line organises a whole field. The distorter and the divester both end in the same place — the attribute as Allah stated it is gone — but only one of them has put something in its place.

٣ · التَّكْيِيف Specifying the How

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّكْيِيف is to determine the manner of the attribute — to assign it a known “how.” And al-Fawzān gives the reason this is impossible rather than merely forbidden, and the reasoning is clean:

  1. The how of Allah’s attributes is knowledge Allah kept to Himself — so there is no road to reaching it.
  2. And the attribute follows the Essence. Just as no human can know the how of Allah’s Essence, so the how of His attributes cannot be known either.

Which is exactly what Imām Mālik رَحِمَهُ اللهُ answered when a man asked him about الرَّحْمَنُ عَلَى العَرْشِ اسْتَوَى — how did He rise? His reply has served the ummah ever since:

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

الاسْتِوَاءُ مَعْلُومٌ، وَالكَيْفُ مَجْهُولٌ، وَالإِيمَانُ بِهِ وَاجِبٌ، وَالسُّؤَالُ عَنْهُ بِدْعَةٌ.

الإِمَامُ مَالِكٌ رَحِمَهُ اللهُ — “The rising is known, the how is unknown, belief in it is obligatory, and asking about it is an innovation.” And al-Fawzān notes: the same is said of every one of the attributes.

Four clauses, and each answers a different error. Known — against the divester who says it means nothing. The how is unknown — against the one who likens. Belief in it is obligatory — against the one who suspends judgement. Asking about it is an innovation — against the one who will not stop until he has a diagram.

٤ · التَّمْثِيل Likening

الشَّرْح · the explanation

التَّمْثِيل is likening: to say that Allah's attributes are like the attributes of created things — as one might say, Allah's hand is like our hands, His hearing is like our hearing. Allah is exalted far above that.

لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ

"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."

Sūrat al-Shūrā 42:11 · سورة الشورى

الشَّرْح · the explanation

And notice the construction of that āyah, because it is the whole creed in one verse: لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ negates likeness, and وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ, in the very same breath, affirms hearing and sight. Negation of resemblance and affirmation of the attribute, side by side. Whoever takes only the first half becomes a divester; whoever takes only the second becomes a likener.

So it is not said of His attributes that they are like ours, or resemble ours, or are as ours — just as it is not said that His Essence is like or resembles our essences.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān closes the section by setting the three parties side by side, and the summary is worth keeping as a test you can apply to any statement you read about the attributes:

PartyWhat he does with the attributes
the believing muwaḥḥidaffirms them all, in the manner befitting Allah’s greatness and majesty
the muʿaṭṭilnegates them, or negates some of them
the mumaththilaffirms them — but in a manner that does not befit Allah, and befits only the creature

Only one of the three both affirms and befits. That is the narrow road the Wāsiṭiyyah walks for the rest of the book — and you now have the four markers that keep you on it.

You studied these four doors topically in Unit 4 of the ʿAqīdah track. Here they arrive where they belong: in the author's own sentence, as the method he announces before bringing a single attribute.

What is the difference between taḥrīf and taʿṭīl?

They are the same thing
Taḥrīf negates the correct meaning and substitutes another; taʿṭīl negates it without substituting — so every muḥarrif is a muʿaṭṭil, but not the reverse
Taʿṭīl substitutes a meaning; taḥrīf does not
Taḥrīf concerns the Qur’ān only, taʿṭīl the Sunnah

Because the scholars forbade asking

مَنْ أَثْبَتَ الصِّفَاتِ عَلَى وَجْهٍ لَا يَلِيقُ بِاللهِ فَهُوَ مُمَثِّلٌ. Whoever affirms the attributes in a manner unbefitting Allah is a mumaththil.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

الاسْتِوَاءُ مَعْلُومٌ، وَالكَيْفُ مَجْهُولٌ، وَالإِيمَانُ بِهِ وَاجِبٌ، وَالسُّؤَالُ عَنْهُ بِدْعَةٌ

Imām Mālik’s four clauses. Memorise them in order — every one of them is doing work, and between them they close all four doors the author named.
