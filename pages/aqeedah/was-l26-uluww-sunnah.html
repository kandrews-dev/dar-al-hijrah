العُلُوُّ فِي السُّنَّةِ · رُقْيَةُ المَرِيض

The Highness in the Sunnah — the Ruqyah of the Sick

شَرْحُ الشَّيْخِ صَالِحٍ الفَوْزَان · ص ٩٩–١٠١
Sharḥ al-ʿAqīdah al-Wāsiṭiyyah, Ṣāliḥ al-Fawzān — pp. 99–101.

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Give the two readings of الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ and why either one yields the ʿuluww.
  • Explain why تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ covers all of Allah’s names.
  • Distinguish the two kinds of رَحْمَةُ اللهِ, and say which one is asked to be sent down.
  • List the six things by which this duʿāʾ performs التَّوَسُّل.
٦ · إِثْبَاتُ عُلُوِّ اللهِ عَلَى خَلْقِهِ وَاسْتِوَائِهِ عَلَى عَرْشِه Section 6 — His Highness above His Creation, and His Istiwāʾ

This is the longest section of Part 2, and the author assembles four separate texts for it. This lesson takes the first two; the next takes the ḥadīth of the Throne above all created things, and the ḥadīth of the slave-girl. Note that the section pairs the ʿuluww and the istiwāʾ exactly as Lesson 16 did from the Qurʾān — the Sunnah is here saying what the Qurʾān said, which is the fourth function of the Sunnah from Lesson 22.

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ فِي رُقْيَةِ المَرِيضِ: «رَبَّنَا اللهُ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ، أَمْرُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالأَرْضِ، كَمَا رَحْمَتُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ اجْعَلْ رَحْمَتَكَ فِي الأَرْضِ، اغْفِرْ لَنَا حَوْبَنَا وَخَطَايَانَا، أَنْتَ رَبُّ الطَّيِّبِينَ، أَنْزِلْ رَحْمَةً مِنْ رَحْمَتِكَ وَشِفَاءً مِنْ شِفَائِكَ عَلَى هَذَا الوَجَعِ فَيَبْرَأَ» حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ رَوَاهُ أَبُو دَاوُدَ وَغَيْرُهُ.

رَبَّنَا اللهُ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ، أَمْرُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالأَرْضِ، كَمَا رَحْمَتُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ اجْعَلْ رَحْمَتَكَ فِي الأَرْضِ، اغْفِرْ لَنَا حَوْبَنَا وَخَطَايَانَا، أَنْتَ رَبُّ الطَّيِّبِينَ، أَنْزِلْ رَحْمَةً مِنْ رَحْمَتِكَ وَشِفَاءً مِنْ شِفَائِكَ عَلَى هَذَا الوَجَعِ فَيَبْرَأَ

"Our Lord is Allah, who is above the heaven. Hallowed be Your name. Your command is in the heaven and the earth. As Your mercy is in the heaven, place Your mercy in the earth. Forgive us our sin and our wrongs. You are the Lord of the pure. Send down a mercy from Your mercy and a healing from Your healing upon this pain, that it may be cured."

حَدِيثٌ حَسَنٌ — graded ḥasan by the author; reported by Abū Dāwūd and others

الشَّرْح · the explanation

فِي رُقْيَةِ المَرِيضِ — that is: the reciting over the sick person, seeking his cure. And al-Fawzān states the ruling in one sentence, which is worth having before the creed: it is legislated when it is with the Qurʾān and with permitted supplications, and forbidden when it is with words of shirk or acts of shirk.

So the author's proof-text for the ʿuluww is a bedside duʿāʾ. That is itself part of the argument: this doctrine is not an abstraction defended in debate. It is what a Muslim says out loud over a sick child.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

رَبَّنَا اللهُ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ — that is: عَلَى السَّمَاءِ. And al-Fawzān gives the two ways of taking the فِي, both of which arrive at the same place:

١ فِي = عَلَى“above the heaven” — as in فَسِيحُوا فِي الْأَرْضِ, “travel upon the earth” (al-Tawbah 9:2), where nobody reads it as travelling inside the earth
٢ فِي in its own adverbial senseand then what is meant by “the heaven” is absolute height — i.e. “in the loftiness”

You met both readings in Lesson 16 on al-Mulk 67:16. The point of giving two is that the doctrine does not depend on winning a grammatical argument. Whichever way the particle is taken, Allah is above — and no reading available in Arabic puts Him inside a created heaven.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ — that is: Your names are hallowed above every deficiency. And then a point of grammar with real reach: it is a singular annexed, so it takes in all of the names of Allah.

A singular noun in a possessive construction generalises in Arabic. So the worshipper does not hallow one name and leave the rest; اسْمُكَ hallows every name He has — which is why the clause is enough on its own.

أَمْرُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالأَرْضِ — and al-Fawzān splits the command into the two kinds the student has met before: Your الكَوْنِيُّ القَدَرِيّ command, from which all created things and all events arise — and of it is His saying:

إِنَّمَا أَمْرُهُ إِذَا أَرَادَ شَيْئًا أَنْ يَقُولَ لَهُ كُنْ فَيَكُونُ

"His command, when He intends a thing, is only that He says to it: be — and it is."

Sūrat Yā-Sīn 36:82 · سُورَةُ يَس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

— and Your الشَّرْعِيّ command, comprising the legislations which He legislated for His servants.

The same division as the two kinds of irādah in Lesson 11 and the two kinds of ʿibādah on the Islam 101 track: what He decrees, and what He commands. The duʿāʾ says His command runs in the heaven and the earth — and both senses are true at once.

كَمَا رَحْمَتُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ اجْعَلْ رَحْمَتَكَ فِي الأَرْضِ — this is a seeking of nearness to Him by His mercy, which encompassed all the people of the heavens, that He should make a share of it for the people of the earth.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

اغْفِرْ لَنَا حَوْبَنَا وَخَطَايَانَا — this is a request for forgiveness; and المَغْفِرَة is the covering over, and the shielding from the sin.

And then the etymology, which is the kind of detail that makes a word impossible to forget: and from it is المِغْفَر — the helmet worn upon the head, because it covers it and shields it from the blow.

So maghfirah is not an erasure of the record; the root is armour. To ask Allah's forgiveness is to ask Him to put something between you and what your sin would otherwise do to you.

الحَوْب — is the sin; and الخَطَايَا are the wrongs. أَنْتَ رَبُّ الطَّيِّبِينَ — this is another seeking of nearness; and al-ṭayyibīn is the plural of طَيِّب, and they are the prophets and their followers. And the ascribing of His lordship to these is an ascription of honour and ennobling — otherwise He سُبْحَانَهُ is the Lord of everything and its Owner.

That last note is the same precision al-Fawzān applied to عَلَى عَبْدِهِ in Lesson 15: naming a particular group as His does not narrow His lordship — it honours them.

نَوْعَا رَحْمَةِ اللهِ The Two Kinds of Allah’s Mercy

الشَّرْح · the explanation

أَنْزِلْ رَحْمَةً مِنْ رَحْمَتِكَ — that is: the created mercy. And here al-Fawzān makes a distinction the student must have, or half the texts of mercy will be misread: the mercy of Allah is of two kinds:

النَّوْعُ الأَوَّلHis mercy which is an attribute of His attributes — as in وَرَحْمَتِي وَسِعَتْ كُلَّ شَيْءٍ (al-Aʿrāf 7:156)
النَّوْعُ الثَّانِيa mercy ascribed to Him by the ascription of the created thing to its Creator — as in this ḥadīth, and as in the ḥadīth «خَلَقَ اللهُ مِائَةَ رَحْمَةٍ», Allah created a hundred mercies (reported by Muslim and al-Tirmidhī)

The test is the one al-Fawzān applies: a mercy that is created, or sent down, or counted, is of the second kind. An attribute is not created, not sent down, and not numbered at a hundred. So when the duʿāʾ says send down a mercy from Your mercy, it is asking for the created thing that comes from the attribute — which is why the wording is مِنْ رَحْمَتِكَ and not merely رَحْمَتَكَ.

So he ﷺ asked his Lord to send down this mercy upon the sick person, for his need of it, that He might cure him by it.

And the shāhid from the ḥadīth: the affirming of al-ʿuluww for Allah تَعَالَى, and that He is above the heaven; and al-ʿuluww is an attribute of the Essence, as came before. That last clause is the distinction from Lesson 16 restated: ʿuluww belongs to the Essence, istiwāʾ to the Actions.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

al-Fawzān then counts what else is in the duʿāʾ, and the list is a lesson in how to praise Allah before asking Him. In the ḥadīth is the seeking of nearness to Allah تَعَالَى by praising Him —

by His lordship (رَبَّنَا) · His godhood (اللهُ) · His holiness (تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ) · His highness (الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ) · the generality of His command (أَمْرُكَ فِي السَّمَاءِ وَالأَرْضِ) · and His mercy — and then in the ḥadīth is the request for forgiveness from Allah, and the healing of the illness.

Six praises, then two requests. The creed is not an appendix to the duʿāʾ; it is the first two-thirds of it.

«وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ» "And I Am the Trustee of Him Who Is above the Heaven"

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ ﷺ: «أَلَا تَأْمَنُونِي وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ».

أَلَا تَأْمَنُونِي وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ

"Will you not trust me, when I am the trustee of Him who is above the heaven?"

al-Fawzān comments on it without restating a source at this point

الشَّرْح · the explanation

تَنْبِيهٌ — al-Fawzān explains this ḥadīth’s wording without restating a source or a grading for it here, having given gradings for the texts around it. This page therefore does not supply a grading of its own, per the rule set out in Lesson 22: the ḥadīth is among those the author brought under his own condition — the authentic aḥādīth which the people of knowledge received with acceptance — and the ʿuluww it evidences rests on the other three texts of this section as well.

أَلَا تَأْمَنُونِي — this is an address from him ﷺ to the one who objected against him regarding some of his distribution of wealth. And أَلَا is a particle of opening and of calling to attention.

تَأْمَنُونِي — from الأَمَانَة, and it is the absence of favouritism and of betrayal; that is: will you not trust me in the distribution of the wealth?

وَأَنَا أَمِينُ مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ — and He is Allah سُبْحَانَهُ. He has entrusted me with His revelation, and His message, and the conveying of His law — and that suffices as a testimony to his trustworthiness and his truthfulness ﷺ.

The argument is one a child can follow and an objector cannot escape. The man was questioning the Prophet's ﷺ handling of money. And the reply is: the One above the heaven trusted me with His revelation — and you are worried about the dates?

And the shāhid: the affirming of al-ʿuluww for Allah سُبْحَانَهُ — since he said مَنْ فِي السَّمَاءِ, and the explanation of that phrase came just above.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

رَبَّنَا اللهُ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ تَقَدَّسَ اسْمُكَ

The opening of a duʿāʾ said over the sick — and the author's proof that Allah is above His creation. Learn it as both. A creed you would say at a bedside is a creed you actually hold.
