الوَاسِطِيَّة · الدَّرْسُ الحَادِي وَالعِشْرُونYear 3

رُؤْيَةُ المُؤْمِنِينَ لِرَبِّهِمْ — وَخَاتِمَةُ القِسْمِ الأَوَّل

The Believers Seeing Their Lord — and the Close of Part One

Objectives — أَهْدَافُ الدَّرْس

  • Distinguish نَاضِرَة from نَاظِرَة in al-Qiyāmah 75:22–23, and say what each contributes.
  • Show how the veiling of the disbelievers in al-Muṭaffifīn is a proof for the believers’ seeing.
  • Give the Prophet’s ﷺ own tafsīr of وَزِيَادَةٌ, and where it is established.
  • Answer all three objections of those who deny the ruʾyah.
٢٣ · إِثْبَاتُ رُؤْيَةِ المُؤْمِنِينَ لِرَبِّهِمْ يَوْمَ القِيَامَة Section 23 — The Believers Will See Their Lord on the Day of Rising

مِنَ المَتْن · the author’s own words

وَقَوْلُهُ: ﴿وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَةٌ ۝ إِلَى رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ﴾، ﴿عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنْظُرُونَ﴾، ﴿لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَى وَزِيَادَةٌ﴾، ﴿لَهُمْ مَا يَشَاءُونَ فِيهَا وَلَدَيْنَا مَزِيدٌ﴾. وَهَذَا البَابُ فِي كِتَابِ اللهِ كَثِيرٌ. وَمَنْ تَدَبَّرَ القُرْآنَ طَالِبًا لِلْهُدَى تَبَيَّنَ لَهُ طَرِيقُ الحَقِّ.

وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَةٌ إِلَى رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

"Faces, on that Day, radiant — looking at their Lord."

Sūrat al-Qiyāmah 75:22–23 · سُورَةُ القِيَامَة

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وُجُوهٌ — the faces of the believers; يَوْمَئِذٍ — the Day of Rising.

And now the point al-Fawzān is careful to flag, because the whole āyah turns on two letters. نَاضِرَة — with the ض — is from النَّضَارَة, which is splendour and beauty: that is, soft, fresh, beautiful, luminous, shining. And نَاظِرَة — with the ظ — is from looking.

نَاضِرَة — بالضَّادfrom النَّضَارَة: radiant, fresh, shining — what the faces ARE
نَاظِرَة — بالظَّاءfrom looking — what the faces DO

Two words a careless reader would run together, and the āyah uses both — in that order. The radiance comes first and the looking second, and the second is the cause of the first. This is also a lesson in why the ḥarakāt and the exact letters are not decoration: swap the ض for the ظ and you have lost half the āyah.

إِلَى رَبِّهَا — their Creator — نَاظِرَةٌ — they look at Him with their eyes. And then the attestation, which al-Fawzān states in four stacked layers: as the authentic aḥādīth have come mutawātir with it; and the Ṣaḥābah and the Tābiʿūn and the predecessors of this Ummah were agreed upon it; and the imāms of Islam concurred upon it.

So the shāhid from the noble āyah: the affirming of the believers' seeing of their Lord on the Day of Rising.

عَلَى الْأَرَائِكِ يَنْظُرُونَ

"Upon couches, looking."

Sūrat al-Muṭaffifīn 83:23 · سُورَةُ المُطَفِّفِين

الشَّرْح · the explanation

الْأَرَائِكِ is the plural of أَرِيكَة, and it is the couches; and يَنْظُرُونَ — at Allah عَزَّ وَجَلَّ.

And al-Fawzān draws in the context, which is where the argument lies: and as for the disbelievers, it has already come in the āyāt before this āyah that they are عَنْ رَبِّهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَمَحْجُوبُونَ — «veiled from their Lord on that Day» (al-Muṭaffifīn 83:15).

Sit with the structure of the sūrah for a moment, because it argues by contrast. A few āyāt apart, Allah describes the disbelievers as veiled from Him and the righteous as looking. Now: being veiled is named as part of their punishment. A veil is only a deprivation if there is something behind it to be seen — and the punishment of one party establishes the reward of the other. If nobody can see Him, then the disbelievers have been threatened with nothing.

And the shāhid from the āyah: the affirming of the believers' seeing of their Lord عَزَّ وَجَلَّ.

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا الْحُسْنَى وَزِيَادَةٌ

"For those who did good is the best — and more."

Sūrat Yūnus 10:26 · سُورَةُ يُونُس

الشَّرْح · the explanation

لِلَّذِينَ أَحْسَنُوا — by undertaking what Allah obliged upon them of deeds, and holding back from what He forbade them of acts of disobedience. Again the two halves: doing and refraining.

الْحُسْنَى — the goodly recompense; and it has been said: the Garden.

وَزِيَادَةٌ — and here is the decisive point: it is the looking at the Noble Face of Allah — as its being explained with that is established from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ in «صَحِيحُ مُسْلِم» and elsewhere, and as the predecessors of this Ummah explained it with that.

Note the form of the proof. The āyah by itself says only «and more» — it does not say what the increase is. The content comes from the Prophet ﷺ himself, in a named collection. This is the standard the whole track works to: nobody is guessing at زِيَادَة, and nobody needs to.

لَهُمْ مَا يَشَاءُونَ فِيهَا وَلَدَيْنَا مَزِيدٌ

"They will have whatever they wish for therein — and with Us is more."

Sūrat Qāf 50:35 · سُورَة ق

الشَّرْح · the explanation

لَهُمْ مَا يَشَاءُونَ فِيهَا — for the believers in the Garden is whatever their souls desire and their eyes delight in, of the varieties of bliss and the kinds of good.

وَلَدَيْنَا مَزِيدٌ — an increase beyond that, and it is the looking at the Noble Face of Allah.

Read the two halves against each other and the whole doctrine of the section is in them. The first half is everything they could wish for — the ceiling of what a desire can reach. And then the āyah says: and there is more. Whatever that is, it is by definition something the soul would not have known to ask for.

مَا يُسْتَفَادُ · وَأَعْظَمُ النَّعِيم What the Āyāt Yield — the Greatest Bliss

What is derived from them is the affirming of the believers' seeing of their Lord on the Day of Rising, and that it is the greatest of the bliss which they attain. And this is the statement of the Ṣaḥābah, the Tābiʿūn and the imāms of the Muslims — contrary to the Rāfiḍah, the Jahmiyyah and the Muʿtazilah, who negate the seeing and thereby oppose the Book, the Sunnah and the consensus of the predecessors of this Ummah and its imāms, relying upon flimsy objections and false reasonings — among them:

الشُّبْهَةُ الأُولَى · لَوْ رُئِيَ لَكَانَ فِي جِهَة First Objection — To Be Seen Is to Be in a Direction

الشَّرْح · the explanation

Their statement: affirming the seeing necessitates affirming that Allah is in a direction; and were He in a direction, He would be a body — and Allah is free of that.

The answer: we say: the word الجِهَة is ambiguous — and the whole reply is in that single observation. al-Fawzān then splits it:

— If what is intended by “direction” is that He is indwelling in something of His creation, then this is false, and the proofs reject it — and it does not follow from affirming the seeing.

— And if what is intended by “direction” is that He سُبْحَانَهُ is above His creation, then this is established for Allah سُبْحَانَهُ and its negation is false — and it is not in conflict with His being seen.

This is the method of Lesson 6 applied to a word rather than to a text. The objection works only while الجِهَة is left vague enough to carry both a false meaning and a true one at once. Separate the two meanings and the argument has nothing left to stand on: the false sense was never entailed, and the true sense was never a problem.

الشُّبْهَةُ الثَّانِيَة · ﴿لَنْ تَرَانِي﴾ Second Objection — "You Shall Not See Me"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

They argued with His saying to Mūsā: لَنْ تَرَانِي — you shall not see Me (al-Aʿrāf 7:143).

The answer: the noble āyah has come regarding the negation of the seeing in this world, and it does not negate its being established in the Hereafter, as is established in the other proofs. And the state of people in the Hereafter differs from their state in this world.

The distinction is not a convenience invented for the occasion; the āyah itself supplies it. Mūsā asked while alive in the dunyā, and the refusal came with a demonstration — the mountain was levelled by the mere unveiling. What the āyah proves is that this world cannot bear it, which is a statement about the asker's condition, not about Allah's being visible.

الشُّبْهَةُ الثَّالِثَة · ﴿لَا تُدْرِكُهُ الْأَبْصَارُ﴾ Third Objection — "The Eyes Do Not Grasp Him"

الشَّرْح · the explanation

They argued with His saying: لَا تُدْرِكُهُ الْأَبْصَارُ (al-Anʿām 6:103).

The answer: that the āyah contains only the negation of الإِدْرَاك, and there is no negation of الرُّؤْيَة in it. And the meaning of al-idrāk is الإِحَاطَة — encompassing. So Allah سُبْحَانَهُ وَتَعَالَى is seen by the believers, and they do not encompass Him.

And then al-Fawzān turns the objection completely around, which is the most instructive move in the section: rather, the negation of al-idrāk necessitates the existence of the seeing — so the āyah is from the proofs affirming the ruʾyah.

Follow why. To say that eyes do not encompass something is to speak about how that thing is seen, not whether it is. One does not say of the invisible that it is not fully taken in by the eye. The denial of a limit on the seeing presupposes the seeing. The verse the deniers lean on hardest is counted among the proofs against them.

And the distinction it leaves behind is the one to carry: the believers see Him and do not encompass Him. Which is the same shape as every attribute in this book — real, and not comprehended.

خَاتِمَةُ القِسْمِ الأَوَّل · وَمَنْ تَدَبَّرَ القُرْآن The Close of Part One — Whoever Ponders the Qurʾān

With this section the author closes the first part of his book: the evidence from the Book of Allah. He does not claim to have exhausted it, and says so.

الشَّرْح · the explanation

وَهَذَا البَابُ فِي كِتَابِ اللهِ كَثِيرٌ — al-Fawzān: that is, the chapter of affirming the names of Allah and His attributes in the Qurʾān is abundant, and the author mentioned only some of it; for the affirming of Allah's names and attributes, in the manner that befits Him, has come in many āyāt of the Book of Allah.

Twenty-three sections and more than two hundred āyāt, and the author's own verdict is that this is a selection. That is worth registering before Part 2 begins: the case was never short of material.

وَمَنْ تَدَبَّرَ القُرْآنَ — that is: pondered over it and reflected upon what it indicates of guidance — تَبَيَّنَ لَهُ طَرِيقُ الحَقِّ — that is: the way of correctness became clear to him.

And note the condition the author attaches: طَالِبًا لِلْهُدَى — seeking guidance. The promise is not made to whoever reads, nor to whoever studies hardest. It is made to whoever comes looking for guidance — which is why two people can read the same āyāt and only one of them is addressed by this sentence.

And the pondering of the Qurʾān is what is demanded by its recitation. al-Fawzān closes the part with three āyāt, and they are worth reading in sequence:

كِتَابٌ أَنْزَلْنَاهُ إِلَيْكَ مُبَارَكٌ لِيَدَّبَّرُوا آيَاتِهِ وَلِيَتَذَكَّرَ أُولُو الْأَلْبَابِ

"A Book We have sent down to you, blessed, so that they may ponder its āyāt and that those of understanding may take heed."

Sūrat Ṣād 38:29 · سُورَة ص

أَفَلَا يَتَدَبَّرُونَ الْقُرْآنَ أَمْ عَلَى قُلُوبٍ أَقْفَالُهَا

"Will they not ponder the Qurʾān? Or are there locks upon their hearts?"

Sūrat Muḥammad 47:24 · سُورَةُ مُحَمَّد

أَفَلَمْ يَدَّبَّرُوا الْقَوْلَ

"Have they then not pondered the speech?"

Sūrat al-Muʾminūn 23:68 · سُورَةُ المُؤْمِنُون

Three āyāt, and the middle one names the alternative to pondering. Not ignorance, and not lack of access — locks. Part 1 has spent twenty-three sections showing that the Qurʾān states these attributes plainly and repeatedly. So where someone has read all of it and arrived at the opposite, the Qurʾān's own diagnosis is not that the evidence was unclear.

المُؤْمِنُونَ يَرَوْنَ اللهَ وَيُحِيطُونَ بِهِ. The believers see Allah and encompass Him.

Memorize & Reflect — احْفَظْ وَتَدَبَّرْ

وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ نَاضِرَةٌ إِلَى رَبِّهَا نَاظِرَةٌ

Memorize it with the two letters distinct — the ض of radiance and the ظ of looking. The order is the argument: the faces are radiant because of what they are looking at.
