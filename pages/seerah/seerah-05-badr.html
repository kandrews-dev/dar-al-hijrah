الإِذْنُ بِالقِتَالِ وَغَزْوَةُ بَدْر

Permission to Fight & the Battle of Badr

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand why and when fighting was permitted — after thirteen years of patience.
  • Know the numbers at Badr and how the believers prepared.
  • See the signs of Allah's aid — the angels, the rain, and the security of sleep.
  • Know what became of the prisoners, and the great lesson: victory is from Allah alone.
الإِذْنُ بِالقِتَال The Permission to Fight

For thirteen years in Makkah the believers were commanded to be patient and not to raise a hand, even under torture. Only after the Hijrah, when they had a land and a community to protect, did Allah permit them to defend themselves against those who had driven them out and made war on them:

أُذِنَ لِلَّذِينَ يُقَاتَلُونَ بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا ۚ وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ نَصْرِهِمْ لَقَدِيرٌ

"Permission [to fight] is given to those who are being fought, because they were wronged — and indeed Allah is competent to give them victory." (al-Ḥajj 22:39)

The ghazawāt (the expeditions the Prophet ﷺ himself led) were to protect the religion and the Muslims, not to force faith on anyone — for "there is no compulsion in religion" (al-Baqarah 2:256).

قَبْلَ اللِّقَاء Before the Encounter

The Prophet ﷺ set out with a small company to intercept a Makkan trade caravan led by Abū Sufyān. Abū Sufyān learned of it and escaped by a coastal route, then sent word to Makkah, and Quraysh marched out with a large army to protect their trade and their pride. The two sides met at the wells of Badr. The Prophet ﷺ consulted his Companions on whether to proceed — the Anṣār affirmed they would follow him even into the sea — and the small band of believers advanced to face a force several times their number.

غَزْوَةُ بَدْر The Battle of Badr (Ramaḍān, 2 AH)

About 313 believers — poorly armed, with only two horses and around seventy camels shared between them — faced a Makkan army of roughly a thousand, well-supplied and confident. The night before the battle, Allah sent rain that firmed the sand under the Muslims' feet while turning the ground behind the enemy to mud, and cast sleep over the believers as a sense of security from Him:

إِذْ يُغَشِّيكُمُ النُّعَاسَ أَمَنَةً مِّنْهُ وَيُنَزِّلُ عَلَيْكُم مِّنَ السَّمَاءِ مَاءً لِّيُطَهِّرَكُم بِهِ

"[Remember] when He covered you with drowsiness as a security from Him, and sent down upon you rain from the sky to purify you thereby."

al-Anfāl 8:11

The Prophet ﷺ spent the night before the battle in prayer, prostrating and begging Allah for the victory He had promised, until Abū Bakr told him: "Enough, O Messenger of Allah — you have implored your Lord." Allah then sent down angels to support the believers:

إِذْ تَسْتَغِيثُونَ رَبَّكُمْ فَاسْتَجَابَ لَكُمْ أَنِّي مُمِدُّكُم بِأَلْفٍ مِّنَ الْمَلَائِكَةِ مُرْدِفِينَ

"[Remember] when you sought help from your Lord, and He answered you: 'Indeed, I will reinforce you with a thousand angels, one after another.'"

al-Anfāl 8:9

By Allah's aid, the Muslims won a decisive victory. The leaders of Quraysh — among them Abū Jahl, the fiercest enemy of the daʿwah — were killed. Around seventy of the enemy were killed and seventy taken prisoner; the Prophet ﷺ treated the captives with mercy, and those who could not pay ransom were freed if they taught ten Muslim children to read and write — an act of worship in itself. The Qurʾān called that day يَوْمُ الفُرْقَان, the Day of Distinction between truth and falsehood (al-Anfāl 8:41).

The great lesson — الدرس الأعظم

وَمَا النَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ — "Victory is only from Allah" (Āl ʿImrān 3:126). The believers took every means available to them — they marched, they armed themselves, they consulted — and then their trust was in Allah, not in their numbers or their weapons. This is the balance the whole ummah is taught at Badr: work by the means, then place your reliance in the One who grants the outcome.

Reflect — تدبّر

وَمَا النَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ

"Victory is only from Allah." (3:126) At Badr, the believers took every means — then their trust was in Allah, not in their numbers or weapons. Ask the children: what is our part, and what is Allah's part, in any success?
