المَدِينَةُ وَالغَزَوَات

Madinah & the Battles

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand why and when fighting was permitted — after years of patience.
  • Know the great victory of Badr and that victory comes from Allah.
  • Learn the lesson of Uḥud — the fruit of obedience and the cost of disobedience.
  • Know how Allah defended the believers at al-Khandaq (the Trench).
  • Understand why the treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah was called "a clear victory."
الإِذْنُ بِالقِتَال The Permission to Fight

For thirteen years in Makkah the believers were commanded to be patient and not to raise a hand, even under torture. Only after the Hijrah, when they had a land and a community to protect, did Allah permit them to defend themselves against those who had driven them out and made war on them:

أُذِنَ لِلَّذِينَ يُقَاتَلُونَ بِأَنَّهُمْ ظُلِمُوا ۚ وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ عَلَىٰ نَصْرِهِمْ لَقَدِيرٌ

"Permission [to fight] is given to those who are being fought, because they were wronged — and indeed Allah is competent to give them victory." (al-Ḥajj 22:39)

The ghazawāt (the expeditions the Prophet ﷺ himself led) were to protect the religion and the Muslims, not to force faith on anyone — for "there is no compulsion in religion" (al-Baqarah 2:256).

غَزْوَةُ بَدْر The Battle of Badr (2 AH)

The first great battle. About 313 believers, poorly armed, faced a Makkan army of roughly a thousand. The Prophet ﷺ spent the night in prayer, begging Allah for the victory He had promised. Allah sent down angels to support the believers, and by His aid the Muslims won a decisive victory — the leaders of Quraysh, including Abū Jahl, were killed. The Qurʾān called that day يوم الفرقان, the Day of Distinction between truth and falsehood. Its great lesson: victory is from Allah alone — وَمَا النَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ — after taking the means.

غَزْوَةُ أُحُد The Battle of Uḥud (3 AH)

The next year Quraysh returned for revenge. The Prophet ﷺ placed archers on a hill and ordered them never to leave their post, whatever happened. At first the Muslims were winning — but when some archers saw victory and came down to gather the spoils, disobeying the command, the enemy cavalry swept around the exposed flank and turned the battle. Many Companions were martyred that day, among them the Prophet's beloved uncle Ḥamzah, and the Prophet ﷺ himself was wounded. The lasting lesson of Uḥud: obedience to the command of the Messenger ﷺ is the key to victory, and disobedience — even by the sincere, even for a good motive — brings loss.

غَزْوَةُ الخَنْدَق (الأَحْزَاب) The Battle of the Trench (5 AH)

The tribes of Arabia allied together — al-Aḥzāb, the Confederates — and marched on Madinah with a huge army to wipe out Islam. On the advice of Salmān al-Fārisī, the Muslims dug a trench (khandaq) around the exposed side of the city, a tactic the Arabs had never seen. The siege was long and the believers were tested severely — "there the believers were tried and shaken with a mighty shaking" (al-Aḥzāb 33:11). Then Allah defended them without a great battle: He sent a fierce wind and unseen hosts that tore up the enemy camp until the Confederates gave up and dispersed. Allah spared the believers the fighting — وَكَفَى اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ الْقِتَالَ (al-Aḥzāb 33:25).

صُلْحُ الحُدَيْبِيَة The Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah (6 AH)

The Prophet ﷺ set out with his Companions to perform ʿUmrah, but Quraysh blocked them at al-Ḥudaybiyah. Rather than fight at the Sacred Precinct, he agreed to a treaty — a ten-year truce whose terms seemed, to many Companions, humiliating. Yet Allah revealed: إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا — "Indeed We have granted you a clear victory" (al-Fatḥ 48:1). And so it proved: in the peace that followed, people met the Muslims freely, heard the message, and entered Islam in far greater numbers than all the years of war — so much so that the Conquest of Makkah two years later came almost without a battle. The lesson: what looks like a setback can be, in Allah's wisdom, the greatest of openings.

Reflect — تدبّر

وَمَا النَّصْرُ إِلَّا مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ

"Victory is only from Allah." From Badr to the Trench, the believers took every means — then their trust was in Allah, not in their numbers or weapons. Ask the children: what is our part, and what is Allah's part, in any success?
