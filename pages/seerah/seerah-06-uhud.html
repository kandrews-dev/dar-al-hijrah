Stage 6 · The Madinan PeriodYear 2

غَزْوَةُ أُحُد

The Battle of Uḥud

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know why Quraysh returned a year after Badr, and the numbers on each side.
  • Understand the Prophet's ﷺ order to the archers — and what happened when it was disobeyed.
  • Know the martyrdom of Ḥamzah and the wounding of the Prophet ﷺ.
  • Learn the lasting lesson of Uḥud: obedience to the Messenger ﷺ is the key to victory.
رُجُوعُ قُرَيْش Quraysh Returns for Revenge

A year after their humiliation at Badr, Quraysh marched on Madinah with an army of about three thousand, determined to avenge their dead. The Prophet ﷺ consulted his Companions: remain in Madinah and fight defensively within its streets, or go out to meet the enemy on open ground? The majority — especially the younger Companions eager to fight, having missed Badr — preferred to go out, and the Prophet ﷺ agreed. He marched out with about seven hundred men (after the hypocrite ʿAbdullāh ibn Ubayy withdrew with roughly three hundred of his followers) to the foot of Mount Uḥud.

أَصْحَابُ الجَبَل The Archers on the Hill

The Prophet ﷺ placed fifty archers on a small hill overlooking the battlefield, under ʿAbdullāh ibn Jubayr, and gave them an explicit order:

احْمُوا ظُهُورَنَا، فَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا نُقْتَلُ فَلَا تَنْصُرُونَا، وَإِنْ رَأَيْتُمُونَا غَنِمْنَا فَلَا تُشْرِكُونَا

"Protect our backs. If you see us being killed, do not come to help us; and if you see us taking the spoils, do not join us."

al-Bukhārī

At first the battle went strongly in the Muslims' favour, and the enemy ranks broke. Seeing what looked like a clear victory, most of the archers came down from the hill to gather the spoils — disobeying the Prophet's ﷺ explicit command, despite the objection of their commander who reminded them of his order. The Makkan cavalry commander Khālid ibn al-Walīd (still a disbeliever at the time) saw the hill was undefended, swept his horsemen around it, and struck the Muslims from behind. The battle turned in moments.

In the chaos, a false rumor spread that the Prophet ﷺ had been killed, and many fighters scattered in shock and grief. He had in fact been struck and wounded — his face injured, a tooth broken — but a small band of Companions shielded him and helped him to safety. Seventy Companions were martyred that day, among them his beloved uncle Ḥamzah ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, whom the Prophet ﷺ named Sayyid al-Shuhadāʾ — the master of martyrs.

وَلَا تَهِنُوا وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا وَأَنتُمُ الْأَعْلَوْنَ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ ۝ إِن يَمْسَسْكُمْ قَرْحٌ فَقَدْ مَسَّ الْقَوْمَ قَرْحٌ مِّثْلُهُ

"Do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are [true] believers. If a wound should touch you, there has already touched the [opposing] people a wound similar to it."

Āl ʿImrān 3:139–140

الدَّرْسُ الخَالِد The Lasting Lesson

Uḥud teaches what Badr alone could not: that closeness to the Prophet ﷺ, sincerity of intention, and past success do not exempt anyone from the consequence of disobedience. The archers were not hypocrites or cowards — many of them were sincere believers who simply saw what looked like victory and moved too soon. Obedience to the command of the Messenger ﷺ is the key to victory, and disobedience — even by the sincere, even for what seems a good reason — brings loss. Even in defeat, the Prophet ﷺ later called Uḥud a mountain he loved: "Uḥud is a mountain that loves us, and we love it" (al-Bukhārī) — for the blood of the martyrs it holds.

Reflect — تدبّر

وَلَا تَهِنُوا وَلَا تَحْزَنُوا وَأَنتُمُ الْأَعْلَوْنَ إِن كُنتُم مُّؤْمِنِينَ

"Do not weaken and do not grieve, and you will be superior if you are [true] believers." (3:139) A setback is not the end of the story for a believer — it is a test, and a teacher. Ask the children: what would you do differently if you had been one of the archers?
