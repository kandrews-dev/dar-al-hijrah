Stage 7 · The Madinan PeriodYear 2

الخَنْدَقُ وَصُلْحُ الحُدَيْبِيَة

The Trench & the Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know how Allah defended the believers at al-Khandaq (the Trench) without a major battle.
  • Understand the outcome for the Banū Qurayẓah, who broke their treaty during the siege.
  • Know what happened at Bayʿat al-Riḍwān, the Pledge under the Tree.
  • Understand why the treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah was called "a clear victory."
غَزْوَةُ الخَنْدَق (الأَحْزَاب) The Battle of the Trench (5 AH)

The tribes of Arabia allied together — al-Aḥzāb, the Confederates, numbering around ten thousand — and marched on Madinah to wipe out Islam once and for all. On the advice of the Persian Companion Salmān al-Fārisī, the Muslims dug a trench (khandaq) around the one side of the city left exposed — a defensive tactic the Arabs had never before seen, which left the enemy's cavalry unable to cross.

The siege dragged on for roughly a month. The believers were tested severely — cold, hunger, and fear on every side — and to make matters worse, the Jewish tribe of Banū Qurayẓah, who had a treaty of mutual defence with the Muslims, broke their pact and conspired with the Confederates against Madinah from within.

إِذْ جَاءُوكُم مِّن فَوْقِكُمْ وَمِنْ أَسْفَلَ مِنكُمْ وَإِذْ زَاغَتِ الْأَبْصَارُ وَبَلَغَتِ الْقُلُوبُ الْحَنَاجِرَ ۚ هُنَالِكَ ابْتُلِيَ الْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَزُلْزِلُوا زِلْزَالًا شَدِيدًا

"[Remember] when they came at you from above you and from below you, and when eyes shifted [in fear], and hearts reached the throats, and you assumed about Allah [various] assumptions. There the believers were tried and shaken with a severe shaking."

al-Aḥzāb 33:10–11

Then Allah defended the believers without a great battle: He sent a fierce, freezing wind and unseen hosts that overturned the enemy's tents and fires and threw their camp into confusion until the Confederates lost heart and dispersed. Allah spared the believers the fighting:

وَكَفَى اللَّهُ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ الْقِتَالَ ۚ وَكَانَ اللَّهُ قَوِيًّا عَزِيزًا

"And Allah sufficed the believers in the battle. And ever is Allah Powerful and Exalted in Might."

al-Aḥzāb 33:25

After the siege lifted, the Prophet ﷺ turned to Banū Qurayẓah for their betrayal during Madinah's greatest hour of need, and the case was judged, at their own request, by their former ally Saʿd ibn Muʿādh, according to the law of the Torah for a besieged tribe that betrays its covenant in war.

صُلْحُ الحُدَيْبِيَة The Treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah (6 AH)

The following year, the Prophet ﷺ set out with some fourteen hundred Companions — unarmed for battle, dressed for ʿUmrah — to visit the Kaʿbah. Quraysh, fearing the political cost of letting them enter, blocked the way at al-Ḥudaybiyah, just outside Makkah's boundary.

بَيْعَةُ الرِّضْوَان — Bayʿat al-Riḍwān

When a rumor spread that Quraysh had killed the Prophet's ﷺ envoy ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān, the Prophet ﷺ called his Companions to renew their pledge — to fight to the death if it came to that. Sitting beneath a tree, every man present placed his hand in the Prophet's ﷺ and swore it. Allah revealed His pleasure with them by name:

لَقَدْ رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنِ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ إِذْ يُبَايِعُونَكَ تَحْتَ الشَّجَرَةِ

"Certainly was Allah pleased with the believers when they pledged allegiance to you, [O Muhammad], under the tree."

al-Fatḥ 48:18

News then arrived that ʿUthmān was safe, and the crisis passed without battle.

Rather than fight at the edge of the Sacred Precinct, the Prophet ﷺ agreed to a treaty — a ten-year truce whose terms seemed, to many Companions, one-sided: the Muslims would return that year without completing ʿUmrah, and any Makkan who fled to Madinah without his guardian's permission would be sent back, while the reverse did not apply. ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb himself questioned the Prophet ﷺ over terms that looked like a concession. Yet Allah revealed:

إِنَّا فَتَحْنَا لَكَ فَتْحًا مُّبِينًا

"Indeed, We have granted you a clear victory."

al-Fatḥ 48:1

And so it proved: in the two years of peace that followed, tribes and individuals met the Muslims freely, heard the message without the fear of war, and entered Islam in far greater numbers than in all the years of fighting before it — so much so that the Conquest of Makkah two years later came almost without a battle. The lesson: what looks like a setback can be, in Allah's wisdom, the greatest of openings.
