Stage 2 · The Makkan PeriodYear 1

الدعوة في مكة

The Call in Makkah

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know how the first revelation came at the cave of Ḥirāʾ with اقْرَأْ (Read!).
  • Understand the two phases of the Makkan daʿwah: the secret years, then the public call.
  • Know the first believers (السابقون الأولون) and why they mattered.
  • Understand the persecution the weak endured — and the steadfastness of Bilāl and the family of Yāsir.
  • Know the two migrations to Abyssinia and the turning point of Ḥamzah and ʿUmar accepting Islam.
الوحي في غار حراء The First Revelation at Ḥirāʾ

As he approached the age of forty, the Prophet ﷺ began to withdraw to the cave of Ḥirāʾ in the mountain above Makkah, worshipping Allah and reflecting away from the idolatry of his people. There, on a night in Ramaḍān, the angel Jibrīl came to him and commanded: اقْرَأْ — "Read!" He replied, "I am not one who reads." The angel embraced him firmly three times, then revealed the first words of the Qurʾān:

اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ ۝ خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ ۝ اقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ الْأَكْرَمُ

"Read in the name of your Lord who created — created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the most Generous." (al-ʿAlaq 96:1–3)

He returned home trembling, saying "Cover me! Cover me!" His wife Khadījah رضي الله عنها reassured him with words that reveal his character: "Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep good relations, carry the burden of others, honour the guest, and help those struck by calamity." She took him to her cousin Waraqah ibn Nawfal, a man who knew the earlier scriptures, who recognised that this was an-Nāmūs — the same angel that had come to Mūsā — and that his people would drive him out. Revelation then paused for a time before resuming, and the mission began.

الدعوة السرية ثم الجهرية The Secret Call, Then the Public Call

For about three years the call was given quietly, to those near and trusted. The very first to believe were Khadījah (the first of all), Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq among the men, ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib among the young, and Zayd ibn Ḥārithah among the freed. Abū Bakr in turn brought many of the best of the Companions. These are the السابقون الأولون — the foremost and first.

Then Allah commanded him to proclaim the message openly: فَاصْدَعْ بِمَا تُؤْمَرُ — "So declare openly what you are commanded" (al-Ḥijr 15:94). He climbed the hill of aṣ-Ṣafā and called the clans of Quraysh: "If I told you an army was behind this hill about to attack, would you believe me?" They said, "Yes — we have never known you to lie." He said: "Then I am a warner to you before a severe punishment." From that day, the leaders of Quraysh — led by his own uncle Abū Lahab — turned against him.

الابتلاء والصبر Persecution & Steadfastness

Quraysh could not touch those protected by strong clans, so they fell upon the weak and the slaves. Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ was pinned under a burning rock in the desert sun, yet he answered only: "Aḥad, Aḥad" — "One, One." The family of Yāsir were tortured for their faith; Sumayyah رضي الله عنها, Yāsir's wife, was killed for refusing to leave Islam — the first martyr of this ummah — and her son ʿAmmār was tormented terribly. The Prophet ﷺ would pass them and say: "Patience, O family of Yāsir — your appointed place is Paradise."

الهجرة إلى الحبشة — The Migration to Abyssinia

When the persecution grew unbearable, the Prophet ﷺ told a group of believers to seek refuge with the Negus (an-Najāshī), the just Christian king of Abyssinia (al-Ḥabashah): "for he is a king in whose land no one is wronged." Two migrations followed. When Quraysh sent envoys demanding their return, Jaʿfar ibn Abī Ṭālib stood before the Negus and described Islam: how they had been people of ignorance who now worshipped Allah alone, spoke truth, kept ties, and were honest — and he recited to him from Sūrat Maryam. The Negus wept and refused to hand them over. It was the first time Islam found safety and honour in a foreign land.

In Makkah itself, two mighty conversions strengthened the believers: Ḥamzah ibn ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib, the Prophet's ﷺ uncle and one of the bravest of Quraysh; and then ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, who set out to kill the Prophet ﷺ but was turned by hearing the Qurʾān recited in his own sister's house. With ʿUmar's Islam the Muslims prayed openly at the Kaʿbah for the first time. Yet the harder the believers were pressed, the firmer their faith grew — a pattern that would define the whole Makkan period.
