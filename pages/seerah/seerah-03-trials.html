Stage 3 · The Makkan PeriodYear 1

الابْتِلَاءُ وَالإِسْرَاء

Trials & the Night Journey

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand the boycott of Banū Hāshim and how the believers endured it.
  • Know why the tenth year is called عَامُ الحُزْن — the Year of Grief.
  • Learn the lesson of Ṭāʾif and the Prophet's ﷺ mercy toward those who rejected him.
  • Know al-Isrāʾ wal-Miʿrāj and the gift that came from it — the five daily prayers.
  • Understand the pledges of ʿAqabah and how they opened the door to Madinah.
الحِصَارُ فِي الشِّعْب The Boycott in the Valley

Unable to stop the message, the leaders of Quraysh drew up a written pact to boycott Banū Hāshim — the Prophet's ﷺ clan — cutting off all trade and marriage with them until they surrendered him. The clan was besieged in the valley (shiʿb) of Abū Ṭālib for about three years. They suffered such hunger that they ate leaves; the cries of hungry children could be heard outside the valley. Yet they did not give up the Prophet ﷺ. In the end Allah exposed the injustice: the pact document was found eaten away by termites — except the words "In Your name, O Allah." Even some of those who had signed it were ashamed, and the boycott was broken.

عَامُ الحُزْن The Year of Grief

Soon after, within a short span, the Prophet ﷺ lost the two people who had shielded him most: his beloved wife Khadījah رضي الله عنها — his first supporter and comfort — and his uncle Abū Ṭālib, who though he never accepted Islam had protected him from Quraysh for decades. With their loss the Prophet ﷺ was left exposed, and Quraysh grew bolder in their harm. This year of double loss is remembered as عَامُ الحُزْن, the Year of Grief.

رِحْلَةُ الطَّائِف The Journey to Ṭāʾif

Seeking new ground for the daʿwah, the Prophet ﷺ travelled to Ṭāʾif. But its leaders rejected him harshly and set their children and slaves to pelt him with stones until his blessed feet bled. Exhausted, he made one of the most moving duʿās ever spoken, complaining of his weakness only to Allah and ending: "If You are not angry with me, then I do not care." Allah offered him, through an angel, to crush the people of Ṭāʾif between the two mountains — but the Messenger of mercy ﷺ refused, saying: "Rather, I hope Allah will bring from their offspring those who worship Him alone." His mercy at the very moment of his own suffering is a lasting lesson for every caller to Allah.

الإِسْرَاءُ وَالمِعْرَاج The Night Journey & Ascension

In the darkest stretch of his mission, Allah honoured His Messenger ﷺ with a miracle. In a single night he was carried — al-Isrāʾ — from the Sacred Mosque in Makkah to al-Masjid al-Aqṣā in Jerusalem, where he led all the prophets in prayer. Then came al-Miʿrāj, the ascension through the heavens, where he met prophets including Ādam, Mūsā, and Ibrāhīm, and was shown wonders of the unseen. Allah referred to the first part in His words:

سُبْحَانَ الَّذِي أَسْرَىٰ بِعَبْدِهِ لَيْلًا مِّنَ الْمَسْجِدِ الْحَرَامِ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ الْأَقْصَى

"Exalted is He who took His servant by night from al-Masjid al-Ḥarām to al-Masjid al-Aqṣā…" (al-Isrāʾ 17:1)

The greatest gift of that night was the five daily prayers (aṣ-ṣalawāt al-khams). They were first enjoined as fifty, then eased by Allah's mercy to five in deed but fifty in reward. This is why the prayer is so beloved — it is the one act of worship given directly above the seven heavens, without an angel in between. When Quraysh mocked the story, Abū Bakr said without hesitation: "If he said it, then it is true" — and earned the title aṣ-Ṣiddīq, the great affirmer of truth.

بَيْعَتَا العَقَبَة The Pledges of ʿAqabah

At the pilgrimage seasons the Prophet ﷺ presented Islam to the visiting tribes. A group from Yathrib (later Madinah) believed. They returned the next year and gave the First Pledge of ʿAqabah — to worship Allah alone and obey Him. The Prophet ﷺ sent Muṣʿab ibn ʿUmayr back with them as the first teacher of Islam in Madinah, and Islam spread quickly there. The following year some seventy believers came and gave the Second Pledge of ʿAqabah, pledging to protect the Prophet ﷺ as they would their own families. This pledge opened the door to the Hijrah — a city was now ready to receive the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and his religion.

Reflect — تدبّر

إِنْ لَمْ يَكُنْ بِكَ غَضَبٌ عَلَيَّ فَلَا أُبَالِي

Bleeding and rejected at Ṭāʾif, the Prophet ﷺ complained only to Allah — "If You are not angry with me, I do not care." Ask the children: where do we turn when people let us down, and why was his first thought Allah's pleasure?
