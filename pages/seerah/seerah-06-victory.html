Stage 6 · The Completion

الفتح والوداع

Victory & Farewell

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the Conquest of Makkah and the mercy the Prophet ﷺ showed in his hour of victory.
  • Understand the year of delegations — people entering Islam in crowds.
  • Know the Farewell Pilgrimage and the key teachings of its great sermon.
  • Know that the religion was completed: اليوم أكملت لكم دينكم.
  • Learn how the Prophet ﷺ passed away, and Abū Bakr's words that steadied the ummah.
فتح مكة The Conquest of Makkah (8 AH)

When Quraysh broke the treaty of al-Ḥudaybiyah, the Prophet ﷺ marched on Makkah with an army of about ten thousand. The city that had tortured, boycotted, and expelled him surrendered almost without a fight. He entered it not as a proud conqueror but with his head bowed in humility upon his mount, so low it almost touched the saddle, thanking Allah. He cleansed the Kaʿbah of its 360 idols, reciting: وَقُلْ جَاءَ الْحَقُّ وَزَهَقَ الْبَاطِلُ — "Truth has come and falsehood has vanished" — and Bilāl رضي الله عنه climbed upon the Kaʿbah and gave the adhān.

مرحمة في ساعة النصر — Mercy in the Hour of Victory

Now holding total power over the very people who had persecuted him for twenty years, the Prophet ﷺ asked them: "What do you think I will do with you?" They said, "Good — a noble brother, the son of a noble brother." He answered with the words of Yūsuf عليه السلام: لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ، اذْهَبُوا فَأَنْتُمُ الطُّلَقَاءُ — "No blame upon you today. Go, for you are free." This general pardon, in the moment he could have taken revenge, is one of the greatest displays of mercy in all of history — and it won hearts that battle never could.

عام الوفود The Year of Delegations (9 AH)

After Makkah fell, the tribes of Arabia saw that the truth had prevailed, and delegations (wufūd) poured into Madinah from every direction, accepting Islam — the fulfilment of the promise in Sūrat al-Naṣr: وَرَأَيْتَ النَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِي دِينِ اللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا — "and you see the people entering the religion of Allah in crowds." What thirteen years of persecution could not stop, Allah brought to victory.

حجة الوداع The Farewell Pilgrimage (10 AH)

In the tenth year the Prophet ﷺ made his only Ḥajj, joined by over a hundred thousand Companions. On the plain of ʿArafah he delivered the Farewell Sermon — a summary of the religion and the rights of people. Among its teachings: the sanctity of every Muslim's life, wealth, and honour; the abolition of the practices of jāhiliyyah (blood-vengeance and usury); kind treatment of women as a trust from Allah; that all people are equal — "no Arab is superior to a non-Arab except by taqwā"; and his charge: "I have left among you that which, if you hold fast to it, you will never go astray: the Book of Allah and my Sunnah." During the pilgrimage the great āyah was revealed:

الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينًا

"This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favour upon you, and have approved for you Islam as your religion." (al-Māʾidah 5:3)

وفاة النبي ﷺ The Passing of the Prophet ﷺ (11 AH)

Shortly after, the Prophet ﷺ fell ill. In his final days he continued to pray behind Abū Bakr and to advise the believers, his last words including care for the prayer and those under their authority. He passed away in the room of ʿĀʾishah رضي الله عنها, his head resting near her, at about sixty-three years of age — his mission complete, the message fully delivered.

The Companions were stunned with grief; some could not believe he had died. Then Abū Bakr رضي الله عنه rose and steadied the ummah with words that echo to this day: "Whoever used to worship Muḥammad — Muḥammad has died. And whoever worships Allah — Allah is Ever-Living and never dies." Then he recited: وَمَا مُحَمَّدٌ إِلَّا رَسُولٌ قَدْ خَلَتْ مِن قَبْلِهِ الرُّسُلُ — "Muḥammad is but a messenger; messengers have passed away before him" (Āl ʿImrān 3:144). The man had gone to his Lord; the religion he brought remained, complete and protected, for all who would come after.
