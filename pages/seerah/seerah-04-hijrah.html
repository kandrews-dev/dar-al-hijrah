Stage 4 · The Turning Point

الهجرة إلى المدينة

The Hijrah to Madinah

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand the plot of Quraysh and how Allah protected His Messenger ﷺ.
  • Know the story of the cave of Thawr and the lesson of "Do not grieve — Allah is with us."
  • Know the events of the arrival in Madinah and the building of al-Masjid an-Nabawī.
  • Understand the brotherhood (muʾākhāh) between the Muhājirūn and the Anṣār.
  • Know how the Prophet ﷺ founded the first Muslim community and why the Hijrah begins the Islamic calendar.
مؤامرة قريش The Plot Against Him ﷺ

As Islam took root in Madinah and the believers of Makkah began migrating there, Quraysh saw their danger. They gathered in their council and agreed on a plot: a strong young man from each clan would strike the Prophet ﷺ together, so his blood would be spread among all the tribes and Banū Hāshim could not avenge him. Allah revealed their scheme to His Messenger ﷺ. On the night they surrounded his house, he had ʿAlī رضي الله عنه sleep in his bed — to return people's trusts that were kept with him — and walked out through them unseen, reciting from the Qurʾān. Their plan failed completely.

غار ثور The Cave of Thawr

The Prophet ﷺ set out with his closest companion, Abū Bakr aṣ-Ṣiddīq رضي الله عنه. They hid three nights in the cave of Thawr as Quraysh searched with trackers, offering a huge reward. The pursuers came so close that they stood at the mouth of the cave. Abū Bakr whispered, "If one of them looks down at his feet he will see us." The Prophet ﷺ answered:

لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا

"Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us." (at-Tawbah 9:40) — a line every believer clings to in fear.

Allah shielded them and turned the pursuers away. They then travelled the long desert road north to Yathrib, guided quietly, arriving safely by Allah's protection.

الوصول إلى المدينة Arrival in Madinah

The people of Yathrib had waited daily for their arrival. When the Prophet ﷺ finally reached them, they welcomed him with the famous song of joy, Ṭalaʿa al-badru ʿalaynā ("The full moon has risen over us"). The city was renamed Madīnat an-Nabī — the City of the Prophet ﷺ — and the migration itself, the Hijrah, became so pivotal that ʿUmar رضي الله عنه later made it the start of the Islamic calendar (the Hijrī year). The Hijrah was not flight but the founding of a state where Islam could finally be lived in full.

بناء المجتمع الأول Building the First Community

The Prophet ﷺ began at once to build a society on three foundations:

  • The Mosque (al-Masjid an-Nabawī): His first project — a place of prayer, learning, governance, and community. The heart of the city was the house of Allah, not a palace.
  • The Brotherhood (al-muʾākhāh): He paired each Muhājir (emigrant from Makkah) with an Anṣārī (helper of Madinah) as brothers in faith. The Anṣār shared their homes and wealth with astonishing generosity — a bond of religion stronger than blood.
  • The Covenant (aṣ-Ṣaḥīfah): He drew up a written agreement ordering relations between the Muhājirūn, the Anṣār, and the other groups of Madinah — rights, duties, and mutual defence — the first constitution of the Muslim community.

The call to prayer, the adhān, was established, its words seen in a dream by a Companion and confirmed by the Prophet ﷺ, so that five times a day the city was called to stand before Allah. In a few short months, a scattered, persecuted band had become an ordered nation with a mosque at its centre — ready for the trials and victories that lay ahead.

Reflect — تدبّر

لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا

Surrounded, hunted, with enemies at the mouth of the cave, the Prophet ﷺ was completely calm: "Do not grieve; Allah is with us." Ask the children: what does real trust in Allah (tawakkul) look like when everything seems against you?
