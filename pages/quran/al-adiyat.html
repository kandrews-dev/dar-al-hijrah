سورة العاديات

Sūrat al-ʿĀdiyāt — The Racing Horses

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand the five-fold oath by horses — why Allah swears by them and what quality they embody.
  • Know what كَنُود truly means — al-Saʿdī's precise definition goes beyond ingratitude.
  • See the connection between love of wealth and withholding what is owed — including to Allah.
  • Understand why Allah says He will be "fully aware" on the Day of Judgement, when He is always aware.
الآيات The Āyāt

وَٱلْعَٰدِيَٰتِ ضَبْحًا ۝ فَٱلْمُورِيَٰتِ قَدْحًا ۝ فَٱلْمُغِيرَٰتِ صُبْحًا ۝ فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِۦ نَقْعًا ۝ فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِۦ جَمْعًا ۝ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لِرَبِّهِۦ لَكَنُودٌ ۝ وَإِنَّهُۥ عَلَىٰ ذَٰلِكَ لَشَهِيدٌ ۝ وَإِنَّهُۥ لِحُبِّ ٱلْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ ۝ أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِى ٱلْقُبُورِ ۝ وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِى ٱلصُّدُورِ ۝ إِنَّ رَبَّهُم بِهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّخَبِيرٌ

By the charging horses, panting — striking sparks with their hooves — raiding at dawn — stirring up therewith clouds of dust — making their way thereby into the midst of the enemy — verily man is ungrateful to his Lord — and he himself bears witness to that — and verily, because of his love of wealth, he is stingy. Does he not know what will happen when the contents of the graves are turned over — and what is hidden in people's hearts is brought to light? Verily their Lord, on that day, will be fully aware of them.

al-ʿĀdiyāt 100:1–11

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

الخيل — the five-fold oath by horses

Al-Saʿdī says Allah swears by horses here because of what they contain of the dazzling signs of Allah and obvious blessings — things well known to all people. The oath unfolds across five images:

الآية Image
الْعَادِيَاتِ ضَبْحًا Running swiftly — with energy and vigour
الْمُورِيَاتِ قَدْحًا Striking sparks with their hooves on rocks — from the solidness of their shoes as they gallop
الْمُغِيرَاتِ صُبْحًا Raiding at dawn — this is what usually happens: raids begin at dawn
فَأَثَرْنَ بِهِ نَقْعًا Stirring up clouds of dust as they gallop and raid
فَوَسَطْنَ بِهِ جَمْعًا Making their way — by their riding — into the midst of the enemy forces

إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لِرَبِّهِ لَكَنُودٌ (verily man is ungrateful to his Lord): Al-Saʿdī's definition of كَنُود is precise — not merely "ungrateful" in a vague sense, but specifically: reluctant to give what his Lord commanded him to give — of charity, duties, and the like. The natural disposition of man is to hold back, to not give dues in full, whether financial or physical. Only those whom Allah guides are changed.

وَإِنَّهُ عَلَى ذَلِكَ لَشَهِيدٌ (and he himself bears witness to that): Man knows his own stinginess and miserliness. He does not deny it — because it is clear and obvious to himself. Al-Saʿdī notes an alternative reading: the pronoun "he" may refer to Allah — in which case Allah bears witness to man's ingratitude. Either reading carries weight. If it means Allah bears witness, the warning is even more serious: He is watching.

وَإِنَّهُ لِحُبِّ الْخَيْرِ لَشَدِيدٌ (because of his love of wealth, he is intense in his stinginess): الخير here means wealth. Al-Saʿdī explains the chain: man loves wealth intensely — and it is that love that causes him to withhold the duties he owes, and to give precedence to his desire for wealth over the dues of his Lord. The root cause is that he is short-sighted, focused on this world, and heedless of what comes after it.

يوم القيامة The Day — does he not know?

أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِي الْقُبُورِ (Does he not know when the contents of the graves are turned over?): Al-Saʿdī says: why does this deluded person not know what awaits him? When Allah brings forth the dead from their graves for the resurrection — that will happen.

وَحُصِّلَ مَا فِي الصُّدُورِ (and what is hidden in people's hearts is brought to light): Whatever is concealed — of good and evil — will be made known. Secrets will be disclosed. The outcome of people's deeds will become visible on their faces.

إِنَّ رَبَّهُم بِهِمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ لَّخَبِيرٌ (their Lord, on that Day, will be fully aware of them): Al-Saʿdī addresses a natural question — Allah is always aware, so why specify "on that Day"? Because the context is requital: on that Day is when checking and recompense of deeds takes place, based on His knowledge. The awareness leads to action — and on that Day, it leads to judgement.

الدرس لنا The Lesson for Us

The contrast in this sūrah is striking: Allah opens with the most energetic, giving image possible — a horse galloping without holding back, giving everything, sparks flying, charging into the midst of danger at dawn. Then He says: man is the opposite. Man holds back. The horse gives all it has; man withholds what he owes. The contrast is the lesson. What does the horse have that man lacks? It obeys without argument. It gives without calculating. It goes forward without turning back.

What is the root cause of man's withholding, according to al-Saʿdī?

Memorize & Give — احفظ وأعطِ

إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لِرَبِّهِ لَكَنُودٌ · أَفَلَا يَعْلَمُ إِذَا بُعْثِرَ مَا فِى الْقُبُورِ

Ask the children: the horse in this sūrah holds nothing back. What does it mean to hold back from what Allah commanded? Find one thing you owe — to Allah, to a parent, to someone in need — and give it. The horse charges into the midst of the enemy without calculating. What would it look like to give without calculating?
