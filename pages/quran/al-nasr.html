سورة النصر

Sūrat al-Naṣr — The Help & Victory

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand what سورة النصر (Sūrat al-Naṣr) foretold about the conquest of Makkah.
  • Learn the command given after every victory: glorify Allah, praise Him, and seek forgiveness.
  • See the two hidden hints al-Saʿdī finds in this sūrah — about the Ummah and about the Prophet's own life.
  • Understand why شكر (shukr — gratitude) is inseparable from نصر (naṣr — divine help).

سورة النصر is one of the last sūrahs revealed. It carries glad tidings, a command, and two subtle hints — and it changed how the Prophet ﷺ prayed in the final months of his life.

الآيات The Āyāt — سورة النصر

إِذَا جَآءَ نَصْرُ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلْفَتْحُ ۝ وَرَأَيْتَ ٱلنَّاسَ يَدْخُلُونَ فِى دِينِ ٱللَّهِ أَفْوَاجًا ۝ فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابًا

When the help of Allah comes and the conquest, and you see people entering the religion of Allah in multitudes — then glorify the praises of your Lord and seek His forgiveness. He is ever Accepting of repentance.

al-Naṣr 110:1–3

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

Al-Saʿdī explains that this sūrah contains three things together: بشارة (glad tidings), أمر (a command), and إشارتان (two hidden hints).

The glad tidings: Allah promised His Prophet ﷺ that نصر (naṣr — divine help) and الفتح (al-fatḥ — the conquest, namely the conquest of Makkah) would come. And it came exactly. Makkah — the city that had expelled and persecuted the believers — was opened, and people who had once been enemies came into Islam أفواج (afwāj — in multitudes, wave upon wave). What was foretold came to pass.

The command after the victory: Allah did not say "celebrate" or "rest." He said: فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَاسْتَغْفِرْهُ — glorify your Lord with praise, and seek His forgiveness. These two acts — تسبيح وحمد (glorifying and praising Allah) and استغفار (seeking forgiveness) — are how a believer responds to every blessing. Not with pride, but with gratitude and humility.

Two hidden hints — الإشارتان

First hint — for the Ummah: Divine help comes when you give thanks. Allah says elsewhere: «لَئِن شَكَرْتُمْ لَأَزِيدَنَّكُمْ» — "If you give thanks, I will surely give you more" (Ibrāhīm 14:7). The early Caliphs glorified and praised Allah, and Islam spread to lands unimaginable. When the Ummah turned away from Allah's commands, divisions and defeats followed.

Second hint — for the Prophet ﷺ: Al-Saʿdī explains that this sūrah was also a sign that the Prophet's life was drawing to a close. Great deeds end with seeking forgiveness — just as prayer ends with salām and then استغفار, and Hajj ends with استغفار. Allah was telling His Messenger ﷺ: prepare to meet your Lord. And the Prophet ﷺ understood this — he began saying in every bowing and prostration: «سبحانك اللهم ربنا وبحمدك، اللهم اغفر لي» — "Glory be to You, O Allah, our Lord, and with Your praise; O Allah, forgive me." (Bukhāri and Muslim)

الدرس لنا The Lesson for Us

When something good happens — a test you pass, a problem that resolves, a blessing received — the believer's first response is تسبيح وحمد: glorifying Allah and thanking Him. Pride shrinks your blessing; gratitude grows it. And whenever a believer finishes something important — a prayer, a gathering of knowledge, a task completed — they end with استغفار, acknowledging they could have done more.

Memorize & Act — احفظ وعمل

فَسَبِّحْ بِحَمْدِ رَبِّكَ وَٱسْتَغْفِرْهُ ۚ إِنَّهُۥ كَانَ تَوَّابًا

Teach the children this āyah and its meaning: after every blessing, we say سبحان الله وبحمده — "Glory and praise be to Allah." And we end every good deed with استغفار. This is how the Prophet ﷺ himself ended his prayers in his final months.
