سورة الممتحنة

Sūrat al-Mumtaḥanah — The Examined Woman

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand الولاء والبراء (allegiance and disavowal): loving and allying for Allah, and disowning enmity to His religion.
  • Know why believers are forbidden from taking as intimate allies those who fight the religion and expelled the believers.
  • Know the أُسْوَة حَسَنَة (excellent example) of Ibrāhīm عليه السلام and his open disavowal of his people's false worship.
  • Grasp the crucial balance of āyah 8: kindness and justice to peaceful non-Muslims is not forbidden — "Allah loves the just."
  • Know why the sūrah is called al-Mumtaḥanah — from the testing (امتحان) of the believing women who emigrated.
الآيات — القسم الأولPart One: Do Not Ally With Allah's Enemies (1)

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا لَا تَتَّخِذُوا عَدُوِّي وَعَدُوَّكُمْ أَوْلِيَاءَ تُلْقُونَ إِلَيْهِم بِالْمَوَدَّةِ وَقَدْ كَفَرُوا بِمَا جَاءَكُم مِّنَ الْحَقِّ يُخْرِجُونَ الرَّسُولَ وَإِيَّاكُمْ ۙ أَن تُؤْمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ رَبِّكُمْ

O you who believe, do not take My enemy and your enemy as allies, extending to them affection while they have disbelieved in what came to you of the truth, having driven out the Messenger and yourselves [only] because you believe in Allah, your Lord.

al-Mumtaḥanah 60:1

الآيات — القسم الثانيPart Two: The Example of Ibrāhīm (4)

قَدْ كَانَتْ لَكُمْ أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ فِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَالَّذِينَ مَعَهُ إِذْ قَالُوا لِقَوْمِهِمْ إِنَّا بُرَآءُ مِنكُمْ وَمِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ كَفَرْنَا بِكُمْ وَبَدَا بَيْنَنَا وَبَيْنَكُمُ الْعَدَاوَةُ وَالْبَغْضَاءُ أَبَدًا حَتَّىٰ تُؤْمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَحْدَهُ

There has already been for you an excellent example in Ibrāhīm and those with him, when they said to their people, "Indeed, we are disassociated from you and from whatever you worship other than Allah. We have denied you, and there has appeared between us and you animosity and hatred forever until you believe in Allah alone."

al-Mumtaḥanah 60:4

الآيات — القسم الثالثPart Three: Justice and Kindness to the Peaceful (8)

لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ

Allah does not forbid you from those who do not fight you because of religion and do not expel you from your homes — from being righteous toward them and acting justly toward them. Indeed, Allah loves those who act justly.

al-Mumtaḥanah 60:8

التفسيرExplanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

Al-Saʿdī explains that this sūrah is a foundation for the principle of الولاء والبراء — allegiance and disavowal. It was revealed when one of the believers had privately sent word to the disbelievers of Makkah out of a worldly motive, and Allah warned against extending مَوَدَّة (affection and secret alliance) to عَدُوِّي وَعَدُوَّكُمْ — "My enemy and your enemy" — those who rejected the truth and drove the Prophet ﷺ and the believers from their homes simply for saying "our Lord is Allah." Al-Saʿdī: how can a believer offer love and loyalty to those at open war with his faith and his Lord? The bonds of religion are stronger than the bonds of tribe and blood, and on the Day of Resurrection relatives and children will be of no benefit — Allah alone will judge.

أُسْوَةٌ حَسَنَةٌ فِي إِبْرَاهِيمَ — The Model of Barāʾah

Al-Saʿdī highlights that Allah sets Ibrāhīm عليه السلام before the believers as أُسْوَة حَسَنَة — an excellent, beautiful example to follow. Ibrāhīm and those with him openly told their idol-worshipping people: إِنَّا بُرَآءُ مِنكُمْ وَمِمَّا تَعْبُدُونَ مِن دُونِ اللَّهِ — "we disown you and all you worship besides Allah." Note carefully what the disavowal targets: their false worship and enmity to the truth, not their humanity. And note its condition: it lasts حَتَّىٰ تُؤْمِنُوا بِاللَّهِ وَحْدَهُ — "until you believe in Allah alone." The moment they turn to tawḥīd, the enmity dissolves. This is barāʾah for the sake of Allah: a disowning of shirk and hostility to the religion, held out as an open invitation, not a call to cruelty. Al-Saʿdī also notes the one exception the āyah records — Ibrāhīm's promise to seek forgiveness for his father — which was before it became clear his father was an enemy of Allah, and is therefore not part of the example to imitate.

The Balance: Justice and Kindness to the Peaceful. Al-Saʿdī stresses that this sūrah must be read whole, for āyah 8 guards it from misuse. Barāʾah is directed at those who war against the religion and expel the believers — not at every non-Muslim. Allah says plainly: لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ ... أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ — Allah does not forbid you from being kind and dutiful (birr) and just (qisṭ) toward those who do not fight you over your religion or drive you out. Al-Saʿdī: this includes good neighbourliness, keeping trusts, honouring agreements, and fairness in dealings. And it is crowned with a statement Allah loves to affirm about Himself and His people: إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ — Allah loves the just. Islam disowns hostility to the truth, but it never licenses injustice.

The Examined Women and the Women's Pledge. The sūrah's name — al-Mumtaḥanah, "the woman who is examined" — comes from its command that the believing women who fled to Madinah be tested (imtaḥinūhunna) to confirm the sincerity of their faith, and if found to be true believers, not returned to the disbelievers, with the marriage and dowry rulings that follow. It also records the بَيْعَة (pledge) the women gave the Prophet ﷺ: not to associate anything with Allah, not to steal, not to commit unlawful intercourse, not to kill their children, and to obey him in what is right. Al-Saʿdī: the whole sūrah thus draws the believing community's boundary — firm loyalty to Allah and His people within, sincere faith tested at the door, and justice extended to all who come in peace.

Memorize & Hold On — احفظ وثبت

لَّا يَنْهَاكُمُ اللَّهُ عَنِ الَّذِينَ لَمْ يُقَاتِلُوكُمْ فِي الدِّينِ وَلَمْ يُخْرِجُوكُم مِّن دِيَارِكُمْ أَن تَبَرُّوهُمْ وَتُقْسِطُوا إِلَيْهِمْ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الْمُقْسِطِينَ

The āyah that keeps loyalty and disavowal in balance: Allah does not forbid you, toward those who did not fight you over religion nor drive you out, from being kind and just to them — indeed Allah loves the just. Firmness against hostility to the truth never becomes a licence for injustice to the peaceful.
