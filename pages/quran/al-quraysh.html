سورة قريش

Sūrat Quraysh — The Quraysh

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand what الإيلاف (the bonding/privilege) means and why it is the entire subject of this sūrah.
  • See the connection between this sūrah and the one before it — al-Fīl — and why many scholars read them as one unit of meaning.
  • Learn the two greatest worldly blessings Allah names here: food against hunger, and security against fear.
  • Understand why all these blessings lead to one conclusion: لِيَعْبُدُوا رَبَّ هَذَا الْبَيْتِ — worship the Lord of this House alone.
الآيات The Āyāt

لِإِيلَٰفِ قُرَيْشٍ ۝ إِۦلَٰفِهِمْ رِحْلَةَ ٱلشِّتَآءِ وَٱلصَّيْفِ ۝ فَلْيَعْبُدُوا۟ رَبَّ هَٰذَا ٱلْبَيْتِ ۝ ٱلَّذِىٓ أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَءَامَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍ

For the bonding of Quraysh — their bonding of the winter and summer journeys — let them worship the Lord of this House, Who fed them against hunger and secured them against fear.

Quraysh 106:1–4

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

The connection to سورة الفيل

Al-Saʿdī notes that many of the scholars of tafsīr said these two sūrahs are connected in meaning: We did what We did to the army of the elephant for the sake of Quraysh — to keep them safe, guard their interests, and secure their regular journeys. The destruction of Abraha's army was not just a miracle; it was specifically a gift to Quraysh, protecting the prestige of the Ḥaram and its people in the hearts of the Arabs. And that is precisely why the conclusion is: so now let them worship.

لِإِيلَافِ قُرَيْشٍ (for the bonding of Quraysh): الإيلاف means the bonding, the privilege, the covenant of safe passage. Quraysh were the guardians of the Sacred House in Makkah and had a unique status among the Arabs because of it. Because of this status, they could travel freely in safety — to Yemen in the winter for trade, and to Syria in the summer — while other Arab tribes respected and did not attack them.

رِحْلَةَ الشِّتَاءِ وَالصَّيْفِ (the winter and summer journeys): These were trade journeys upon which the entire economy of Quraysh depended. Winter: south to Yemen (warm climate, incense, goods from the Arabian Peninsula). Summer: north to Syria (the produce of the Levant, trade with the Roman world). Al-Saʿdī says: if there had been no other blessing than this ability to travel in safety, it alone would have been enough to demand gratitude and worship.

فَلْيَعْبُدُوا رَبَّ هَذَا الْبَيْتِ (let them worship the Lord of this House): The فَ here is the فاء السببية — the "because" particle. Because of all these blessings, the obligation that follows is: worship Him alone, affirming His oneness, devoting worship sincerely to Him and no other. Al-Saʿdī says: Allah specifically says "the Lord of this House" — honouring the Kaʿbah. But He is of course the Lord of all things.

أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَآمَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍ (Who fed them against hunger and secured them against fear): Al-Saʿdī says: plentiful provision and security against fear are among the greatest of worldly blessings — and both are mentioned here together as the two pillars of a life in which worship is possible. A person cannot focus on ibādah when they are starving. A person cannot give their heart to Allah when they are in constant fear. When Allah gives food and safety, He has removed the two greatest obstacles to gratitude and worship.

الدرس لنا The Lesson for Us

The structure of this sūrah is the structure of all gratitude: Allah names the blessing → states who gave it → and draws the only conclusion that follows — worship. Quraysh had food, safety, status, and the honour of living near the Kaʿbah. And the conclusion was not "be proud of your position" or "enjoy your wealth." It was: فَلْيَعْبُدُوا — worship.

Teach the children this pattern. You have food on the table — who gave it? أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ. You live in a country where you can pray and learn freely — who gave you that? آمَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍ. What do these blessings demand? فَلْيَعْبُدُوا رَبَّ هَذَا الْبَيْتِ. The logic of gratitude always ends at worship.

Memorize & Reflect — احفظ وتدبّر

أَطْعَمَهُم مِّن جُوعٍ وَآمَنَهُم مِّنْ خَوْفٍ · فَلْيَعْبُدُوا رَبَّ هَذَا الْبَيْتِ

At the next family meal: point to the food and say these two lines. Allah fed you against hunger. Allah secured you against fear. The conclusion is not complicated: فَلْيَعْبُدُوا — so let them worship. Use this sūrah to teach children the logic of gratitude — it always ends at Allah.
