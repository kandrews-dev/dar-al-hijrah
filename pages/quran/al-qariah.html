سورة القارعة

Sūrat al-Qāriʿah — The Striking Catastrophe

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know why the Day of Judgement is named القارعة — what it does to people and to the world.
  • Understand the two vivid images: people like فَرَاشٍ مَّبْثُوث (scattered moths) and mountains like عِهْنٍ مَّنفُوش (tufts of dyed wool).
  • Learn the outcome of the scales: heavy good deeds → pleasant life; light good deeds → الهاوية (the abyss).
  • See the pattern of this sūrah: the Day is described, then asked "what is it?", then explained — a rhetorical structure the Quran uses for its most terrifying events.
الآيات The Āyāt

ٱلْقَارِعَةُ ۝ مَا ٱلْقَارِعَةُ ۝ وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْقَارِعَةُ ۝ يَوْمَ يَكُونُ ٱلنَّاسُ كَٱلْفَرَاشِ ٱلْمَبْثُوثِ ۝ وَتَكُونُ ٱلْجِبَالُ كَٱلْعِهْنِ ٱلْمَنفُوشِ ۝ فَأَمَّا مَن ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ ۝ فَهُوَ فِى عِيشَةٍ رَّاضِيَةٍ ۝ وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ ۝ فَأُمُّهُۥ هَاوِيَةٌ ۝ وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا هِيَهْ ۝ نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌ

The Catastrophe! What is the Catastrophe? And how could you know what the Catastrophe is? On the day when people will be like scattered moths — and the mountains will be like tufts of dyed wool. Then as for him whose good deeds weigh heavily — he will have a well-pleasing life. And as for him whose good deeds weigh lightly — his abode will be the abyss. And how could you know what it is? A blazing, scorching fire.

al-Qāriʿah 101:1–11

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

الْقَارِعَةُ · مَا الْقَارِعَةُ · وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْقَارِعَةُ: Al-Saʿdī says القارعة is one of the names of the Day of Resurrection. It is called القارعة because it will disturb (تَقْرَع) and distress people with its horrors — it strikes hard. The structure — named, then questioned, then questioned again — emphasises its severity. As with الحطمة in the previous lesson: the rhetorical question signals what lies beyond description.

Two images of that Day

كَالْفَرَاشِ الْمَبْثُوثِ (like scattered moths): Al-Saʿdī says: on that day, because of the intensity of its hardship and horror, people will move in waves — like locusts, like moths that flutter at night in scattered groups, not knowing where they are going, rushing toward fire out of confusion and fear. This is the image of created beings who, despite all their reason, are completely disoriented by what is happening around them.

كَالْعِهْنِ الْمَنفُوشِ (like tufts of dyed wool): The great solid mountains — which in this world are the symbol of weight, permanence, and unmovability — will become like tufts of wool that have been carded and fluffed out: light, scattered, blown away with the slightest puff of wind. Then they will become scattered dust; nothing visible will remain of them.

الميزان The Scales — two outcomes

After the mountains are gone, the balance (الميزان) is set up and people divide into two groups. Al-Saʿdī explains:

ثَقُلَتْ مَوَازِينُهُ

Good deeds outweigh bad deeds

فَهُوَ فِي عِيشَةٍ رَّاضِيَةٍ — He will have a well-pleasing life, in the gardens of bliss

خَفَّتْ مَوَازِينُهُ

Good deeds do not outweigh bad deeds

فَأُمُّهُ هَاوِيَةٌ — His abode is الهاوية (the abyss) — one of the names of Hell

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا هِيَهْ · نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌ (and how could you know what it is? A blazing, scorching fire): Again the rhetorical question to signal what transcends ordinary description. Then the answer: نَارٌ حَامِيَةٌ — a fire blazing with intense heat. Al-Saʿdī: its heat is seventy times hotter than the fire of this world. We seek refuge with Allah from it.

الدرس لنا The Lesson for Us

This sūrah teaches children three things in eleven āyāt: (1) that Day is real and will be unlike anything in this world; (2) that on that Day nothing will matter except the weight of your good deeds in the scales; (3) that the abyss is a place that beggars description — a fire seventy times hotter than what we know. The Quran asks "how could you know what it is?" precisely because we cannot imagine it. That gap between imagination and reality should produce action now.

Memorize & Weigh — احفظ وزِن

فَأَمَّا مَن ثَقُلَتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ فَهُوَ فِى عِيشَةٍ رَّاضِيَةٍ · وَأَمَّا مَنْ خَفَّتْ مَوَٰزِينُهُۥ فَأُمُّهُۥ هَاوِيَةٌ

Memorize this sūrah. Then ask the children: what is in our scales today? We spent time — was it used in obedience? We spoke — was it good speech? We ate — were we grateful? Every moment is something placed on one side of the scale or the other. The image of the moth that doesn't know where it's going is the image of a person who doesn't weigh their deeds.
