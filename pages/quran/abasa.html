سورة عبس

Sūrat ʿAbasa — He Frowned

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know the incident of ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm and what Allah's rebuke to the Prophet ﷺ teaches about whom knowledge truly benefits — the eager seeker over the indifferent grandee.
  • Understand the contrast between أَمَّا مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى (the self-sufficient one given attention) and مَن جَاءَكَ يَسْعَى وَهُوَ يَخْشَى (the one coming striving and fearing — turned away).
  • Know the seven stages of man in this sūrah (sperm → proportioned → path made easy → death → grave → resurrection) and what al-Saʿdī says about human ingratitude despite all of this.
  • Know what the Deafening Blast (الصَّاخَّة) means and the two faces described: bright and laughing vs. dark and dust-covered — the wicked disbelievers.
الآيات — القسم الأولThe Āyāt — Part One: The Incident and Its Lesson (1–16)

عَبَسَ وَتَوَلَّى ۝ أَن جَاءَهُ الْأَعْمَى ۝ وَمَا يُدْرِيكَ لَعَلَّهُ يَزَّكَّى ۝ أَوْ يَذَّكَّرُ فَتَنفَعَهُ الذِّكْرَى ۝ أَمَّا مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى ۝ فَأَنتَ لَهُ تَصَدَّى ۝ وَمَا عَلَيْكَ أَلَّا يَزَّكَّى ۝ وَأَمَّا مَن جَاءَكَ يَسْعَى ۝ وَهُوَ يَخْشَى ۝ فَأَنتَ عَنْهُ تَلَهَّى ۝ كَلَّا إِنَّهَا تَذْكِرَةٌ ۝ فَمَن شَاءَ ذَكَرَهُ ۝ فِي صُحُفٍ مُّكَرَّمَةٍ ۝ مَّرْفُوعَةٍ مُّطَهَّرَةٍ ۝ بِأَيْدِي سَفَرَةٍ ۝ كِرَامٍ بَرَرَةٍ

He frowned and turned away, because the blind man came to him. But how could you know? Perhaps he would purify himself, or take heed and the reminder would benefit him. As for the one who considers himself self-sufficient — you give him your full attention, though it is not your concern if he does not purify himself. But as for the one who comes to you striving while he fears — you are distracted from him. No! Indeed this is a reminder; so let whoever wills, take heed. It is in honoured scriptures, exalted and purified, carried by the hands of noble scribes, honourable and righteous.

ʿAbasa 80:1–16

الآيات — القسم الثانيThe Āyāt — Part Two: Man's Ingratitude and Creation (17–32)

قُتِلَ الْإِنسَانُ مَا أَكْفَرَهُ ۝ مِنْ أَيِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ ۝ مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُ فَقَدَّرَهُ ۝ ثُمَّ السَّبِيلَ يَسَّرَهُ ۝ ثُمَّ أَمَاتَهُ فَأَقْبَرَهُ ۝ ثُمَّ إِذَا شَاءَ أَنشَرَهُ ۝ كَلَّا لَمَّا يَقْضِ مَا أَمَرَهُ ۝ فَلْيَنظُرِ الْإِنسَانُ إِلَى طَعَامِهِ ۝ أَنَّا صَبَبْنَا الْمَاءَ صَبًّا ۝ ثُمَّ شَقَقْنَا الْأَرْضَ شَقًّا ۝ فَأَنبَتْنَا فِيهَا حَبًّا ۝ وَعِنَبًا وَقَضْبًا ۝ وَزَيْتُونًا وَنَخْلًا ۝ وَحَدَائِقَ غُلْبًا ۝ وَفَاكِهَةً وَأَبًّا ۝ مَّتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِكُمْ

Woe to man — how ungrateful he is! From what did Allah create him? From a sperm-drop He created him and proportioned him, then made the path easy for him, then caused him to die and placed him in a grave, then when He wills He will resurrect him. But no — he has not yet fulfilled what Allah commanded him. So let man look at his food: We poured down water in abundance, then split the earth open, and caused grain to grow therein, and grapes and herbage, and olives and date palms, and dense lush gardens, and fruit and fodder — a provision for you and your livestock.

ʿAbasa 80:17–32

الآيات — القسم الثالثThe Āyāt — Part Three: The Deafening Blast (33–42)

فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الصَّاخَّةُ ۝ يَوْمَ يَفِرُّ الْمَرْءُ مِنْ أَخِيهِ ۝ وَأُمِّهِ وَأَبِيهِ ۝ وَصَاحِبَتِهِ وَبَنِيهِ ۝ لِكُلِّ امْرِئٍ مِّنْهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ شَأْنٌ يُغْنِيهِ ۝ وُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ مُّسْفِرَةٌ ۝ ضَاحِكَةٌ مُّسْتَبْشِرَةٌ ۝ وَوُجُوهٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ عَلَيْهَا غَبَرَةٌ ۝ تَرْهَقُهَا قَتَرَةٌ ۝ أُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْكَفَرَةُ الْفَجَرَةُ

Then when the Deafening Blast comes — the Day when a man will flee from his own brother, and from his mother and father, and from his wife and children — every person on that Day will have enough to preoccupy him. Some faces that Day will be bright, laughing and rejoicing at good news. And other faces that Day will have dust upon them, covered in darkness — those are the wicked disbelievers.

ʿAbasa 80:33–42

التفسيرExplanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

The sūrah begins by addressing the Prophet ﷺ in the third person — a subtle indication that this is a loving correction, not a harsh rebuke. The incident: ʿAbdullāh ibn Umm Maktūm, a blind Companion, came to the Prophet ﷺ while he was engaged in conversation with some of the leading men of Quraysh, hoping to guide them to Islam. The Prophet ﷺ frowned at being interrupted. Allah gently corrects him: how do you know this blind man will not purify himself? Or take heed and benefit from the reminder? Meanwhile the man from Quraysh — you give him your full attention, even though you bear no responsibility if he refuses to be guided.

أَمَّا مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى / وَأَمَّا مَن جَاءَكَ يَسْعَى — The Two Contrasts

Al-Saʿdī draws a sharp teaching principle from this rebuke. The one who considers himself self-sufficient — who has no need for the reminder, who feels he is above it — is given the da'wah effort, yet the outward success of that effort is not the caller's responsibility. But the one who comes striving and fearing — driven by urgency and تقوى (fear of Allah) — is the one most deserving of attention and time. This is the rank ordering of a da'wah investment: focus on those who want to learn, who fear Allah, who approach with sincerity. They are most likely to benefit and grow.

قُتِلَ الْإِنسَانُ — Woe to Man, How Ungrateful! Al-Saʿdī: this is an expression of profound astonishment at human ingratitude. Look at what man is: he began as a worthless drop of fluid. Allah created him from it, fashioned his every feature, made him fully proportioned. Then He made the path easy for him — the path of guidance, livelihood, and knowledge. Then He will cause him to die and place him in a dignified grave. Then — when Allah wills — He will resurrect him. This entire arc of creation, sustenance, death, and resurrection is Allah's — and still man does not fulfil what was commanded of him.

فَلْيَنظُرِ الْإِنسَانُ إِلَى طَعَامِهِ — Let Man Look at His Food: Before the Day of Judgment is described, Allah points to the most ordinary proof of His power and care: food. We poured down rain. We cracked open the earth. We made grain, grapes, herbage, olives, date palms, dense gardens, fruits, and pasture — for you and your animals. Every meal on the table required water from the sky, soil split by roots, and the orderly workings of seasons Allah maintains. Al-Saʿdī: man should look at how thoroughly he is provided for, then ask himself whether he has been equally thorough in gratitude and obedience.

الصَّاخَّة — The Deafening Blast: A name for the Day of Judgment drawn from its overwhelming noise. On that Day, a man will flee from his brother, his mother, his father, his wife, and his children — each person so consumed with terror and self-concern that they cannot turn to anyone else. لِكُلِّ امْرِئٍ مِّنْهُمْ يَوْمَئِذٍ شَأْنٌ يُغْنِيهِ — every person will have enough to occupy him. Two groups emerge: faces bright, laughing, rejoicing — the people of Paradise. And faces dusted, darkened, covered in shadow — أُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْكَفَرَةُ الْفَجَرَةُ — those are the wicked disbelievers. The face reflects the heart's condition on that Day.

Memorize & Hold On — احفظ وثبت

أَمَّا مَنِ اسْتَغْنَى ۝ فَأَنتَ لَهُ تَصَدَّى ۝ وَأَمَّا مَن جَاءَكَ يَسْعَى ۝ وَهُوَ يَخْشَى ۝ فَأَنتَ عَنْهُ تَلَهَّى

Read these five āyāt as a mirror for your own pursuit of knowledge. Which one are you? The one who comes striving and fearing, sitting early in the front row, eager to understand — or the one who drifts in and out, self-sufficient, feeling little urgency? The rebuke here is gentle but the lesson is permanent: the attitude of يَسْعَى وَيَخْشَى (striving and fearing) is what opens the heart to receive knowledge and benefit from it.
