Sūrah 59 · Juzʾ 28 · Madani · 24 āyāt

سورة الحشر

Sūrat al-Ḥashr — The Gathering / Exile

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand the lesson (اعتبار) from the exile of Banū al-Naḍīr, whose fortresses could not shield them from Allah.
  • Know the great principle drawn from the āyah of the فَيْء: whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; whatever he forbids you, abstain from it.
  • Know the praise of the Muhājirūn and Anṣār — and the beautiful duʿāʾ for those who preceded us in faith, free of rancor.
  • Understand الإيثار (self-sacrifice): the Anṣār preferred others over themselves though they were in need.
  • Take to heart وَلْتَنظُرْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ لِغَدٍ and reflect on the majestic closing āyāt of the Names of Allah.
الآيات — القسم الأولPart One: The Exile of Banū al-Naḍīr — Take Heed (2)

هُوَ الَّذِي أَخْرَجَ الَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا مِنْ أَهْلِ الْكِتَابِ مِن دِيَارِهِمْ لِأَوَّلِ الْحَشْرِ ۚ مَا ظَنَنتُمْ أَن يَخْرُجُوا ۖ وَظَنُّوا أَنَّهُم مَّانِعَتُهُمْ حُصُونُهُم مِّنَ اللَّهِ فَأَتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَمْ يَحْتَسِبُوا ۖ وَقَذَفَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الرُّعْبَ ۚ يُخْرِبُونَ بُيُوتَهُم بِأَيْدِيهِمْ وَأَيْدِي الْمُؤْمِنِينَ فَاعْتَبِرُوا يَا أُولِي الْأَبْصَارِ

It is He who expelled those who disbelieved among the People of the Book from their homes at the first gathering. You did not think they would leave, and they thought that their fortresses would protect them from Allah; but Allah came upon them from where they had not expected, and He cast terror into their hearts, so they destroyed their houses by their own hands and the hands of the believers. So take a lesson, O people of insight.

al-Ḥashr 59:2

الآيات — القسم الثانيPart Two: Obey the Messenger; the Self-Sacrifice of the Anṣār (7, 9)

وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانتَهُوا ۚ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ ۖ إِنَّ اللَّهَ شَدِيدُ الْعِقَابِ ۝ وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ ۚ وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ

And whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it. And fear Allah; indeed Allah is severe in penalty. … And they [the Anṣār] give preference [to others] over themselves, even though they are in privation. And whoever is protected from the greed of his own soul — it is those who are the successful.

al-Ḥashr 59:7, 9

الآيات — القسم الثالثPart Three: Look to What You Send Ahead for Tomorrow (18–19)

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَلْتَنظُرْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ لِغَدٍ ۖ وَاتَّقُوا اللَّهَ ۚ إِنَّ اللَّهَ خَبِيرٌ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ ۝ وَلَا تَكُونُوا كَالَّذِينَ نَسُوا اللَّهَ فَأَنسَاهُمْ أَنفُسَهُمْ ۚ أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ

O you who believe, fear Allah. And let every soul look to what it has put forth for tomorrow — and fear Allah. Indeed Allah is Aware of what you do. And do not be like those who forgot Allah, so He made them forget themselves. It is those who are the defiantly disobedient.

al-Ḥashr 59:18–19

الآيات — القسم الرابعPart Four: The Awe of the Qurʾān and the Beautiful Names (21–24)

لَوْ أَنزَلْنَا هَٰذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٍ لَّرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُّتَصَدِّعًا مِّنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ ۚ وَتِلْكَ الْأَمْثَالُ نَضْرِبُهَا لِلنَّاسِ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَتَفَكَّرُونَ ۝ هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ ۖ عَالِمُ الْغَيْبِ وَالشَّهَادَةِ ۖ هُوَ الرَّحْمَٰنُ الرَّحِيمُ ۝ هُوَ اللَّهُ الَّذِي لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْمَلِكُ الْقُدُّوسُ السَّلَامُ الْمُؤْمِنُ الْمُهَيْمِنُ الْعَزِيزُ الْجَبَّارُ الْمُتَكَبِّرُ ۚ سُبْحَانَ اللَّهِ عَمَّا يُشْرِكُونَ ۝ هُوَ اللَّهُ الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ ۖ لَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ ۚ يُسَبِّحُ لَهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ ۖ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْحَكِيمُ

Had We sent down this Qurʾān upon a mountain, you would have seen it humbled and splitting apart from the fear of Allah. And these examples We present to the people that perhaps they may reflect. He is Allah, other than whom there is no deity, Knower of the unseen and the witnessed. He is the Most Compassionate, the Most Merciful. He is Allah, other than whom there is no deity, the Sovereign, the Pure, the Perfection, the Bestower of Faith [and security], the Overseer, the Exalted in Might, the Compeller, the Supreme. Exalted is Allah above whatever they associate with Him. He is Allah, the Creator, the Originator, the Fashioner; to Him belong the most beautiful names. Whatever is in the heavens and earth glorifies Him, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Wise.

al-Ḥashr 59:21–24

التفسيرExplanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

Al-Saʿdī explains that the sūrah takes its name — al-Ḥashr, "the Gathering / Exile" — from the expulsion of the treacherous tribe of Banū al-Naḍīr from Madinah after they broke their covenant with the Prophet ﷺ and plotted against him. Their story is set out as a lesson in Allah's power: they were certain their strong fortresses would protect them, and the believers themselves did not expect them to leave, فَأَتَاهُمُ اللَّهُ مِنْ حَيْثُ لَمْ يَحْتَسِبُوا — Allah came at them from a direction they never reckoned on, and قَذَفَ فِي قُلُوبِهِمُ الرُّعْبَ, cast terror into their hearts, until they were pulling down their own houses with their own hands. The āyah ends with a command that is really the theme of the whole passage: فَاعْتَبِرُوا يَا أُولِي الْأَبْصَارِ — take heed and draw a lesson, O people of insight. No fortress, wealth, or scheme shields anyone from Allah.

وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ — A Foundation of the Sunnah

In the āyah of the فَيْء (property gained from an enemy without battle, whose distribution Allah details so it does not circulate only among the rich), a sentence occurs that the scholars take as a pillar of following the Prophet ﷺ: وَمَا آتَاكُمُ الرَّسُولُ فَخُذُوهُ وَمَا نَهَاكُمْ عَنْهُ فَانتَهُوا — "Whatever the Messenger gives you, take it; and whatever he forbids you, abstain from it." Al-Saʿdī: though its immediate context is the spoils, the wording is general, and so it is a proof that the Prophet's ﷺ commands and prohibitions in the religion are to be obeyed exactly as Allah's own — for he does not command or forbid except by revelation. This single clause establishes the binding authority of the Sunnah alongside the Qurʾān.

Three Generations of Believers, and the Self-Sacrifice of the Anṣār: Al-Saʿdī points out how the sūrah then honours the believers in three ranks: the Muhājirūn who were driven from their homes and wealth seeking Allah's pleasure; the Anṣār who had settled in Madinah and in faith before them, who love those who emigrated to them and يُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰ أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ — prefer them over themselves even when they themselves are in need; and those who came after, whose mark is the duʿāʾ: رَبَّنَا اغْفِرْ لَنَا وَلِإِخْوَانِنَا الَّذِينَ سَبَقُونَا بِالْإِيمَانِ وَلَا تَجْعَلْ فِي قُلُوبِنَا غِلًّا لِّلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا — asking forgiveness for their believing predecessors and that Allah place no rancor in their hearts toward any believer. Al-Saʿdī: from this the scholars derived the manners of Ahl al-Sunnah toward the Ṣaḥābah — love, seeking forgiveness for them, and a heart free of resentment. And here too returns the key of success: وَمَن يُوقَ شُحَّ نَفْسِهِ فَأُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الْمُفْلِحُونَ, whoever is guarded from the greed of his soul is among the truly successful — for it was overcoming that greed that produced the Anṣār's astonishing generosity.

Look to Your Tomorrow: The sūrah then turns the believer's gaze inward and forward: يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا اتَّقُوا اللَّهَ وَلْتَنظُرْ نَفْسٌ مَّا قَدَّمَتْ لِغَدٍ — fear Allah, and let every soul examine what it has sent ahead for "tomorrow," meaning the Day of Resurrection. Al-Saʿdī: this is a call to daily self-accounting — to look honestly at one's deeds as a merchant reviews his ledger, before the reckoning that cannot be revised. And it warns against the opposite state: وَلَا تَكُونُوا كَالَّذِينَ نَسُوا اللَّهَ فَأَنسَاهُمْ أَنفُسَهُمْ — do not be of those who forgot Allah, so He made them forget their own souls: forgetting what would save them and the good they should do for themselves. To forget Allah is, in the end, to lose oneself.

The Majestic Seal — the Names of Allah: Al-Saʿdī describes the closing āyāt as among the greatest passages of the Qurʾān on tawḥīd and the Names of Allah. First their power is shown: لَوْ أَنزَلْنَا هَٰذَا الْقُرْآنَ عَلَىٰ جَبَلٍ لَّرَأَيْتَهُ خَاشِعًا مُّتَصَدِّعًا مِّنْ خَشْيَةِ اللَّهِ — had this Qurʾān been sent down on a mountain, you would have seen it humbled and shattered from the fear of Allah; so how should the human heart, that received it, not soften? Then follow the sealing āyāt naming Allah as الْمَلِكُ الْقُدُّوسُ السَّلَامُ الْمُؤْمِنُ الْمُهَيْمِنُ الْعَزِيزُ الْجَبَّارُ الْمُتَكَبِّرُ … الْخَالِقُ الْبَارِئُ الْمُصَوِّرُ, and declaring لَهُ الْأَسْمَاءُ الْحُسْنَىٰ — to Him belong the most beautiful names. Al-Saʿdī: every name is perfect, containing an attribute of perfect majesty, beauty, and power; reflecting on them fills the heart with love, awe, and reliance. It is fitting that a sūrah which began with the downfall of those who defied Allah ends with the perfection of the One they defied.

Recite it slowly, letting each name settle in the heart.
