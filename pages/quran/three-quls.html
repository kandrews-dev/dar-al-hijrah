The Three Quls — al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq & al-Nās

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Read and understand the last three sūrahs of the Qur'an.
  • See how al-Ikhlāṣ teaches the oneness of Allah's names and attributes.
  • Learn what al-Falaq and al-Nās seek refuge from, and why.
  • Know the sunnah of reciting them for protection, morning and night.

These three short sūrahs are the first many of us memorize — and, as Imam al-Saʿdī shows, they carry tremendous meaning: the purest statement of who Allah is, and a complete shelter from every evil.

سورة الإخلاص Sūrat al-Ikhlāṣ — Sincerity (112)

قُلْ هُوَ ٱللَّهُ أَحَدٌ ۝ ٱللَّهُ ٱلصَّمَدُ ۝ لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ ۝ وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُۥ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌۢ

Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Self-Sufficient Master, sought by all. He neither begets nor was begotten, and none is equal to Him.

al-Ikhlāṣ 112:1–4

Say it with certain faith: He, Allah, is One — utterly unique, alone in perfection, with the most beautiful names and the most sublime attributes, His deeds far above any flaw; He has no equal. al-Ṣamad is the Master whom all creation turns to in every need — He is perfect in knowledge, mercy, forbearance, and every attribute. He neither begets nor was begotten, for He is wholly free of need; and none is comparable to Him in His names, attributes, or actions. Al-Saʿdī notes this sūrah establishes tawḥīd al-asmāʾ wa-l-ṣifāt — the oneness of Allah's names and attributes.

سورة الفلق Sūrat al-Falaq — The Daybreak (113)

قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلْفَلَقِ ۝ مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ ۝ وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ ۝ وَمِن شَرِّ ٱلنَّفَّٰثَٰتِ فِى ٱلْعُقَدِ ۝ وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ

Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the daybreak, from the evil of what He created, from the evil of darkness when it settles, from the evil of those who blow on knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies.

al-Falaq 113:1–5

I seek refuge — I turn for protection — in the Lord of the daybreak, the One who splits the dawn. First a general refuge: from the evil of all that He created — human, jinn or animal. Then the specifics: darkness when it settles — the night, when harm spreads; those who blow on knots — sorcerers who work their magic by blowing on tied knots (al-Saʿdī notes this shows magic is real and its harm is to be feared); and the envier when he envies — one who wishes a blessing gone from another and strives to remove it, which al-Saʿdī says includes the evil eye. So al-Falaq is a shelter from every evil, in general and in detail.

سورة الناس Sūrat al-Nās — Mankind (114)

قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝ مَلِكِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝ إِلَٰهِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝ مِن شَرِّ ٱلْوَسْوَاسِ ٱلْخَنَّاسِ ۝ ٱلَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ ٱلنَّاسِ ۝ مِنَ ٱلْجِنَّةِ وَٱلنَّاسِ

Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the Sovereign of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer, who whispers into the hearts of mankind, from among jinn and mankind.

al-Nās 114:1–6

Here refuge is sought through three of Allah's relationships to people: Rabb (the Lord who creates and sustains them), Malik (the Sovereign who owns and controls them — He holds the forelock of every being), and Ilāh (the only true God worthy of their worship). The refuge is from the retreating whisperer — the Shayṭān, the root of all evil — who whispers into the hearts, making evil look beautiful and good look ugly. He is "al-khannās," the one who retreats the moment a person remembers Allah. And these whisperers come from among jinn and mankind. Al-Saʿdī's lesson: we repel this enemy by remembering Allah and worshipping Him — the very purpose for which we were created.

A gem & a sunnah — فائدة وسنة

Notice the balance: al-Falaq names one of Allah's attributes (Lord of the daybreak) and seeks refuge from many evils; al-Nās names three attributes and seeks refuge from one — the whisperer — because the corruption of the heart is the gravest evil of all.

The sunnah: recite these three sūrahs (al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq, al-Nās) three times each morning and evening, and before sleep — a daily shield for you and the children.

Memorize & Protect — احفظ وتحصّن

Recite al-Ikhlāṣ, al-Falaq and al-Nās — three times each — every morning and evening, and before sleep. Make it a daily habit with the children: knowledge of Allah, and a shield from every harm.
