سورة التحريم

Sūrat al-Taḥrīm — The Prohibition

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand the opening: a servant should not forbid upon himself what Allah has made lawful, even to please others.
  • Know the meaning of تَحِلَّة الأَيْمَان (the expiation that dissolves an oath) referred to in āyah 2.
  • Grasp the command قُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَارًا — to protect oneself and one's family from the Fire, and what "protecting" them means in practice.
  • Understand تَوْبَة نَصُوح (sincere repentance) and its conditions as al-Saʿdī explains them.
  • Know the four women Allah set as examples — two who were near the righteous yet perished, and two who were surrounded by evil yet were saved — and the principle each teaches.
الآيات — القسم الأولPart One: Do Not Forbid the Lawful (1–5)

يَا أَيُّهَا النَّبِيُّ لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللَّهُ لَكَ ۖ تَبْتَغِي مَرْضَاتَ أَزْوَاجِكَ ۚ وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ ۝ قَدْ فَرَضَ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ تَحِلَّةَ أَيْمَانِكُمْ ۚ وَاللَّهُ مَوْلَاكُمْ ۖ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيمُ الْحَكِيمُ

O Prophet, why do you forbid [yourself] what Allah has made lawful for you, seeking the pleasure of your wives? And Allah is Forgiving, Merciful. Allah has already ordained for you [the manner of] the dissolution of your oaths. And Allah is your Protector, and He is the All-Knowing, the All-Wise.

al-Taḥrīm 66:1–2

الآيات — القسم الثانيPart Two: Guard Yourselves and Your Families; Sincere Repentance (6–8)

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَارًا وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ عَلَيْهَا مَلَائِكَةٌ غِلَاظٌ شِدَادٌ لَّا يَعْصُونَ اللَّهَ مَا أَمَرَهُمْ وَيَفْعَلُونَ مَا يُؤْمَرُونَ ۝ يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا تُوبُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ تَوْبَةً نَّصُوحًا عَسَىٰ رَبُّكُمْ أَن يُكَفِّرَ عَنكُمْ سَيِّئَاتِكُمْ وَيُدْخِلَكُمْ جَنَّاتٍ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ

O you who believe, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones, over which are angels, harsh and severe, who do not disobey Allah in what He commands them and do whatever they are commanded. O you who believe, repent to Allah with a sincere repentance. Perhaps your Lord will remove from you your misdeeds and admit you into gardens beneath which rivers flow.

al-Taḥrīm 66:6, 8

الآيات — القسم الثالثPart Three: Four Women Set as Examples (10–12)

ضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا لِّلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا امْرَأَتَ نُوحٍ وَامْرَأَتَ لُوطٍ ۖ كَانَتَا تَحْتَ عَبْدَيْنِ مِنْ عِبَادِنَا صَالِحَيْنِ فَخَانَتَاهُمَا فَلَمْ يُغْنِيَا عَنْهُمَا مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا وَقِيلَ ادْخُلَا النَّارَ مَعَ الدَّاخِلِينَ ۝ وَضَرَبَ اللَّهُ مَثَلًا لِّلَّذِينَ آمَنُوا امْرَأَتَ فِرْعَوْنَ إِذْ قَالَتْ رَبِّ ابْنِ لِي عِندَكَ بَيْتًا فِي الْجَنَّةِ وَنَجِّنِي مِن فِرْعَوْنَ وَعَمَلِهِ وَنَجِّنِي مِنَ الْقَوْمِ الظَّالِمِينَ ۝ وَمَرْيَمَ ابْنَتَ عِمْرَانَ الَّتِي أَحْصَنَتْ فَرْجَهَا فَنَفَخْنَا فِيهِ مِن رُّوحِنَا وَصَدَّقَتْ بِكَلِمَاتِ رَبِّهَا وَكُتُبِهِ وَكَانَتْ مِنَ الْقَانِتِينَ

Allah presents an example for those who disbelieved: the wife of Nūḥ and the wife of Lūṭ. They were under two of Our righteous servants but betrayed them, so those two availed them nothing against Allah, and it was said, "Enter the Fire with those who enter." And Allah presents an example for those who believed: the wife of Pharaoh, when she said, "My Lord, build for me near You a house in Paradise, and save me from Pharaoh and his deeds, and save me from the wrongdoing people." And [the example of] Maryam, the daughter of ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity, so We breathed into it from Our spirit, and she believed in the words of her Lord and His scriptures, and she was of the devoutly obedient.

al-Taḥrīm 66:10–12

التفسيرExplanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

Al-Saʿdī explains that the sūrah opens with a gentle correction of the Prophet ﷺ. To please some of his wives, he had forbidden upon himself something Allah had made lawful for him. Allah does not rebuke him harshly — the āyah is framed as tender reproach followed immediately by وَاللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ (and Allah is Forgiving, Merciful) — but it establishes a lasting principle: a person should not declare unlawful for himself what Allah has permitted, whether by an oath or otherwise, and if he does bind himself with an oath, Allah has already ordained the way out through تَحِلَّة الأَيْمَان — the expiation (kaffārah) that dissolves an oath. This is why the fiqh of oaths is drawn partly from this sūrah: one does not remain trapped by a rash vow; one expiates and returns to what is good.

The sūrah then turns to every believer with one of the weightiest commands in the Qurʾān concerning the family: قُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَارًا — protect yourselves and your families from a Fire. Al-Saʿdī: protecting oneself is by leaving sin and doing what Allah commands; protecting one's family is by teaching them, disciplining them, commanding them to good and forbidding them from evil, and helping them upon the obedience of Allah. A man is not a guardian of his household in provision alone — he is responsible before Allah for their religion. The Fire described here is terrifying: its very fuel is النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ (people and stones), and it is guarded by angels who are غِلَاظٌ شِدَادٌ — harsh and severe — who never disobey a single command of Allah.

توبة نصوح — What Makes Repentance "Sincere"

The command تُوبُوا إِلَى اللَّهِ تَوْبَةً نَّصُوحًا (repent to Allah with a sincere repentance) gives the whole sūrah its practical heart. Al-Saʿdī explains that a naṣūḥ repentance is one that is truthful and complete — it gathers the conditions of accepted tawbah: (1) immediately abandoning the sin, (2) sincere regret over having done it, (3) firm resolve never to return to it, and — where the sin involves the rights of others — (4) restoring those rights or seeking pardon. It is called naṣūḥ (from نُصْح, sincere advice) because the servant is, in effect, sincerely advising his own soul back to Allah. Notice the word عَسَىٰ ("perhaps") that follows: even after such repentance the servant keeps hope and fear together, never presuming upon Allah, always trusting His mercy.

The Four Women — Two Warnings and Two Examples: Al-Saʿdī draws out the striking symmetry Allah closes the sūrah with. First, two women who were married to prophets — the wife of Nūḥ and the wife of Lūṭ عليهما السلام — yet disbelieved and betrayed them (not in chastity, but in religion, siding with the deniers). Being in the very household of a messenger did not save them: فَلَمْ يُغْنِيَا عَنْهُمَا مِنَ اللَّهِ شَيْئًا. The lesson: no relationship, however close and honoured, benefits a person if they themselves reject faith. Against them stand two believing women surrounded by evil. The wife of Pharaoh (Āsiyah), living in the house of the worst tyrant on earth, asked her Lord for a house near Him in Paradise and rescue from Pharaoh and his deeds — her faith flourished in the darkest place. And Maryam bint ʿImrān, who guarded her chastity, believed in her Lord's words and scriptures, and was مِنَ الْقَانِتِينَ (among the devoutly obedient). The lesson: a righteous soul is saved by its own faith even without a righteous spouse or society. Each person carries their own record before Allah.

Al-Saʿdī notes how perfectly this closing fits a sūrah that began within the Prophet's own household: it reassures and reminds his wives — and every believer — that nearness to the righteous is a mercy, but salvation rests on one's own faith and obedience, not on lineage or marriage. The tyrant's own wife reached the highest ranks; a prophet's own wife entered the Fire. Faith is the divider.

Memorize & Hold On — احفظ وثبت

يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا قُوا أَنفُسَكُمْ وَأَهْلِيكُمْ نَارًا وَقُودُهَا النَّاسُ وَالْحِجَارَةُ

Hold onto āyah 6 — O you who believe, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones. A believer's responsibility does not end at his own soul; it reaches to everyone Allah placed in his care. Alongside it, keep āyah 8's command — tūbū ilā-llāhi tawbatan naṣūḥā — the doorway back to Allah that never closes while breath remains.
