Sūrah 103 · Juzʾ ʿAmma · Makki · 3 āyāt

سورة العصر

Sūrat al-ʿAṣr — By Time

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Understand why Allah swears by العصر (Time) and what this oath means.
  • Know the meaning of خُسْر (loss) — absolute loss vs. partial loss — and why the default for all mankind is loss.
  • Memorize and explain the four qualities that exempt a person from this loss.
  • Understand the distinction al-Saʿdī draws between the first two qualities (perfecting oneself) and the last two (perfecting others).
الآيات The Āyāt

وَٱلْعَصْرِ ۝ إِنَّ ٱلْإِنسَٰنَ لَفِى خُسْرٍ ۝ إِلَّا ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَعَمِلُوا۟ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا۟ بِٱلصَّبْرِ

By Time — verily man is in a state of utter loss, except those who believe and do righteous deeds, and exhort one another to hold fast to the truth, and exhort one another to be steadfast.

al-ʿAṣr 103:1–3

What the scholars said about this sūrah

Imam al-Shāfiʿī said: "If people reflected only on this sūrah, it would suffice them." Three āyāt contain the complete program of salvation — what you must believe, what you must do, how you must live with others, and what you must endure.

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

وَالْعَصْرِ (By Time): Allah swears by العصر — Time, the flow of night and day in which all human deeds take place. By swearing by it, Allah draws attention to it as something weighty: time is the arena of every deed, every word, every moment of obedience or disobedience. It passes and does not return.

إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ (verily man is in a state of utter loss): Al-Saʿdī explains that خُسْر means loss — the opposite of success. The statement covers every human being as the default: unless something saves them, they are in loss. There are two levels of loss:

نوع الخسارة Type of loss Who
الخسران المطلق Absolute loss Loses in this world AND the hereafter — misses all bliss and deserves Hellfire
الخسران الجزئي Partial loss Loses in some respects but not others — the believer who falls short in some of the four qualities
الصفات الأربع The Four Qualities — the exception from loss

Al-Saʿdī says: those exempted from loss must have all four qualities — not just one or two. Lacking any one of them means remaining in some degree of loss.

١

الإيمان

Faith — believing what Allah commanded us to believe

Al-Saʿdī notes: faith cannot be sound unless it is based on knowledge. Belief stems from knowledge and cannot be perfect without it. This is why seeking knowledge is an obligation — without it, faith has no foundation.

٢

العمل الصالح

Righteous deeds — all good deeds, outward and inward

Al-Saʿdī says this includes all good deeds — both duties toward Allah and duties toward His slaves, both obligatory acts and recommended ones. It is not limited to prayer alone; it is the full scope of righteous action.

٣

التواصي بالحق

Exhorting one another to the truth

Not just doing righteous deeds yourself — but urging, encouraging, and reminding others. Al-Saʿdī says: they exhort one another and encourage one another to believe and act rightly. This is why a family that learns together is far more powerful than one person learning alone.

٤

التواصي بالصبر

Exhorting one another to steadfast patience

Patience in obeying Allah, patience in refraining from disobedience, and patience in accepting the painful decrees of Allah. Al-Saʿdī says: the first two qualities perfect oneself; the last two help others perfect themselves. You cannot be truly exempt from loss if you only look after yourself.

Al-Saʿdī's summary

By perfecting all four qualities a person will be safe from loss and attain great success. The first two perfect yourself; the last two help perfect others. This is also why Dār al-Hijrah exists as a family school — the third and fourth qualities require a community, a household, people exhorting one another.

Memorize & Live — احفظ وعِش

إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ

This sūrah is three āyāt. Every Muslim should have it memorized. The family practice: after ʿAṣr prayer, recite it together. Then name one way you lived each of the four qualities today — one act of faith, one righteous deed, one time you urged someone toward good, one moment you held firm with patience. That discussion alone is the third and fourth quality in action.
