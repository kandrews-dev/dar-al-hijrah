Sūrah 104 · Juzʾ ʿAmma · Makki · 9 āyāt

سورة الهمزة

Sūrat al-Humazah — The Backbiter

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Distinguish between الهمزة (the backbiter — who mocks with actions and gestures) and اللمزة (the fault-finder — who criticises in words).
  • Understand the root disease: wealth-worship — hoarding, counting, thinking money buys immortality.
  • Learn what الحطمة is — and why Allah asks "what could tell you what it is?" — a sign of its extreme severity.
  • Understand the unique description of this fire: it reaches into the hearts — because the sin was of the heart (pride, envy, contempt).
الآيات The Āyāt

وَيْلٌ لِّكُلِّ هُمَزَةٍ لُّمَزَةٍ ۝ ٱلَّذِى جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُۥ ۝ يَحْسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُۥٓ أَخْلَدَهُۥ ۝ كَلَّا ۚ لَيُنۢبَذَنَّ فِى ٱلْحُطَمَةِ ۝ وَمَآ أَدْرَىٰكَ مَا ٱلْحُطَمَةُ ۝ نَارُ ٱللَّهِ ٱلْمُوقَدَةُ ۝ ٱلَّتِى تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى ٱلْأَفْـِٔدَةِ ۝ إِنَّهَا عَلَيْهِم مُّؤْصَدَةٌ ۝ فِى عَمَدٍ مُّمَدَّدَةٍۭ

Woe to every backbiter, fault-finder — who accumulates wealth and keeps on counting it, thinking that his wealth will make him live forever. By no means! He will surely be flung into the crushing fire — and how could you know what the crushing fire is? It is Allah's own kindled fire, which will reach right into the hearts. It will confine them behind doors barred with huge beams.

al-Humazah 104:1–9

التفسير Explanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

وَيْلٌ (Woe): Al-Saʿdī says this is a warning of bad consequences and severe punishment — a word that signals that what follows describes a person heading for destruction.

الهمزة واللمزة — two kinds of contempt

Al-Saʿdī distinguishes the two words precisely:

الهمزة — one who mocks and criticises people through actions and gestures: a sneer, a rolled eye, a dismissive wave, a mimicking motion. The contempt is non-verbal but unmistakable.

اللمزة — one who criticises people in words: finding fault openly, putting others down in speech, speaking ill of people behind their backs or to their face.

Together they describe a person who has contempt for others — in both action and word. This person looks down on people.

الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ (who accumulates wealth and keeps counting it): Al-Saʿdī says this is the root characteristic of the backbiter and fault-finder — he is entirely consumed by wealth accumulation. He collects it and counts it repeatedly, exulting in it. He has no desire to spend it on good causes, on upholding ties of kinship, or on anything beyond himself.

يَحْسَبُ أَنَّ مَالَهُ أَخْلَدَهُ (thinking his wealth will make him live forever): In his ignorance, he believes his wealth will extend his life indefinitely — so all his energy goes toward growing it. Al-Saʿdī adds a gem here: stinginess shortens life and leads to ruin; generosity increases one's lifespan. The very thing he clings to is destroying him.

الحطمة The Crushing Fire

كَلَّا (By no means!): A sharp rebuke — his wealth will not save him, will not extend his life, will not protect him from what comes next. Then the sentence: لَيُنبَذَنَّ فِي الْحُطَمَةِ — he will be flung into the crushing fire. The word لَيُنبَذَنَّ carries force: thrown, hurled with contempt.

وَمَا أَدْرَاكَ مَا الْحُطَمَةُ (and how could you know what the crushing fire is?): Al-Saʿdī says this question is used to highlight how serious and terrifying the matter is — the style that says: it is beyond what you can imagine. Then Allah Himself describes it.

نَارُ اللَّهِ الْمُوقَدَةُ (Allah's own kindled fire): Not an ordinary fire — it is specifically attributed to Allah, highlighting its enormity.

الَّتِي تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى الْأَفْئِدَةِ (which reaches right into the hearts): Al-Saʿdī says it penetrates deeply into the body and reaches the heart. There is a profound justice here: the sin that brought this person here was one of the heart — pride, contempt, envy of others, love of wealth. The fire reaches exactly where the disease lived.

مُؤْصَدَةٌ فِي عَمَدٍ مُّمَدَّدَةٍ (confined behind doors barred with huge beams): They are detained and sealed in — they will despair of ever emerging. As another āyah states: كُلَّمَا أَرَادُوا أَن يَخْرُجُوا مِنْهَا أُعِيدُوا فِيهَا — every time they want to escape, they are driven back into it. (al-Sajdah 32:20)

الدرس لنا The Lesson for Us

This sūrah connects two things that might seem unrelated: mocking people, and hoarding wealth. Al-Saʿdī links them as one character type — the person who has contempt for others is the same person who is consumed by accumulating wealth and has no desire to give. The internal disease is the same: a heart that sees other people as beneath them and sees money as their security. The fire that reaches into the hearts is the fitting consequence — it touches the source of the disease.

Memorize & Guard — احفظ واحذر

وَيْلٌ لِّكُلِّ هُمَزَةٍ لُّمَزَةٍ · الَّتِى تَطَّلِعُ عَلَى الْأَفْئِدَةِ

Memorize this sūrah. Then reflect as a family: is there a habit of mocking in the home — an eye-roll, a mimicking voice, putting people down in word or gesture? The Quran begins this sūrah with وَيْل. That is not a mild word. The antidote is exactly what this sūrah condemns the hoarder for not doing: give freely, speak kindly, see others as people — not as less than you.
