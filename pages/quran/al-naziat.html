سورة النازعات

Sūrat al-Nāziʿāt — Those Who Drag Forth

Objectives — أهداف الدرس

  • Know al-Saʿdī's explanation of the five opening oaths — the angels with different tasks: those who tear out souls, those who release them gently, those who glide, those who race ahead, and those who manage affairs.
  • Know the story of Mūsā and Firʿawn in this sūrah: the gentle call هَلْ لَّكَ إِلَى أَن تَزَكَّى, the great sign shown to him, Firʿawn's claim أَنَا رَبُّكُمُ الْأَعْلَى, and his punishment نَكَالَ الْآخِرَةِ وَالْأُولَى.
  • Understand the two decisive paths at the Day of the Greatest Calamity (الطَّامَّة الْكُبْرَى): the transgressor who preferred this world → Hell; the one who feared his Lord's standing and restrained his desires → Paradise.
  • Know that the exact time of the Hour is known only to Allah — the Prophet ﷺ's role is only to warn those who fear it.
الآيات — القسم الأولPart One: The Five Oaths and the First Trumpet (1–14)

وَالنَّازِعَاتِ غَرْقًا ۝ وَالنَّاشِطَاتِ نَشْطًا ۝ وَالسَّابِحَاتِ سَبْحًا ۝ فَالسَّابِقَاتِ سَبْقًا ۝ فَالْمُدَبِّرَاتِ أَمْرًا ۝ يَوْمَ تَرْجُفُ الرَّاجِفَةُ ۝ تَتْبَعُهَا الرَّادِفَةُ ۝ قُلُوبٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ وَاجِفَةٌ ۝ أَبْصَارُهَا خَاشِعَةٌ ۝ يَقُولُونَ أَإِنَّا لَمَرْدُودُونَ فِي الْحَافِرَةِ ۝ أَإِذَا كُنَّا عِظَامًا نَّخِرَةً ۝ قَالُوا تِلْكَ إِذًا كَرَّةٌ خَاسِرَةٌ ۝ فَإِنَّمَا هِيَ زَجْرَةٌ وَاحِدَةٌ ۝ فَإِذَا هُم بِالسَّاهِرَةِ

By those who drag forth violently, by those who release gently, by those who glide smoothly, by those who race on ahead, by those who manage affairs — on the Day the first Blast shakes everything, followed by the second — hearts on that Day will be pounding, their eyes cast down. They say: are we really to be returned to our former state? Even after we have become crumbling bones? They say: that would be a disastrous return! But it will be only a single mighty shout — and they will all be on the open surface of the earth.

al-Nāziʿāt 79:1–14

الآيات — القسم الثانيPart Two: Mūsā and Firʿawn (15–26)

هَلْ أَتَاكَ حَدِيثُ مُوسَى ۝ إِذْ نَادَاهُ رَبُّهُ بِالْوَادِ الْمُقَدَّسِ طُوًى ۝ اذْهَبْ إِلَى فِرْعَوْنَ إِنَّهُ طَغَى ۝ فَقُلْ هَل لَّكَ إِلَى أَن تَزَكَّى ۝ وَأَهْدِيَكَ إِلَى رَبِّكَ فَتَخْشَى ۝ فَأَرَاهُ الْآيَةَ الْكُبْرَى ۝ فَكَذَّبَ وَعَصَى ۝ ثُمَّ أَدْبَرَ يَسْعَى ۝ فَحَشَرَ فَنَادَى ۝ فَقَالَ أَنَا رَبُّكُمُ الْأَعْلَى ۝ فَأَخَذَهُ اللَّهُ نَكَالَ الْآخِرَةِ وَالْأُولَى ۝ إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً لِّمَن يَخْشَى

Has the story of Mūsā reached you? When his Lord called to him in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā: Go to Firʿawn — he has transgressed all bounds — and say to him: Would you like to purify yourself? And I will guide you to your Lord, so that you may fear Him. Then Mūsā showed him the greatest sign — but he denied and disobeyed, then turned his back and strove against it, gathered his people and called out, saying: I am your highest lord. So Allah seized him with the punishment of the next life and this one. Indeed in this there is a lesson for whoever fears.

al-Nāziʿāt 79:15–26

الآيات — القسم الثالثPart Three: Signs in Creation, Then the Two Paths (27–46)

أَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا أَمِ السَّمَاءُ بَنَاهَا ۝ رَفَعَ سَمْكَهَا فَسَوَّاهَا ۝ وَأَغْطَشَ لَيْلَهَا وَأَخْرَجَ ضُحَاهَا ۝ وَالْأَرْضَ بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ دَحَاهَا ۝ أَخْرَجَ مِنْهَا مَاءَهَا وَمَرْعَاهَا ۝ وَالْجِبَالَ أَرْسَاهَا ۝ مَتَاعًا لَّكُمْ وَلِأَنْعَامِكُمْ ۝ فَإِذَا جَاءَتِ الطَّامَّةُ الْكُبْرَى ۝ يَوْمَ يَتَذَكَّرُ الْإِنسَانُ مَا سَعَى ۝ وَبُرِّزَتِ الْجَحِيمُ لِمَن يَرَى ۝ فَأَمَّا مَن طَغَى ۝ وَآثَرَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا ۝ فَإِنَّ الْجَحِيمَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَى ۝ وَأَمَّا مَن خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى ۝ فَإِنَّ الْجَنَّةَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَى ۝ يَسْأَلُونَكَ عَنِ السَّاعَةِ أَيَّانَ مُرْسَاهَا ۝ فِيمَ أَنتَ مِن ذِكْرَاهَا ۝ إِلَى رَبِّكَ مُنتَهَاهَا ۝ إِنَّمَا أَنتَ مُنذِرُ مَن يَخْشَاهَا ۝ كَأَنَّهُمْ يَوْمَ يَرَوْنَهَا لَمْ يَلْبَثُوا إِلَّا عَشِيَّةً أَوْ ضُحَاهَا

Are you more difficult to create, or is the heaven that He built — raising its canopy high and making it perfect, darkening its night and bringing forth its morning brightness? After that He spread out the earth, bringing forth from it its water and pasture, and He set the mountains firmly in place — a provision for you and your livestock. Then when the Greatest Overwhelming Calamity comes — the Day when man will recall all that he strove for, and Hell is made visible to all who can see — as for him who transgressed and preferred the life of this world, then Hell is his abode. But as for him who feared the standing before his Lord and restrained his soul from its desires — then Paradise is his abode. They ask you about the Hour: when will it anchor? What concern is it of yours to know its time? To your Lord belongs its end. You are only a warner to those who fear it. On the Day they see it, they will feel as if they had only stayed a single evening or its morning.

al-Nāziʿāt 79:27–46

التفسيرExplanation — based on Imam al-Saʿdī

The sūrah opens with five oaths by angels — each group with its own task. Al-Saʿdī: النَّازِعَاتِ are the angels who tear souls out of the bodies of the wicked with tremendous force, like pulling a thorn through wet wool; النَّاشِطَاتِ are those who draw out the souls of the righteous gently, releasing them easily; السَّابِحَاتِ glide through the heavens swiftly; السَّابِقَاتِ race ahead to carry out Allah's commands; الْمُدَبِّرَاتِ manage and arrange the affairs of the creation. All of this is testimony to an orderly cosmos run by beings who answer only to Allah — and this order itself is evidence that resurrection is not beyond the One who created it.

هَلْ لَّكَ إِلَى أَن تَزَكَّى — Would You Like to Purify Yourself?

Mūsā's call to Firʿawn was gentle: would you like to purify yourself? Not a demand. A gentle invitation. Al-Saʿdī notes the wisdom: even the most arrogant tyrant is approached with softness first, in case there remains a flicker of willingness. The great sign was shown — and Firʿawn's response was to turn his back, gather his people, make speeches, and declare himself their highest lord. The fall was total: Allah seized him with the punishment of both worlds — drowned in this life; the fire in the next. The lesson is stated plainly: إِنَّ فِي ذَلِكَ لَعِبْرَةً لِّمَن يَخْشَى — in this there is a lesson for whoever fears. The one who does not fear does not learn from history.

أَأَنتُمْ أَشَدُّ خَلْقًا أَمِ السَّمَاءُ — Are You Harder to Create than the Heaven? The deniers of resurrection thought themselves too complex to be re-created from crumbled bones. Allah replies: look at the heaven above you — raised high, perfected, its night darkened, its light brought out. Look at the earth spread out, with water and pasture drawn from it, mountains firmly set. If Allah created all of that, He can certainly recreate you. The provision in it — water, pasture, mountains — is for you and your animals. Al-Saʿdī: this combination of cosmic sign and immediate benefit is how the Quran makes the abstract concrete.

الطَّامَّة الْكُبْرَى — The Greatest Overwhelming Calamity: On that Day everything a person ever worked for will be brought to mind. Hell will be shown openly. Then comes the fork in the road — the two outcomes: مَن طَغَى وَآثَرَ الْحَيَاةَ الدُّنْيَا — whoever transgressed and chose this world over the next: Hell is his final home. مَن خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى — whoever feared standing before his Lord and restrained his soul from its desires: Paradise is his final home. Al-Saʿdī: نَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى means actively opposing the soul's cravings — the nafs wants what harms it; the believer's work is to discipline it. This is the greatest jihad. The sūrah closes: they will feel, on the Day they see it, as if the whole of their worldly life was only an afternoon or morning — so short in comparison to what awaits.

Memorize & Hold On — احفظ وثبت

وَأَمَّا مَن خَافَ مَقَامَ رَبِّهِ وَنَهَى النَّفْسَ عَنِ الْهَوَى ۝ فَإِنَّ الْجَنَّةَ هِيَ الْمَأْوَى

These two āyāt are a complete map for the believer's inner life. Fear the standing before your Lord — hold it present when desire calls and anger rises. Restrain your soul from what it craves that displeases Allah — its food, its anger, its arrogance, its laziness, its lust. The work is hard, the nafs resists, the path is steep. But the destination is stated plainly: then Paradise is his abode. Not "maybe" — Paradise, full stop.
